Study 47, a Scripture & Theology study of the book of Leviticus. The folder keeps the study's planned name, *the law of Moses and the Christian*, as its content folder does. Its one habit: **read each law twice, first where it stood in Israel, then with what the New Testament does with it, and only then ask what it asks of you.** The page draws that habit as one plan of the camp read two ways, and as Israel and the New Testament side by side in every part, with "where the reading stops" beneath.

## What is built

- **The standard heading:** study trail, five concepts in gold capitals, *Leviticus:* in ink with *holiness and sacrifice* in gold italic, the lead (Exodus ends with a closed door), Leviticus 1:1 printed, and a line drawing of the court and the Tent of Meeting with the cloud settled over it and smoke rising slowly from the altar, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, with the motto "The LORD called / from the Tent of Meeting." (Leviticus 1:1's own words, checked). No face or figure of God. A bar of four counted figures, each saying what it means, and links to every part.
- **00 The camp, read twice:** the study's opening paragraphs, its three questions, and the signature graphic: a plan of the land, the camp, the court with the altar, the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place behind the veil, with the wilderness beyond. Each of parts 01–07 is a numbered point where its chapters happen. A switch reads the plan *In Israel* (the verse that puts the part there) or *As the New Testament reads it* (the verse that answers it); in the second reading the veil opens and a dashed way runs through it. Pointing at or choosing a number shows its line, which opens the verse and the part. The reading and the point live in the address (`?read=nt&at=03`).
- **01–08 The parts:** a wide head (large numeral, a miniature of the same plan with that part's place lit, title with its turn in italics, the hook, the main readings), then *In Israel* and *How the New Testament reads it* in two columns, then *Where the reading stops* under a dashed rule, then Consider and Reflect. Part 06 adds **Holy time counts in sevens**: day, month and year with the seventh marked, then the fifty days and the fifty years drawn as seven sevens and one more, each row in its verse's words. Part 08 sets its three Baptist families in three equal columns, then **Two verses, three routes**: choose Leviticus 19:18 or 19:19 and each family's route to it is shown in the study's own words, ending in the same place. The study's sentence is the caption: they differ more in how they reach a command than in where they arrive, though the Sabbath is one place they arrive differently. The page does not choose.
- **Close:** Leviticus 26:11–12 beside 2 Corinthians 6:16, the study's closing paragraph and question, "Read the passage again before you answer", Leviticus 19:9–18 printed in full beside them, the sources with what was read, and a back link.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet (Berean), with the verse in the address (`?ref=`); Escape closes it and returns focus to the link.

**Could be confused with:** *Covenants and promises* ([covenants-and-promises-directions](../covenants-and-promises-directions/)), the study in the same category that owns Sinai and the law and the promise, and *The good news* ([the-good-news-directions](../the-good-news-directions/)), also in God & Christian Faith. It is not like either. Covenants and promises opens on a sky of concentric stars with a lettered switcher; this page's figure is a rectilinear plan of a place, read twice. The good news runs its parts down one dotted thread with a sticky rail; this page has no thread or rail, and each part is a wide head over two parallel columns with a dashed edge beneath. Its colour is History orange; theirs are gold and violet.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py) reads the study's content.md, checks it, and writes [data.js](data.js). It uses [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (a copy of The good news mock-up's reader, only its first line changed), [held_text.py](held_text.py) (word matching, the held outside sources and the Berean's footnotes; the parser and held-source check follow the Angels, demons and spiritual powers build) and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's own lists: the title, concepts and motto, the plan, the sevens, the routes, the named studies and the sources). Run from the Website folder:

- `python design/the-law-of-moses-and-the-christian-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js after every check passes;
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is stale (data.js carries a fingerprint of content.md and the four build files, so a change to any of them makes it stale);
- `--selftest` feeds wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused; it writes nothing.

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/lev.js](js/lev.js), [js/plan.js](js/plan.js) (the plan's fixed geometry), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the hero and closing drawings) and [css/lev.css](css/lev.css), with the frame borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups. Nothing under `src/` was touched, and content.md was not changed.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

Results of the last run, 2026-11-07, from the Website folder: build "all checks pass; wrote data.js"; `--check` "all checks pass; data.js is current" (exit 0); `--selftest` "16 of 16 wrong inputs refused as expected; nothing written".

- **By script on every build:** every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page; the opening, the three questions, parts 01 to 08 with their exact sub-heads, and the close have the shape the page draws. Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference. Every reference exists in the Berean with no gap in a range; "Leviticus 16" and "Leviticus 23" in the main readings open the whole chapter. A quotation followed by its own reference must be in that reference; any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings or the passage printed just above; a quotation under three words with no reference beside it must be pinned to a verse (seven are: "sins defiantly" Numbers 15:30, "perfection" Hebrews 7:11, "unauthorized" Leviticus 10:1, "Scapegoat" Leviticus 16:8, "Be holy" Leviticus 19:2, "liberty" Leviticus 25:10, and the close's "Be holy, because I am holy." 1 Peter 1:16). A short reference such as "(4:35)" is Leviticus unless listed; the build refuses one that follows another book's reference with no "Leviticus" between and is not listed (five are listed: 10:4 Hebrews in part 01, 10:28 Acts in 03, 9:12 and 10:20 Hebrews in 04, 10:37 Luke in 05). No reference is left unlinked. Links go only to this site.
- **Outside quotations, checked against held copies on this PC:** all thirteen, each found within 1,500 characters of a place-marker that fixes it to the right chapter or article: the Baptist Faith and Message (Articles II and VIII, from the copy in the site's question sources), the Second London Baptist Confession (chapter 19, from the creeds collection), Calvin's Institutes 4.20.14 (Beveridge), Matthew Henry on Leviticus 10 and Bonar on leprosy. One scanning fault in the held Calvin is corrected in memory and named in study_lists.py: "well-known" is split across pages 536–537 with the running head between.
- **The Berean's footnotes:** the three footnote phrases the study quotes ("thereby expelling all foods" on Mark 7:19, "as a propitiation" on Romans 3:25, "to Azazel" on Leviticus 16:8) are in the footnotes of those verses in the site's marked-up Berean.
- **The drawn lists:** every plan line (seven places and their New Testament answers, and the goat sent into the wilderness), the plan's sentence ("once a year" 16:34; "not to enter freely" 16:2), every row of the sevens and "seven times seven years" (25:8), and the motto are in their verses. Every phrase in *Two verses, three routes* is the study's own words in part 08, and the families are the study's three in its order. The figure 8 is counted from the Berean: New Testament verses holding "love your neighbor as yourself" (Matthew 19:19, 22:39; Mark 12:31, 12:33; Luke 10:27; Romans 13:9; Galatians 5:14; James 2:8). The 27 chapters are counted from the Berean too.
- **Refused on purpose (`--selftest`):** sixteen wrong inputs, all refused for the right reason: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quote cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist; a short reference after another book with no book given; a link that leaves the site; a line the page cannot place; a changed word in an outside quotation; a changed word in a quoted footnote; a named study with no link; a plan point whose words are not in its verse; a seven whose words are in another verse; a route the study does not say; a footnote looked for on the wrong verse; and an outside quotation given to the wrong held source.
- **What a pass does not prove:** that each reference is the right verse for the point being made, or that the reading of it is right; a verse-existence check proves only that the verse exists. The places on the plan are this page's reading of where each part stands, each anchored to a verse; the plan is a schematic, not to scale, and says so. Whether each family would put its route in exactly these words is the study's claim, not the page's.

## One allowance, for the owner's eye

The block quotation of **Leviticus 16:21** prints only the verse's first sentence ("...in regard to all their sins."); the verse goes on, "He is to put them on the goat's head and send it away...". The build allows a block that stops at the end of a sentence, and the page marks it "BSB, part of the passage"; the full verse is in its sheet. Every other block is the whole passage, or marks its cut with "...".
