Study 41, a Scripture & Theology study in Jesus Christ. Its one habit: **draw only the lines the New Testament draws, then look for where each picture stops.** The shape comes from the text. In Luke 24:44 Jesus names the three parts of His disciples' Bible, "the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms", and the five figures the study walks reach all three. Colossians 2:17 gives the method its word, a shadow: it has the shape of what casts it, and it ends somewhere. So the page draws lines, and marks where they stop.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py), with [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (a copy of The good news mock-up's Berean reader, unchanged) and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's own lists), reads the study's content.md, checks it against the site's Berean text and the held copies of the three outside sources, and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder:

- `python design/christ-in-the-old-testament-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js (through a temporary file, so a failed write cannot leave half a file).
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is not exactly what its inputs build now. It writes nothing. data.js records a fingerprint of each of its four inputs.
- `--selftest` feeds twelve wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused for the right reason. It writes nothing.

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/cot.js](js/cot.js) (the page), [js/map.js](js/map.js) (the two data drawings), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the fixed line drawings) and [css/cot.css](css/cot.css). The frame is borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups, as in the model. Nothing under `src/` was touched. The parser is this page's own, because this study's text has its own shape (a numbered list of three questions, sub-heads in every part, short references like "(12:27 · BSB)", and two "[check: …]" notes). Only the Berean reader is shared. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

**content.md was not changed.** Its two "[check: …]" notes (part 05: David Kimchi's reading of Isaiah 53, and the Targum's wording at Isaiah 52:13) are notes to check facts, not link notes, so the brief does not allow turning them into anything. The page shows each as a small gold "to be checked" mark, with the note's words in its title and for screen readers, so an unread source is never presented as read. They must be checked, or the sentences cut, before release.

## The page

- **Opening.** The standard heading: the study trail (Study / Jesus Christ / Christ in the Old Testament); the concepts in gold capitals, The Law of Moses · The Prophets · The Psalms, the three parts Jesus names, checked in Luke 24:44; the title in ink with *Old Testament* as the gold italic turn; the lead, the study's own opening on the Emmaus road; Luke 24:27 printed in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: the road at evening, a village on a rise with one lit window, the sun low, and an unrolled scroll in front whose columns fall into three groups. No one is drawn on the road. The motto, "Beginning with Moses / and all the Prophets", is Luke 24:27's own words, checked in that verse.
- **Four counts**, each computed on the build and each with what it means: 5 figures; 19 lines drawn from a New Testament verse to an Old Testament one, 4 of them dashed because the source is uncertain; 3 of 3 parts of Luke 24:44 reached; 36 verses printed in full.
- **00 The lines it draws.** The frame paragraphs. Then **the anatomy of one line**: a New Testament verse at the bottom (question 1, where the New Testament draws the line), the curve up to the Old Testament passage (question 2, what it meant first), and a bar where the line ends (question 3, where the picture stops), with the three questions set under their places. Then **the map**: the Old Testament across the top in Luke 24:44's three parts, the New Testament along the bottom in canonical order, one curve per line. Buttons pick a figure, "Further lines" shows the six part 06 names, "Every line" shows all 25. Pointing at or tabbing to a curve shows both verses' words on the line beneath; choosing it opens the New Testament verse. Solid curves: the verse quotes or names it. Dashed: one of several possible sources (John 1:29 to Exodus 12 or Isaiah 53; John 19:36 to Exodus 12:46 or Psalm 34:20), as the study itself says. Below 720 pixels wide the map is left out and the same lines are listed by figure. "This is a selection, not a census" follows, in the study's words.
- **01–05 The five figures.** Each part opens with a large number, a small drawing (a doorframe marked on the posts and lintel; the tent under the cloud; the veil with the censer; a crown; a chariot wheel and an open scroll), the title with its turn in gold italic, the hook in italic and the main readings. The reading runs in one column; its sub-heads carry the same three marks as the anatomy (a circle for the first meaning, a curve for the New Testament's line, a bar for where it stops; part 05's "How Jewish readers read the song" has its own double circle, in gold). Beside the reading, **the line** for that figure runs down a stem: "Then" (the first meaning, in the Old Testament's words), "Drawn by" (each New Testament verse's words), and "Stops", each opening its verse. The temple's stem does not stop: it "Runs on" to 1 Corinthians 3:16 and Revelation 21:22, as the study says. Consider and Reflect close each part.
- **06 The line and its limits.** The same layout, with a different stem: the six further lines the New Testament draws (Adam, the sea, the flood, Jonah, the bronze snake, the manna), then below a dotted rule the bronze snake that became an idol (2 Kings 18:4), and First Clement's scarlet cord, drawn dotted and grey and labelled "Beyond the line": Joshua 2 does not say it.
- **Close.** "Read the Old Testament with Him": the closing paragraph with Luke 24:32, the reader's question, "Read the passage again before you answer.", and Luke 24:13–35 printed in full beside them. Then a table at evening (a broken loaf, a lamp, the scroll, a lit window), the sources, and a link back to Jesus Christ.
- **Every reference**, including the short ones ("12:27"), opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean. The sheet's reference goes in the address (`?ref=`).

**Colour:** the Prophets' blue. The study asks how the Old Testament speaks ahead of Christ; Jesus names the Prophets in Luke 24:27 and 24:44. The site's gold marks the concepts, the turns, the anatomy's numbers and the notes still to be checked.

## Why it does not look like its neighbours

- **The page it could be confused with is Covenants and promises** ([covenants-and-promises-directions](../covenants-and-promises-directions/)). Both draw lines from an Old Testament passage to the later verses that come back to it, and both note that Hebrews 1:5 stops before 2 Samuel 7:14's "when he does wrong". They differ in shape and in purpose. Covenants and promises draws the whole Bible as one line with arcs rising from it, one bundle per covenant, and its unit is the five-question table. This page has two shelves, the Old Testament split into the three parts Jesus names and the New Testament beneath, with every curve crossing between them; its unit is one line with three places on it, and each part carries that line as a vertical stem that ends in a stop. The covenants page is in God & Christian Faith; this one is in Jesus Christ.
- **[Angels, demons and spiritual powers](../angels-demons-and-spiritual-powers-directions/)** also has three questions with marks and a "where it stops" step. There the boundary is one dashed divide that each part crosses, with two columns of prose and a veiled band beyond. Here there is no divide and no band: the prose is one column, and the stop is a point at the end of a drawn line beside it.
- **[The good news](../the-good-news-directions/)** runs its parts down one dotted thread with a sticky rail on the left. Here the parts are separate, the number sits in the part's header, and the sticky column on the right is a data stem, not a title rail.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

**Recorded run, 2026-11-07:** `python design/christ-in-the-old-testament-directions/build-data.py` printed "build-data: all checks pass; wrote data.js (88,836 bytes)" (the script counts characters; the file is 89,650 bytes on disk), then `--check` printed "build-data: all checks pass; data.js is current" and exited 0, and `--selftest` printed "12 of 12 wrong inputs refused for the right reason".

**By script on every build:**
- Every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, or the build fails. The study must have the lead, the opening quotation, exactly three numbered questions, parts 01–06 in order, each with main readings, a hook, Consider and Reflect, and a close with the reader's question and "Read the passage again before you answer." The draft's status line ("Study 41 · Jesus Christ · First content draft") is kept in data.js but not shown.
- Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference: Luke 24:27, Exodus 12:13 and 12:14, John 1:14, Hebrews 7:27, 2 Samuel 7:12, Isaiah 52:13 and 53:5, Acts 8:32–35 and Colossians 2:17.
- Every reference exists in the Berean, with no gap in a range: 106 different passages.
- A bare reference such as "(12:27 · BSB)" takes its book only from a table in study_lists.py, keyed by part. The build fails on a bare reference with no entry, and on an entry the text no longer uses. "1 Clement 12:7" is listed as not Scripture.
- A quotation with its own reference beside it must be in that reference. Any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above it. One word is quoted as a word, not a quotation ("type"), and is on a named list.
- **All three quotations from outside Scripture are found by script in copies held on this PC**, each within 1,500 characters after a named marker: the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Article I, in the site's question sources; the Berean's own footnote on John 1:14 ("and tabernacled among us") in the site's Berean text with notes; and First Clement 12:7 in J. B. Lightfoot's translation, paragraph `ii.i-p72` of the local CCEL file.
- **Every drawn phrase is in its verse**, as whole words, exact case, at least two words long: both ends of all 25 lines on the map, each part's "Then" phrase and stop phrases, the bronze snake and the scarlet cord, the concepts and the motto. Two absences are proved: Exodus 12 never has "sin offering", and Hebrews 1:5 has no "discipline". Each map line's Old Testament end must lie in one of Luke 24:44's three parts, its New Testament end in the New Testament, and the five figures together must reach all three parts.
- A study named in the text links to its mock-up only if that folder has an index.html; the build fails if a listed folder loses it, or if the text names a study not on the list. Four link (Who do you say that I am?, The cross, the empty tomb and the throne, Covenants and promises, The book of Psalms). Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice and What Scripture promises the sick are being designed in this same run and are shown in italics as not yet pages.
- Links may only go to this site: anything not starting with "/", starting with "//", or containing ".." fails.

**Refused on purpose** (`--selftest`, twelve wrong inputs in memory, data.js untouched, each refused with the expected reason): a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist (John 1:99); a link off the site; a sub-head before any part; a bare reference with no book; an altered outside quotation; a map phrase not in its verse; an absence that is not absent; a stop phrase not in its verse.

**What these checks do not prove:** that the reading is right, or that a line is the one the New Testament writer had in mind. The map's endpoints are this page's choices. Most are the study's own references; seven are added by this page and are the likeliest to be questioned: Exodus 12:21 for 1 Corinthians 5:7, Hebrews 7:17 for the Psalm 110:4 quotation, Psalm 2:7 as the other half of Hebrews 1:5, Genesis 2:17 for Adam in Romans 5:14 (the command he broke; Genesis 3 would also serve), Exodus 14:22 for 1 Corinthians 10:1, Genesis 7:13 for 1 Peter 3:20, and Exodus 16:4 for John 6:32.

**A fault found and fixed in the inherited reader logic:** the model's pattern for short continuations ("; 44:1") also read "Romans 5:14; 1 Corinthians" as Romans 5:1, and "; 2 Kings" as verse 2. This page's copy of the pattern no longer takes a number followed by a book name. [The good news build](../the-good-news-directions/build-data.py) still has the old pattern; it was not touched, because it is another study's folder.

**By browser against the always-on preview** (2026-11-07, Playwright):
- 1440 wide, light: the page is exactly as wide as the window, 6 parts, 223 verse links, 25 curves, "19 lines shown", no console errors or warnings.
- 390 dark, 320 dark and 320 light: the page is exactly as wide as the window (nothing scrolls sideways); the map is hidden and the list shown.
- A verse link (Leviticus 16:15 in part 03) opens its sheet with the verse and puts `?ref=LEV.16.15` in the address; Escape closes it, clears the address and returns focus to the link. Tabbing to a curve fills the readout; Enter opens Hebrews 7:17. "The king" button shows 4 lines and hides the rest from the tab order. `?ref=ISA.53.5` opens Isaiah 53:5 on load; `?ref=__proto__` is ignored.

**Not tested:** 200% zoom, a real screen reader, a real phone, and reduced motion in a browser (the CSS stops every loop and transition under `prefers-reduced-motion`, but this was read, not watched). Colour contrast was not measured; the dashed curves and the grey "beyond the line" entry are the likeliest places for a problem.
