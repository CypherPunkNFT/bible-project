Study 56, a Scripture & Theology study in Living the Christian Life. Its one habit: **when a passage records someone's darkest words, keep reading to what God does next.** The page's signature measures that, in verses of the Berean: from Moses' "I cannot carry all these people by myself" to "Bring Me seventy of the elders of Israel" is two verses; from Elijah's "I have had enough, LORD" to the angel's "Get up and eat", one; from Job's "Why is light given to the miserable" to "Then the LORD answered Job out of the whirlwind", 870 verses and 35 chapters; Psalm 56:3 holds the fear and the trust in one sentence; and Heman's Psalm 88 runs four verses on to its end with no turn at all. Help comes first, before the lead.

The study has no working.md; the page was made from content.md and passage-notes.md.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py), with [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (a byte-for-byte copy of The good news mock-up's Berean reader), [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's own lists) and [drawn.py](drawn.py) (the checks behind each figure), reads the study's content.md, checks it against the site's Berean text and the one outside source held on this PC, and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder:

- `python design/fear-anxiety-and-despair-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js (through a temporary file, so a killed run never leaves half a file).
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is stale. It writes nothing.
- `--selftest` ([selftest.py](selftest.py)) feeds twenty wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused for the expected reason. It writes nothing.

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/fad.js](js/fad.js) (help, heading, section 00, the parts, questions and close), [js/fad-figures.js](js/fad-figures.js) (each part's figure), [js/fad-core.js](js/fad-core.js) (text runs, frame, verse sheet), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the drawings), [css/fad.css](css/fad.css) and [css/fad-parts.css](css/fad-parts.css). The frame is borrowed, read-only, from the Authors mock-ups, as in the model. The parser started from the [Learning to discern](../learning-to-discern-directions/build-data.py) build, the nearest in format, and was made this page's own: a help section before the lead, a "Words" field, part fields Hook, Line, Setting and Limit, "[source:…]" notes, whole-chapter references ("Job 3", "Psalm 88", "Psalms 42–43"), a close with its own quotation, and quotations whose wording is not the Berean's. Nothing under `src/` was touched, and **content.md was not changed**: its "[check: …]" notes are about crisis numbers and a lecture's place in Spurgeon's book, not links on this site, so none could be turned into a link. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

The page as built is described section by section in [design.md](../../content/studies/christian-life/fear-anxiety-and-despair/design.md).

## Why it does not look like its neighbours

- **The page it could be confused with is [What Scripture promises the sick](../illness-and-healing-directions/).** Both are Christian Life studies that read Job and Psalm 88, and both set people in rows with their words in quotation marks; Heman's "darkness is my closest companion" closes a row on both. They differ in what the rows measure. That page is a register of outcomes: what was wrong, what was prayed, and whether the text says the sick person recovered, with buttons to filter by outcome. This page is a measure of reading: each row is a line of verse marks from the cry to the next thing the text records, so its point is the distance, and it has no outcome to filter. That page has no help band and no "keep reading" line in its parts; it opens each part with a full-width head and reaches figures out of one column, as this page also does, so the parts here add the hook-and-line rule and the thread with ringed sub-headings to tell them apart.
- **[Faith under pressure](../faith-under-pressure-directions/)** sets six pressures as dark cards with large drawings on a near-black panel; this page has no dark panel and no cards. **[The good news](../the-good-news-directions/)** runs its parts down a thread with a sticky left rail and a ledger of news and response; this page has no rail and no ledger. **[Learning to discern](../learning-to-discern-directions/)** keeps a sticky rail with a three-dot mark beside every part; this page's parts have none.
- **Its own look:** a help band with a gold rule above the heading; a page ground that moves from dusk at the top to first light at the close; lines of verse marks; poems set in small type with their refrain in full ink.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

**Recorded run, 2026-11-07, about 08:50 local time:** `python design/fear-anxiety-and-despair-directions/build-data.py` printed "all checks pass; wrote data.js (111,486 bytes; 14 points still marked to check before publishing)"; `--check` printed "all checks pass; data.js is current" and exited 0; `--selftest` printed "20 of 20 wrong inputs refused for the expected reason; nothing written".

**By script on every build:**
- Every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page. A heading, note marker or other line the page does not know fails the build, as does a missing help list or care note before the lead, a part without main readings, hook, line, Consider or Reflect, a study without parts 01–06 and four questions, and a close without its way, quotation, invitation, help, question and passage. Part 06 has no "Limit:" line in the study; the page shows a limit only where the study gives one.
- Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference. Seven quote part of their verse (Numbers 11:17, 1 Kings 19:3, 1 Kings 19:4, Jeremiah 20:11, Psalm 43:3, Psalm 46:1–3 and 46:10–11, the last two without the closing "Selah"); a partial block must be an unbroken run of at least six of the verse's own words, and the page labels it "part of the verse".
- Every reference exists in the Berean, with no gap in a range: 99 different passages. Whole chapters are read to their last verse in the Berean, and a chapter that does not exist fails. Continuations such as "(Psalm 42:5, 42:11, 43:5)" and "Jeremiah 20:13 and 20:14" take their book from the reference before them; any other bare "chapter:verse" fails.
- A quotation with its own reference beside it must be in that reference. Any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above it; under three words it must be pinned to a verse ("Be still" to Psalm 46:10). Quotations in part headings are checked too. Matching is by whole words, ignoring quotation marks.
- **Three quotations are not the Berean's wording, and the build proves both halves of that:** each is not in its verse, and the Berean's own words named beside it are. The page marks each with a gold † and lists them for the owner (see below). If the study's text is corrected, the build fails until the entry is removed.
- The one outside quotation, "contend for the sanctity of all human life from conception to natural death" (Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article XV), is found by script in the site's [DOCTRINAL-FOUNDATION.md](../../../DOCTRINAL-FOUNDATION.md). Held sources may only be read from inside the BibleProject folder. Two titles from outside Scripture (Spurgeon's lecture "The Minister's Fainting Fits"; Luther's "A Mighty Fortress Is Our God") are named, not quoted, and not held.
- Every phrase drawn outside the running text is in its verse: the eight rows (cry and what follows), the motto's two sources (1 Kings 19:6 and 19:13), the seventy elders, the three word pairs of part 01, the ten steps of part 02 (and they must be in the verses' order), the six marked verses of part 03, Psalm 88's three words of trust, Psalm 46's four clauses and refrain, Psalm 56:3, the five commands of part 06 with their reasons, and Isaiah 43:2.
- Counts are computed, never typed: each row's distance in verses and chapters (Psalm 56:3 must be 0); the refrain must be found exactly three times in Psalm 42:1–43:5; every "do not fear" or "do not be afraid" in Isaiah 41:8–13 and 43:1–7 must be listed (five); Psalm 88:18 must be the psalm's last verse; Jeremiah 20:13 and 20:14 must be neighbours; and Jeremiah 20:17 may never be marked.
- Every entry in the lists (outside quotations, pinned quotations, the study's own terms, wording flags, named studies) must still be used by content.md, so a stale entry fails the build.
- A study named in italics links to its mock-up only where study_lists.py says so, and the build fails if that folder has no index.html. *What Scripture promises the sick*, *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount*, *Covenants and promises*, *Faith under pressure* and *The good news* link. *The wisdom books* has no mock-up yet. *Reading the Bible well* and *Grief and dying* are being designed beside this page in the same run, so they are named in italics only; the run's last step can link them.
- Links may only go to this site; `//…` and `/\…` are refused as well as `https://…`.
- data.js records a fingerprint of each input (content.md, study_lists.py, build-data.py, drawn.py and scripture_text.py, with line endings normalised), so `--check` notices any change.

## For the owner

- **Three quotations are not the Berean's words.** Part 01's heading quotes Moses as "Kill me now"; the Berean reads "please kill me right now" (Numbers 11:15). Part 06's heading quotes "Do not fear, I am with you"; the Berean reads "Do not fear, for I am with you" (Isaiah 41:10). The questions quote Jesus as "deeply grieved" (Mark 14:34); the Berean reads "My soul is consumed with sorrow to the point of death". The study's own notes flag the third. The page marks all three with † and prints them as written; the wording is yours to decide.
- **The crisis lines must be confirmed before publishing**, as the study says: fourteen points, all listed at the foot of the page.
- **Jonah has no row in the signature**, on purpose: his complaint is the wish to die itself ("please take my life from me"), and the page does not set those words in large type. He is read in part 03 as the study has him.
