# Baptism and the Lord's Supper: the mock-up and its record

**Class:** LIVING · **Built:** 2026-11-07, in the page-design run (study 59) · **State:** mock-up for the owner's review; not on the live site
Open: http://127.0.0.1:8931/mockups/baptism-and-the-lords-supper-directions/ · Up: [gallery](../README.md) · Study text: [content.md](../../content/studies/god-and-christian-faith/baptism-and-the-lords-supper/content.md) · Design: [design.md](../../content/studies/god-and-christian-faith/baptism-and-the-lords-supper/design.md) · Run brief: [DESIGN_RUN.md](../../../Pages/Topics/StudyPlan/DESIGN_RUN.md) · Standard: [STUDY-STANDARD.md](../../../Pages/Study/STUDY-STANDARD.md)

A Scripture & Theology study in God & Christian Faith. Its one habit: **ask what the sign points to, and ask what came before it.** The page draws the study's own motto, "Water once. Bread again and again.", as one line of a believer's life that goes under the water once and then passes the table again and again until it runs off the page toward "until He comes". Seven parts are then read one at a time with the same two questions.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py) takes the study's content.md and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py), checks them against the site's Berean text and the outside sources held on this PC, and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder: `python design/baptism-and-the-lords-supper-directions/build-data.py`. `--check` rebuilds in memory, runs the refusal tests and fails if data.js is stale or a file the page loads is missing; `--selftest` runs only the refusal tests. It writes nothing outside this folder (unlike the model build, it writes no file under `src/`).

- [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) is an unchanged copy of The good news mock-up's reader.
- [page_checks.py](page_checks.py) and [selftest.py](selftest.py) are adapted from the Life together mock-up (crisis numbers, study links, page files; the refusal-test harness).
- [held.py](held.py) reads the held outside sources: article VII of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, the creeds collection in [Research/Creeds](../../../Research/Creeds/MANIFEST.md) (1677 confession, New Hampshire, Westminster, Trent, Luther's Small Catechism, the Orthodox Longer Catechism) and the CCEL copies of Barnes and Jamieson, Fausset and Brown.
- The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/bl.js](js/bl.js), [js/art.js](js/art.js) and [css/bl.css](css/bl.css), with the site frame borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups as every study mock-up does. Nothing under `src/` was touched.

One direction was built, as for The good news, because the text already existed.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

- **By script on every build** (run 2026-11-07: `python design/baptism-and-the-lords-supper-directions/build-data.py`, then `--check`, both exit 0; 71 references, 16 verses printed in full):
  - every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, and the page is drawn for parts 01 to 07 exactly;
  - every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference (double quotation marks are not compared, as in the model build, because Mark 14:24's speech runs on into 14:25 and the study closes it);
  - every reference exists with no gap, except one gap the Berean makes on purpose: Acts 8:36–38 steps over 8:37, which the Berean leaves out, and the verse sheet says so; any other gap is refused;
  - every quoted phrase is in the verse its own reference names, or else in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above it; a phrase straight after a "not" is refused unless named (one is: "Cup of blessing", from the question "Is not the cup of blessing…?");
  - each of the ten river crossings quotes only its own passage; the study's "ten" (twice) matches the ten rows; each one-word name is taken from its row's own text; the two households drawn as believing are proved in their verses (Acts 16:34, 18:8), and Lydia's, drawn as left open, is proved to have no verse saying the household believed;
  - each answer in the two-signs figure, both ends of the two back-and-forward strips and the four steps of Acts 2:41–42 are in their one verse, exactly once, not after a negation; the study's own sentence behind each figure is in content.md word for word;
  - 20 of the 23 quotations from outside Scripture are found in the copy held on this PC, each searched only in its own part (article VII; one confession section; one commentary note); the other three are named as not held;
  - three facts about held sources that answer the study's own check notes (see Findings);
  - four crisis numbers match the site's checked list; links go only to this site; named studies link only to mock-up folders that exist.
- **Refused on purpose:** 26 wrong inputs, tried in memory without writing data.js (data.js is hashed before and after), were all refused for the expected reason. Among them: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a quotation that drops its verse's "not"; pieces stitched across verses with no reference of their own; a reference that does not exist; a range over a gap that is not a known omission; a link that leaves the site, and one that climbs out of it; a line the page cannot place; curly quotes the build cannot check; an outside quotation looked for in the wrong section, article or commentary note; a proof text the confession does not print; a wrong count of baptisms; a household drawn as believing, or as left open, against its verses; a sign answer not in its verse; a text patch no longer needed; a bad study link; a crisis number the site's list does not hold. The list is in [selftest.py](selftest.py); `--check` runs it every time.
- **Not checked by script:** whether the readings are right. A script proves the words are in their verses and that the study says what the page says it says; which words answer "what it points to" and "what came first", and the one-word names on the river, are the study's and the page's judgement. The three outside quotations not held here (above) were not compared.
- **By browser against the always-on preview** (Playwright, 2026-11-07): 1440, 390 and 320 wide, light and dark. At every width the page is exactly as wide as the window (scroll width equals window width); the only element reaching past the edge is the shared site header's own scrolling menu, clipped inside it as on every mock-up. No console errors or warnings. A verse link opens its verses in the sheet from the right and puts the reference in the address; Escape closes it, clears the address and returns focus to the link. `?ref=ACT.8.36-38` opens directly and says the Berean has no verse 37. Choosing a river crossing reads it below and puts it in the address (`?part=03&row=8`). I looked at the screenshots myself.
- **Faults found in the browser and fixed:** the page first stopped drawing after section 00 (a helper used before it was defined); the river's one-word names sat inside the water band with the stems struck through them, and a stray "null" printed under the first crossing; the back-and-forward strip's rule was hidden and its arrowheads sat outside the column. Each was fixed and re-checked in a new screenshot.
- **Not tested:** a real phone, a screen reader, 200% zoom, keyboard-only use beyond focusing and opening a link and Escape, the reduced-motion version (written, not viewed), and colour contrast (not measured).

## Which page it could be confused with, and why it is not

- **The church and its calling** has "Ten baptisms, read down the water": the same ten passages as a vertical list with a drop between the two sides. This page's study owns those passages, so the material overlaps on purpose; the drawing does not. Here the ten are crossings of one river running across the page, in Acts' order, each with a one-word name above the bank and a household mark below; the full words open under the river for the crossing chosen. Only on phones, where the river is left out, does it become a list.
- **Life together** also shows one part at a time. The behaviour is shared; the look is not: here the parts are chosen in three groups (both signs, the water, the table), and inside each part every note's voice is named in a gutter to the left of one reading column, with a filled, hollow or dashed mark, rather than in a margin to the right.
- No other study page draws a life as one line that dips under water once and then passes a row of table marks.

## Findings for the editor (content.md was not changed)

- **A doubled word in a quotation.** Part 03 quotes Acts 16:32 as "Paul and Paul and Silas spoke the word of the Lord…". The Berean has "Paul and Silas". The build corrects this in memory only (a named patch in study_lists.py) and the page shows the Berean's words; once content.md is fixed, the build fails until the patch is removed, so the patch cannot outlive the error.
- **Luther's Small Catechism on baptism.** The study quotes a modern translation ("works forgiveness of sins … gives eternal salvation to all who believe this"). The translation held on this PC reads "It worketh forgiveness of sins, delivers from death and the devil, and gives everlasting salvation to all who believe, as the Word and promise of God declare." The study's own note asks which translation to use; the page marks the quotation as not compared.
- **The 1677 confession, 29.2.** The held text spells "professe"; the study's "profess" is a modernised spelling of the same words, and the build accepts that one spelling and no other change. This answers the study's note on 29.2 and 29.4: the wording matches.
- **Three of the study's check notes are answered by held sources**, each proved by the build: Colossians 2:11–12 is printed among the Bible verses cited for Westminster 28.4; the 1677 confession's 30.7 uses Westminster 29.7's words on spiritual feeding ("spiritually receive, and feed upon Christ crucified") but leaves out "in, with, or under"; and the Orthodox Longer Catechism (1839) gives a source for triple immersion: "having his body thrice plunged in water" (and "Trine immersion in water"). The study's notes can be closed, or the Orthodox paragraph can quote that catechism.
- **Not held, so not compared by script:** the Catechism of the Catholic Church (1213), the Formula of Concord's Solid Declaration ("in, with and under"; only the Epitome is held, and the words are not in it), and the modern Luther translation above.
- **Argentina's 135** is not in the site's checked crisis list ([life.json](../../src/data/resources/life.json)); 988, 024, 800 911 2000 and 188 are, and the build matches each.
- **No link notes were turned into links.** The study has no "[check: …]" note asking for a link on this site, so content.md was left exactly as it was. *Children and the home* has no mock-up yet (a sister job is building it now), so it is named in italics, not linked.
