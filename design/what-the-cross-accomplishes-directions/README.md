Study 25, a Scripture & Theology study in God & Christian Faith. Its one habit: **every picture of the cross has a home** — ask where the word comes from, what it says Christ did, and where the writer stops pressing it; then, at the empty tomb, change the question to who saw what and what they said.

## The shape, and where it comes from

Paul gives the gospel in four lines (1 Corinthians 15:3–5: Christ died for our sins, He was buried, He was raised, He appeared), and Philippians 2:8–9 carries it from "death on a cross" to "Therefore God exalted Him". The page climbs those lines:

- **Heading.** The standard study heading (trail, concepts in gold capitals, title with "and the throne" as its gold italic turn, the lead, 1 Corinthians 15:3–4) and a drawing of a plain cross on a low hill, a rock tomb with its stone rolled aside and a throne on its steps, joined by one rising dotted thread, in a faint circle and dotted orbit with the two-line motto. No face or figure of anyone. The drawing is left out on phones.
- **Figures.** 6 pictures · 4 lines · 6 appearances in Paul's list (it names no women) · 46 verses printed in full. All computed by the build.
- **The way up the page.** The four lines and the throne as five steps, each a step higher, each opening its parts. It is the page's contents.
- **01–06 One cross. Six pictures.** The frame, then a window: six circles (temple, slave market, broken friendship, law court, battlefield, family, each with its own small drawing) around one centre, "In our place, for our sins". Part 04's line to the centre is solid, because the study says that claim in the site's voice. Choosing a circle shows its home, what it says Christ did (with the verse phrase) and where it stops, beside the window, and opens that part's whole reading below, one at a time and in the address (`?picture=04`). In the reading, "Where the picture stops" is drawn as a dashed rule: the picture ends on the page where the writer stops pressing it.
- **The turn.** "Not a picture. A report." The three picture questions give way to the two report questions.
- **07–08 An empty tomb. Who saw what.** A register: Luke 24 in story order on the left, Paul's list on the right, a line joining the same people (Simon to Cephas, the Eleven to the Twelve). Then parts 07 and 08, read like a filed account, with the quotations standing in the margin on a wide screen.
- **09 The throne.** Isaiah 45:23 beside Philippians 2:10–11, with "every knee" and "every tongue" marked in both, and the caption that these verses do not say everyone will be saved. Then part 09.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer" beside 1 Corinthians 15:1–11 printed in full, a line-art foot (hill and cross, tomb, throne), the sources and the way back.

## What it could be confused with, and why it is not

- **The hope to come** (same category) has an empty tomb and a throne in its opening drawing, and its horizon version a path to a city. This page's drawing is one rising thread from a cross to a throne, and nothing below the heading repeats that page: it uses six cards, a table and ordered maps; this one uses a six-circle window, a turn, a register and a two-verse echo.
- **The good news** (same category) also reads parts down a page with Consider and Reflect. It has a ledger and a sticky rail with a dotted thread; this one shows one picture's reading at a time under the window, and files the reports with marginal quotations.
- **The church and its calling** uses rings of points. The window here is six chosen pictures around one claim, not a count on rings.
- Colour: the Epistles' gold, because seven of the nine parts read the letters and the spine is 1 Corinthians 15. It sits close to the site's accent gold, so the look rests on the shapes above, not the colour.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

- **By script on every build:** every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page; the study has parts 01 to 09 in order, each with its hook, readings, Consider and Reflect. Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference; a quotation of part of a verse (Hebrews 9:26, Luke 24:5–7) is allowed only as whole sentences or a whole speech ending where the passage ends. Every reference exists with no gap in a range, and a list such as "(Acts 8:32–35; 1 Peter 2)" is never read as a second Acts verse. A quoted phrase with a reference after it in the same sentence must be in that reference; any other quoted phrase must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's readings or quoted passages, or the passage printed just above or below it. Links go only to this site ("//host" and "https:" are refused). Each named study links only to a mock-up that is on this PC.
- **The page's own lists, by script:** each of the six pictures' verse phrases is in its verse, and each "where it stops" sentence is word for word in that part of content.md; the six homes appear in the Lead in that order. The four lines' phrases are in 1 Corinthians 15:3–5, and "according to the Scriptures" is marked exactly where Paul has it (twice). Every witness name and "what they saw" phrase is in its verse. "Every knee" and "every tongue" are in both Isaiah 45:23 and Philippians 2:10–11. Seven stated facts are tested, including: Mark 10:35–45 names no devil or Satan; Luke 24:4 has "two men" and Mark 16:5 "a young man"; 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 names no women; Philippians 2:9–11 does not say "saved"; Paul's four words in Romans 5:6–10 come in the order powerless, ungodly, sinners, enemies.
- **Refused on purpose** ([refusal-tests.py](refusal-tests.py), run from the Website folder; it writes nothing and fails if any input slips through). Twelve wrong inputs, tried in memory, were each refused for the expected reason: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quote cited to the wrong verse a few words after it; a reference that does not exist (Leviticus 5:99); an outside link; a "//example.com" link; a line the page cannot place; a picture's limit that is not in the study; a witness who is not in the verse; a study link to a mock-up that does not exist; a fact the verse contradicts. data.js was byte for byte unchanged afterwards. The fourth case first got through: a quote's own reference was only recognised when it came straight after the quote, as in the model build. It now counts the first bracket later in the same sentence. That stricter rule then caught the study's `the "we" of these verses` (followed by Isaiah 52:15, while the word is Isaiah 53's), which is the study naming a word, so "we" is on the named list of own terms.
- **Two spacing allowances:** the Berean prints "death— even" in Philippians 2:8 where the study writes "death—even"; a space after a dash is treated as spacing. Quote marks and dash kinds are compared as the model does.
- **Not checked by script:** the seven phrases from outside Scripture (the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, articles II, IV and V; the Second London Confession 8.5, twice; the Assemblies of God's Statement of Fundamental Truths, section 12). None is held in full on this PC. They are listed by name in study_lists.py; several carry the study's own "[check]" note. Compare them by eye before release. The site's own summaries in the window ("what it says Christ did") are this page's words, written from the study, and are not checked by script.
- **`--check`:** passes on the built data.js; with a line appended to data.js it fails with exit 1.
- **By browser against the always-on preview:** 1440, 390 and 320 wide, light and dark. No console errors at any of the six. Nothing past the right edge (the page's scroll width equals the window at every width; the only items beyond it are the shared site header's own scrolling menu row). A verse link opens its verses in the sheet and puts `?ref=` in the address beside `?picture=`; Escape closes it, removes only `?ref=`, and returns focus to the link. Arrow keys move through the six pictures; a link to `#part-06` chooses picture 06; an unknown `?picture=` or `?ref=` is ignored. A focused control has no outline; the chosen picture shows its single gold border.
- **Not tested:** 200% zoom, a screen reader, a real phone, Safari. Colour contrast was not measured.
