A Scripture & Theology study of article IV (salvation) of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, walked through six passages. Its one habit: **watch who is doing the verbs.** The page's shape comes from something the passages already do: five of the six turn on the word "But" (Ephesians 2:4, Luke 18:13, Titus 3:4, Romans 3:21, Galatians 4:4), and on one side of that word people act, on the other God acts. So the page's signature is a **verb chart**: six passages as six columns, read top to bottom as what people were doing, where it turns, what God does, and what people do. Every marked verb is also marked inside the printed Scripture, and a switch ("Mark the verbs") turns the marks on and off everywhere at once.

One direction was built, as for The good news, because the text already existed.

## What is built

- **Opening (the standard heading).** Study trail; the concepts in gold capitals (Gift · Verdict · New birth · Family · Growth); "Saved by *grace*" with the turn in gold italic; the subtitle; the lead; Ephesians 2:4–5. Beside it a drawing in a faint circle and dotted orbit: one open, empty hand, palm up, and a sealed scroll coming down to it out of plain light. The light is abstract; there is no face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father. Motto: "Dead, then alive. / Guilty, then declared right." Four counts, each computed by the build and captioned: 6 passages walked, 26 verbs of God marked, 5 of 6 passages turning on "But", 32 verses printed in full.
- **00 Who is acting? Watch the verbs.** The habit's three paragraphs, then the verb chart with a key. Shape carries who acts, not only colour: God a filled dot; a passive verb that names no actor a half-filled dot; people an open ring; words named only to be ruled out ("not by works") struck through with a cross; what people do in reply a diamond. The turn band is a gold rule threaded through the five "But" phrases; James's column says plainly that it has no such turn. Pointing at a verb, or reaching it with Tab, writes who acts, the phrase and the reference in a line under the chart (read aloud by screen readers); choosing it opens the verse. "What this study covers" follows in small type.
- **01–07 The parts.** Each opens with a large faint numeral behind the kicker (part and main readings), the title and the hook in gold italic. The reading runs on the left; a margin on the right, held in view while the part scrolls, gives the part's drawing, the switch, where the passage turns, and its verbs grouped by who acts. Small labels mark every kind of paragraph: Setting; With care (crisis lines and pastoral notes); Their teaching, in their words (Roman Catholic and other traditions, named and quoted); How Baptists read it; A named source, in paraphrase (Calvin); What the passage does not do; Read more (links to sibling studies).
- **A graphic in five parts, each with its question and one interpreting sentence:** 02, the two prayers in Luke 18 side by side, every "I" in the Pharisee's prayer marked and counted by script (4), "God" and "have mercy" marked in the tax collector's; 04, Romans 4's four bookkeeping words (credited, forgiven, covered, never count against him); 05, Galatians 4:7's three standings (no longer a slave, but a son, also an heir); 06, Abraham's two moments on one line (Genesis 15:6, quoted by Paul and by James; Genesis 22, pointed to by James), with the ages the script finds between them (eighty-six at Ishmael's birth, a hundred at Isaac's); 07, Romans 8:29–30 as a chain of five verbs of God, and Philippians 2:12–13's two verbs in one breath.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Ephesians 2:1–10 printed in full with its verbs marked; the closing drawing (the same hand, now holding the opened scroll); sources; a back link to God & Christian Faith.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean, and puts `?ref=` in the address; Escape closes it and returns focus to the link. `?verbs=off` keeps the marks off and survives opening and closing the sheet.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py) reads the study's content.md and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py), checks them against the site's Berean (data/plain/bsb), and writes [data.js](data.js) by writing a temporary file and swapping it in, so a half-written data.js is never served. [page_checks.py](page_checks.py) checks the lists, the crisis lines and the page's own files; [selftest.py](selftest.py) holds the refusal tests; [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) is a copy of The good news mock-up's reader. The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/sg.js](js/sg.js), [js/art.js](js/art.js) and [css/sg.css](css/sg.css), with the frame borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups, as The good news does. Unlike the model, this build writes nothing under `src/`.

Run from the Website folder:
- `python design/grace-faith-and-salvation-directions/build-data.py` builds data.js.
- `python design/grace-faith-and-salvation-directions/build-data.py --check` rebuilds in memory, runs the refusal tests, checks that every file index.html loads exists, and fails if data.js is stale.
- `--selftest` runs only the refusal tests.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

- **By script on every build** (`--check` passed on 2026-11-07: 94 references, 12 editorial notes listed): every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page; every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference; every reference exists, with no gap in a range; a quotation followed by its own reference is in that reference; any other quotation is in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above; pieces of a quotation split by an ellipsis must appear in order in one reference; a quoted phrase that the verse puts straight after "not" (or "no", "never", "nor", "neither", "without") does not count, so a quotation cannot reverse its verse; a line with an odd number of quotation marks is refused; a reference after a quotation that names a book but does not resolve is refused; links go only to paths on this site, and a named study links only if its mock-up folder is listed. Every verb on the chart and in the margins, every "But" turn (proved to begin a verse with "But"), each prayer (taken from the verse's own quotation marks), each bookkeeping word, standing, chain link and age is in the verse named. Every mark inside printed Scripture is proved by slicing the verse back to the phrase; no two marks overlap. The counts on the page are computed, not typed.
- **Crisis lines:** 988 (United States), 024 (Spain), 800 911 2000 (Mexico) and 188 (Brazil) are each in the care note beside their country and in the site's checked list [life.json](../../src/data/resources/life.json) under the named entry. **Argentina's 135 is not in that list**, so it is unverified here.
- **Refused on purpose:** fourteen wrong inputs, made in memory from the real files and handed to the real build, were all refused with the expected message, and data.js was hashed before and after to prove nothing was written; the unchanged input passes as the control. They are: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a quotation that drops the verse's "not" ("by works" for "not by works"); a reference that does not exist (Ephesians 2:23); a link to another site; a link written as `//example.com/` to slip past a leading slash; a line the page cannot place; a chart verb that is not in its verse; a "turn" that does not begin with "But"; a crisis number given for the wrong country; the Galatians 3:28 quotation as content.md has it; a text patch that no longer matches content.md.
- **Not checked by script:** which column a verb is filed under. The script proves the words are in the verse; that "went home justified" is God's act (drawn as a passive with a half-filled mark) is this study's reading, said on the page under the chart, with Romans 4:5 as the reason. The 23 quotations from outside Scripture (the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, articles I, IV, V and VII; the Baptist Catechism; the Council of Trent; the Catechism of the Catholic Church) are listed with their sources in study_lists.py and compared by eye only; none of those texts is held in full on this PC. That "at least 14 years" separate Genesis 15 and Genesis 22 is an inference from the narrative order and the two ages, said as such.
- **By browser against the always-on preview**, 2026-11-07: 1440, 390 and 320 wide, light and dark. Nothing past the right edge at any width (the page's scroll width equals the window's); no console errors or warnings. A verse link opens its sheet with the reference in the address and focus inside the sheet; Escape closes it, clears `?ref=` and returns focus to the link. `?ref=GAL.4.4-7&verbs=off` opens the sheet with the marks off; `?ref=__proto__` is ignored. The switch sets `aria-pressed` on every copy of itself and puts `?verbs=off` in the address. Tab reaches the chart's verbs, the line under the chart reports each one, Enter opens its verse, and no focus outline is drawn. Screenshots were looked at, not only taken.
- **Colour contrast, computed:** the gold used for text in the light theme (#86610d) is 5.0:1 on the page; the dark theme's gold is 11.4:1. The brighter Epistles gold (2.6:1 on the light page) is used only for marks, dots and rules, never for text.
- **Not tested:** 200% zoom, a screen reader, a real phone, and the reduced-motion version in a browser (the CSS stops the floating scroll, rays, sparkles and switch movement under `prefers-reduced-motion`, but it was not seen running).

## Which page it could be confused with, and why it is not

**The good news** ([the-good-news-directions](../the-good-news-directions/README.md)) is the nearest: the same category, the same build code underneath, and it too teaches one habit of reading about salvation. The difference is in the shape. The good news reads God's act and our response as a two-column ledger of seven verses, then walks six parts down one dotted thread with a sticky rail on the left, in the Gospels' violet, under a drawing of a house with its door open. This page files verbs by who does them on a six-column chart whose middle band is the "But" each passage turns on; marks those verbs inside the Scripture itself with a switch; holds each part's verbs in a margin on the right, under a large faint numeral; and is drawn in the Epistles' gold under an open hand and a sealed scroll. It also differs from *Faith under pressure* (load-bearing wall, pressures as arrows) and *The hope to come* (horizon, a city at sunrise, a sprouting stump), which is why this hero avoids a stump or a shoot.
