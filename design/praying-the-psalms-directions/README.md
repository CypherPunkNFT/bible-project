Study 48, a Scripture & Theology study of the book of Psalms itself (the folder keeps its planned name, `praying-the-psalms`). Its one habit: **before you read a psalm, find where it stands in the book.** The study's three questions (where am I in the book, what kind of psalm is this, how does the rest of Scripture read it) are turned into four ways of looking at one picture of all 150 psalms, and every part points back into that picture.

## What is built

- **The standard heading:** study trail (Study / Living the Christian Life / The book of Psalms), the concepts in gold capitals, *The book of* in ink with *Psalms* in gold italic, the lead, then Matthew 26:30 and Luke 24:44. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, an open gateway with a tree planted by streams of water beyond it (Psalm 1:3), the water moving slowly, and the motto "A gate. Five books. / A last word of praise." No figure of God or of Christ; no staves, strings or notes.
- **Four counts with what they mean:** 150 psalms (the most chapters of any book), 5 books, 116 headings (34 psalms have none), 40 psalms quoted in 82 New Testament passages.
- **Three questions to carry,** each with a button that opens its view of the whole book.
- **00 The whole book:** the 150 psalms in five rows, one per book, each psalm a stroke as tall as it is long (the square root of its verse count, so Psalm 117's two verses and Psalm 119's 176 both fit). Each row ends with its closing blessing in its own words. Four views, chosen by buttons: *Five books* (the gate and the Hallelujah ending in gold), *Who the headings name*, *What the headings and notes mark* (songs of ascents, the thirteen days in David's life, the alphabet poems, the Hallelujah ending), and *Quoted in the New Testament* (each stroke as dark as its count, the six most quoted labelled). Chips with counts isolate one group. Point at a stroke and its heading shows on the line beneath; choose it and its sheet opens. The view, the group and the psalm live in the address (`?lens=writers&only=asaph&psalm=73`).
- **01–07 The parts.** Each opens with its number, a small drawing, the title, its hook, its main readings, and a strip of the 150 psalms with the ones this part cites lit. Then the text, with the passages printed in full, and Consider and Reflect side by side. One figure beside the text, only where the text has the shape: 01 the five closings side by side; 02 the gate, "Blessed" opening Psalm 1 and closing Psalm 2, and 1:1's walk, set foot, sit; 03 where each named writer's psalms fall in the book, and the thirteen headings tied to a day in David's life; 04 Psalm 13's three movements; 05 Psalm 119's twenty-two stanzas under their letters, *nun* lit; 06 the most quoted psalms as ranked bars. Part 07, the cursing psalms, has no drawing: its four "look closely" points and its three readings are set out in words.
- **Close:** "Read the Psalms again", the question for the reader and "Read the passage again before you answer.", the gateway again, where to go next (Hymns, Long Memory, How Hebrew works, The Dead Sea Scrolls, and the Prayer in the letters mock-up), sources, and a back link to Living the Christian Life.
- **The sheet:** every reference opens in one sheet, in the Berean. A passage shows its verses. A whole psalm shows its book, length, heading, first verse, every New Testament passage the Berean's notes say quotes it (each opens in turn), and a link to read the whole psalm at `/read/bsb/PSA/<n>`. A run of psalms (for example 120–134) lists each with its heading. Escape closes it and returns focus to what opened it.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py) reads the study's content.md and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder: `python design/praying-the-psalms-directions/build-data.py`. `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is not exactly what the build would write. `--selftest` feeds sixteen wrong inputs in memory and fails unless every one is refused; it writes nothing. The build is split so each file stays small:

- [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py): a copy of The good news mock-up's Berean reader.
- [psalter.py](psalter.py): reads the structured Berean in `data/text/bsb/PSA/` (30 files of five psalms): the headings, the five book markers, the footnotes, the New Testament citations the notes mark ("Cited in …", "see also" dropped), the acrostic notes, and Psalm 119's stanza letters.
- [refs.py](refs.py): finds references in the text. A chapter:verse with no book counts as a Psalm only when a quotation stands right before it (the quotation check then proves it is the right verse); otherwise it must be named in a list. Psalm numbers in brackets with no book are read only when the bracket is on a named list word for word. Nothing is guessed.
- [facts.py](facts.py): recomputes every count the study states, and builds the figures.
- [study_lists.py](study_lists.py): this page's own choices: the concepts and motto, the counts the text states and the exact words that state them, the thirteen event headings, the gate's and Psalm 13's phrases, the quotations from the Berean's notes and from outside sources, and the related studies.
- [selftest.py](selftest.py): the refusal tests.

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/kit.js](js/kit.js) (shared helpers), [js/field.js](js/field.js) (the whole book), [js/figures.js](js/figures.js) (the part figures), [js/ps.js](js/ps.js) (the page and the sheet), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the drawings) and [css/ps.css](css/ps.css), with the frame borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups as The good news does. Text goes in as text nodes; addresses and ids from the URL are looked up, never trusted. Nothing under `src/` was touched, and content.md was not changed (it had no "[check: …]" link notes).

The plan was reviewed three times before any code was written (security and correctness, reliability, edge cases). What changed because of the reviews: bare references and bare psalm lists became named lists instead of a default; links must start with one slash; `--check` compares bytes; data.js is written whole or not at all; the self-test injects text in memory and proves data.js untouched; the field is one Tab stop with arrow keys.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

- **By script on every build:** every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, and a line of a kind the page does not know (a stray heading, a table, a check note) is refused. Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference, and the Psalm 51 heading word for word the Berean's heading. Every reference exists, with no gap in a range; every psalm number is 1–150. A quotation followed by its own reference is in that exact verse; any other quotation is in a verse or heading its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above; a single quoted word must be bound to one reference or named as the study's own term. The five quotations from the Berean's footnotes are in the notes of the psalm named for each. All ten outside quotations held on this PC (Spurgeon five, Edersheim two, Calvin two, Watts one) are found in the held copies; for those scans, line breaks, spaces and hyphens are ignored, because the Spurgeon scan breaks "The desire- for" across a line. Links go only to this site.
- **Every count the study states is recomputed from the Berean and must match,** with the exact words that state it still in the text: 116 of 150 headed and 34 not; Psalms 1, 2, 10 and 33 the only unheaded psalms in Book I; David 73, Asaph 12 (50, 73–83), the sons of Korah 11 (42, 44–49, 84–85, 87–88), Solomon 2 (72, 127), Moses 1 (90), Heman and Ethan 1 each (88, 89); thirteen event headings, each naming David; "For the choirmaster" 55; the songs of ascents 120–134; the alphabet poems 9, 25, 34, 37, 111, 112, 119, 145; Psalm 119 the longest chapter in the Bible, 176 verses in 22 stanzas of 8; 40 psalms cited, Psalm 118 in 13 passages, Psalm 110 in 8, Psalms 2, 22, 69 and 95 in 5 each, Psalm 16 in 2; Psalms 146–150 each open and close with "Hallelujah"; the Psalms have more chapters than any other book. The page's own sentences in the field and the figures (for example "55 of David's 73 stand in Books I and II") are computed from the same data when the page draws.
- **The check caught three of its own faults while it was written,** which is what it is for: the Hallelujah frame first counted every psalm that opens and closes with it (106, 113 and 135 do too), when the study's claim is about the last five; the longest-chapter test compared the wrong field; and 72:19 says "blessed be" in lower case. Each was a fault in the build, not in the study text.
- **Refused on purpose (`--selftest`, in memory, data.js untouched):** sixteen wrong inputs, all refused: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist (Matthew 26:80); a psalm that does not exist (Psalm 153); a link to another site; a link starting with two slashes; a count changed in the text; a changed list of Asaph's psalms; a bare reference with no book and no quotation before it; a changed word in Psalm 51's heading; a changed outside quotation; one quoted word with nothing bound to it; a line the page could not place; and a count in the checked list that differs from the Berean's. The tenth and eleventh are refused because the stated words no longer match the list they are checked against; the sixteenth proves the count itself is recomputed.
- **Not checked by script:** the one phrase from the Baptist Faith and Message ("truth, without any mixture of error"), which is not held in full on this PC; compare it by eye before release. That a reference is the *right* verse for the point being made, beyond containing the quoted words, is checked by hand only, as the passage notes say. The Hallel at Passover rests on Edersheim, as the text says.
- **By browser against the always-on preview:** 1440, 390 and 320 wide, each in light and dark. Nothing past the right edge at 390 or 320 (page width equals window width), no console errors or warnings at any width. A verse link (Psalm 41:13) opens its sheet and puts `?ref=` in the address; Escape closes it, clears the address and returns focus to the link. The field takes one Tab stop; ArrowRight from Psalm 1 goes to 2, ArrowDown to 43 (the same place in Book II); Enter opens Psalm 43's sheet; Escape returns focus to its stroke. `?ref=PSR.120-134` opens the fifteen songs of ascents. `?ref=__proto__&lens=constructor&psalm=999` is ignored. I looked at the screenshots: the five rows, the closings, every part figure and the close draw as intended; on phones the rows wrap, the strips and small drawings are left out, and the motto under the drawing is hidden (the Manuscripts frame does this below 560 wide).
- **Not tested:** 200% zoom, a screen reader, a real phone, Safari. Colour contrast was not measured. Reduced motion is written (the water, leaves and sparkles stop) but was not switched on in a browser.

## Which page it could be confused with, and why it is not

- **The approved "Where is he named? (chapter grid)"** in the apostle mock-ups, one square per chapter: this page also draws one mark per chapter, but its marks are sized by length and laid in Scripture's own five books, each closed by its blessing; it is a picture of one book's structure, not of where a person is named.
- **Faith under pressure** (load-bearing) also reads passages beside a graphic, but its graphic is phrase lanes inside verses; **The hope to come** and **Covenants and promises** draw a horizon and a ring; **The good news** runs its parts down a sticky left rail on one thread. This page has no rail: each part carries a strip of the whole book, and its one figure sits beside the text on the right.
- **The Hymns study** sings the psalms; this page draws no music at all, because the owner ruled out a musical score and "musical theme" (2026-10-08).

## Things that need the owner's word

- **Psalm 3's heading reads "A Psalms of David"** in the site's Berean data and its source file; it shows that way in part 03's list of David's days. It looks like a slip in that edition of the Berean. The page shows the data as it is.
- **The Hymns page opens psalms in the King James** (`/read/kjv/PSA/<n>`), against the Berean-only rule; this page links to the Hymns study in its close. Not touched here.
- **Category:** the study sits under Living the Christian Life (as its folder does); the passage notes ask whether a study of one book belongs under Explore the Bible.
- **Named holders still missing** (from the passage notes): the shape-of-the-Psalter reading is not attributed, because Gerald Wilson's book was not read; each side of whether the headings are original, and a modern holder of the "honest prayer" reading of the cursing psalms, are still unnamed. The page shows them as the text does, without names.
