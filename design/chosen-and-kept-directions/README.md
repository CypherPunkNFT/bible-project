Study 58, a Scripture & Theology study in God & Christian Faith, on election and assurance. Its one habit: **before you ask whether a verse is about you, ask what it was written to do.** The study names four purposes and holds to them in its close, part by part: Ephesians 1 was written to make you praise; Romans 8, John 6 and 10 and 1 John to comfort; Hebrews 6 to warn drifters so that they stay; Romans 9 to answer the charge that God is unfair; Mark 3 both warns and comforts. So the page sorts its eight passages by purpose before it reads any of them, marks each part with its purpose, and ends each part on the close's own sentence for it. Its motto is the study's own, "Two hands. One grip. / No one snatches." (John 10:28–29), and its hero is a small flock asleep in a stone fold at night, inside the heading's faint circle and dotted orbit, with a line of tracks that comes along the outer ring and turns away.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py), with [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (an unchanged copy of The good news mock-up's Berean reader), [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's own named lists) and [figures_data.py](figures_data.py) (the figures' data, every phrase pinned to its verse), reads the study's content.md, checks it against the site's Berean text and the held copy of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder:

- `python design/chosen-and-kept-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js through a temporary file in the same folder, so a failed write cannot leave half a file.
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is not exactly what its inputs build now. It writes nothing. data.js records a fingerprint of each of its five inputs.
- `--selftest` feeds 21 wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused for the right reason, and proves four citations are read right. It writes nothing. The cases are in [build_selftest.py](build_selftest.py).

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/ck.js](js/ck.js) (the page and the verse sheet), [js/figures.js](js/figures.js) (the data figures), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the fixed line drawings) and [css/ck.css](css/ck.css). The frame is borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups, as in the model ([the-holy-spirit-directions](../the-holy-spirit-directions/)). The parser is adapted from that model to this study's shape: Concepts, Motto and Lead lines; [care] notes (four, one of them the crisis note that must open part 07); bold paragraph labels the study uses for its limits, its setting and the site's reading; two named Baptist readings set side by side; a close with Question, the closing line and Passage. All text is put on the page as text; the only markup strings are the fixed drawings in art.js. Nothing under `src/` was touched. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The standard heading: the study trail (Study / God & Christian Faith / Chosen and kept); the three concepts in gold capitals, Chosen · Kept · Known; the title *Chosen and kept:* with *can I be sure?* as the gold italic turn; the lead; 1 John 5:13 printed in full. Beside it the fold at night: five sheep in a low round wall of stones, four lying asleep and one standing with its head lifted to listen (John 10:27), a moon, sparkles, and paw tracks that come along the outer ring and turn away without crossing it. No hand, face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father is drawn. The motto beneath.
- **Four counts**, each computed on the build: 8 passages walked in full (5 from the letters, 3 from the Gospels; the study says "The study walks eight passages", and the build fails if the parts do not have eight main readings); 4 purposes; 5 fears named in the study's own words; 26 verses printed in full. A contents line follows.
- **00 What was it written to do?** The study's frame, with the reading key placed straight after the habit: four quarters, two by two (Praise, Comfort, Warning, Answer), each with a small mark of its own, the study's one-line description, its warning against misreading it ("A song is not a sorting machine." "A promise is not a riddle." "A warning is not a verdict."), and the parts written for it. Pointing at a passage shows the close's sentence for it on a line beneath; choosing it goes to its part. 4 of the 7 parts were written, at least in part, to comfort.
- **01–07 The passages.** Each part opens with its purpose mark and number, "Written to praise" (or comfort, warn, answer) in gold capitals, the title with its turn in gold italic, and the main readings. The reading follows in one column. Paragraphs that open with the study's own labels get a small mark: what it does not say, the setting, the site's reading, what both readings hold. Each part has its own figure, from its own verses, placed after the paragraph that first speaks of it:
  - 01: **One sentence.** Ephesians 1:3–14 as twelve verse ticks: a dot above each of the 9 verses with "in Him", "in Christ" or "in the Beloved One" (found by script), the three refrain verses in gold, and three bands under them, the Father chooses, the Son redeems, the Spirit seals and guarantees. Pointing at a verse shows its words with those phrases marked. On a phone the bands carry numbers and a key beneath names them.
  - 02: **The chain** of Romans 8:29–30, five verbs as five joined rings, asking which link depends on you holding on; then **the ring nothing crosses**: "the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord" in the centre and the 17 things Paul names in Romans 8:35 and 38–39 around it, none of them inside.
  - 03: **What question is Paul answering?** Romans 9's three questions, each beside the answer Paul gives (9:6, 9:14–15, 9:19–21). The two Baptist readings are set side by side under the study's own sub-head.
  - 04: **One verse, two sides**: John 6:37 split at its comma, God's side and yours. **Two hands**: the Son's hand inside the Father's hand, as two rings around the sheep (John 10:28–29), each opening its verse.
  - 05: **Two fields, one rain** (Hebrews 6:7–8): rows of grain and a field of thorns under the same rain, with what each produces and how each ends, then the writer's turn, "we are convinced of better things in your case" (6:9). The Wesleyan and Arminian reading is shown as another tradition's, in its own words, under its own mark.
  - 06: **Where do you look first?** Assurance rests first on what God has said (1 John 5:11), and the Spirit and a changed life confirm it (5:6, 5:10, 5:1, 5:2), with the study's line "They are not the price of it."
  - 07: **What does Jesus say first?** Mark 3:28–30 as All, One and Why, then two who were restored, Peter (John 21:15) and Paul (1 Timothy 1:13). The crisis note comes first in the part, before anything else, as the study places it; the build fails if it moves.
  Each part then ends with **Written to**, the close's sentence for that part, then Consider and Reflect.
- **Close.** "Held, and knowing it": the close paragraphs beside John 10:27–30 printed in full. Then **Which fear brought you here?**: the five fears in the study's own words, each joined to the promise its part sends you to; 2 of the 5 go to the same verse, John 6:37. Then the reader's question, "Read the passage again before you answer.", the fold again at morning, the sources, a line explaining the gold mark, and a link back to God & Christian Faith.
- **Notes still to check.** The study's 18 "[check…]" notes are each shown as a small gold four-point mark, with the note in its title and, for screen readers, in hidden text. So an unread source is never presented as read.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean. The sheet's reference goes in the address (`?ref=`), and a shared address opens it again.

**Colour:** the site's gold for the page; each part takes the colour of the part of the Bible its passage is in (the letters for 01, 02, 03, 05 and 06; the Gospels for 04 and 07), and the close's passage the Gospels'. The words always carry the meaning; colour only repeats it.

## Why it does not look like its neighbours

- **The page it could be confused with is Saved by grace** ([grace-faith-and-salvation-directions](../grace-faith-and-salvation-directions/)): same category, much of the same ground (article IV and V of the confession, Ephesians and Romans), and both teach "one habit". Saved by grace is built around one verb chart, six passages as six columns read top to bottom, with a switch that marks the verbs in every printed verse. This page has no chart across the passages and no verb marking: its signature is the reading key that sorts passages by purpose before reading them, and each part's own small figure drawn from its own verses.
- **[The good news](../the-good-news-directions/)** runs its parts down one dotted thread with a two-column ledger and a sticky title rail. Here each part's head carries its purpose mark and there is no rail.
- **[The Holy Spirit](../the-holy-spirit-directions/)** (the model for the code) has one large floodlight figure and a line of questions. This page shares its frame and marks, but none of its figures; its drawing is a sheepfold, not a hillside.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

**Recorded run, 2026-11-07:** `python design/chosen-and-kept-directions/build-data.py` printed "build-data: all checks pass; wrote data.js (102,204 characters)". `--check` printed "build-data: all checks pass; data.js is current" and exited 0. `--selftest` printed "build-data selftest: 25 of 25 cases right (21 wrong inputs, 4 citations)".

**By script on every build:**
- Every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, or the build fails. Any heading other than parts 01–07 and the close fails. Each part must have main readings, a hook, Consider and Reflect. The close must run Question, then "Read the passage again before you answer.", then Passage, and the passage must be John 10:27–30. Part 07 must open with its crisis note.
- Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference. Verses ending on a dash are joined without a space, as in Hebrews 6:5–6.
- Every reference exists in the Berean, with no gap in a range: 86 different passages.
- A quotation with its own reference beside it must be in that reference; any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above it. A quotation split by an ellipsis must have its pieces in one reference, in order. A quotation under three words must be pinned to its verse on a named list ("in Him" to Ephesians 1:4, "hated" to Romans 9:13, and so on), or be one of the study's own terms ("Once saved, always saved").
- **All nine quotations from the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 are found by script** in the copy held in the site's question sources ([baptist-know-the-truth-catechism.json](../../content/questions/sources/normalized/baptist-know-the-truth-catechism.json)), each within 1,500 characters after its article's heading (eight from article V, one from article IV).
- **Every drawn phrase is in its verse**, as whole words in exact case: the five links of the chain (and in the study's order), the 17 dangers, the love in the centre, the three questions and answers of Romans 9, the two halves of John 6:37, the two hands, the two fields and the rain, the turn of Hebrews 6:9, the five lines of 1 John 5, Mark 3's three and the two restored. The "in Him" dots are found in the verses by script, not typed. Each part's "Written to" sentence must be in the study's close. Each fear must be in the study's own words, and its promise in a verse its part cites. Every count on the page is computed.
- A study named in italics must be on the named list. It links to its mock-up only if that folder has an index.html. Seven link: The Holy Spirit, The hope to come, Who do you say that I am?, A new creation, The good news, Saved by grace, and Life together. *Fear, anxiety and despair* has no mock-up yet and is shown in italics as not yet a page. Four book titles are named, never linked.
- Links may only go to this site: anything that is not a plain site path, or that starts with "//" or contains "..", fails.
- Every named list entry must still be used by the text.

**Two quotations in the study are not word for word the Berean.** The build found them. A design job may not change the study's words, so the page marks each with a gold mark that gives the Berean's words. They are on a named list in study_lists.py, and the build fails the moment the text is corrected, so the mark cannot outlive the fault:
1. Part 03: "great sorrow and unceasing anguish" (Romans 9:2). The Berean reads "deep sorrow and unceasing anguish".
2. Part 04: "I … will draw all men to Myself" (John 12:32). The Berean reads "I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw everyone to Myself".

**A refrain the Berean does not quite repeat.** The study calls "to the praise of His glory" a refrain that comes back three times in Ephesians 1. In the Berean those words are exact only at 1:14; 1:6 reads "to the praise of His glorious grace" and 1:12 "for the praise of His glory". The page shows each verse's own words and says so under the figure.

**Refused on purpose** (`--selftest`, in memory, data.js untouched, each refused with the expected reason): a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist; a link off the site; a link off the site by a backslash; a sub-head before any part; a note in square brackets the page cannot place; an altered confession quotation; a short quotation on no list; a study title on no list; a misquotation corrected without clearing its mark; the crisis note moved after the hook; a purpose sentence the close does not say; a chain link not in its verse; the chain out of the study's order; a danger Paul does not name; a fear the study does not voice; a promise its part never cites; a wrong count of readings.

**What these checks do not prove:** that the reading is right, or that each passage's purpose is the one every reader would name. The purposes are the study's own, from its close, and the build checks only that each part's sentence is there. The division of Ephesians 1 into three movements after verses 6 and 12 is this page's, following the study's sentence "The Father chooses, the Son redeems, and the Spirit seals and guarantees." **Not checked by script:** the quotations from outside Scripture that are not held on this PC: the Abstract of Principles, the Second London Baptist Confession, the Methodist Articles of Religion, and the titles of the 2012 statement and the 2013 report. Each carries the study's own "to be checked" mark.
