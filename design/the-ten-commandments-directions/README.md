Study 61, a Scripture & Theology study in God & Christian Faith. Its one habit: **before you read a command, read who is speaking and what He has already done. Rescue first, then the words.** The page takes its shape from Scripture's own form (ten words on two stones, under one opening sentence that is a rescue and not a command) and from Scripture repeating itself (the ten are given twice, in Exodus 20 and Deuteronomy 5, and the page draws where the two differ).

## What is built

- **The standard heading:** the study trail, the three concepts in gold capitals, *The Ten* in ink with *Commandments* in gold italic, the subtitle, the lead, and Exodus 20:2 printed. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, two stone tablets on a ledge at the foot of a mountain under a drifting cloud, with a dotted road climbing to them from the waves of the sea, and the motto "Two stones. One voice. / Rescue first, then the words." No face or figure of God. A bar of four counted figures, each saying what it means, then links to every part, then the frame and the selection paragraph side by side.
- **01, the counting table:** the study's own table, as a real table, with a choice of three ways of counting (Jewish; most Protestants and the Orthodox; Catholics and Lutherans). A brace column draws the chosen way: rows that make one commandment are joined by one line, the split tenth is shown "9 · 10" on a dashed line, the preface is dotted. The choice lives in the address (`?count=jewish|protestant|catholic`).
- **02 and 03, the two stones:** part 02 opens on the opening sentence set as a lintel over two arched stones, words one to four on the left and five to ten on the right; each word goes to its place in the reading. Under each word's heading a **"Given twice"** line sets the Exodus verse over the Deuteronomy verse, word for word, with the words that differ marked by underline and a wash. The Sabbath's lines sit in part 05, where the study reads its two reasons.
- **04, which words each passage takes up:** a row of the ten, and three passages to choose (Matthew 5, Matthew 22, Romans 13:9). The words each passage takes up light, in the Gospels' violet or the Epistles' gold, with the phrase and verse beneath (`?take=mt5|mt22|rom13`).
- **05, the week:** the first day and the seventh, with the midweek days between. Verses above the rule at the day they speak of; under it, the practices the study names, each set at the day it keeps, marked "shown, not ranked".
- **Close:** *Rescue first,* *then the words*; the study's closing paragraph, the way of reading, the invitation labelled as this site's own word, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Exodus 20:1–17 printed in full as verses 1–2 over two arched stones (3–11 and 12–17). Sources with what was read, and a back link.
- **On wide screens** the page reads like an annotated text: "Given twice" and the notes of other traditions (Lutheran teaching, another tradition, further reading not yet written) sit in the right margin beside the words they belong to, and the three figures use the full width.
- **Notes keep their kind:** "With care" for the care notes; "Lutheran teaching, not this site's"; "Another tradition"; "Further reading · not yet written"; and a small ringed mark after each open "[check: …]" note (27 of them, "?") or problem the build found (5, "!"). Choosing a mark opens its note in place.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet (Berean), with the verse in the address (`?ref=`); Escape closes it and returns focus to the link.

**Could be confused with:** *Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice* ([the-law-of-moses-and-the-christian-directions](../the-law-of-moses-and-the-christian-directions/)), the study in the same category on the law of Moses, also in History orange; *Covenants and promises* ([covenants-and-promises-directions](../covenants-and-promises-directions/)), which owns Sinai; and *The good news* ([the-good-news-directions](../the-good-news-directions/)), the model build. It is not like any of them. Leviticus draws a rectilinear plan of the camp read twice, with parts as wide heads over two parallel columns; this page has no plan and no columns: one reading column with a margin of notes, arched stones, a counting table with a brace, and a week line. Covenants and promises opens on a sky of concentric stars with a lettered switcher. The good news runs its parts down one dotted thread with a sticky rail and a ledger; this page has no thread, no rail and no ledger, and its part heads run across the page with a stones miniature that lights the part's words.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py) reads the study's content.md, checks it, and writes [data.js](data.js), and nothing else. It uses [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (an unchanged copy of The good news mock-up's reader), [tc_checks.py](tc_checks.py) (the checks behind the page's own figures, the outside quotations and the self-test) and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's lists: the ten with their Exodus and Deuteronomy verses, the passages of part 04, the week, the practices, the named studies, the outside quotations and the problems found). Run from the Website folder:

- `python design/the-ten-commandments-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js after every check passes (through a temporary file, so a failed write leaves the old one);
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is stale (data.js carries a fingerprint of content.md and the four build files);
- `--selftest` feeds wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused for its own reason; it writes nothing.

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/tc.js](js/tc.js), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the fixed drawings), [js/theme.js](js/theme.js) (a local copy of the theme starter, so another job's file cannot change this page) and [css/tc.css](css/tc.css). Only the site frame (header, footer, palette, fonts) is borrowed, from [authors-directions/shared/](../authors-directions/shared/). Nothing under `src/` was touched, and content.md was not changed.

Unlike the model, this build does not write into `src/`: the brief forbids it, and the page is not built into the site.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

Results of the last run, 2026-11-07, from the Website folder: build "all checks pass; wrote data.js"; `--check` "all checks pass; data.js is current" (exit 0); `--selftest` "12 of 12 wrong inputs refused as expected; nothing written" (exit 0).

- **By script on every build:**
  - every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, and a line the page cannot place stops the build;
  - every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference. One may stop at the end of a sentence inside its last verse, and the page then says so ("verse 22 in part": the Matthew 5:21–22 quotation stops before "Again, anyone who says…");
  - every reference exists, with no gap in a range; whole-chapter references ("Exodus 20", "1 Samuel 21") resolve to the whole chapter;
  - a quotation followed by its own reference must be in that reference. Any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, or, only where the paragraph cites nothing, in its part's main readings, or in the quotation printed just above. This is tighter than the model;
  - a line with an odd number of straight quotation marks is refused;
  - the "[check: …]" notes are lifted out before anything else reads the line, so a reference or quotation inside a note never counts as the text's own;
  - links go only to this site (`/…`, never `//host` or `scheme://`);
  - **the counting table:** each row's verse holds its words ("brought you out", "idol", "Sabbath" and so on), and each of the three ways comes to ten, every number 1 to 10, in order;
  - **"Given twice":** computed from the Berean, verse by verse and word by word. A pair of verses that share less than half their words (Exodus 20:11 and Deuteronomy 5:15, the two reasons for the Sabbath) is marked whole. 8 of the 11 lines are identical; 264 of Deuteronomy's 363 words stand where Exodus has them;
  - **the study's stated facts:** the Berean writes "Ten Commandments" in Exodus 34:28, Deuteronomy 4:13 and 10:4; Remember in Exodus 20:8, Observe in Deuteronomy 5:12; "that it may go well with you" and "field" only in Deuteronomy; house before wife in Exodus 20:17, wife before house in Deuteronomy 5:21; "murder" and not "kill" in Exodus 20:13; "third and fourth" and "thousand"; Abiathar in Mark 2:26, Ahimelech in 1 Samuel 21:1 and Abiathar in 1 Samuel 22:20; and the fourth word is the longest of the ten (98 words in the Berean);
  - every phrase in part 04 and on the week line is in its verse; every practice's phrase is in the study's own text;
  - the six crisis numbers in the care note are found, as whole numbers, in the site's checked list of help lines ([life.json](../../src/data/resources/life.json), read only);
  - **outside quotations held on this PC are found in the site's copy:** four phrases of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 (articles I, IV, VIII), "as the Christian Sabbath" (Second London Confession, 22.7) and "the preface to the ten commandments" (the Baptist Catechism, question 48, capitals aside). The three Luther quotations do **not** match the site's copy and are marked; the build fails if one is corrected, so the mark cannot outlive the problem;
  - every list entry is still used by the text, and every problem the build marks still holds.
- **Refused on purpose** (`--selftest`, in memory, each refusal checked for its own reason): a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist; a link to another site; a link written as `//host`; a line the page could not place; a way of counting that does not come to ten; a quotation left open; a crisis number changed in the text (refused because the checked number is gone from the care note; the new number is not itself looked up); an editor's mark whose problem was fixed in the text. The unchanged content.md is built first as the control.
- **Not checked by script:** the quotations whose source is not held on this PC: the 1963 Baptist Faith and Message ("resting from secular employments"), the Seventh-day Adventist Fundamental Belief 20, and Heschel's "a palace in time". Named sources quoted nowhere (Maimonides, Philo, Josephus, Augustine, the Mishnah, the Talmud, the Mekhilta, Targum Onkelos, Kline, Lane) carry the study's own check notes. The placing of Matthew 22 over the two stones and of Revelation 1:10 on the first day is the study's reading, and the page says so. A verse-existence check proves the reference exists, not that the reading is right.
- **By browser against the always-on preview:** 1440, 390 and 320 wide; light and dark. The page's width equals the window at every size (the shared site header scrolls its own menu inside itself at phone widths, as on every mock-up). No console errors. The counting choice redraws the braces and puts the way in the address; `?count=catholic` opens on that way; a verse link opens its sheet and puts it in the address; Escape closes the sheet, clears the address and returns focus to the link, with no outline drawn. An editor's mark opens and closes its note.
- **Faults found and fixed during the browser pass:** the page did not draw at first (a closing bracket too many, then a function used before it was defined); a word's heading and its "Given twice" line were appended as text instead of elements; the editor's notes showed although closed; on phones the way-of-counting buttons were hidden with the table's columns; at 320 wide the word *Commandments* ran 6 pixels past the edge (the title is now smaller below 400 wide); the first diff matched stray words such as "the LORD" between the two different Sabbath reasons (now compared verse by verse, with an unrelated pair marked whole).
- **On phones** the hero drawing, the stones miniatures, the midweek days, the Exodus verse column of the table, the other two ways of counting and the word names in part 04's row are left out rather than shrunk; the two stones, the week's two days and the closing stones stack.
- **Not tested:** 200% zoom, a screen reader, a real phone, the reduced-motion version in a browser (it is in the CSS: the cloud, road, waves and sparkles stop). Colour contrast was not measured.

## For the editor (the study's text was not changed)

1. **Romans 13:9 and false witness.** content.md (part 04, "How do the ten apply to Christians now?") says the commands against murder, adultery, theft, false witness and coveting "still describe how love treats a neighbour, since Romans 13:9 names them." The Berean's Romans 13:9 names four of the five; it does not name false witness. Marked on the page.
2. **Mark 12:26.** The study quotes Mark 12:26 as speaking of "the passage about the bush". The Berean reads "about the burning bush". Marked on the page.
3. **Luther's Small Catechism.** The site's copy ([luthers-small-catechism-1529.b1.json](../../data/creeds/doc/luthers-small-catechism-1529.b1.json)) reads: First, "We should fear and love God, and trust in him, above all things."; Fifth, "…not to do our neighbor any injury or harm in his body, but help and befriend him in all bodily troubles."; Eighth, "…but defend him, speak well of him, and make the best of all he does." The study's three quotations are another translation. Each mark shows the site's reading.
4. **Resolved by looking, for the study's own check notes:** the Baptist Catechism phrase is question 48 of the site's copy (dated 1695 there; the study says approved in 1693). "as the Christian Sabbath" is in the Second London Confession 22.7. The Baptist Faith and Message 2000 phrases match the site's held copy. The six crisis numbers are on the site's checked list.
5. **The two "[open]" notes** ask for apologetics answers on children punished for their parents' sins and on whether the church changed the Sabbath. Neither exists on the site (searched the site's apologetics routes on 2026-11-07), so no link note could be turned into a link, and content.md was not changed.
6. **Named studies:** those with a mock-up on this PC link to it. *Money, possessions and generosity*, *Children and the home* and *Every life in God's image* are being designed alongside this one and are named, not linked, so this page's data does not change when they land; the run's last step can link them.
