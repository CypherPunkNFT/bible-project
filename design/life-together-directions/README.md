A Living the Christian Life study of belonging to a church, walked through eight passages in six parts, from John 13 and 1 Corinthians 13 to Philippians 2, Ephesians 4 and Romans 14–15. Its one habit: **look under the command.** Before asking what you must do for the other person, look for what God has already done for you, in the same paragraph. The page's shape comes from something the passages already do: Jesus says "Love one another" and "As I have loved you" in one verse; Hebrews puts "He who promised is faithful" one verse before "spur one another on"; Philippians puts four "ifs" before its command. So the page's signature is **ground and roots**: every "one another" command stands above one line, and a root runs down to the gift it rests on, as deep as the gift is far from the command in the text, measured by script.

One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## What is built

- **Opening (the standard heading).** Study trail (Study / Living the Christian Life / Life together); the six concepts in gold capitals (One another · Burdens · Confession · Restoring · Gathering · Unity); "Life *together*" with the turn in gold italic; "belonging to a church" under it; the lead; John 13:34–35. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, a basin, a jug and a folded towel, the things of John 13:1–17, set down after the washing. No person is drawn: no face or figure of God, of Christ or of anyone. Motto: "Loved first. / Then one another." Four counts, each computed by the build and captioned: 8 passages walked; 6 commands drawn with the ground the study names (2 more drawn with none); 6 of 6 gifts within two verses of their command, 3 in the same verse; 24 verses printed in full.
- **00 Look under the command.** The frame paragraphs, then the ground. Eight commands stand above one line, each with its part number. Below it, a root runs down to the gift, at one of three depths: in the same verse (John 13:34, Ephesians 4:3, Romans 15:7), one verse away (Hebrews 10:23, Philippians 2:1), two verses away (1 John 1:9 under 1 John 2:1). **Only the six pairs the study itself names get a root.** Galatians 6:2 (part 02) and Matthew 18:15 (part 04) are drawn with "Ground not named here", so the page does not supply a ground the text does not give. Pointing at a command, or reaching it with Tab, writes the pair and both references in a line under the drawing (read aloud by screen readers); choosing it opens the verses. One caption reads the drawing and says what a script proves and what is the study's own reading. On a phone the ground becomes a list. "What this study covers" and the opening care note follow; the care note links straight to the help at the start of part 04.
- **The six parts, one at a time.** A row of six, numbered 01 to 06 with their titles, chooses which part is open below it; the open one has a single gold rule under it. The choice is kept in the address (`?part=03`), so a part can be shared, and the browser's back button steps back through the parts read. The closed parts are kept in the page as `hidden="until-found"`, so the browser's find-in-page still reaches their words. Each part opens with a small drawing, its number, its title, its hook in gold italic and its main readings; then its own roots in large type (command above, gift below); then the reading. Notes sit in the margin beside the paragraph they belong to, each with a small label: With care, Left open, How this site reads it, Other Christians. On a narrow screen they fall into the reading in their place. Consider and Reflect close each part side by side, then a button to the next part.
- **Part 04 adds one figure: four steps, one aim.** Matthew 18:15–17 as four steps (privately; one or two others; the church; outside the fellowship), each in the verse's own words with its "if", and above all four the one aim the passage names: "you have won your brother over". The last step carries 2 Thessalonians 3:15, "warn him as a brother". The help note that opens part 04 stays first, full width, above everything else in the part.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the close, the invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and John 13:34–35 printed in full; the basin again, with water being poured; the sources; a link back to Living the Christian Life.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean, and puts `?ref=` in the address; Escape closes it and returns focus to the link.
- **Colour.** The Epistles' gold, because six of the eight passages are in the letters. Gifts are gold italic, commands ink, so the two differ by type as well as colour. Text in the light theme uses a darker gold (#86610d); the bright Epistles gold is kept for lines, dots and roots.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py) reads the study's content.md and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py), checks them against the site's Berean (data/plain/bsb), and writes [data.js](data.js) through a temporary file that is then swapped in, so a half-written data.js is never served. data.js ends with a fingerprint of what it was built from, so a stale file names its cause. [page_checks.py](page_checks.py) checks the lists, the crisis lines and the page's own files; [selftest.py](selftest.py) holds the refusal tests; [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) is a copy of The good news mock-up's reader. The page is [index.html](index.html) (a strict content security policy: scripts only from this site, no inline script), [js/lt.js](js/lt.js), [js/art.js](js/art.js) and [css/lt.css](css/lt.css), with the frame borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups and the theme script from Who do you say that I am, as the other pages in this run do. The build writes nothing outside this folder, and nothing under `src/`.

Run from the Website folder:
- `python design/life-together-directions/build-data.py` builds data.js.
- `python design/life-together-directions/build-data.py --check` rebuilds in memory, runs the refusal tests, checks that every file index.html loads exists, and fails if data.js is stale.
- `--selftest` runs only the refusal tests.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

- **By script on every build** (`--check` passed on 2026-11-07, also under two different hash seeds: 95 references, 13 editorial notes listed). Every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, and a line the page cannot place stops the build. Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference. Every reference exists, with no gap in a range. A quotation followed by its own reference is in that reference; any other quotation is in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above. Pieces of a quotation split by an ellipsis must appear in order in one reference. A quoted phrase that its verse puts straight after "not" (or "no", "never", "nor", "lest" and the like, in the same clause) does not count, so a quotation cannot reverse its verse. A line with an odd number of quotation marks, or with curly double quotes the build cannot check, is refused. Links go only to paths on this site, and a named study links only if its mock-up folder is listed and exists. The study's count ("walks eight passages") is checked against its main readings.
- **The ground and roots, by script:** every command and every gift is in the one verse named, as whole words, exactly once. The depth of each root is measured from the two references, and a row whose stated depth does not match is refused, as is a gift from another book. Each root must carry the study's own sentence naming that pair, and that sentence must be in content.md word for word; this is what keeps a root from being drawn where the study names none. The four steps of Matthew 18, their "if"s, the aim and 2 Thessalonians 3:15 are each in their verse. The counts on the page are computed, not typed.
- **Crisis lines:** 988 (United States), 024 (Spain), 800 911 2000 (Mexico), 188 (Brazil), 911, 1-800-799-7233 (the National Domestic Violence Hotline) and 1-800-422-4453 (Childhelp) are each in the study text as whole numbers and in the site's checked list [life.json](../../src/data/resources/life.json) under the named entry. **Five numbers are not in that list, so they are unverified here:** 112 (emergency, Spain), 190 (police, Brazil), 016 (gender violence, Spain), 180 (Ligue 180, violence against women, Brazil) and 100 (Disque 100, violence against children, Brazil). They need checking before this study is published.
- **Refused on purpose:** twenty wrong inputs, made in memory from the real files and handed to the real build, were all refused for the expected reason; data.js was hashed before and after, and the lists compared, to prove nothing was written or changed. The unchanged input passes as the control. They are: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase ("Love never ends"); a quotation cited to the wrong verse, even though the phrase is in the part's main readings; a quotation that drops its verse's "not"; pieces of a quotation stitched from two verses; a reference that does not exist (Galatians 6:30); a chapter that does not exist (1 Corinthians 17); a link to another site; a link written to climb out of the site with ".."; a line the page cannot place; curly double quotes; a stated count the readings do not bear out ("nine passages"); a gift that is not in its verse; a gift at the wrong stated depth; a gift taken from another book; a pairing the study does not name (giving Galatians 6:2 a root); a study link that leaves the design folder; a crisis number the site's list does not hold under the named entry; a crisis number found only inside a longer number.
- **Not checked by script:** which words are "the gift" and which "the command" is the study's own reading; the script proves the words and the distance, not the reading, and the page says so under the drawing. The six quotations from outside Scripture (the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, articles IV, VI and VII; the Second London Baptist Confession, chapter 26) are listed with their sources in study_lists.py and compared by eye only; neither confession is held in full on this PC. Two quoted phrases follow a "not" in their verse and are used correctly, so they are listed by name with the reason: "overwhelmed by excessive sorrow" (2 Corinthians 2:7, the danger the study names) and "meeting together" (Hebrews 10:25, where "not" belongs to "neglect").
- **By browser against the always-on preview**, 2026-11-07: 1440 wide light and dark, 390 wide light, 320 wide light and dark, and 390 dark after the colour fix. Nothing past the right edge at any width (the page's scroll width equals the window's); no console errors or warnings. A verse link opens its sheet with focus on its close button and the reference in the address; Escape closes it, clears `?ref=` and returns focus to the link. By keyboard, a command in the ground takes focus, the line under the drawing reports it, and Enter opens its verses. The part row shows one part at a time and puts `?part=` in the address; the back button returns to the part before; the opening's link reaches the help in part 04. `?ref=__proto__` is ignored; `?part=06&ref=ROM.15.7` opens part 06 with Romans 15:7 in the sheet. Screenshots were looked at, not only taken.
- **Colour contrast, computed:** text gold in the light theme (#86610d) is 5.0:1 on the page; the dark theme's gold (#ebc55e) is 11.4:1. The brighter Epistles gold (2.6:1 on the light page) was first used for text too; that was found while writing this record and fixed, and the browser confirms the text now uses the darker gold.
- **Not tested:** 200% zoom, a screen reader, a real phone, and the reduced-motion version in a browser (the CSS stops the drawings' movement under `prefers-reduced-motion`, but it was not seen running).

## Changes to the study's text, and what needs the owner's word

- **content.md was not changed.** The brief allows only turning "[check: ...]" link notes into real links, and none of this study's notes are link notes.
- **One quotation that is not word for word the Berean, corrected in memory only.** Part 06 quotes Philippians 4:2 as "to agree in the Lord"; the Berean reads "I urge Euodia and Syntyche to agree with each other in the Lord". The page shows the verse's own words, through `TEXT_PATCHES` in study_lists.py. The build stops if the patch no longer matches content.md exactly once, so fixing content.md retires the patch. **Needs the owner's word, then a fix in content.md.**
- **Editorial notes still open in content.md** (kept off the page and listed by the build in data.js, 13 in all): nine "[check before publishing]" marks and one note on other countries' lines, all on the crisis lines in parts 03 and 04; the wording of Luther's Small Catechism; representative commentators for each view on whether tongues and prophecy continue; the use of a Greek verb, against a lexicon.
- **Studies named but not linked**, because they have no mock-up on this PC yet: *Baptism and the Lord's Supper*, *The Holy Spirit*, *Money, possessions and generosity*, *Chosen and kept*. The others the text names link to their mock-ups.

## Which page it could be confused with, and why it is not

**A new creation** ([a-new-creation-directions](../a-new-creation-directions/)) is the nearest: the same category, and it too teaches reading a gift before a command ("already so, then to do"). The difference is in the shape. A new creation lays each pair out horizontally, what is already so on the left, "Therefore", what to do on the right, read across. This page reads down: the command above one ground line, the gift beneath it as its root, and the depth is a measured distance in the text; two commands are drawn with no root at all. It also differs from **The church and its calling** ([the-church-and-its-calling-directions](../the-church-and-its-calling-directions/)), which draws the church as rings of dots (this page has no rings), and from **Faith in daily life** ([faith-in-daily-life-directions](../faith-in-daily-life-directions/)), which marks reasons inside sentences with a switch (this page has no switch and marks nothing inside verses). The hero avoids people and church buildings for the same reason: a basin, a jug and a towel belong to John 13 and to no other page in the run.
