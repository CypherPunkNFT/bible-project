Study 23, a Scripture & Theology study in God & Christian Faith. Its one habit: **when a passage speaks of the Spirit, ask where He is pointing.** The shape comes from the text. Jesus says the Spirit "will glorify Me by taking from what is Mine and disclosing it to you" (John 16:14). The study names the three places He points in its readings ("to Jesus, to new life in a person who was dead toward God, and outward to other people"), and its close says, reading by reading, where He pointed. It borrows J. I. Packer's picture of a floodlight, which is not meant to be looked at. So the page draws a floodlight whose lamp is never shown: six beams, one per reading, falling on three places, two on each.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py), with [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (an unchanged copy of The good news mock-up's Berean reader) and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's own lists), reads the study's content.md, checks it against the site's Berean text and the held copy of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder:

- `python design/the-holy-spirit-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js through a temporary file in the same folder, so a failed write cannot leave half a file.
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is not exactly what its inputs build now. It writes nothing. data.js records a fingerprint of each of its four inputs.
- `--selftest` feeds eighteen wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused for the right reason, and proves five tricky citations are read right. It writes nothing. The cases are in [build_selftest.py](build_selftest.py).

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/hs.js](js/hs.js) (the page, the questions and the verse sheet), [js/figures.js](js/figures.js) (the data figures), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the fixed line drawings) and [css/hs.css](css/hs.css). The frame is borrowed from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups, as in the model. Nothing under `src/` was touched. The parser is this page's own, because this study's text has its own shape: Concepts, Motto and Lead lines; four kinds of marked note ([care], [other], [open], [read]); parts with and without a turn in their titles; part 07 as five questions under sub-heads; a close with Question, the closing line and Passage. Only the Berean reader is shared. All text is put on the page as text; the only markup strings are the fixed drawings in art.js. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

Before the code was written, the plan was reviewed once in three lenses (security and correctness, failure modes, edge cases). Its findings were adopted: the safer continuation pattern (so "; 1 Corinthians" is not read as verse 1), a stricter link check that refuses `/\evil.com`, short quotations pinned to a verse, a check that no note in square brackets is left unplaced, exact heading matching, and the temporary-file write.

## The page

- **Opening.** The standard heading: the study trail (Study / God & Christian Faith / The Holy Spirit); the four concepts in gold capitals, Promised · Poured out · Living within · Given for others; the title *The Holy* with *Spirit* as the gold italic turn; the lead; John 16:14 printed in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: a hillside, grass and a small tree bent by a wind that is not drawn, leaves drifting, and a beam from beyond the circle falling on an empty cross on the hill. The lamp stays outside the drawing. The motto beneath: "Wind you cannot see. / A light that falls on Jesus." No face or figure of God, of Christ or of anyone; the Spirit is not drawn as a figure or a dove.
- **Four counts**, each computed on the build and each with what it means: 8 readings (the study says "These eight passages", and the build fails if parts 01–06 do not have eight main readings); 3 places He points; 10 things the passages say He does that only a person does; 33 verses printed in full.
- **00 Where is He pointing?** The study's opening paragraphs, then the floodlight: three places along the foot (Jesus, an empty cross; New life, a sprouting seed; Other people, three points joined by lines) and six beams falling from above the frame, each in the colour of the part of the Bible its verse is in (the Gospels, Acts, the Prophets, the Letters) and labelled with its part number and verse. Pointing at or tabbing to a beam dims the others and shows, on the line beneath, the verse's words and the close's own sentence; choosing it (click, Enter or Space) opens the verse. Below 720 pixels wide the drawing is left out and the same six are listed under their three places.
- **01–06 The readings.** Each part opens with a large number, its concept in gold capitals with a small arrow for its direction (coming toward; down; in; out), the title (with its turn in gold italic where the text has one: parts 01 and 03), the hook in italic and the main readings. Under that, a ruled row: "In this passage, the Spirit" and the phrases from the reading that say what He does, each opening its verse. The reading follows in one column, with quotations printed in full and the four kinds of marked note. Each part has one figure of its own, placed after the paragraph that first speaks of it:
  - 01: named together at a supper table: the Son asks, the Father gives, the Spirit comes, Father and Son make their home (John 14:16, 17, 23).
  - 02: the fifteen peoples and places of Acts 2:9–11 in Luke's order, joined by fine rules, then "declaring the wonders of God in our own tongues".
  - 03: which comes first in Ezekiel 36:27: the gift, then the walking, with the study's line "The obedience comes from the gift."
  - 04: two names in one verse: "the Spirit of God" and "the Spirit of Christ" (Romans 8:9).
  - 05: two harvests. The fifteen acts of the flesh, the eight the study quotes from the middle underlined ("8 of 15 are about how people treat one another"); beside it the fruit as one ring with nine ticks, the four the study names for a quarrelling church marked. On a phone the ring is left out and the nine words are listed.
  - 06: Spirit, Lord and God named together (1 Corinthians 12:4–6), and the gifts of 1 Corinthians 12 and Romans 12 in two numbered lists, in each letter's order, with prophecy marked "on both lists".
  Each part then ends with **Where He points**: the place its beam lands on, the verse words and the close's sentence, in the colour of its part of the Bible; then Consider and Reflect.
- **07 Questions readers bring.** The five questions as words on one fine vertical line; choosing one shows its answer beside the line, and the choice goes in the address (`?q=`). Arrow keys, Home and End move along the line. The first question, "a person, or a power?", carries the ten verbs from the six parts that only a person does (abides, teaches, reminds, convicts, guides, speaks and hears, discloses, enables, testifies, apportions and determines), each with its words and verse. Below 900 pixels wide the line is left out and all five answers are shown in order.
- **Marked notes.** *This site's reading* (a filled circle), *Another tradition, in its own words* (a hollow circle), *Left open: Baptists differ* (a half-filled circle), *With care* (a gold bar). Each mark has its words beside it, so colour is never the only carrier.
- **Notes still to check.** The study's 18 "[check…]" notes (Packer's wording, the Shavuot sources, the Assemblies of God and Watch Tower wording, the four named holders on the miraculous gifts, the crisis lines, and so on) are each shown as a small gold four-point mark. The note's words are in the mark's title and, for screen readers, in hidden text. So an unread source is never presented as read.
- **Close.** "Following the light": the close paragraphs, the reader's question, "Read the passage again before you answer.", and John 16:7–15 printed in full beside them. Then the hillside at evening, the sources, a line explaining the gold mark, and a link back to God & Christian Faith.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean. The sheet's reference goes in the address (`?ref=`), and a shared address opens it again.

**Colour:** the Gospels' colour for the page, because the habit is Jesus' own words in John 14 and 16 and the light falls on Him. The beams and the "Where He points" notes take the colour of their verse's part of the Bible. The site's gold marks the concepts, the turns, the care notes and the notes still to check.

## Why it does not look like its neighbours

- **The page it could be confused with is The good news** ([the-good-news-directions](../the-good-news-directions/)): same category, the same colour, the same frame, and both teach "one habit". They differ in shape. The good news has a two-column ledger (what God has done, then what it calls for) and runs its parts down one dotted thread with a sticky title rail on the left. This page has a floodlight figure with beams falling on three places, part headers that span the page with a concept and its direction arrow, a ruled row of what the Spirit does in each passage, and every part ending on where its beam lands. Its questions are a line of words with the answer beside them, not a column of drop-downs.
- **[Christ in the Old Testament](../christ-in-the-old-testament-directions/)** also draws lines from verses to places and puts a number in each part's header. There the lines are curves between two shelves of books, and each part carries a sticky data stem on the right. Here the beams are wedges of light from one unseen source, there is no right-hand column, and each part's data runs across the top as a row of phrases.
- **[The church and its calling](../the-church-and-its-calling-directions/)** reads Acts 2 and 1 Corinthians 12 too. It is built around who was in the room and a line of water for baptisms, in the colour of Acts; this page does not repeat its readings of 1 Corinthians 12:12–31 or chapter 14 (the study only quotes them) and draws neither rings nor water.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

**Recorded run, 2026-11-07:** `python design/the-holy-spirit-directions/build-data.py` printed "build-data: all checks pass; wrote data.js (106,140 characters)". Then `--check` printed "build-data: all checks pass; data.js is current" and exited 0. `--selftest` printed "build-data selftest: 23 of 23 cases right (18 wrong inputs, 5 citations)". Earlier the same day, after build-data.py was edited and before rebuilding, `--check` printed "build-data: stale: data.js; run without --check" and exited 1. That is the staleness check doing its job.

**By script on every build:**
- Every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page, or the build fails. Any heading other than parts 01–06, 07 and "Following the light" fails. Each of parts 01–07 must have main readings, a hook, Consider and Reflect. Part 07 says "Five questions" and must have five. The close must run Question, then "Read the passage again before you answer.", then Passage, and the passage must be John 16:7–15.
- Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference (17 of them, from John 16:14 to 1 Corinthians 14:39–40).
- Every reference exists in the Berean, with no gap in a range: 100 different passages. Two references may not overlap. The five citations most likely to be misread are tested in the self-test: "John 14:23, 26; 1 Corinthians 12:4–6", "Acts 2:6, 9–11", "Mark 3:22, 30", "Acts 4:31 and Ephesians 5:18", and "Leviticus 23:15–21; Deuteronomy 16:9–12".
- A quotation with its own reference beside it must be in that reference. Any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above it. A quotation split by an ellipsis ("His own Son ... as an offering for sin") must have its pieces in one reference, in order. A quotation under three words must be on a named list: "all people" is pinned to Acts 2:17; "fiftieth", "word", "Word of Faith" and the study's own hypothetical sentence ("Obey, and then I will give you My Spirit.") are the study's own words.
- **All eight quotations from the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 are found by script** in the copy held in the site's question sources ([baptist-know-the-truth-catechism.json](../../content/questions/sources/normalized/baptist-know-the-truth-catechism.json)), each within 1,500 characters after its article's heading (II C, IV, V, VII).
- **Every drawn phrase is in its verse,** as whole words in exact case: the six beams, the eighteen "what He does" phrases, every step of the four strips, the fifteen names of Acts 2:9–11, the fifteen acts of the flesh and nine words of the fruit, the nine and seven gifts. Each beam's sentence must be in the study's close. Each place must have exactly two beams, and every part exactly one. The flesh's marked run must be the words the study itself quotes. The four marked fruit must be in the study's own sentence about a quarrelling church. The words of each place ("to Jesus", "outward to other people") must be in the study. The "on both lists" gift and every count on the page are computed, not typed.
- A study named in italics must be on the named list. It links to its mock-up only if that folder has an index.html; the build fails if a listed folder loses it. Nine link: Genesis 1–11, The good news, The church and its calling, Encounters with Jesus, A new creation, Covenants and promises, The cross, the empty tomb and the throne, The hope to come, and What Scripture promises the sick. Five have no mock-up yet and are shown in italics as not yet pages: What the Bible says about itself, Baptism and the Lord's Supper, Chosen and kept, Life together (being designed in this same run), and Fear, anxiety and despair. Two book titles are named, never linked.
- Links may only go to this site: anything that is not a plain site path (letters, digits, `/ _ ? = & # . -`), or that starts with "//" or contains "..", fails.
- Every named list entry must still be used by the text. So a list cannot quietly outlive the sentence it was made for.

**Three quotations in the study are not word for word the Berean.** The build found them. The run brief does not allow a design job to change the study's words, so the page marks each with a gold mark that gives the Berean's words. They are on a named list in study_lists.py, and the build fails the moment the text is corrected, so the mark cannot outlive the fault:
1. Part 05: "bite and devour" (Galatians 5:15). The Berean reads "biting and devouring".
2. Part 03: "the teacher of Israel" (John 3:10). The Berean reads "You are Israel's teacher".
3. Part 02: "your daughters will prophesy" (Joel, as quoted in Acts 2:17). The Berean reads "Your sons and daughters will prophesy".

**Refused on purpose** (`--selftest`, in memory, data.js untouched, each refused with the expected reason): a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist (Mark 14:99); a link off the site; a link off the site by a backslash; a sub-head before any part; a note in square brackets the page cannot place; an altered confession quotation; a short quotation on no list; a study title on no list; a misquotation corrected without clearing its mark; a beam phrase not in its verse; a beam sentence the close does not say; a fruit word not in Galatians 5:22–23; a gift on the wrong list; a wrong count of readings.

**What these checks do not prove:** that the reading is right, that a beam lands where every reader would put it, or that a phrase in the "what He does" row is the best one to show. Those are this page's choices, checked only for being in their verses. Two are the likeliest to be questioned. Pentecost's beam lands on Jesus, because the study says its gift of languages declares "the wonders of God" and "Peter's explanation leads to Jesus". Romans 8's beam lands on new life, with the close's own sentence "In Romans He points a frightened person to a Father". **Not checked by script:** the four quotations from outside Scripture that are not held on this PC: the Shavuot prayer phrase, "active force" (Watch Tower), and the two Assemblies of God phrases. Each already carries the study's own "to be checked" mark.
