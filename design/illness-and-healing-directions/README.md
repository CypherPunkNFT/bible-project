# What Scripture promises the sick: the mock-up and its record

Study 45, a Scripture & Theology study in Living the Christian Life. Its one habit: **before you hold a verse as God's promise to you in your illness, ask what kind of word it is, when it is kept, and who is speaking.** Those are the study's own three questions. The page's signature is a register of the ten sick people the study reads, from Publius's father to Trophimus: what was wrong, what was prayed or said, and what the text says happened, each in the verse's own words. Four recover in the text; six are left ill, kept in their weakness, or not told. One line runs beneath them all: the body's redemption and every tear wiped away (Romans 8:23; Revelation 21:4).

The study has no working.md; the page was made from content.md and passage-notes.md.

## How it is built

[build-data.py](build-data.py), with [scripture_text.py](scripture_text.py) (a byte-for-byte copy of The good news mock-up's Berean reader) and [study_lists.py](study_lists.py) (this page's own lists), reads the study's content.md, checks it against the site's Berean text and the outside sources held on this PC, and writes [data.js](data.js). Run it from the Website folder:

- `python design/illness-and-healing-directions/build-data.py` writes data.js (through a temporary file, so a killed run never leaves half a file).
- `--check` runs every check and fails if data.js is stale. It writes nothing.
- `--selftest` feeds fifteen wrong inputs in memory and proves each is refused for the expected reason. It writes nothing.

The page is [index.html](index.html), [js/ih.js](js/ih.js) (opening, register, the words offered to the sick), [js/ih-parts.js](js/ih-parts.js) (the seven parts and the close), [js/ih-figures.js](js/ih-figures.js) (each part's figure), [js/ih-core.js](js/ih-core.js) (text runs, frame, verse sheet), [js/art.js](js/art.js) (the drawings) and [css/ih.css](css/ih.css). The frame is borrowed, read-only, from the Authors and Manuscripts mock-ups, as in the model. Nothing under `src/` was touched, and **content.md was not changed**: its six "[check: …]" notes are about the wording of outside sources (the Targum, the Assemblies of God, the Christian and Missionary Alliance, a Word of Faith source), not links on this site, so none could be turned into a link.

The study text has its own shape (a byline, three numbered questions, "[care]" and "[open]" notes, view bullets, whole-chapter readings such as "Psalm 6", and references like "(Hebrews 1:8 · BSB; 1:10–12)"), so the parser is this page's own. Only the Berean reader is shared. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

No one is drawn. The motto is "I have heard your prayer; / I have seen your tears.", checked in Isaiah 38:5. Four counts, each with what it means, and links to every part.

Each row: who, the part that reads them, then three phrases on one fine line (what was wrong · prayed or said · what the text says happened). The outcome is told three ways at once: a mark whose shape differs (full dot, ring with a dot, struck ring, open ring, dashed ring, half-dark ring), a word ("Recovered", "Not taken away", "Left ill"…) and the verse. Three large buttons, with counts, light the four who recover or the six who do not; the choice goes in the address (`?show=recovered`). Under the rows, one gold line: "Not yet, for all who are His", Romans 8:23 and Revelation 21:4.
- **Test the words offered to the sick.** Nine sayings, seven from Scripture and two not (the argument of Job's friends in the study's paraphrase, and the 1966 tract Criswell read), each with three answers: what kind of word, kept when, who is speaking. Each answer names the part that argues it. The page says the answers are this study's reading.
- **01–07.** Each part opens across the full width with a large number, the title with its turn in blue italic, the hook in italic, the main readings and a small drawing. The reading runs in one column; each part's figure reaches out to the full width:
  - 02 *Where does each prayer end?* Psalms 6, 38, 41, 102 and 88 as rows of ticks, one per verse (10, 22, 13, 28 and 18, counted from the Berean). The verse the part quotes is marked; the last verse is quoted. Psalm 88 "Ends in the dark".
  - 03 Hezekiah in five steps, from "you will not recover" to "Hezekiah rested with his fathers", with the fifteen years drawn as a span.
  - 04 Philippians 2:27 and 2 Timothy 4:20 side by side, as the part's own question asks, with 2 Corinthians 12:9 beneath both.
  - 05 *Does more faith mean healing?* The woman, the boy's father, Paul and Jesus in the garden: each prayer in its own words and what followed, under the study's own line, "Healing comes from Christ's power and mercy; it is not paid for with a quantity of faith."

A block that quotes only part of its verse is printed in the study's words and labelled "part of the verse".

**Colour:** the Prophets' blue. The drawing and motto come from Isaiah 38, and part 07 turns on Isaiah 53. Gold marks the care notes, the heading's turn and the "not yet" line.

## Why it does not look like its neighbours

- **The page it could be confused with is The hope to come** ([the-hope-to-come-directions](../the-hope-to-come-directions/)). Both speak of what is kept now and what is kept at Christ's return, and both set promises beside what they ask. They differ in what the page is made of. The hope to come lays out tables of promises tied to calls and four orders of events across the whole Bible; it is about the end. This page is about people: ten named sick people on one register, read for what Scripture says happened to each, with the "not yet" as a single line beneath them rather than a section.
- **The other Christian Life pages:** A new creation uses a fold and a thread, Bearing witness doors, Faith in daily life rooms, Faith under pressure dark layers, Prayer in the letters prayers read upward in columns, The book of Psalms the Psalter's strokes. The Psalms page also draws psalms as ticks, but there all 150 psalms are strokes sized by length; here five psalms are read for one thing only, where each ends.
- **[The good news](../the-good-news-directions/)** runs its parts down a thread with a sticky left rail; **[Angels, demons and spiritual powers](../angels-demons-and-spiritual-powers-directions/)** splits every part across a dashed line. This page has no rail and no dividing line: a full-width head, one column, and figures that reach out of it.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

**Recorded run, 2026-11-07, 07:49 local time:** `python design/illness-and-healing-directions/build-data.py` printed "all checks pass; wrote data.js (114,884 bytes)"; `--check` printed "build-data: all checks pass; data.js is current" and exited 0; `--selftest` printed "15 of 15 wrong inputs refused for the expected reason; nothing written".

**By script on every build:**
- Every non-blank line of content.md is placed on the page. A heading, note marker or other line the page does not know fails the build, as does a part with no main readings, Consider or Reflect, a study without exactly three questions and seven parts, and a close without its question.
- Every block quotation is word for word the Berean at its reference. Nine of the eleven are whole verses or ranges. Two quote part of their verse, Isaiah 38:5 (God's words, without "Go and tell Hezekiah…") and 2 Corinthians 12:9 (the Lord's answer, without Paul's reply); a partial block must be an unbroken run of at least six of the verse's own words, and the page labels it "part of the verse".
- Every reference exists in the Berean, with no gap in a range: 98 different passages. Whole chapters ("Psalm 102") are read to their last verse in the Berean; "Psalm 102" is never read as "Psalm 10". Continuations such as "(Hebrews 1:8 · BSB; 1:10–12)" and "12:9, 28–30" take their book from the reference before them; "; 1 Peter" is never read as verse 1. Overlapping references fail the build.
- A bare "chapter:verse" takes its book only from a table in study_lists.py, keyed by part (three: 52:13 and 53:4–6 in Isaiah, 8:18–25 in Romans). Any other bare one fails the build.
- A quotation with its own reference beside it must be in that reference. Any other quotation must be in a verse its paragraph cites, its part's main readings, or the passage printed just above it. Headings are checked too ("Your faith has healed you" in Mark 5:34). Matching is by whole words, ignoring quotation marks.
- **20 of the 24 quotations from outside Scripture are found by script in the copies held on this PC:** Criswell's sermons of 1966 and 1974, Calvin on James 5, Piper's sermon of 1990 and his answer of 2014, the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 (Articles II and IV) and the site's copy of the Council of Trent. Held sources may only be read from inside the BibleProject folder.
- The four not held are the Assemblies of God's Fundamental Truth 12 (quoted whole once, then twice in part) and the Christian and Missionary Alliance's statement. They are listed by name only. The study's six "[check: …]" notes are taken out of the text and become a † at the place each stood, explained under the sources: after the Targum sentence, after the full Assemblies of God and Alliance quotations, after "they leave to His will", after "so none is quoted" and after the 1980 paper's title. The two later part-quotations of the Assemblies of God statement carry no † of their own.
- Every phrase drawn outside the running text is in its verse: the motto, all ten register rows (up to four phrases each), the nine offered sayings, the five psalm endings and their marked verses (the last verse is found from the Berean, not typed), Hezekiah's five steps, the part 04 pair, the four prayers of part 05 and what followed, the four parts of James 5, the two readings of Isaiah 53, and the six promises of the close. The two offered sayings that are not Scripture must be word for word in content.md; so must part 05's line and every line of the close's two columns.
- Every outside quotation and every title listed in study_lists.py must still be quoted in content.md, so a stale entry fails the build.
- The counts (10 in the register, 4 recovered, 16 verses printed in full, 7 parts, the psalms' verse counts) are computed on every build.
- A study named in italics links to its mock-up only where study_lists.py says so, and the build fails if that folder has no index.html. *The signs and works of Jesus*, *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* and *The hope to come* link; *Christ in the Old Testament* is shown in italics as not yet a page, because it is being designed in this same run and was not finished when this page was built. The run's last step can link it.
- Links may only go to this site; `//…` and `/\…` are refused as well as `https://…`.
- data.js records a fingerprint of each input (content.md, both lists and both scripts, with line endings normalised), so `--check` notices any change.

**Refused on purpose.** `--selftest`, run 2026-11-07, refused all fifteen, each for the expected reason, and wrote nothing. Thirteen are changes to the text: a changed word in a block quotation; a block quotation under the wrong reference; a changed word in a quoted phrase; a quotation cited to the wrong verse; a reference that does not exist (2 Timothy 4:23); a chapter that does not exist (Psalm 152); a link off the site; a link that looks local but leaves it (`//example.com`); a bare "2:27" with no book; a heading the page cannot place; a note marker it does not know ("[aside]"); a changed word in Criswell's quotation (it then no longer matches its listed source and is refused as Scripture not found); and a changed line in the close. Two are changes to study_lists.py: a Criswell quotation said to be in Calvin's commentary, and Trophimus's row given the wrong verse.

**Not checked by script:**
- The wording of the Assemblies of God and Alliance statements, the Targum's reading of Isaiah 52:13, and the date of the 1980 position paper. The study's own notes say these need checking; the page marks them with †.
- That a quotation from a held source comes from the part of the source named (the check finds the words anywhere in the file).
- The reading of any passage, and the answers in "Test the words" and the register's one-word summaries. The checks prove the words are the Berean's and sit in the verses cited, not that the study reads them rightly.

## For the owner

- **Six wordings to confirm** before release, all in part 07 and all already flagged in the study's own text: the Targum on Isaiah 52:13, the Assemblies of God's Fundamental Truth 12 and their 1980 paper on positive confession, the Alliance's statement, the claim that these churches leave healing now to God's will, and a Word of Faith source in its own words. The page shows each with a †.
- **The register's one-word summaries** ("Never told why" for Job, "Not taken away" for Paul's thorn, "Heard; recovery not told" for Psalm 6) are this design's words, not the study's. Paul's thorn is in the register because the study reads it among the unwell (part 04), but the study also says Paul "does not say what it was". The row shows only the verse's words, "a thorn in my flesh", and does not name an illness; if you would rather keep the register to people Scripture calls sick, it drops to nine, with four recovered.
- **On phones** the opening drawing and its motto are left out rather than shrunk, following the run's rule.
