# Collection charter

Version 1 · 2026-10-05 · Owner-directed scope; initial screening by Codex (AI-assisted).

## Purpose and priorities

Build a substantial, traceable Christian library centered on sermons and Scripture exposition. A reader should be able to move from a passage, doctrine, objection or pastoral need to complete teaching, its context and its source. Prioritize complete sermon corpora and series, reliable texts, useful Scripture coverage and faithful attribution. Report unique works separately from editions, files, recordings and excerpts.

The owner explicitly selected the **broader Calvinist evangelical tradition** and **reusable full text plus authorized source links**. Collection breadth may include historic Reformed, Presbyterian, Congregational, Calvinistic Baptist, evangelical Anglican and other demonstrably Calvinist evangelical authors. Denominational labels alone do not establish eligibility.

## Theological basis

Scripture is the final authority. Receive the 66-book Protestant canon as the doctrinal norm. The existing site's other Bible editions and historical texts remain available in their established documentary roles.

The site's authored Apologetics teaching retains its existing Westminster Confession of Faith basis, subordinate to Scripture. The broader acquisition roster does not change that editorial policy. Relevant existing policy: [Apologetics editorial record](../apologetics/editorial/library.json) and [site README](../../README.md).

Core teaching belongs within historic Trinitarian Christianity: one God in three persons; Christ truly God and truly man; his incarnation, sinless life, substitutionary atonement, bodily resurrection and return; the authority and truthfulness of Scripture; humanity's fall and need of grace; justification through faith alone in Christ alone; regeneration by the Spirit; God's sovereign grace in salvation and perseverance of his people; resurrection, judgment and eternal life. Good works are the fruit of saving grace, not its meritorious ground. Preserve the distinction between common grace and saving grace.

Use the Westminster standards, Three Forms of Unity, Savoy Declaration, Second London Baptist Confession and the Reformation-era Anglican Articles as historically identified comparison standards. Record which standard and edition a work actually invokes. Do not attribute subscription to an author from a denomination, surname, publisher or modern label.

The roster allows differing positions on baptism, church government, covenant structure, spiritual gifts and eschatology within the selected tradition. Document substantive differences with source evidence. Do not collapse those differences into a synthetic statement or silently rewrite an author's language to match the site.

## Author-selection criteria

An initial acquisition-eligible author needs:

1. **Resolved identity:** distinguish namesakes, initials and posthumous editors; identify the relevant person and body of work.
2. **Documented theological fit:** the author's own teaching or a responsible institutional/scholarly account supports inclusion in the selected tradition.
3. **Substantive reception:** sustained use or responsible assessment within Reformed scholarship, pastoral ministry, theological education or established publishing. Popularity and follower counts are insufficient.
4. **Usable provenance:** authentic works or authorized source records can be identified. An attractive repost is not an authenticated edition.
5. **Work-level judgment:** assess the actual work, edition and recommendation context. Author eligibility is not blanket approval of every claim or permission to reproduce every file.

For the initial historic roster, PRDL's curated classification and bibliography support initial fit and documented reception; this is bibliographic screening, not an exhaustive doctrinal audit. Modern ministry biographies and the author's teaching support narrower decisions where available. Publisher descriptions are identified as interested institutional sources. Strengthen these records during each corpus mission.

Status vocabulary:

| Status | Consequence |
|---|---|
| `eligible` | May inventory and acquire qualifying works when rights and source-access conditions allow |
| `provisional` | Research identity/fit and record candidate links; resolve stated eligibility questions before admitting works as core teaching |
| `context-only` | Documentary or historical evidence with a named purpose; not eligible for the core teacher collection |
| `excluded` | Outside current collection criteria; record the reason if already encountered |

Every decision records its basis, evidence, limitations, review date and reviewer type. All initial decisions are AI-assisted. No author, ministry or external theologian is represented as endorsing the project. Evidence-backed corrections remain possible; retain the prior decision in version history.

## Work roles and editorial care

- `core-teaching`: qualifying preaching, exegesis, theology and ministry instruction.
- `confessional-standard`: an identified historical confession or catechism, with tradition and version.
- `historical-context`: a primary record or historical work used for a stated research purpose.
- `opposing-position`: a source documenting a belief or argument being examined.
- `editorial-guide`: the project's own synthesis or study guide with claim-level citations.

Contextual authors can appear in debates or source evidence without entering the core teacher roster. Existing uses of Aquinas, Augustine, Athanasius, Craig, Qur'anic passages and humanist statements retain the explicit limits already recorded in Apologetics. This charter does not start a new general collection of their writings.

In apologetics, represent the other position from reliable sources, retain the strongest relevant objections, distinguish premises from conclusions, and document translation choices. A persuasive presentation must make its evidence inspectable. In pastoral material preserve historical context and identify editorial applications as such.

Preserve complete source text. Record abridgments, modernizations, translations, OCR and generated summaries as distinct derivatives. Disputed attribution, incomplete series, poor text quality and unresolved permissions stay visible. Never fill a missing sermon or quotation with generated prose.

## Collection and recommendation are separate

`draft` and `catalogued` are research states. `reviewed` means a documented work-level editorial check, not publication. `published` is available only after the relevant content/publication workflow is implemented and the actual edition, source, rights and presentation are checked. A full-text asset is not an endorsement; a link is not a license.

The current mission establishes governance. Future category prompts authorize bounded acquisition in that category. Website integration, search adapters, bulk transcription and new learning paths must be part of their requested mission. The earlier deferred Phase 4 curated Bible-connections analysis remains deferred.
