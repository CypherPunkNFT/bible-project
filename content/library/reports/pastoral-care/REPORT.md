## Concern routes

### Prayer

- [The Lord’s Prayer: The Preface to the Lord's Prayer](https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/prayer/prayer.iii.html) — Christians learning to pray. Begin with the address to the Father before the petitions; preserve Watson's ordered exposition of the Lord's Prayer.
- [The Lord’s Prayer: The Fifth Petition in the Lord's Prayer](https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/prayer/prayer.viii.html) — Those seeking forgiveness and learning to forgive. The fifth petition treats forgiveness, sin and repentance together; its account of repentance includes turning from sin toward God.
- [The Lord’s Prayer: The Sixth Petition in the Lord's Prayer](https://ccel.org/ccel/watson/prayer/prayer.ix.html) — Christians praying for deliverance. Read the sixth petition as part of the whole prayer, retaining the relationship between temptation and deliverance from evil.
- [The Crook in the Lot: Part 2](https://ccel.org/ccel/boston/crook/crook.iii.html) — Christians waiting for relief. Prayer, humility and patient waiting belong together; Boston conditions relief in this life on what is good for the believer.
- [A Body of Practical Divinity: Chapter 6. Of the Lord’s Prayer](https://ccel.org/ccel/gill/practical/practical.iv.vi.html) — Christians and teachers studying how to pray. Gill argues that the Lord's Prayer supplies a directory or model; preserve his reasons rather than silently presenting it as a prescribed verbal formula.

Scripture companion: **Matthew 6:9-13**. Sermon leads: [Christ the Support of the Tempted](../../catalog/works/work-l03-whitefield-works-5-19.json); [The Fatherhood of God](../../catalog/works/work-spurgeon-sermon-0213.json); [Lead Us Not Into Temptation](../../catalog/works/work-spurgeon-sermon-0509.json) These links use catalog main texts, not a fresh sermon-body comparison.

### Assurance

- [Holiness: VII. Assurance](https://ccel.org/ccel/ryle/holiness/holiness.iii.vi.html) — Doubting believers and pastoral helpers. Ryle argues that assurance is biblical and desirable while distinguishing it from saving faith: a believer may be saved without settled assurance.
- [Keeping the Heart (A Saint Indeed): Season of Doubting](https://ccel.org/ccel/flavel/saintindeed/saintindeed.v.iii.x.html) — Believers in spiritual darkness and doubt. Flavel addresses the danger of drawing desperate conclusions about one's spiritual condition when comfort and evidence seem absent.

Scripture companion: **1 John 5:13**. Sermon leads: [Praying and Waiting](../../catalog/works/work-spurgeon-sermon-0596.json); [Helps to Full Assurance](../../catalog/works/work-spurgeon-sermon-1791.json); [The Blessing of Full Assurance](../../catalog/works/work-spurgeon-sermon-2023.json) These links use catalog main texts, not a fresh sermon-body comparison.

### Sanctification

- [Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers: Chapter III.](https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/mort/mort.i.vi.html) — Believers struggling with sin. Read Owen on the Spirit as the sufficient agent of mortification; retain his distinction between spiritual obedience and merely external remedies.
- [Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers: Chapter XIV.](https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/mort/mort.i.xvii.html) — Believers struggling with recurring sin. Read the concluding directions in light of the preceding chapters: faith in Christ and the work of the Spirit govern the practical counsel.
- [Holiness: II. Sanctification](https://ccel.org/ccel/ryle/holiness/holiness.iii.ii.html) — Believers and teachers of Christian doctrine. Ryle distinguishes sanctification from justification while treating both as necessary to Christian salvation; do not merge their functions.
- [Holiness: VI. Growth](https://ccel.org/ccel/ryle/holiness/holiness.iii.v_1.html) — Believers seeking spiritual growth. Keep the discussion of growth within Ryle's wider account of holiness; this is a chapter within that argument.

## Boundaries

The section index preserves original chapter titles, XML identifiers, parent works, authors, source Scripture citations and reproducible hashes. Reading purposes are editorial paraphrases; full chapters retain the original argument. Boston and Flavel's providential explanations and Gill's household teaching remain attributed historical positions, not a merged interpretation. Modern editorial notes and quoted authors must not be mistaken for the principal author.

These are usable transcriptions, not a claim of flawless proofreading or exhaustive pastoral coverage. Six existing work/edition identities are reused; Owen's Of Temptation adds one. CCEL permits specified personal/educational uses; public redistribution of the electronic files is not cleared. [CCEL permissions](https://www.ccel.org/about/copyright.html).
