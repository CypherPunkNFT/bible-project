# Christ and justification in deeper study

Study Christ’s representative work, the meaning of justification, and the evidence of living faith in sequence.

**For:** Readers ready for sustained doctrinal argument

Begin after the grace path or equivalent grounding. Read the older prose slowly and define terms from the author’s argument.

Three stages, usually one stage per week; take longer where useful. This is a suggested pace, not measured reading time.

Read Scripture first, then the assigned sermon selection and substantial reading. Annotations and exercises are this library’s editorial guidance. Sources open at their hosts; page references distinguish assigned excerpts from whole works.

## 1 Christ as the sinless representative

Establish whose work bears the weight of reconciliation.

**Scripture:** [2 Corinthians 5:14–21](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/2CO/5?v=14). Read verse 21 within Paul’s ministry-of-reconciliation argument.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [Christ Our Substitute](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs310.pdf#page=2). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 2–3.

Introduces the contrast between Christ’s personal sinlessness and his bearing of sin.

**Reading note:** Selected argument, not endorsement of every historical analogy in the sermon.

**Substantial reading:** John Owen, [2 Corinthians v. 21](https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/just/just.xxii.vii.html). Justification, chapter XVIII, 2 Corinthians 5:21 treatment; printed pp. 347–351.

Supplies a longer exegetical argument after the sermon’s explanation.

**Reading note:** Owen’s argument is retained as his interpretation; later footnotes have distinct authorship.

**Reflect:** How does Owen distinguish bearing sin from becoming morally sinful?

**Put it into practice:** Outline the argument with the biblical wording beside each inference.

## 2 Justification and sanctification

Clarify a change in standing before God and a change within the believer.

**Scripture:** [Romans 4:1–8](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/ROM/4?v=1). Examine Paul’s use of Abraham, faith and the forgiveness of sins.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [Justification by Grace](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs126.pdf#page=4). Sermon excerpt, PDF page 4.

Return to the courtroom illustration now with more precise questions about its scope.

**Reading note:** The main text is Romans 3:24. Connections to other passages are editorial doctrinal companions.

**Substantial reading:** Charles Hodge, [Justification is a Forensic Act](https://ccel.org/ccel/hodge/theology3/theology3.iii.iii.ii.html). Systematic Theology III, chapter XVII, section 2; printed pp. 118–121.

Explains the declarative character of justification while distinguishing it from inward renewal.

**Reading note:** Only the assigned opening is assessed. Hodge’s descriptions of opponents are attributed polemic.

**Reflect:** Where does the analogy clarify Paul, and where might an analogy oversimplify?

**Put it into practice:** Write separate definitions of justification and sanctification, then explain their relation.

## 3 Living faith and obedient action

Test whether your account can take James’s warning seriously.

**Scripture:** [James 2:14–26](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/JAS/2?v=14). Follow James’s examples and his contrast between dead and living faith.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [Faith and Repentance Inseparable](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs460.pdf#page=1). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 1–2.

A thematic companion recalls that the gospel calls for repentance as well as belief; it is not a commentary on James.

**Reading note:** Main text Mark 1:15. Connections to other biblical books are thematic companions, not exposition of those books.

**Substantial reading:** John Owen, [Chapter XX. The doctrine of the apostle James concerning faith and works. Its agreement with that of St Paul.](https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/just/just.xxiv.html). Justification, chapter XX; printed pp. 392–397 on James 2.

Offers Owen’s sustained reconciliation of Paul and James for evaluation against both texts.

**Reading note:** This is Owen’s reconciliation of Paul and James, not a claim that every tradition agrees.

**Reflect:** Does Owen distinguish the ground of acceptance from its evidence convincingly?

**Put it into practice:** Explain his position fairly, then identify the strongest question you would ask him.

## When you finish

Distinguish substitution, justification and the fruits of faith without separating salvation from holiness.
