# Scripture miracles and resurrection

Define the claim, distinguish possibility from evidence, and then examine a specific resurrection argument.

**For:** Christians learning to examine and explain foundational truth claims

Use a notebook with separate columns for source statement, author inference and your own question. Read 1 Peter 3:15–16 as a posture for discussion.

Three stages, usually one stage per week; take longer where useful. This is a suggested pace, not measured reading time.

Read Scripture first, then the assigned sermon selection and substantial reading. Annotations and exercises are this library’s editorial guidance. Sources open at their hosts; page references distinguish assigned excerpts from whole works.

Begin with [1 Peter 3:15–16](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/1PE/3?v=15). Sets a posture of gentleness and respect for answering questions.

## 1 What does inspiration mean

Understand the doctrine before deciding whether an argument supports it.

**Scripture:** [2 Timothy 3:14–17](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/2TI/3?v=14). Locate inspiration within the passage’s emphasis on salvation, teaching and formation.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Bible](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs15.pdf#page=1). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 1–2.

Hear the doctrine preached to Christian hearers; the sermon itself says it is not developing external proofs.

**Reading note:** He explicitly declines to develop external proofs here. His hostile description of unbelievers is not the conversational model proposed by this path.

**Substantial reading:** A. A. Hodge, [Inspiration](https://archive.org/details/outlinesoftheolo00hodguoft/page/n73/mode/2up). Outlines of Theology, 1877, chapter IV, questions 1–5; printed pp. 70–72, PDF pp. 74–76.

Clarifies terms that are easily confused in conversations about the Bible.

**Reading note:** A doctrinal definition, not by itself independent proof of scriptural authority.

**Reflect:** Which statements define inspiration, and which would need further evidence for a questioning reader?

**Put it into practice:** Give a fair definition before offering an argument; avoid caricaturing the person who disagrees.

## 2 Could a miracle occur

Separate a philosophical objection from a historical investigation.

**Scripture:** [Acts 26:1–8](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/ACT/26?v=1). Read Paul’s resurrection question within his defense before Agrippa.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Resurrection Credible](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs1067.pdf#page=5). Sermon excerpt, PDF page 5.

Its treatment of natural analogies helps separate illustration from proof.

**Reading note:** This excerpt does not prove that Jesus rose. Elsewhere the sermon includes Spurgeon’s disputed millennial interpretation, not adopted by this path.

**Substantial reading:** Archibald Alexander, [Evidences of the Christian Religion](https://ccel.org/ccel/alexander_a/evidences/evidences.ii.vi.html). Evidences of the Christian Religion, complete chapter V; printed pp. 68–73.

Alexander argues that revelation and divine intervention are reasonable if God exists; he states the limit of that argument.

**Reading note:** The argument assumes God’s existence; its historical generalizations about peoples and religions are not endorsed or independently verified here.

**Reflect:** What must Alexander assume, and what further evidence would establish a particular event?

**Put it into practice:** State the strongest objection to the argument and a reply that does not assume the conclusion.

## 3 What supports the resurrection claim

Examine a historical argument without confusing theological importance with proof.

**Scripture:** [1 Corinthians 15:1–19](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/1CO/15?v=1). Read Paul’s report, named appearances and reasoning about the consequences of denial.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Resurrection of Our Lord Jesus](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs1653.pdf#page=1). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 1–2.

Explains why the claim is bodily and central to the gospel; it is a later interpretation of the evidence.

**Reading note:** A nineteenth-century theological sermon, not an additional first-century eyewitness.

**Substantial reading:** Archibald Alexander, [Evidences of the Christian Religion](https://ccel.org/ccel/alexander_a/evidences/evidences.ii.viii.html). Chapter VII, discussion of Paul’s testimony; printed pp. 109–111.

Lets you inspect Alexander’s use of Paul and early Christian belief rather than merely repeat his conclusion.

**Reading note:** Paul’s report of many witnesses is one surviving report, not hundreds of independent documents. General early Christian belief is not identical with eyewitness testimony. The nearby Josephus footnote and current textual scholarship are outside this assessment.

**Reflect:** What is directly reported, what is inferred, and which alternative explanation requires examination?

**Put it into practice:** Draft a respectful answer distinguishing Paul’s testimony, Alexander’s inference and your remaining research question.

Present a claim and its reasons while stating what the evidence does and does not establish.
