# Grief assurance and care at home

Practice patient listening, ground assurance in Christ, and carry care into ordinary household life.

**For:** Christians facing sorrow or doubt and those accompanying them

Take one stage at a time with someone you trust. These readings offer spiritual companionship, not a complete response to every crisis.

Three stages, usually one stage per week; take longer where useful. This is a suggested pace, not measured reading time.

Read Scripture first, then the assigned sermon selection and substantial reading. Annotations and exercises are this library’s editorial guidance. Sources open at their hosts; page references distinguish assigned excerpts from whole works.

## 1 Make room for sorrow

Attend to grief before reaching for an explanation of another person’s experience.

**Scripture:** [Ruth 1:1–22](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/RUT/1?v=1). Notice bereavement, Naomi’s words and Ruth’s companionship before the later resolution.

**Sermon:** John Piper, [Ruth: Sweet and Bitter Providence](https://www.desiringgod.org/messages/ruth-sweet-and-bitter-providence). Written sermon, focusing on Naomi’s misery, Ruth’s faithfulness and the signs of mercy.

Provides a narrative account of grief and mercy to discuss, while requiring care with its applications.

**Reading note:** The recommendation rests on written sections, not audio playback. Do not use its interpretation of Naomi to diagnose a particular sufferer’s guilt or promise an earthly happy ending.

**Substantial reading:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Minister’s Fainting Fits](https://archive.org/details/lecturestomystud00spurrich/page/n254/mode/2up). The Minister’s Fainting Fits, printed pp. 249–251; PDF pp. 255–257.

An experienced preacher’s admission of weakness resists the assumption that usefulness requires constant emotional strength.

**Reading note:** Written to ministers; application to other readers is editorial. Historical physiology is not present-day clinical guidance.

**Reflect:** Which promises can you offer, and which explanations of a particular loss would go beyond what you know?

**Put it into practice:** Listen without correcting the person’s first expression of grief; offer one specific form of practical help.

## 2 Distinguish weak faith from no faith

Consider assurance without requiring an unwavering emotional state.

**Scripture:** [1 John 5:1–21](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/1JN/5?v=1). Read the testimony to the Son, the purpose of assurance, and Newton’s main text in context.

**Sermon:** John Newton, [Of the Assurance of Faith](https://archive.org/details/worksrevjohnne02newt/page/n596/mode/2up). Of the Assurance of Faith, opening and first proposition; printed pp. 583–586, PDF pp. 597–600.

Explicitly rejects making full assurance essential to the existence of genuine faith.

**Reading note:** 1810 posthumous collected witness; individual delivery date unknown. Source main text 1 John 5:19; assigned Scripture includes its broader context.

**Substantial reading:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [All of Grace](https://ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/grace/grace.xx.html). All of Grace, complete chapter Why Saints Persevere.

Directs hope toward God’s faithfulness and union with Christ.

**Reading note:** Historic English digital witness; no full-text copy or new critical edition is supplied.

**Reflect:** How can you encourage trust without demanding that someone first feel certain?

**Put it into practice:** Identify one promise in the text and discuss it without measuring faith by the intensity of a feeling.

## 3 Care through Scripture prayer and example

Move from general concern to sustained household practices.

**Scripture:** [Joshua 24:14–24](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/JOS/24?v=14). Read the household declaration within covenant commitment and the people’s response.

**Sermon:** George Whitefield, [The Great Duty of Family-Religion](https://archive.org/details/worksofreverendg05whit/page/n61/mode/2up). The Great Duty of Family-Religion, opening; printed pp. 52–54, PDF pp. 62–64.

Brings household instruction and prayer into view alongside public worship.

**Reading note:** 1772 posthumous printed sermon. Its household-governor language and severe responsibility rhetoric require historical context; parents cannot manufacture another person’s faith.

**Substantial reading:** J. C. Ryle, [The Duties of Parents](https://www.monergism.com/duties-parents). The Duties of Parents, counsels V and VI on Scripture and prayer.

Provides sustained counsel on introducing Scripture and prayer to children.

**Reading note:** Underlying print impression unestablished. These selected counsels do not endorse every disciplinary practice elsewhere in the work.

**Reflect:** What can a caregiver faithfully do, and what belongs to God rather than parental control?

**Put it into practice:** Choose a short, sustainable time for Scripture and prayer; readers without children can consider how to support a family without taking over its responsibilities.

## When you finish

Offer encouragement that neither promises a quick resolution nor makes spiritual confidence a condition of being heard.
