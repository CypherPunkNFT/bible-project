# Character teaching and mission in ministry

Put character before technique, learn responsible application, and connect preparation to service and mission.

**For:** Prospective pastors elders teachers and group leaders

Study within the oversight of your church. Completing a path does not establish a call, qualification or office.

Three stages, usually one stage per week; take longer where useful. This is a suggested pace, not measured reading time.

Read Scripture first, then the assigned sermon selection and substantial reading. Annotations and exercises are this library’s editorial guidance. Sources open at their hosts; page references distinguish assigned excerpts from whole works.

## 1 Watch your life and teaching

Begin with the person who serves rather than a collection of methods.

**Scripture:** [1 Timothy 4:6–16](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/1TI/4?v=6). Hold example, doctrine, public reading and perseverance together.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Parent's and Pastor's Joy](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs1148.pdf#page=1). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 1–2.

Connects a pastor’s joy to people walking in truth, rather than the teacher’s prominence.

**Reading note:** Its main text is 3 John 1:4. The pairing with 1 Timothy is about ministry character, not identical passage exposition.

**Substantial reading:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Minister’s Self-watch](https://archive.org/details/lecturestomystud00spurrich/page/n16/mode/2up). The Minister’s Self-watch, printed pp. 11–12; PDF pp. 17–18.

Explains why the minister’s inward life belongs to the work itself.

**Reading note:** Initial college address excerpt; neither a credential nor a full account of ordination.

**Reflect:** What would people who know you well say about the relation between your teaching and conduct?

**Put it into practice:** Ask a trusted church leader for one concrete area of growth and a way to follow it up.

## 2 Understand before applying

Learn to explain a passage and address real hearers responsibly.

**Scripture:** [2 Timothy 4:1–5](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/2TI/4?v=1). Read the charge to preach alongside patience, teaching and endurance.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [How to Read the Bible](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs1503.pdf#page=1). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 1–2.

Challenges the equation of frequent reading with genuine understanding.

**Reading note:** Main text Matthew 12:3–7. Historical polemical descriptions are the preacher’s, not independent research about other traditions.

**Substantial reading:** William Perkins, [Use and Application](https://banneroftruth.org/uk/resources/book-excerpts/2019/how-to-use-and-apply-doctrines/). The Art of Prophesying, chapter 7; complete authorized publisher excerpt on use and application.

Shows how application takes account of hearers while preserving the relation of law and gospel.

**Reading note:** Modernized excerpt, not the full chapter or a reusable transcription of the 1592 text.

**Reflect:** Does your application arise from the passage, and how would it differ for two different hearers?

**Put it into practice:** Prepare a ten-minute lesson with a text summary, main claim and two reasoned applications; invite review.

## 3 Serve and participate in mission

Let preparation lead to faithful witness in cooperation with others.

**Scripture:** [Matthew 28:16–20](https://bibleproject.io/read/kjv/MAT/28?v=16). Read disciple-making under Christ’s authority and promised presence.

**Sermon:** Charles Haddon Spurgeon, [The Soul-Winner](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs1292.pdf#page=1). Sermon excerpt, PDF pages 1–2.

Joins character and intentional witness, rather than treating evangelism as technique alone.

**Reading note:** Main text Proverbs 11:30; this numbered sermon is distinct from the later Soul Winner anthology.

**Substantial reading:** William Carey, [An Enquiry into the Obligations of Christians to Use Means for the Conversion of the Heathens](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11449/pg11449-images.html). An Enquiry, sections I and V on the continuing commission and means of mission.

Provides a substantial historical argument for continuing obligation and shared practical means.

**Reading note:** 1792 context. Distinguish the commission from Carey’s proposed organization; historical demographics and colonial assumptions are not current mission research.

**Reflect:** Which parts of Carey’s proposal express an enduring obligation, and which are context-dependent methods?

**Put it into practice:** Plan one act of service or witness with your church, including whom to learn from and how to evaluate it.

## When you finish

Prepare a short passage-based lesson, invite correction, and identify one accountable opportunity to serve.
