Completed selected-edition acquisition and initial component catalog, 2026-10-05. This is **not an exhaustive census of everything these authors preached**. Records are catalogued; none of this batch is marked reviewed or published.

## Bibliography and publication boundaries

The [selection bibliography](bibliography.json) was established before corpus acquisition. Edwards volumes 5–6 and then 1 and 3 were added to it before their respective downloads; the revision notes preserve that expansion. Edition findings subsequently correct the initial catalog descriptions. The [inventory](inventory.json) provides every component's title, main text, group position, volume, published page reference, checked PDF heading page, classification and source witness.

- **Edwards:** *The Works of President Edwards, with a Memoir of His Life*, New York: G. & C. & H. Carvill, 1830, volumes I, III, V–X. Volume IX is a screened contextual volume, with no individual sermons counted. Volume I supplies the Farewell Sermon; III contains the sermon-derived *History of Redemption* and *Distinguishing Marks*; V supplies *Five Discourses*; VI–VIII contain numbered sermon divisions; X supplies the Brainerd funeral sermon. [Volume VI](https://archive.org/details/worksofpresidents06edwa), [volume X](https://archive.org/details/worksofpresident10edwa).
- **Whitefield:** *Works*, London: Edward and Charles Dilly; Edinburgh: Kincaid and Creech, volumes V–VI, **1772 on both title pages**. The host's broader 1771–1772 set date is not assigned to these individual volumes. Contents asterisks identify first publication from the author's manuscripts. [Volume V](https://archive.org/details/worksofreverendg05whit), [volume VI](https://archive.org/details/worksofreverendg06whit).
- **Newton:** *Works*, New York, volumes II–V, Williams and Whiting, **1810**; volume VI, Samuel Whiting & Co., **1811**. Volume III's history and hymns are retained as context and excluded from sermon counts. *Messiah* first appeared in two volumes in 1786; its title identifies preaching at St Mary Woolnoth in 1784–1785. The acquired Works IV reprints all 50 discourses. [First-edition bibliography](https://archive.org/details/bim_eighteenth-century_messiah-fifty-expositor_newton-john_1786_1), [acquired Messiah](https://archive.org/details/worksrevjohnne04newt).
- **Ryle:** *The Two Bears, and Other Sermons for Children*, William Hunt, **1869**, and *The Christian Race and Other Sermons*, Hodder & Stoughton, **1900**. The latter was selected and prepared by **T. J. Madden with Ryle's permission**, according to Madden's preface dated March 1, 1900. Its publication day relative to Ryle's death is unknown; it is not automatically labelled posthumous. [Children's collection](https://archive.org/details/twobearsandothe00rylegoog), [Christian Race](https://archive.org/details/thechristianrace00ryleuoft).

## What editorial history changes

Edwards's revival setting and the Northampton dismissal are retained in preface witnesses. His *Justification by Faith Alone* combines two public lectures and was enlarged for print. Erskine explicitly turned *History of Redemption* from sermons into a continuous treatise (III, PDF p.177). Williams combined three unfinished sermons into *Man's Natural Blindness* (VII, PDF p.15). Three Practical Sermons entries explicitly consolidate several discourses. These are preserved as distinct kinds of material rather than inflated into invented individual deliveries.

Newton's *Six Discourses* says they were prepared for an expected preaching opportunity that was overruled (II, PDF p.271). *Messiah* says most discourses were considerably abridged for print (IV, preface). The 1800 charity sermon's advertisement explicitly disclaims an exact copy of delivery: Newton reconstructed it without notes after an interval (VI, PDF p.508). His occasional sermons retain their specific ordination/ministry, funeral, fast, charity and thanksgiving context where documented; title-page mottoes are distinguished from the actual main texts.

Ryle's *Happy Little Girl* is a short narrative and *Little Things* a pastoral address, not two securely documented delivered sermons. The latter's introductory account and biblical applications do not justify assigning a fabricated single main text. Madden's prefatory claim about Ryle not previously publishing a sermon volume is preserved in its context; it is not used to erase the 1869 children's book.

## Reconciliation and limitations

- Edwards VI no. XVIII directs the reader to the Brainerd funeral sermon in X. Both series references resolve to **one component work**, not two copies of a sermon. Pemberton's ordination sermon in X is not attributed to Edwards.
- Contents leaves are misplaced between acquired Edwards VI and VII. Printed contents references sometimes differ from the actual heading; the inventory retains both. Edwards's Practical Sermons footnote prints **March 1773**, after his death (VI, PDF p.482). This is a source conflict, not a valid delivery date. Manuscript headings that merely say “dated” remain source witnesses, not asserted preaching dates.
- Whitefield's date “Sunday, May 17, 1738” is retained as a printed witness with an unresolved calendar/day discrepancy. Historical dates are not silently converted between Old and New Style.
- Main-text corrections use the sermon heading and quotation: for example Ryle's *Lord Our Righteousness* is Jeremiah 23:6, and Whitefield's *Satan's Devices* is 2 Corinthians 2:11. OCR readings of the contents are not treated as authoritative. Newton's General Resurrection heading gives 1 Corinthians 15:51–52 while the quotation continues into verse 53.
- The existing published `work-whitefield-sermons` describes an **1830 selection**. It is not overwritten or asserted identical to these 1772 volumes. Existing Edwards *Religious Affections*, Newton *Cardiphonia*, and Ryle *Holiness* records likewise retain their separate identities. See [reconciliation](reconciliation.json).
- No historical audio is claimed. Future recordings by modern readers must be separately identified as **later readings**, with reader, recording date, source and rights.

All 17 selected scans and all 243 selected components have been located; no missing component is reported within these bounded divisions. This does **not** certify every internal leaf or OCR word. Edwards's wider manuscript corpus, Whitefield's Gurney reports and other pamphlet witnesses, and further Ryle collections remain explicit expansion gaps. Edwards II and IV and Newton I are outside this sermon extraction, not missing members of a claimed complete collected-works acquisition.

## Rights, verification and reuse

The identified historic printed texts are public domain in the US under the publication-term rules; modern additions and source files receive separate assessments. [US Copyright Act, chapter 3](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap3.html). The Google-derived Ryle children's scan carries usage guidelines on its first page: attribution and watermarks are retained, and public redistribution/full-text indexing remains unresolved. Metadata snapshots are provenance evidence, not a claim to republish modern descriptions. No Monergism curated files or permission-dependent modern editions were acquired.

Acquisition used the public Internet Archive API with caching, an identified agent, bounded sequential requests and retry handling. An ordinary alternate public download server supplied Edwards X after the primary endpoint returned HTTP 500. No access restriction was bypassed.

Checks: original hashes and available host MD5 values; PDF readability; all component locators within the correct scan; all **241 supplied main-text references** map to valid biblical verses; independent printed-series expected counts and cross-volume reconciliation. Representative title, contents and editorial pages were rendered and visually checked for all four authors. This is a metadata review, not complete OCR proofreading or human theological review.
