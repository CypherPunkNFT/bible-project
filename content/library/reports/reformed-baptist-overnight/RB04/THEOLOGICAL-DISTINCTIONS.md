# RB04: distinctions that the knowledge base must preserve

This is an AI-assisted reading map grounded in identified source sections. It is not an exhaustive human doctrinal review or a replacement for Scripture. The campaign uses the 1689 confession as its Baptist reference; the site's existing Westminster-based editorial policy remains unchanged.

| Question | Source locations | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| What makes a sinner accepted before God? | Keach, *Marrow*, sermons 1-2; Booth, *Reign of Grace*, ch.6; Fuller, sermons XVII-XIX; Owen, *Justification* | Justification concerns righteous standing through Christ, received by faith. Regeneration and sanctification concern inward life and holiness. Neither repentance nor holiness becomes a meritorious substitute for Christ's righteousness. |
| Does faith mean bare assent? | Fuller, *Strictures*, letters III-IV, VI-VIII; held *Gospel Worthy*, parts I-III | Fuller disputes Sandeman's account of justifying faith. Quotations of Sandeman must be attributed as the position Fuller answers. A keyword hit for "faith" cannot settle whether the author means saving faith, historical assent or assurance. |
| How do regeneration, the Word and faith relate? | Fuller, *Strictures*, letters VI-VIII and *Regeneration by the Word*; Booth, *Glad Tidings*, ch.III; Owen, *Pneumatologia*, book III | Preserve the exact definition of regeneration in each argument. Fuller distinguishes the Spirit's efficient causality and the relation of disposition, perception and believing; Booth argues against making antecedent holiness a warrant to believe. These treatments share important commitments but should not be merged into an invented unanimous Baptist account of causal ordering. |
| What warrants the gospel invitation? | Fuller, *Gospel Worthy*, part II; Booth, *Glad Tidings*, ch.I-II | The gospel invitation and obligation to believe differ from a person's evidence that he already possesses grace. Booth's argument does not make a prior discovery of holiness the admission ticket to Christ. Fuller distinguishes moral inability from lack of natural capacity. |
| What is adoption? | Booth, *Reign*, ch.7; Fuller, sermon XVI, lines 118170-118234; Watson, *Body*, Adoption | Regeneration gives a new nature; adoption concerns filial status and privileges. Adoption, justification and regeneration are closely related benefits, not interchangeable database topic labels. |
| Can a believer lack felt assurance? | Goodwin, *Child of Light*, part I, ch.I-II; *Justifying Faith*, part II book II; Watson, *Body*, Assurance; Pink, *Assurance* | Distinguish saving faith from assurance of one's personal interest in Christ. Also distinguish temporary darkness, pastoral comfort, evidences of grace and false confidence based only on a past profession. Do not diagnose a reader from isolated quotations. |
| How does holiness follow grace? | Booth, *Reign*, ch.8-10; *Death of Legal Hope*, sections V-VII; Owen, *Mortification* and *Pneumatologia*, book IV; Pink, *Sanctification* | Holiness is the fruit of grace and indispensable to the Christian life; it is not the purchase price of justification. Moral law considered as a covenant differs from law as a rule of obedience. Perseverance, mortification and growth remain distinct concepts. |
| What does "definitive sanctification" mean? | Murray, *Definitive Sanctification*: Fact, Character and Agency sections | Murray describes a decisive breach with sin's dominion grounded in union with Christ's death and resurrection. He also affirms progressive sanctification and remaining sin. His discussion of Romans 6:7 must not erase the forensic meaning of justification elsewhere. His Presbyterian covenant/ordinance framework remains attributed. |
| Did Fuller and Booth agree about imputation? | Fuller, *Three Conversations*, I-III; six letters to Ryland about Booth, especially II-IV and VI; imputation essays, lines 178486-179510 | The conversations are Fuller-authored literary dialogue, not an independent transcript of Booth's words. The letters distinguish the author's position from charges made against it. The editor explicitly notes later revised imputation wording at lines 178779-178781, pointing to p.951. Preserve author, interlocutor, date where stated and editor attribution. |

## Edition limits affect theological interpretation

Keach's 2026 electronic edition makes light wording/format corrections to a historical work. Its postscript identifies two first sermons as this publication; later contemplated sermons are not a missing tail of this acquired file.

Fuller's 1846 collected witness contains editor Andrew Gunton Fuller's memoir, historical accounts, author works, opponents' quotations and indexes. Its nine *Systematic Divinity* letters were all that Fuller completed of a planned larger series. The edition intentionally refers letter I to a previously printed sermon at pp.559-560 rather than repeating it. Neither editorial choices nor an unfinished authorial project should be called a failed download.

Booth's 1813 volume contains *Reign of Grace* as well as *Death of Legal Hope* and a memoir. The already held clean *Reign* remains the starting edition; acquiring the volume does not create another new logical work. The historical OCR is rough. Chapel Library's 2017 text preserves seven sections but explicitly identifies its work as **abridgment and annotations**. Definitions, new subheadings and anonymous notes are not automatically Booth's wording. Use the complete historical witness when testing whether an argument or qualification was omitted.

Owen, Goodwin, Sibbes, Watson and Murray provide scoped conservative Reformed comparisons. Baptism, covenant structure and polity passages must remain author-specific. Their presence does not select a new Baptist model or change the collection charter. Held authors with matching surnames are not automatically these people: Andrew Fuller is not Francis Fuller; John Murray is not Andrew Murray or the American universalist namesake.

## Consequences for the later relationship tables

Use separate fields for work, edition, author/contributor, source role, topic sense, claim, Scripture passage and exact locator. A quoted opposing claim needs an `answered-position` role; it must not become an endorsed claim merely because it occurs inside an eligible book. For chronology, preserve an explicit unknown date rather than infer one from position in a collected volume.

Connect chapters to topics as attributed treatments before asserting agreement/disagreement between authors. Word overlap alone does not prove shared doctrine. Distinguish doctrine definition, textual argument, pastoral application, historical controversy and editorial annotation. These distinctions prepare the study tables the owner requested before exploratory graph views.
