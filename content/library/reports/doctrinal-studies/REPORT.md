# Systematic theology, doctrine and theological lectures

The first L07 collection indexes **129 chapters, lectures and substantial sections across four existing works**, under the library's shared doctrine hierarchy. Every section retains its author, edition, position and parent work, and has Scripture companions and links to existing sermons. Two historical books were acquired; Charles Hodge and Owen remain authorized reading links. All new records are catalogued research inputs, with no new public publication.

Checked 2026-10-05. Start with the [doctrine hierarchy](doctrine-hierarchy.json), [section connections](section-connections.json), or [source inventory](inventory.json). The [bibliography](bibliography.json) was established before acquisition. All four authors were already eligible under the broader Calvinist evangelical charter.

Each section has an **editorial Scripture companion**, with a reason for its relevance. Those suggestions are not attributed to the author and do not enter canonical author-citation or exposition fields. For example, Genesis 2:15–17 accompanies covenant-of-works material, while the note makes clear that covenant terminology is the theological interpretation being studied. Related readings for 41 sections widen the available sermon connections; their narrower or adjacent relevance is explained locally.

**Nineteen sections have source-verified passage assignments**, from explicit source headings, lecture mottos or selected body reading. Four body assessments retain precise locators and separate citation from substantial exposition. Warfield's mottos are citations, not claims that each lecture expounds its verse throughout. ## Existing sermon connections

The connection index supplies **322 links to 124 distinct existing sermons**, reaching all 129 sections. Selection uses verified English-numbered sermon main texts and canonical verse overlap; unresolved references and incidental citations do not qualify. Every edge retains the matched reference, overlapping verse IDs, source destination and evidence basis:

- 43 edges match a passage actually assigned to the doctrinal source.
- 185 match an explicitly editorial companion reading.
- 94 match an explicitly editorial related-topic reading.

These categories describe how the links were found, not proof of identical interpretation. Two selected connections were also checked in the sermon bodies:

| Doctrinal section | Sermon | Why the connection is useful |
|---|---|---|
| Hodge, volume III, chapter XVII, section 2; opening pp. 118–121 | Spurgeon no. 126, Justification by Grace, PDF p. 4, division II | Both explain justification as forensic and distinguish it from mere pardon. Hodge's inspected paragraph cites Romans 4:5; the sermon opens with Romans 3:24. This is a doctrinal comparison across related passages. |
| Owen, chapter XVIII, 2 Corinthians 5:21 treatment, especially pp. 347–351 | Spurgeon no. 310, Christ Our Substitute, PDF pp. 2–3 | Both distinguish Christ's personal sinlessness from his representative bearing of sin. Extended exegesis can be read beside congregational explanation. |

Sources: [Hodge's section](https://ccel.org/ccel/hodge/theology3/theology3.iii.iii.ii.html), [Spurgeon 126](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs126.pdf), [Owen's subsection](https://ccel.org/ccel/owen/just/just.xxii.vii.html), [Spurgeon 310](https://www.spurgeongems.org/sermon/chs310.pdf). The remaining 320 edges are metadata study leads. Spurgeon supplies 274 of all 322 edges, reflecting the existing catalog's concentration rather than a judgment that other preachers are less suitable. Newton, Edwards, Ryle and Whitefield supply the remainder. The matcher prefers different authors where the same passage permits, but does not invent diversity. ## Edition history and rights

The [1877 Outlines scan](https://archive.org/details/outlinesoftheolo00hodguoft) has its title on PDF p. 7, British editor's preface on pp. 9–10, author preface on pp. 11–13, and contents on pp. 15–16. All 40 chapter openings were checked. Its author explains that these outlines supported extemporaneous congregational lectures and acknowledges his father's questions and some lectures; the book is not a verbatim sermon transcript. Goold's editor credit is retained in the edition-witness report, separately from the author's chapters. It is not silently merged with a later expanded edition.

The [Warfield witness](https://archive.org/details/planofsalvation00warf) gives **June 1914** as the lecture-series delivery month and **1915** as the edition year on PDF p. 5. Individual delivery days remain unknown. Contents are on p. 9; motto leaves are pp. 12, 38, 64, 88 and 112. Notes at printed pp. 137–144 are supporting apparatus, not a sixth lecture. Warfield's label “Universalism” belongs to his particular classification; it should not automatically be reduced to the modern claim that every person is finally saved.

Four new content files total **35,047,686 bytes**. Two retained host metadata snapshots are evidence files, excluded from content-asset counts. The [acquisition manifest](acquisition-manifest.json) records each original's URL, timestamp, byte count, host MD5 and local SHA-256. Raw bytes remain under `BibleProject/sources/library/source-internet-archive/`, attached to the existing A. A. Hodge and Warfield edition IDs. OCR is unproofread. Title and contents pages were inspected visually, and all indexed scan openings were checked.

The historical editions have a scoped United States public-domain assessment. Modern additions and worldwide rights are excluded from that conclusion. Download, public hosting and full-text indexing remain distinct decisions. [US Copyright Office duration guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf).

The four CCEL contents pages are retained privately as bibliographic evidence, with ten-second request spacing. [CCEL's policy](https://www.ccel.org/about/copyright.html) requests permission for republication; this mission does not mirror its full works. Existing Spurgeon PDFs were consulted under their recorded asset conditions, without reacquisition or redistribution. [Source-access decisions and edition witnesses](source-access.json) contain the detailed boundaries.

## Completion and remaining scope

This batch creates **129 section works, four assets, six series inventories and one run record**, reusing four root works and six edition identities. No root work is duplicated or promoted; existing public reading selections remain unchanged. All authors retain their own positions, including distinct treatments of justification, covenants and sacramental efficacy. Subject placement and a sermon connection do not manufacture doctrinal consensus.

Remaining work includes identified reusable print witnesses for the larger Hodge and Owen texts, wider eligible systematic authors, more substantial-section indexing, full-body citation review, OCR correction, and evaluation of sermon arguments before treating all matches as recommendations. Four works are an initial collection, not an exhaustive systematic-theology library. See the [resumption checkpoint](checkpoint.json).

Reproduce the offline catalog with `python -X utf8 scripts/catalog-doctrinal-studies.py`. `--prepare` reconstructs the inventory from retained contents and scans; `scripts/acquire-doctrinal-studies.py --fetch` explicitly retrieves only the named acquisitions and refuses to overwrite originals. Eight focused tests check context, hierarchy, citation boundaries, sermon overlap, scoped pairing reviews, file hashes, complete chapter boundaries, and delivery/publication distinctions. Run `python -X utf8 -m unittest discover -s scripts/tests -p test_doctrinal_studies.py` and `node scripts/validate-library.mjs`. Validation checks structure and evidence relationships, not complete theological accuracy.
