# Christianity and Islam: nine questions

Source comparisons prepared 2026-10-05. These are editorial study notes, not debate transcripts or fully reviewed public teaching. Muslim claims below are contextual sources, distinct from recommended Christian teaching. See [the collection report](REPORT.md) for acquisition and review limits.

## God: Does shared monotheism mean agreement about who God is?

**Christian claim.** Reformed Christianity confesses one God and inseparable Father, Son and Spirit; knowing the Father includes the Son.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 112 affirms divine unity and rejects begetting. This supplies an actual point of disagreement, while sharing a word for God alone settles neither reference nor doctrinal agreement.

**Premises to examine.** Distinguish numerical monotheism, reference to the Creator, and agreement about divine identity. A doctrinal disagreement does not by itself prove speakers intend different creators.

**Serious objection.** If both communities intend the Creator of Abraham, denying all common reference may confuse false beliefs about someone with referring to someone else.

**Christian response.** State the Christian disagreement precisely: the Father is revealed in the Son, and triune identity is essential to Christian worship. Defend that revelation rather than making the Arabic word Allah the argument.

**Scripture companions:** Deuteronomy 6:4; John 14:6-11.

**Source locators:**

- Christian: [Westminster Confession 2.1-3](../../catalog/works/work-l08-wcf-chapter-002.json)
- Christian: [Preface pp. 7-9; chapter VIII begins p. 107](https://www.zwemercenter.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Zwemer-Moslem-Doctrine-of-God-.pdf)
- Contextual Muslim: [Qur’an 112:3](https://quran.com/112/3)

**Still unresolved.** Zwemer’s generalizations about Islamic piety are historically situated; they are not evidence about every Muslim. Reference and worship questions need separate philosophical treatment.

## Trinity: Is the Trinity three gods or divine physical procreation?

**Christian claim.** Westminster 2.3 distinguishes three persons in one undivided divine essence. Eternal sonship is not physical reproduction.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 4:171 rejects Three and divine sonship; 5:116 condemns taking Jesus and Mary as deities beside God.

**Premises to examine.** Identify the doctrine being criticized before evaluating it. Qur’an 5:116 names Jesus and Mary; it does not explicitly call that grouping the Christian Trinity.

**Serious objection.** Denying physical procreation does not answer the Muslim objection to any divine Son or to personal plurality within God.

**Christian response.** Explain the person/essence distinction, then defend its scriptural basis. Matthew 28:19 provides a triadic baptismal formula; it requires argument alongside biblical monotheism, not a bare assertion that one verse settles every metaphysical objection.

**Scripture companions:** Matthew 28:19; Deuteronomy 6:4; John 1:1-3.

**Source locators:**

## Christ: Do the Gospels preserve Jesus’ divine identity or develop it later?

**Christian claim.** White’s Toronto report maintains that Jesus’ divine identity is rooted in Jesus himself, not merely later Gospel redaction.

**Muslim claim.** Ally’s attributed reflection acknowledges high Christology in the Gospels but explains it as development from a human Jesus; he appeals to differences between Mark and later accounts.

**Premises to examine.** Distinguish what a Gospel says from whether it reliably preserves Jesus’ teaching. Ally’s redaction argument needs premises about literary dependence, chronology and the significance of editorial differences.

**Serious objection.** Quoting John’s high Christology alone does not answer whether that presentation is historically early or reliable.

**Christian response.** Compare parallel accounts and consider pre-Gospel evidence such as Philippians 2:6-11. A development thesis must account for early worship and testimony; a Christian reply must defend dating and interpretation rather than presume them.

**Scripture companions:** Philippians 2:6-11; Mark 9:5; Matthew 17:4.

**Source locators:**

- Christian: [Report paragraphs on deity and critical method](https://www.aomin.org/aoblog/islam/a-quick-report-from-canada/)
- Contextual Muslim: [Opening thesis; eight Matthew/Mark comparisons](https://shabirally.wordpress.com/2012/03/27/did-jesus-claim-deity/)

**Still unresolved.** Ally’s claims about Bruce/Bauckham have not been checked against their original books. His example 4 cites episode-opening verses rather than the precise God/my Father sayings; check Mark 3:35 and Matthew 12:50. Article-to-article disagreement verified; actual live exchange unviewed.

## Crucifixion: Was Jesus crucified, and what would establish that historically?

**Christian claim.** White separates the historical occurrence of crucifixion from its saving meaning. Piper appeals to early Christian testimony and the difficulty of inventing a crucified Messiah.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 4:157 denies that they killed or crucified Jesus and describes an appearance; translations differ in how explicitly they supply a substitute.

**Premises to examine.** An early witness and a later revelation claim are different kinds of evidence. The bracketed another in Sahih International is an interpretive supplement; it is not a named substitute in this verse.

**Serious objection.** A Muslim who accepts Qur’anic revelation may regard it as correcting historical inference. Early Christian testimony could also be disputed for bias.

**Christian response.** Assess Paul’s testimony, its transmission and independent corroboration, then examine the grounds for accepting the later revelation. Explain why bias does not automatically make testimony false, while acknowledging that early testimony alone is not a complete historical demonstration.

**Scripture companions:** 1 Corinthians 15:3-8; Mark 15:24-39.

**Source locators:**

- Christian: [Opening: historical event versus willing sacrifice](https://www.aomin.org/aoblog/islam/opening-and-closing-statements-shabir-ally-debate/)
- Christian: [The Historical Evidence; An Improbable Myth](https://www.desiringgod.org/articles/the-great-offense-was-jesus-really-crucified)
- Contextual Muslim: [Qur’an 4:157](https://quran.com/4/157)

**Still unresolved.** Tacitus and Ignatius quoted by these apologists remain secondary citations here; original editions not yet collated. The embarrassment argument supports an inference; it is not a logically conclusive proof.

## Resurrection: Is Jesus’ resurrection the same claim as his being raised to God?

**Christian claim.** Paul connects Christ’s death, burial, resurrection and appearances; bodily resurrection is essential to his gospel.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 4:158 says God raised Jesus to himself. Qur’an 19:33 speaks of birth, death and being raised alive; this alone does not establish the Christian death-burial-third-day sequence.

**Premises to examine.** Separate resurrection after death, exaltation/rescue, and future general resurrection. Do not assume Muslims reject resurrection as a category.

**Serious objection.** Even sincere reports of appearances do not alone establish that God raised a dead body; alternative explanations and prior beliefs about miracles matter.

**Christian response.** Evaluate the cumulative testimony and its alternatives, including whether Jesus died. State the theological inference openly and avoid treating a claim of many witnesses as many independently preserved testimonies.

**Scripture companions:** 1 Corinthians 15:3-8; 1 Corinthians 15:12-20; Luke 24:36-43.

**Source locators:**

## Scripture: Does Qur’anic recognition of earlier revelation validate the extant Bible?

**Christian claim.** Reformed teaching locates authority in the inspired Scriptures; textual transmission must still be investigated historically.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 5:48 describes the Qur’an as confirming earlier Scripture and exercising authority over it. It does not by itself identify the Injil with every wording in the four canonical Gospels.

**Premises to examine.** Distinguish original revelation, surviving manuscripts, interpretation and canon. A corruption claim needs a specified text, change, time and evidence; an inerrancy claim does not erase manuscript variants.

**Serious objection.** A Muslim may accept an original Gospel while denying the reliability or interpretation of surviving Christian books.

**Christian response.** Ask for concrete manuscript and historical evidence, compare actual variants, and test whether proposed corruption explains the disputed doctrine. The confirmation argument needs additional premises about which texts were accessible and affirmed.

**Scripture companions:** 2 Timothy 3:15-17; Luke 1:1-4.

**Source locators:**

- Christian: [Westminster Confession 1.4,8](../../catalog/works/work-l08-wcf-chapter-001.json)
- Contextual Muslim: [Qur’an 5:48](https://quran.com/5/48)

## Revelation: How should competing claims to divine revelation be tested?

**Christian claim.** Galatians 1:8 rejects a message contrary to the apostolic gospel, even with an angelic claim; Hebrews 1 centers revelation on the Son.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 4:163 places revelation to Muhammad in continuity with Noah, Abraham, Jesus and other prophets.

**Premises to examine.** Each tradition makes an authority claim, not merely a neutral historical report. A Christian canonical test expresses Christian commitments; a Muslim need not already accept that authority.

**Serious objection.** Rejecting the Qur’an solely because it differs from Christian Scripture can seem circular to someone who disputes that Scripture’s authority.

**Christian response.** Make the internal Christian test explicit, then separately offer public reasons for trusting the apostolic witness. Apply comparable standards to competing historical claims, textual evidence and alleged prophecy.

**Scripture companions:** Galatians 1:6-9; Hebrews 1:1-4.

**Source locators:**

- Contextual Muslim: [Qur’an 4:163](https://quran.com/4/163)

## Salvation: Why does Christian forgiveness require Christ’s atonement?

**Christian claim.** Christian justification joins divine grace, Christ’s redemptive work and faith; White’s printed case argues for a willing sacrifice.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 39:53-54 joins mercy with a call to return to God; 4:48 distinguishes association with God from sins God may forgive. This cannot fairly be reduced to earning salvation with no mercy.

**Premises to examine.** Different accounts of forgiveness rest on different accounts of sin, justice and mediation. Christian grace is not permission to continue in sin; Muslim mercy must be represented from its own texts.

**Serious objection.** If God can pardon, why is a substitute necessary, and how can another’s suffering be just?

**Christian response.** Explain the Christian claim that the Son gives himself willingly and that God’s grace and justice meet in Christ, using Romans 3:24-26 and John 10:17-18. The moral objection to representation still needs an argument, not merely the word sacrifice.

**Scripture companions:** Romans 3:24-26; John 10:17-18; Romans 6:1-2.

**Source locators:**

## Prophethood: Do biblical promises identify Muhammad as a coming prophet?

**Christian claim.** Acts 3 applies the prophet-like-Moses text to Jesus, and John 14:26 explicitly identifies the promised Helper as the Holy Spirit.

**Muslim claim.** Qur’an 33:40 identifies Muhammad with the seal of the prophets; 61:6 presents Jesus announcing a messenger named Ahmad.

**Premises to examine.** Compare each text within its own speaker, audience and literary context. A Qur’anic assertion of prediction is distinct from locating that prediction in extant biblical wording.

**Serious objection.** A Muslim may argue that the extant Bible obscures a prediction or that the texts have a broader referent than Christian interpreters allow.

**Christian response.** Read Deuteronomy 18 alongside Acts 3 and John’s Helper promises. Require textual evidence for proposed alterations and explain why the identified referent fits the whole passage; do not rely on an unverified Greek/Arabic word resemblance.

**Scripture companions:** Deuteronomy 18:15-18; Acts 3:22-26; John 14:16-17; John 14:26.

**Source locators:**

- Contextual Muslim: [Qur’an 33:40](https://quran.com/33/40)

**Still unresolved.** No claim made that Hussain used these exact arguments in the unviewed debate. Greek parakletos/periklutos claims and specific manuscript allegations require independent verification.
