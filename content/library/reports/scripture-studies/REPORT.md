## Interpretations remain separate

Two comparison questions link independently sourced positions. The summaries are AI-assisted editorial paraphrases with stated limits, not external theological approval or a manufactured historical exchange.

- **Genesis 17:7:** Calvin describes a spiritual covenant outwardly offered to Abraham's natural descendants while distinguishing effectual reception by faith. Gill distinguishes temporal benefits for natural descendants from enduring saving benefits for spiritual descendants. Their common natural/spiritual distinction does not eliminate the difference in how they describe covenant administration. No complete baptism argument is inferred from these paragraphs. Sources: [Calvin, verse 7](https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom01/calcom01.xxiii.i.html), [Gill, verse 7](https://www.biblestudytools.com/commentaries/gills-exposition-of-the-bible/genesis-17-7.html).
- **Isaiah 65:16–17:** Calvin traces restoration from return out of Babylon through Christ's reign to final renewal; Gill explicitly includes a future Jewish-conversion stage. Both include ultimate renewal of heaven and earth. This is not reduced to a metaphorical-versus-literal opposition, and a stage's absence from one paragraph is not treated as its denial elsewhere. Sources: [Calvin, verses 16–17](https://ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom16/calcom16.xviii.i.html), [Gill, verse 17](https://www.biblestudytools.com/commentaries/gills-exposition-of-the-bible/isaiah-65-17.html).

Translator notes, editorial corrections and later digital additions are not assigned to the original author. In particular, [Calvin's Hebrews metadata](https://ccel.org/c/calvin/comment3/comm_vol44/htm/About.htm) names translator Rev. John Owen; he is not linked to the catalog's seventeenth-century Puritan John Owen identity.
