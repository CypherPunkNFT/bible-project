## Direction

Teach one habit: before you read a command, read who is speaking and what He has already done. Rescue first, then the words. The shape comes from Scripture's own form, ten words on two stones under one opening sentence that is a rescue and not a command, and from Scripture repeating itself: the ten are given twice, at Sinai (Exodus 20) and forty years later (Deuteronomy 5), and the page draws where the two differ. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The study trail, the three concepts in gold (ten words, two tables, one love), *The Ten* in ink with *Commandments* in gold italic, the subtitle, the lead, and Exodus 20:2 printed. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, two stone tablets at the foot of a mountain under cloud, with a dotted road climbing to them from the sea: the rescue comes first, and the words are reached by it. The motto: "Two stones. One voice. / Rescue first, then the words." No face or figure of God. A bar of four counted figures, each with what it means, and links to every part.
- **The frame.** The two givings and the note on the divine name, then the selection paragraph that says what the study walks and what it leaves to other studies.
- **01 Ten words, and the first is a rescue.** The text, then the three ways of counting drawn as the study puts it: the same verses, with the lines drawn in different places. A real table of the eleven rows of Exodus 20:2–17 with a choice of tradition (Jewish; most Protestants and the Orthodox; Catholics and Lutherans). The chosen way's brackets join or split the rows, and the build proves every way comes to ten.
- **02 The first table, 03 the second table.** Part 02 opens on the two stones drawn large, words one to four on the left, five to ten on the right. Each word on the stone goes to its place in the reading. Under each word, a short "given twice" line sets Exodus beside Deuteronomy, word for word, with the words that differ marked (Remember and Observe; the added "that it may go well with you"; "field" and the wife named first). The differences are computed from the Berean, not chosen by hand.
- **04 Jesus reads the ten.** A row of the ten words, and three passages to choose: Matthew 5 (two words followed into the heart), Matthew 22 (the two loves over the two stones) and Romans 13:9 (four words named). The words each passage takes up light, and each mark is checked in its verse.
- **05 The first day.** A week drawn as a line from sunset to sunset: the seventh day and the first day, with the verses set at the day they speak of (Genesis 2:3, Exodus 20:11, Deuteronomy 5:15, Mark 2:27–28 on the seventh; Acts 20:7, 1 Corinthians 16:2, Revelation 1:10 on the first). Under the line, the practices the study names, each placed at the day it keeps, marked "shown, not ranked": Jewish Shabbat, Seventh Day Baptists, Seventh-day Adventists, the Second London Confession, the 1925 and 1963 Baptist Faith and Message, and the 2000 Baptist Faith and Message.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Exodus 20:1–17 printed in full, laid out as the opening sentence over the two stones. Sources with what was read, and a back link.
- **On wide screens** the page reads like an annotated text: the "given twice" lines and the notes of other traditions sit in the right margin beside the words they belong to. On phones the drawings and the extra columns are left out, and the stones stack.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean, with the reference in the address.
- **Notes in the text** keep their kind on the page: "With care" for the care notes, "Lutheran teaching" for Luther's catechism, "Another tradition" for the Seventh Day Baptists and the Adventists, "Not yet written" for the two apologetics answers that do not exist, and a small editor's mark for each open "[check: …]" note.

## Colour

History orange, the colour of the law books, for Exodus and Deuteronomy, which is most of the page. Where Jesus reads the ten (Matthew and Mark), the marks take the Gospels' violet, and Romans takes the Epistles' gold, so the colour says whose words are being read. On the week line the seventh day keeps the law's orange and the first day, the day of the resurrection, the Gospels' violet. The site's gold marks the care notes and the editor's marks.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: salvation by grace through faith in Christ, never by keeping the law (article IV); the Lord's Day as the first day, kept under conscience (article VIII); Scripture as "truth, without any mixture of error" (article I), which is how the Abiathar note is read. Where Baptists differ (how the law of Moses applies; how strictly the Lord's Day is kept) the page shows the views and does not choose. The Jewish counting and Jewish Shabbat are shown in their own terms, the Pharisees' objection as a debate among Jewish teachers, and the Seventh Day Baptists and Adventists as theirs, labelled. The care note on honouring a parent who did harm stays where the study puts it, under the fifth word, with its crisis lines. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
