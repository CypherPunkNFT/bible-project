# The hope to come working notes

**Item 30 · God and Christian Faith · First draft · 2026-10-13**

## Purpose and central question

What future do these passages promise, and how should that hope shape present life? Preserve return, resurrection, judgment and renewal before comparing disputed sequences. Address grief and patient waiting without suppressing judgment or making an unsupported timetable.

## Content divisions

1. Ever be with the Lord — deceased believers, gathering, comfort and watchfulness.
2. Raised in incorruption — Christ as firstfruits, transformed bodies and meaningful labour.
3. The books were opened — Revelation's judgment and attributed differences of sequence.
4. Delivered from the bondage of corruption — creation, bodily redemption and patient hope.
5. Behold I make all things new — God's dwelling, life, healing, righteousness and service.
6. What manner of persons ought ye to be — patience, repentance, holiness, mutual comfort and steadfast work.

Each section contains two subdivisions, readings, consideration and reflection. This is content planning, not a website layout.

## Boundaries and reuse

See [content.md](content.md) and [passage notes](passage-notes.md). Reuse item 19 for Christ's resurrection witness and item 29 for embodiment; this study develops their future horizon. Timeline examples are representative rather than a complete taxonomy of eschatology. Do not infer specific current events, dates, political actors or technologies from apocalyptic images.
