## Direction

The page teaches one habit: when a verse is quoted, read the verses around it and ask what it is being used to do. The page's shape comes from Scripture's own structure. In Matthew 4:1–11 the words "It is written" stand in four verses (4:4, 4:6, 4:7, 4:10), three spoken by Jesus and one by the devil. The words are the same; the use is not. Only one direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** This is the standard heading:
  - the study trail;
  - the six concepts in gold capitals (Breathed out, True, Enough, Clear, About Christ, Opened by the Spirit);
  - *What the Bible says* in ink, with *about itself* in gold italic;
  - the lead, then 2 Timothy 3:16–17 in full.
  - Beside it, in a faint circle and a dotted orbit, is a clay lamp resting by an open scroll in the dark. Its flame breathes, and its light falls on the lines of the scroll nearest to it. The motto reads "A lamp. A dark place. / Read by its own light." No figure is drawn.
  - Four figures follow, each computed from the Berean: "It is written" 4 times (3 by Jesus, 1 by the devil), "opened" 3 times in Luke 24, the verses printed in full, and the 3 questions. A row of links goes to every part.
- **00 It is written. Four times, two speakers.** The frame paragraph comes first. Then the three tests sit side by side, each with a small drawing (stones, the temple's pinnacle, a mountain over the kingdoms) and the devil's offer in his own words. Under each test, every "It is written" is a choice; the devil's is drawn with a dashed border. Choosing one shows three things below:
  - the Matthew verse;
  - the verses it was taken from, read with the verses around them, with the shared words marked;
  - what it is being used to do, in the study's own sentence.

  The devil's quotation of Psalm 91 is marked neutrally. The study says he quotes "accurately", so the page does not mark what he leaves out. On phones the tests stack.
- **01–06, the six claims.** Each part has a wide head: a large numeral, its concept in gold, the title with its turn in italic, the hook, and its main and quoted readings. The reading runs on the left, with its passages printed in full. Notes are marked "With care" (gold rule), "Another view, in its own words" (dashed rule), or by the name of the writer quoted. Consider and Reflect sit side by side at the end. The study's own "to check" notes show as a small gold mark, so nothing unverified looks verified. One figure stands in the margin, held in place as the reading scrolls:
  - 01: the four uses of 2 Timothy 3:16, each with the study's gloss, then the goal in 3:17.
  - 02: the two readings of 2 Peter 1:19, shown without choosing, and the two of 1:20, with the one the study calls best supported drawn solid.
  - 03: "forty days and forty nights" beside "these forty years", then Deuteronomy's 34 chapters with Jesus' three answers numbered in chapters 6 and 8.
  - 04: Psalm 119:105 and 2 Peter 3:16 on either side of the study's sentence "Clarity does not mean everything is easy."
  - 05: Cleopas's six facts in his own words; a switch sets them under Luke 24:46, "The Christ will suffer" and "rise from the dead on the third day". The grouping is labelled as the page's.
  - 06: the three verses of Luke 24 that say "opened": eyes, the Scriptures, minds. The page does not caption 24:45 as the Spirit's act, because the verse does not say so.
- **Questions readers ask.** Three questions are set as words on one line, joined by a rule, with no boxes. The chosen one opens below with the verse it is read with. The first, on whether this is circular, is open from the start.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the close, the invitation and the question for the reader, with Luke 24:13–35 printed in full beside them. Then the lamp again, the sources and a back link.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The poetry books' teal. The study's picture of Scripture is the Psalter's lamp (Psalm 119:105, echoed in 2 Peter 1:19). The letter and Gospel colours are already worn by this page's nearest siblings in God & Christian Faith (The good news, The Holy Spirit, Grace, faith and salvation, The cross, the empty tomb and the throne). The site's gold marks the concepts, the care notes and the notes still to check.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article I (the Scriptures) and article II (the Holy Spirit's work of inspiration and illumination). Its phrases are checked by script against the copy held on this PC.

Where Christians differ, the page shows each view in its own words and names it as theirs:
- the Roman Catholic teaching on Scripture and Tradition and on interpretation;
- the view that the Bible is reliable in faith and life but not in every detail;
- the Catholic and Orthodox lists of books.

The two readings of 2 Peter 1:19 are shown without choosing, as the study does. The care note on Luke 24:20 places no guilt on the Jewish people. The care note on Part 04 points anyone who has had verses used against them to a trusted person, a pastor and, where a crime was committed, the authorities.
