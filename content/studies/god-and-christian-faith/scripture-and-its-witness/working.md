# Scripture and its witness working notes

**Item 27 · God and Christian Faith · First draft · 2026-10-13**

## Purpose and central question

What do the biblical writers say about Scripture, and how do Jesus and the apostles use earlier writings? Connect origin and authority with understanding, formation and faithful use. Distinguish inspiration from canon, textual transmission and translation.

## Content divisions

1. Given by inspiration of God — 2 Timothy's origin and purpose; 2 Peter's account of prophecy.
2. Throughly furnished unto all good works — teaching, correction and patient ministry.
3. Search the scriptures — receptive examination and the misuse of a quotation in the temptation account.
4. The things concerning himself — Luke's account of Jesus opening Scripture and preparing witnesses.
5. Written for our learning — Romans' use of earlier texts to teach hope and mutual reception.
6. The other scriptures — communal reading, Paul's letters, the canon question and its distinction from other questions.

Two subdivisions per section; readings, consideration and reflection remain content units. No site layout is implied.

## Scope and boundaries

See [content.md](content.md) and [passage notes](passage-notes.md). The canon comparison names Westminster and Catholic lists without pretending to cover all Christian traditions or reconstruct the historical process. Preserve uncertainty about unidentified letters and Luke's unreported exposition. Do not turn a claim of inspiration into an unsourced claim about a particular modern translation.

Item 34 will develop reading in context as a fuller practice; item 35 will treat translations. This study supplies the doctrinal and biblical-use introduction, with links to existing work rather than duplicate site features.
