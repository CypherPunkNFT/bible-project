## Owned passages

### 2 Timothy 3:14–17
- **Context:** Paul's last letter, written in prison, to Timothy; the chapter warns of deceivers (3:1–13). Timothy's teachers are named in 1:5 (Lois and Eunice). "Holy Scriptures" known "from infancy" are the Hebrew Scriptures.
- **Main readings:** "God-breathed" (theopneustos) read as "breathed out by God" (B. B. Warfield, "God-Inspired Scripture", 1900, reprinted in *The Inspiration and Authority of the Bible*); Calvin's commentary treats the verse as the principle that God has spoken. The confession's article I takes the verse's teaching as its own. Some older readings take "every Scripture inspired by God is also useful" (a different grammar); not shown on the page; editor may wish to add it as a note.
- **Links:** none required; Part 04 points forward to 2 Peter 3:16 for New Testament writings counted as Scripture.

### 2 Peter 1:16–21
- **Context:** Peter answers those calling the Christian message a myth, first by eyewitness of the Transfiguration (Matthew 17:1–8, not walked), then by the prophetic word.
- **Main readings:** (1) 1:19, the prophets' word confirmed by the event, or the prophets' written word firmer than experience; both shown without choosing, commentators to be named. (2) 1:20, about the origin of prophecy (preferred, because of "For" in 1:21) or about readers' interpretation (alternative, used by some for reading within the church; Catholic teaching on interpretation quoted separately in Part 04). (3) 1:21, dual authorship, human and divine.
- **Links:** none owned elsewhere; Part 06 returns to 1:21 for the Spirit's work.

### Matthew 4:1–11
- **Context:** Jesus' testing in the wilderness after his baptism; three answers from Deuteronomy (8:3, 6:16, 6:13). The devil quotes Psalm 91:11–12 (not supplied; referred to, not quoted).
- **Main readings:** Jesus as the faithful Israel who passes the wilderness test (forty days and forty years); Scripture sufficient in temptation; Scripture read with Scripture against misuse (4:5–7). Split across Part 03 (4:1–4, 8–11) and Part 04 (4:5–7) within this study only.
- **Links:** Deuteronomy 8:3 quoted beside it, per the passage table (Money owns 8:11–18). Angels, demons and spiritual powers (53) quotes this passage; not linked from the page; editor may add.

### Luke 24:13–35
- **Context:** Easter Sunday evening; Cleopas and a companion walk to Emmaus; Jesus explains "what was written in all the Scriptures about Himself" and is recognised in the breaking of bread.
- **Main readings:** Christ as the focus of all Scripture (Christian reading; article I's "testimony to Christ"); Jewish reading of the Tanakh as Israel's Scriptures named first and fairly. Luke gives no list of passages; the page warns against inventing one and against finding Christ in every detail. Isaiah 53 deliberately not named, so the Jewish-reading rule for it is not triggered. 24:20 handled so that no guilt is laid on the Jewish people.
- **Links:** *Christ in the Old Testament* (41); *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* (25) for 24:1–12 and 24:36–43. The breaking of bread is not read as the Lord's Supper; *Baptism and the Lord's Supper* not linked.

### Luke 24:44–49
- **Context:** The risen Jesus with the disciples in Jerusalem; the threefold division of the Hebrew Scriptures; the commission and the promise of the Spirit.
- **Main readings:** illumination (minds opened); Scripture leading to witness; "promise of My Father" as the Holy Spirit at Pentecost.
- **Links:** *The Holy Spirit* (23, owns Acts 2:1–21); *Witness and the nations* (09); *The good news* (54) in the close.
