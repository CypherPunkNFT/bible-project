## Sources for the canon comparison

Consulted 2026-10-13:

- [Westminster Confession, chapter 1.2–3, Orthodox Presbyterian Church](https://opc.org/wcf.html): its canonical list and exclusion of the books termed Apocrypha. This is a confessional account, not an independent history of the canon's formation.
- [Catholic Catechism, paragraph 120](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_one/chapter_two/article_3/iv_the_canon_of_scripture.html): 46 Old Testament and 27 New Testament books, the named additional books, and the Catholic account of discernment through apostolic Tradition. The differing option for counting Jeremiah and Lamentations is not material to the draft's standard 46 count.

The comparison is illustrative, not exhaustive. No single Orthodox or Ethiopian canon is asserted, and no historical council chronology is offered. Further historical treatment requires primary lists and reception evidence, with dates and local scope checked separately. Neither present confessional statement alone establishes that history.

## Interpretive limits

1. **Timothy's childhood Scriptures:** do not imagine a completed modern New Testament already available in his childhood. The draft does not settle the full scope of every use of Scripture in 2 Timothy or every question of authorship and dating.
2. **Inspiration:** retain the KJV claim while distinguishing the text's statement from later detailed formulations of inerrancy, infallibility or dictation. No Greek grammatical or lexical dispute is presented as settled without supporting research.
3. **Private interpretation:** 2 Peter 1:20 must be read with verse 21's explanation of prophetic origin. The draft does not assert that it definitively forbids individual reading or resolves who has final interpretive authority.
4. **Perfect and throughly furnished:** explain the stated equipping for good works without claiming an instant state of sinless perfection for every reader.
5. **Berea:** the episode commends both readiness and searching. It is not used to establish an exhaustive doctrine of church authority from one narrative.
6. **Temptation:** the draft follows Matthew's account and its contrast of proposed actions. It does not build an argument about a quotation's textual omission or identify an ancient manuscript edition without investigation.
7. **Luke 24:** the contents of the entire exposition are not reported. Do not list speculative Emmaus proof texts as if Luke named them. Law, prophets and psalms are retained as Luke's wording, not treated as a complete named canon list.
8. **Romans 15:** Psalm 69:9 supports the reproach connection. The further quotations are discussed as Paul's sequence without a claim that all derive from one book or use identical wording across editions. A fuller source-by-source comparison belongs in later reading work.
9. **Letters:** Colossians names a letter from Laodicea, not necessarily a letter addressed to Laodicea. The draft does not identify it with a surviving book or declare it lost. Second Peter places Paul's letters alongside other Scriptures without naming every letter available to its readers.
10. **Canon versus transmission:** recognise the distinction between a book list, transmitted wording and translation. The local KJV supplies quotations; its use is not evidence that every Christian tradition has the same canon or that this edition resolves manuscript variants.
11. **Historical claims:** no claim that one council invented the Bible, that every book was always uncontested, or that a single verse supplies the final table of contents is made.
12. **Application:** the procedural reading questions are editorial recommendations grounded in these examples, not direct quotations or a claim that one method resolves every difficult text.
