## Direction

Teach one habit: a promise is someone's before it is yours. Ask each covenant the same five questions (who speaks, to whom, what is promised, what is asked, what sign), then see how far forward Scripture carries it. Only one direction was built, as with the two earlier mock-ups, because this was an existing draft.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the five questions in gold, the title *Covenants and promises*, the lead, 2 Corinthians 1:20, and a drawing of a bow, stars, stone, a crown and a cup on one dotted thread. Below it, five computed figures, each with what it means.
- **01 Six readings.** Noah, Abraham, Sinai, David, the new covenant and how the letters read them, as six cards. A card opens its reading in place and in the address. Each reading has the passage first, its setting, the five questions, two or three parts, flagged notes and Look closely and Your response.
- **02 Five covenants, five questions.** A real table, which stacks on a phone. One sentence says what the columns show: God speaks first every time; people, demands and signs differ; 2 Samuel 7 names no sign.
- **03 Spoken in its day, still being quoted.** The whole Bible as one line, each verse in its place by count, with 48 arcs to later verses. Colour is where the later verse sits, and the dash is how it comes back: solid quotes, dashed names, dotted remembers. Five buttons bring forward one covenant while the others stay visible. A full list below is the route for a keyboard or a phone. Noah is the honest finding: only Isaiah 54:9 among these lines takes up his oath.
- **04 One text, several maps.** The confession does not say how the covenants fit, so the page shows the Second London Confession's view, Criswell's dispensations and Wellum's progressive covenantalism without choosing, and labels Westminster as another tradition. Then, in the site's voice, what Baptists do not differ on: believer's baptism and no second way to God.
- **Close.** The way of reading, a question for the reader's own life, the passage again, a back link, a landscape, and the sources with how far each was read.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Articles 1, 4, 6 and 7. Typology is claimed only where the New Testament draws it. Romans 11:26 stays open. No modern land or political programme is drawn from Genesis 15:18. Sinai is not read as earning rescue, and that claim is not put in the mouths of Jewish readers. Hebrews 8:13 is said to be about a covenant, not a verdict on the Jewish people.
