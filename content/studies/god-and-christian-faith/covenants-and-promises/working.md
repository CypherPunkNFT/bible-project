# Covenants and promises working notes

**Item 26 · God and Christian Faith · First draft · 2026-10-13**

## Purpose and central question

How do the covenants and promises connect biblical books, and how do later writers appeal to earlier words? Develop the accepted six divisions while distinguishing biblical wording, contextual interpretation and theological synthesis.

## Content divisions

1. My covenant with you — Noah, descendants, living creatures, preservation and the bow.
2. In thee shall all families be blessed — Abraham, seed, land, faith, covenant obligations and blessing.
3. A kingdom of priests — deliverance before Sinai, covenant words, response and blood.
4. Thy throne shall be established — David's house, royal succession, discipline and Luke's announcement of Jesus.
5. I will make a new covenant — Jeremiah's named recipients, law within, forgiveness, Jesus' blood and Hebrews' argument.
6. Heirs according to the promise — Paul's use of Abraham, law and promise, Spirit and humility; attributed theological frameworks.

Two subdivisions per section, with readings, consideration and reflection. These remain content units, not site cards or layout instructions.

## Boundaries and reuse

The [reader-facing draft](content.md) follows named covenants without claiming to catalogue every covenant in Scripture. The [passage notes](passage-notes.md) record source limits and future comparison questions. Preserve item 25's treatment of sacrifice and item 24's treatment of justification rather than duplicating them.

The covenant of works and covenant of grace are introduced as terms of the Westminster synthesis, not quoted as covenant titles from Genesis. The DTS statement supplies a specified dispensational comparison; neither source stands for every member of a broad tradition. Baptism, the church's identity and eschatological fulfilment receive fuller attention in later studies.
