# Covenants and promises

Concepts: Who speaks · To whom · What is promised · What is asked · What sign

Motto: A bow. Stars. Stone. A crown. A cup. / One God keeping His word.

Lead: God makes promises to real people in real places, and later Scripture keeps coming back to them. This study walks five covenants the Bible names and how the letters read them, and teaches you five questions to ask before you claim any promise as your own.

> For all the promises of God are “Yes” in Christ. And so through Him, our “Amen” is spoken to the glory of God.

2 Corinthians 1:20 · BSB

Frame: A promise to Noah is not a command at Sinai, and a throne promised to David has its own story before Luke speaks of it. So read each covenant where it was first spoken, with the people who first heard it. Then follow it forward. The connections are real, and you will see them better if you have not blurred the people together on the way.

Selection: This is a selection, not a census. These five were chosen because each is called a covenant in the text and later Scripture argues from it. Others are named and not walked here: the covenant with Phinehas and the priests (Numbers 25:13; Malachi 2:5), the covenant in Moab (Deuteronomy 29:1), and whether Adam's story is a covenant, which turns partly on a disputed verse (Hosea 6:7). Other lists differ because they count differently: some Christians gather everything under two great covenants, and some count more than five.

## 01 Noah: every living creature

Slug: noah

Hook: Who is the rainbow for? God says: "I will see it and remember" (Genesis 9:16).

Line: A promise wider than any one people, with a sign God says He Himself will look at.

Read: Genesis 9:1–17

Setting: Noah and his family have just come out of the ark, and the first thing God does is bless them (Genesis 9:1).

Speaks: God, to Noah and his sons (Genesis 9:8).

To whom: Noah, his descendants and "every living creature" that came out of the ark (Genesis 9:10).

Promised: "Never again will all life be cut off by the waters of a flood" (Genesis 9:11).

Asked: The promise names no condition. Commands about blood and human life come just before it (Genesis 9:4–6).

Sign: "My rainbow in the clouds" (Genesis 9:13).

### With every living creature

Before any promise, God blesses Noah's family and guards human life, "for in His own image God has made mankind" (Genesis 9:6). Then the covenant opens wider than you might expect. It takes in the birds, the livestock and "every beast of the earth" (Genesis 9:10).

> And I establish My covenant with you: Never again will all life be cut off by the waters of a flood; never again will there be a flood to destroy the earth.

Genesis 9:11 · BSB

Read the wording closely. God does not promise that no river will rise or that no one will die in a storm. He promises no flood "to destroy the earth" (Genesis 9:11). A promise read for what it says can carry more weight than one stretched to say what it does not.

### The sign God looks at

We usually treat a rainbow as a sign for us. The passage says who does the remembering: "I will see it and remember the everlasting covenant" (Genesis 9:16). The promise does not rest on how you feel when you see it, or on whether you are looking at all.

[care] God says He will remember. That is the passage's way of saying He will act on His word; it does not suggest He might otherwise forget. The passage also calls the rainbow a sign without saying whether anyone had seen one before.

Look closely: Who is included in this covenant? What does God say He will do when the rainbow appears?

Your response: Name a promise of God you lean on. Can you say, in the passage's own words, what it promises and what it does not?

## 02 Abraham: a family for the world

Slug: abraham

Hook: A childless man is told to count the stars. Later he falls asleep, and the fire passes through without him.

Line: Land, family and blessing for all the earth, first announced and then bound by covenant.

Read: Genesis 12:1–7; Genesis 15:1–21; Genesis 17:1–22

Setting: Abram is seventy-five and has no child when the call comes in Haran (Genesis 11:30; Genesis 12:4).

Speaks: The LORD, to Abram (Genesis 12:1).

To whom: Abram and his descendants, with the covenant set on Isaac's line, and "all the families of the earth" to be blessed through him (Genesis 12:3; Genesis 17:19).

Promised: Land, descendants, blessing, and "to be your God" (Genesis 15:18; Genesis 17:7).

Asked: "Walk before Me and be blameless" and "You must keep My covenant" (Genesis 17:1; Genesis 17:9).

Sign: Circumcision, "a sign of the covenant" (Genesis 17:11).

### A land and a family

Genesis 12 gives the promises without using the word covenant. The word comes later, on the day of the split animals (Genesis 15:18). In between, Abram says the hard thing out loud: "You have given me no offspring" (Genesis 15:3). God takes him outside to the stars, and "Abram believed the LORD, and it was credited to him as righteousness" (Genesis 15:6).

The promise does not arrive quickly. His descendants will be "enslaved and mistreated four hundred years" before they come back (Genesis 15:13). Abraham is told plainly that he will not live to see it (Genesis 15:15).

> I will establish My covenant as an everlasting covenant between Me and you and your descendants after you, to be your God and the God of your descendants after you.

Genesis 17:7 · BSB

Genesis 17 holds two things together: "I will establish" and "You must keep" (Genesis 17:7; Genesis 17:9). Ishmael is promised blessing and a great nation, and the covenant is set on Isaac (Genesis 17:20–21). The chapter is careful with names, and a careful reader will be too.

[care] Christians often sort covenants into conditional and unconditional. Those labels are not in the text, so read the commitments and the demands before you apply one. And the land promise has named borders (Genesis 15:18); this study draws no modern political programme from them. How the land promise reaches its end is one of the places where the Baptist views in section 04 differ.

### Who walks between the pieces?

Abram cuts the animals in two, lays out the halves, and falls into a deep sleep (Genesis 15:10–12). Then "a smoking firepot and a flaming torch appeared and passed between the halves" (Genesis 15:17). The chapter does not explain it.

This site's reading: only the fire passes through, and Abram is asleep, so the LORD alone binds Himself. The reason for that reading is the one other place Scripture describes the act, where those who break a covenant will be treated "like the calf they cut in two in order to pass between its pieces" (Jeremiah 34:18).

[source:calvin-gen] Calvin read it differently. He notes that many take the fire as consuming a sacrifice, then says, "I rather interpret it as a symbol of future deliverance": light coming out of darkness, as God has just promised Abram's descendants.

[source:maclaren-gen] Maclaren, a Baptist preacher, reads smoke and flame as symbols "of the divine presence", at once known and hidden, and not as a picture of Israel's changing fortunes.

Look closely: What does each of the three chapters add? Where is the word covenant first used, and who is awake when the fire passes?

Your response: Is there a promise of God you have been waiting on longer than you expected? Tell God honestly what you told yourself it would look like, as Abram did.

## 03 Sinai: rescue first

Slug: sinai

Hook: Rescue first, commands second. They are already out of Egypt before God tells them anything.

Line: A rescued people is given a calling and a real responsibility, sealed with blood.

Read: Exodus 19:1–8; Exodus 20:1–2; Exodus 24:1–11

Setting: The third month after leaving Egypt, camped in front of the mountain (Exodus 19:1–2).

Speaks: The LORD, through Moses (Exodus 19:3).

To whom: "the house of Jacob", a people already brought out of Egypt (Exodus 19:3–4).

Promised: "My treasured possession" and "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (Exodus 19:5–6).

Asked: "obey My voice and keep My covenant" (Exodus 19:5).

Sign: The Sabbath, which the LORD calls a sign between Himself and the Israelites (Exodus 31:16–17).

### Brought to Myself

God starts with what He has done: "how I carried you on eagles’ wings and brought you to Myself" (Exodus 19:4). Only then does He speak of a covenant, and the people answer together, "We will do everything that the LORD has spoken" (Exodus 19:8). The commandments begin the same way, with a name and a rescue before any command: "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery" (Exodus 20:2).

This study reads the frame around the commandments: the offer, the people's answer, the blood and the meal. The ten words themselves, how they are counted and how Jesus reads them, are walked in another study. Read more in *The Ten Commandments*.

[care] So do not read Sinai as people earning their rescue: they are already free. The obedience is still real, and so is the failure that follows, or Jeremiah's complaint would mean nothing (Jeremiah 11:10). And do not put the claim of earning salvation in the mouths of Jewish readers of this passage. This study has not tested it, and the passage does not say it.

### The blood of the covenant

Moses tells the people the words, writes them down and reads them aloud, and twice they answer that they will do them (Exodus 24:3; Exodus 24:7). Around those answers is the blood: half on the altar, half on the people (Exodus 24:6; Exodus 24:8).

> So Moses took the blood, sprinkled it on the people, and said, “This is the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you in accordance with all these words.”

Exodus 24:8 · BSB

Then a meal. The elders go up and "they saw Him, and they ate and drank" (Exodus 24:11). Keep that scene in mind. Hebrews quotes Moses' words (Hebrews 9:20), and at another meal Jesus takes them up, calling the cup His blood of the covenant (Matthew 26:28).

Look closely: What has God already done before the first commandment? What happens between the people's first answer and the meal?

Your response: Do you obey God in order to be accepted, or because He has already brought you out? Pick one command you find hard, and ask that question of it.

## 04 David: a house for a house

Slug: david

Hook: David offers to build God a house. God answers: I will build yours.

Line: A throne promised forever, a psalm that says it has failed, and an angel who names the heir.

Read: 2 Samuel 7:1–29; Psalm 89:3–4; Psalm 89:19–39; Luke 1:30–33

Setting: David is settled in his palace with rest from his enemies (2 Samuel 7:1).

Speaks: The LORD, through the prophet Nathan (2 Samuel 7:4–5).

To whom: David and "your descendant after you" (2 Samuel 7:12).

Promised: "Your house and kingdom will endure forever" (2 Samuel 7:16).

Asked: Nothing David must do first. His sons will be disciplined for wrong, without the loving devotion being taken away (2 Samuel 7:14–15).

Sign: None named in 2 Samuel 7. Psalm 89 calls the moon "a faithful witness in the sky" (Psalm 89:37).

### A house for a house

David wants to build a house for God, and God turns the sentence around: "He Himself will establish a house for you" (2 Samuel 7:11). The word changes meaning as it goes, from a building to a royal line. David does not bargain. He sits before the LORD and asks, "Who am I, O Lord GOD" (2 Samuel 7:18), then prays that God will do what He has said (2 Samuel 7:25).

> Your house and kingdom will endure forever before Me, and your throne will be established forever.

2 Samuel 7:16 · BSB

The promise does not approve of every king. Psalm 132 says "If your sons keep My covenant" (Psalm 132:12), and many of the kings did not. So keep both the oath and the discipline in view.

### When the throne fell

Psalm 89 repeats the oath at length, "I will not violate My covenant" (Psalm 89:34). Then it turns on God: "You have renounced the covenant with Your servant" (Psalm 89:39). Scripture lets that prayer stand. If you have ever held a promise up against what you can see and found they do not match, you are in the Psalter. Then Luke: "The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David" (Luke 1:32).

### I will be his Father

Who is the son? The one who builds the house for God's name is Solomon (2 Samuel 7:13; 1 Kings 8:20). Yet Hebrews quotes the same line of Jesus: "I will be His Father, and He will be My Son" (Hebrews 1:5). Since the New Testament itself draws the link, this study says it plainly.

[source:calvin-heb] Calvin holds both: "Solomon is here referred to", yet it was said of him only "as a type of Christ", because a kingdom that fell cannot exhaust a promise of forever.

[care] The very next sentence reads "When he does wrong, I will discipline him" (2 Samuel 7:14). Hebrews stops before it, and so does the retelling in 1 Chronicles 17:13. That sentence belongs to the kings of David's line. It is no claim about Jesus.

Look closely: How does the word house change in Nathan's speech? Which parts of the promise come back in Luke?

Your response: Pray David's prayer about a promise you already have: not asking for a reward, but "Do as You have promised" (2 Samuel 7:25).

## 05 The new covenant: on the heart

Slug: new

Hook: They broke it. So God promises another, and says where He will write it.

Line: Law within, God known, sins forgotten; and then a cup at a table.

Read: Jeremiah 31:27–37; Luke 22:14–20; Hebrews 8:1–13

Setting: Jeremiah speaks to a people facing or living in exile, with Sinai's covenant broken behind them (Jeremiah 31:32).

Speaks: The LORD, through Jeremiah; then Jesus at the table (Jeremiah 31:31; Luke 22:20).

To whom: "the house of Israel and with the house of Judah", then the disciples at the table (Jeremiah 31:31; Luke 22:14).

Promised: The law on the heart, God known, sins remembered "no more" (Jeremiah 31:33–34).

Asked: Jeremiah's promise is all "I will". At the table: "do this in remembrance of Me" (Jeremiah 31:33; Luke 22:19).

Sign: Jeremiah names none. Jesus gives a cup, "the new covenant in My blood" (Luke 22:20).

### Written on the heart

Jeremiah names the failure first: "a covenant they broke" (Jeremiah 31:32). The answer is not a sterner law. It is the same God's law in a new place.

> But this is the covenant I will make with the house of Israel after those days, declares the LORD. I will put My law in their minds and inscribe it on their hearts. And I will be their God, and they will be My people.

Jeremiah 31:33 · BSB

Then the verse that gives it all a ground: "For I will forgive their iniquities and will remember their sins no more" (Jeremiah 31:34). Knowing God and being forgiven belong together, and "they will all know Me, from the least of them to the greatest" (Jeremiah 31:34).

[source:calvin-jer] Calvin guards the word new: "it is not so called, because it is contrary to the first covenant". God has not changed His mind.

[care] Read on. Jeremiah's next lines promise that Israel's descendants will not cease to be a nation before God (Jeremiah 31:35–37). Jeremiah names Israel and Judah, and Hebrews applies his words to Christ's ministry. Any account has to answer to both, and section 04 shows how Baptists differ on it.

### The cup and the better covenant

At the supper Jesus says, "This cup is the new covenant in My blood, which is poured out for you" (Luke 22:20). The disciples have achieved nothing here, and within hours they will run (Mark 14:50). As Baptists hold it, the Supper is a "symbolic act of obedience" in which the church remembers His death and looks for His return; the covenant stands on His blood, not on the meal.

Hebrews calls Jesus the mediator of a covenant "founded on better promises" and quotes Jeremiah in full (Hebrews 8:6; Hebrews 8:8–12). Notice where it lays the blame: "God found fault with the people" (Hebrews 8:8).

[care] "He has made the first one obsolete" (Hebrews 8:13) is about a covenant, not a verdict on the Jewish people. Paul writes of them: "God's gifts and His call are irrevocable" (Romans 11:29).

Look closely: What failure does Jeremiah name, and what does God promise to do about it? Who does Hebrews say the fault lay with?

Your response: Is there a sin you keep as though God still remembered it? Read Jeremiah 31:34 slowly, and answer Him.

## 06 Heirs: the letters read them

Slug: heirs

Hook: The law came 430 years later. It cannot cancel what God had already promised.

Line: Paul reads Abraham and Moses for Gentile believers, and warns them not to boast.

Read: Galatians 3:6–29; 2 Corinthians 3:3–18; Romans 11:17–32

Setting: Paul writes to churches of Jews and Gentiles arguing over what the law now asks of them (Galatians 3:1–5; Romans 11:13).

Speaks: Paul, reading Genesis and Exodus for the churches (Galatians 3:6–8).

To whom: Believers in Galatia, Corinth and Rome, Gentiles among them (Galatians 3:28; Romans 11:13).

Promised: To be "Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise" (Galatians 3:29).

Asked: Faith in Christ, and for Gentile believers, "do not boast over those branches" (Galatians 3:26; Romans 11:18).

Sign: None new is named here. Paul speaks of being "baptized into Christ" without calling it the covenant's sign (Galatians 3:27).

### What came first still stands

Paul's argument is about order: "The law that came 430 years later does not revoke the covenant previously established by God" (Galatians 3:17). So is the law against the promises? "Certainly not!" (Galatians 3:21). It was "our guardian to lead us to Christ" (Galatians 3:24).

Then he says something surprising about one word: "and to your seed," meaning One, who is Christ (Galatians 3:16). And a few lines on, those who belong to Christ are the seed too.

> And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham’s seed and heirs according to the promise.

Galatians 3:29 · BSB

[source:calvin-gal] Calvin admits the difficulty: "the word seed is a collective noun". His answer is that Paul "does not make his argument rest on the use of the singular number", but on God's calling, which narrowed Abraham's line from the start, with Isaac and not Ishmael.

Your English Bible may say offspring in Genesis and seed in Galatians, as the Berean does (Genesis 17:7; Galatians 3:29). Calvin works the argument through the Hebrew of Genesis as one word. This site reports the reading; it offers no rule of Hebrew grammar of its own.

### Grafted in, not boasting

Second Corinthians sets stone and heart side by side: "not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts" (2 Corinthians 3:3). That is not a licence to set written Scripture aside. Paul is talking about the Spirit's work in a covenant's ministry (2 Corinthians 3:6).

Romans 11 then speaks to Gentile believers directly. You are a wild shoot, and "the root supports you" (Romans 11:18).

[open:calvin-rom] What does "all Israel will be saved" mean (Romans 11:26)? Calvin writes, "I extend the word Israel to all the people of God", Jews and Gentiles together, while still expecting the Jews to return to faith. W. A. Criswell, a Southern Baptist pastor, read it as a day when the Jewish people themselves will turn to Christ, because God keeps the covenant He made with the patriarchs. The Baptist Faith and Message names no view. All of them must answer to verses 18 and 29.

Look closely: What does Paul say the law cannot do to the promise? What attitude does Romans 11 forbid Gentile believers?

Your response: If you are a Gentile believer, whose promises are you standing on? How should that change the way you speak about the Jewish people?

## Maps: One text. Several maps.

Lead: How do these covenants fit together? The Baptist Faith and Message does not say. It says "All Scripture is a testimony to Christ", and that the church as the Body of Christ takes in "all of the redeemed of all the ages". It draws no map. So this study shows maps Baptists use, with who holds each, and does not choose. The old confession is free to quote; the others are under copyright, so they are told in this site's words, and you should check them against the originals linked below.

### One covenant of grace, revealed by steps

[source:london] The Second London Baptist Confession, a 1677 Baptist confession, says of the covenant of grace that it is revealed "first of all to Adam" and "by farther steps, untill the full discovery thereof was compleated in the new Testament" (chapter 7, paragraph 3).

[own:scheiderer] Daniel Scheiderer, writing for Reformed Baptists, explains the view this way: the old covenant promised the covenant of grace and pictured it in types, but was not itself that covenant, which is fully present only when Christ comes. Believers in every age were saved by the same Christ and the same grace.

[own:semrad] Rexford Semrad adds a warning: Baptists who hold this confession also differ among themselves on the details, and its chapter 7 does not spell out all of them.

### Distinct dispensations, and Israel's future

[own:criswell] W. A. Criswell, long pastor of First Baptist Dallas, preached the seven dispensations as periods of God's dealing with people, and called Romans 9 to 11 dispensational. He taught that God's covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob is unconditional, and that God will keep it to Israel by a future conversion.

### Many covenants, fulfilled in the new

[own:wellum] Stephen Wellum, a Baptist theologian, calls his view progressive covenantalism. Many covenants, from creation on, progressively reveal one plan and reach their fulfilment in Christ and the new covenant. He does not reduce them to a covenant of works and a covenant of grace. He holds that the land promise reaches its end in Christ and the new creation, and that the church is neither simply Israel nor Israel's replacement.

### Another tradition, labelled as theirs

[other:westminster] The Westminster Confession of 1647, a Presbyterian standard, teaches "one and the same" covenant of grace "under various dispensations" (chapter 7, paragraph 6), and from there teaches that "the infants of one or both believing parents are to be baptized" (chapter 28, paragraph 4), citing among other texts Colossians 2:11–12 and Acts 2:38–39. This is not Baptist teaching.

### Where Baptists do not differ

All of these Baptist maps baptize believers. The site holds with the Baptist Faith and Message: baptism is "the immersion of a believer in water". The reason is from Scripture. Circumcision was given to every male of the household at eight days old (Genesis 17:12), while of the new covenant Jeremiah says "they will all know Me" (Jeremiah 31:34). Those baptized at Pentecost were "Those who embraced his message" (Acts 2:41). Colossians, often read as tying baptism to circumcision, says they were raised "through your faith" (Colossians 2:12). Even Abraham received his sign "while he was still uncircumcised" in faith, already counted righteous (Romans 4:11).

And none of these maps offers a second way to God. "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord", and Abraham's story is where Paul goes to show it (Galatians 3:6–9).

## Before you go

Close: Six readings, one habit. A promise is someone's before it is yours. Find out whose, and what it says, and you will trust it more, not less.

Way: Ask of any promise: who speaks, to whom, what is promised, what is asked, what sign is given, and where later Scripture picks it up. Then ask how it stands now that Christ has come (2 Corinthians 1:20).

Question: Which promise of God are you leaning on today? Who first heard it, what does it actually say, and what has it become in Christ?

Again: Read the passage again before you answer.

Passage: 2 Corinthians 1:20
