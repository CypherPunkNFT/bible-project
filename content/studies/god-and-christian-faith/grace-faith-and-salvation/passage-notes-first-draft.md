## Official doctrinal sources

Consulted 2026-10-13:

- [Westminster Confession, chapter 11, as published by the Orthodox Presbyterian Church](https://opc.org/wcf.html): justification distinguished from infusing righteousness; Christ's righteousness received through faith; faith as sole instrument accompanied by other graces and love. This is a specified Reformed formulation, not every Protestant account.
- [Catholic Catechism, Grace and Justification](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_one/chapter_three/article_2/grace_and_justification.index.html), especially 1987–1993 and 2001: forgiveness and interior renewal, Christ's Passion, baptism, and grace preceding reception. These summaries are evidence of Catholic teaching, not a substitute for examining the biblical argument.

Avoid caricatures: the Reformed account does not endorse dead faith; the Catholic account does not teach earning initial grace through unaided effort. This draft does not attempt an exhaustive Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Wesleyan, and other comparison.

## Interpretive limits

1. **Ephesians 2:8:** no disputed grammatical claim about the precise antecedent of “that” is required. The draft describes salvation by grace through faith as God's gift and preserves the paragraph's rejection of boasting.
2. **Repentance and faith:** read together without asserting a full chronological order of regeneration, repentance, and faith. No prescribed emotional intensity or conversion script is inferred from these passages.
3. **Romans 3:** retain redemption, blood, and propitiation without building an entire atonement model from one term. Item 25 will develop that language. The draft does not settle the translation discussion around “faith of Jesus Christ.”
4. **Works:** do not collapse every occurrence into an identical polemical setting. Romans discusses wages, law, boasting, and circumcision; James examines an inactive claim and unmet need. Contextual differences begin the comparison, but are not a claim that every doctrinal dispute has thereby been solved.
5. **Abraham:** Paul and James both appeal to the Scripture about believed/counted righteousness. James also discusses offering Isaac. The draft follows those authors' appeals without inventing a reconstructed chronology or saying James only means human reputation without argument.
6. **Titus:** keep washing/regeneration/renewal together. Baptismal interpretation is identified, not quietly imposed or excluded. Further comparison would require primary sources for the additional position represented.
7. **Assurance:** 1 John states a purpose of knowing life in the Son while connecting belief, love, and commandments. It is not a measurement of emotion or proof of flawless conduct. Full perseverance/apostasy comparisons remain outside this short reading.
8. **1 John 5:7–8:** these appear in the local KJV reading, but the draft's assurance argument uses 5:1–5 and 9–13 without relying on the disputed heavenly-witness wording. If a later expansion uses that wording doctrinally or compares editions, explicitly research and explain the textual-history issue first.
9. **Traditions:** no confessional source is described as the wording of Paul or James. Keep attributed synthesis separate from quotations and contextual observations.
