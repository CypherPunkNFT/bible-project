## Direction

Teach one habit: in every sentence about salvation, watch who is doing the verbs, then what the other one does in reply. The shape is already in the passages: five of the six turn on the word "But" (Ephesians 2:4, Luke 18:13, Titus 3:4, Romans 3:21, Galatians 4:4), with people acting before it and God acting after it, and James, who has no such turn, shows what people do once God has acted. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the five concepts in gold (Gift · Verdict · New birth · Family · Growth), the title *Saved by grace* with "grace" as the gold turn and *How God forgives and changes us* under it, the lead, and Ephesians 2:4–5 with its verbs marked. Beside it, one open, empty hand, palm up, and a sealed scroll coming down to it out of plain light: the verdict is given, not earned, and faith is the empty hand that takes it. The motto: "Dead, then alive. / Guilty, then declared right." No face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father. Four computed counts, each with what it means, and a row of links to every part.
- **00 Who is acting? Watch the verbs.** The habit's three paragraphs, then the verb chart: six passages as six columns, read down through what people were doing, where it turns (a gold rule threaded through the five "But" phrases), what God does, and what people do. Shape as well as colour says who acts: a filled dot for God, a half-filled dot for a passive verb that names no actor, an open ring for people, a struck word for what is named only to be ruled out, a diamond for what people do in reply. Point at a verb to read it in a line below; choose it to open the verse. "What this study covers" follows in small type. On a phone the six columns become six short lists, one after another.
- **01–07 The parts.** A large faint numeral behind each part's kicker, then the title and the hook. The reading on the left; on the right a margin held in view: the part's small drawing, the switch that marks or unmarks the verbs, where the passage turns, and its verbs grouped by who acts. Labels for each kind of paragraph: Setting, With care, Their teaching (in their words), How Baptists read it, A named source (in paraphrase), What the passage does not do, Read more. Five graphics, each answering a question: the two prayers of Luke 18 (every "I" in the Pharisee's counted); Romans 4's four bookkeeping words; Galatians 4:7's slave, son and heir; Abraham's two moments, Genesis 15 and Genesis 22, on one line with the ages between them; Romans 8:29–30's chain of five verbs of God and Philippians 2:12–13's two verbs in one breath. Consider and Reflect side by side at the end of each part.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Ephesians 2:1–10 printed in full with its verbs marked; then the same hand, now holding the scroll open, the sources and a back link.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Epistles' gold, because five of the study's seven readings are letters (Ephesians, Titus, Romans, Galatians, James, with Philippians in part 07). A darker gold carries text in the light theme so it can be read; the brighter gold marks the dots, rules and God's verbs. The site's own gold accent marks the care notes.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article IV: regeneration, repentance and faith, justification as God's full acquittal received by faith, sanctification and glorification; article V on the believer kept in grace; article VII on baptism as an ordinance that pictures new birth and does not cause it. Roman Catholic teaching (Trent, session 6; the Catechism of the Catholic Church) is set out in its own words under "Their teaching" and labelled; the Baptist reading follows under its own label. Where Baptists differ (the Calvinist and non-Calvinist readings of Romans 8:29–30), the page says so and does not choose. A passive verb is drawn as God's act only with its own half-filled mark, and the page says why (Romans 4:5). The care notes keep the crisis lines (988, 024, 800 911 2000, 135, 188, and Help for life for other countries), the note for readers for whom "father" brings pain, and the note on anxiety about assurance. Four quotations in content.md are not word for word the Berean and are corrected on the page only; they are listed in the mock-up's README for the owner's word.
