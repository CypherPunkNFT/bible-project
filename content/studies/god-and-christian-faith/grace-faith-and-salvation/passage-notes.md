# Saved by grace: passage notes

Study 24 · god-and-christian-faith/grace-faith-and-salvation · Rewrite narrowed to article IV (salvation) of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000. All quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible text supplied with the job. The older draft's King James quotations (Ephesians 2:8, Acts 20:21, Romans 5:1, Titus 3:5, 1 John 5:13) have been removed, and nothing was carried over without re-quoting. Acts 20:17–24 and 1 John 5:1–13 from the old draft are dropped. 1 John 5:1–13 belongs to *Chosen and kept*.

## Ephesians 2:1–10 (owned; part 01)

**Context.** The passage follows Paul's prayer for knowledge of God's power (1:15–23, owned by 04 *Prayer in the letters*) and comes before the reconciling of Jew and Gentile in one household (2:11–22, owned by 28 *The church and its calling*). It moves from former life (vv. 1–3), through God's action (vv. 4–7), to its summary (vv. 8–9) and purpose (v. 10). The verb "walk" (v. 2) frames the passage with "way of life" (v. 10).

**Main readings.**
- Salvation is wholly God's initiative, and good works are its fruit, not its ground. This is common Protestant and Baptist reading, and the site's voice (BFM article IV).
- The reference of "this" in v. 8 (faith only, or the whole of salvation by grace through faith) is debated. The neuter form leads many to read it as the whole. The study notes this without choosing, and the point about boasting stands either way.
- Calvinist and non-Calvinist Baptists differ over whether faith itself is God's gift given only to the elect. This is left to *Chosen and kept*.

**Links.** *Chosen and kept*; *The church and its calling* (2:11–22); *A new creation*; *What the cross accomplishes*.

## Luke 18:9–14 (owned; part 02)

**Context.** It comes after the parable of the persistent widow (18:1–8) and before the little children (18:15–17, owned by the kingdom study). Luke names the audience in v. 9. The Jewish setting is described first in Jewish terms: temple prayer at the hours of sacrifice, the Pharisees as a respected lay movement devoted to the law, the one commanded fast on the Day of Atonement, the tithe, and the tax collector as a collaborator.

**Main readings.**
- Justification is received by humble petition, not by merit. Jesus' "justified" (v. 14) anticipates Paul's usage. This is the site's reading.
- The verb in v. 13 (hilasthēti) is related to the language of atonement. This is a lexical note only, and no further theology is built on it.
- Care is taken against an anti-Jewish reading: all the figures are Jewish, the target is self-trust in anyone, and the reversal ("I thank You I am not like this Pharisee") is named.

**Links.** *The kingdom he announced* (Mark 1:15; Luke 18:15–17); the parables guide.

## Titus 3:3–7 (owned; part 03)

**Context.** It follows the call to gentleness toward all (3:1–2) and comes before "This saying is trustworthy" (3:8). It is a compact Trinitarian summary: God our Savior, the Holy Spirit, Jesus Christ our Savior.

**Main readings.**
- "Washing of new birth" as baptism, the means of regeneration: held by the Roman Catholic Church (CCC 1992 quoted), the Orthodox, Lutherans and others. Labelled as theirs.
- The washing as the Spirit's inward cleansing, pictured by baptism (echoing Ezekiel 36:25–27): the Baptist reading, in the site's voice, grounded in the verse's naming of the Spirit and its exclusion of "deeds we had done", with BFM article VII.

**Links.** *Who do you say that I am* (John 1:12–13); *Encounters with Jesus* (John 3); the baptism study (title to check); *The Holy Spirit*.

## Romans 3:21–4:8 (owned; part 04)

**Context.** It follows 1:18–3:20, where the whole world is accountable (3:9–20 owned by 29 *Humanity, sin and renewal*). It runs to Abraham's faith before circumcision (4:9–12, referred to only) and on to the peace of 5:1 (quoted; 5:1–5 owned by 03). Romans 3:29–30 echoes the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4). Romans 4:7–8 quotes Psalm 32:1–2.

**Main readings.**
- Forensic justification by faith alone: God declares sinners righteous for Christ's sake. This is Baptist (BFM IV; Baptist Catechism; Second London Confession ch. 11) and wider Protestant teaching, in the site's voice.
- Roman Catholic: justification includes inner sanctification and renewal, is conferred in baptism, and grows by cooperation with grace. Quoted from Trent, session 6 (1547), chapters 7–8, and CCC 1989, 1992, 1993, and labelled.
- The 1999 Joint Declaration is mentioned as history between the Lutheran World Federation and the Catholic Church, with Baptists explicitly not party.
- Romans 3:25–26 is read here for the justice of God in justifying. The fuller account of atonement belongs to the cross study, which quotes 3:25–26.

**Links.** *Humanity, sin and renewal*; *What the cross accomplishes*; *Faith under pressure* (Romans 5:1).

## Galatians 4:4–7 (owned; part 05)

**Context.** In Paul's argument against requiring Gentile believers to take up circumcision and the law, this passage follows the guardian picture (3:23–4:3) and comes before the warning against returning to slavery (4:8–11). It sets two sendings side by side: the Son (v. 4) and the Spirit (v. 6). "Abba" is Aramaic, and Jesus used it in Mark 14:36.

**Main readings.**
- Adoption is a distinct blessing alongside justification. This is common Reformed and Baptist teaching, and the Second London Confession has a chapter on it (ch. 12). The BFM 2000 has no separate clause on adoption; the study cites its "peace and favor" phrase and does not claim the confession uses the word.
- "Sons" is read as including women, by Galatians 3:28.

**Links.** *The Holy Spirit* (Romans 8:1–17, referred to only); *Covenants and promises*.

## James 2:14–26 (owned; part 06)

**Context.** It follows the warning against favouritism toward the rich (2:1–13), which is a concrete ethical setting. The passage cites Genesis 15:6 (via v. 23) and Genesis 22, and Rahab from Joshua 2.

**Main readings.**
- Harmony with Paul: different questions, different senses of "faith" (claimed versus trusting) and of "justified" (shown right versus declared right). Genesis 22 "fulfils" Genesis 15:6. This is the site's reading, supported by Calvin's line answering Trent (wording to check).
- Roman Catholic: James 2:24 as support for growth in justification through works (Trent, session 6, chapter 10, to check). Labelled as theirs.
- Care is taken against using the passage to frighten struggling believers.

**Links.** *Faith in daily life*; *Chosen and kept*.
