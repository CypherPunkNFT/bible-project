## Direction

Teach one habit: whenever a passage tells you what God is like, look in the same passage for what He is doing, and let the deed tell you what the word means. The shape is the study's own. Its closing paragraph names seven pairs ("I AM" with "I have seen", "Holy" with a coal from the altar, "Gracious" with new tablets, "Great and awesome" with a widow's bread, "Love" with a Son sent, "Creator" with breath given to everyone, "providence" with a sparrow and a famine survived), and its concepts line names four headings (Name, Character, Creation, Providence). Scripture adds a third shape: it repeats Exodus 34:6 again and again (stage 2 of the study prompt, "Scripture remembering itself"), and Romans 11:36 gathers creation, providence and purpose into one sentence. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The standard heading: the study trail, the four concepts in gold capitals, *God:* in ink with *who He is and what He does* in gold italic, the lead, and the opening words of Exodus 34:6 marked "(part)". The drawing, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: the bush that burns and is not burned up, a pair of sandals taken off for holy ground, Horeb behind, the stars, and a sparrow on a stone. The flames move slowly and loop. No face or figure of God or of anyone. The motto: "He tells us His name. / He keeps the world He made."
- **Figures.** 9 of 12: the verses of the Berean with "slow to anger" that say it of God. 5,569: the verses that print LORD, the name given at Exodus 3:15. 10: the passages. 35: the verses printed in full. Each figure says what it means.
- **00 Said, and done.** The frame paragraph, then the seven pairs on one dotted orbit: the word on the ring, a fine line out to the deed and its part number, and in the centre the study's sentence "Let the deed tell you what the word means." Pointing at a pair shows both verses beneath; choosing it opens that passage. On a phone the orbit becomes a plain list.
- **01–10 Ten passages, one open at a time.** An index under the four concepts (Name 01; Character 02–05; Creation 06–07; Providence 08–09; "From Him, through Him, to Him" 10). The open passage has its number in a dotted circle, the Scripture phrase of its title in ink with the rest in the study colour, the hook, its main readings and a small drawing, then the text with its passages printed in full, notes marked "With care", "Where readers differ" and "Outside source", and Consider and Reflect at the end, with the previous and next passage. The chosen passage is in the address, so it can be shared. This keeps a long study compact: overflow becomes a choice, not a longer scroll.
- **Part 03: Verses that call God slow to anger.** All nine, printed as text with the words they share with Exodus 34:6–7 underlined, each with one line on who says it (Moses praying, the Levites confessing, Jonah complaining, Nahum warning the same city). The rule and what it misses are stated.
- **Part 10: From Him, through Him, to Him.** Romans 11:36 as three words on a loop, joined to the parts on creation and providence and to the close.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the close, the invitation, the further answers, the question and "Read the passage again before you answer", with Exodus 34:5–9 printed in full beside them. Then a mountain with a cloud on it and two new tablets at its foot, the sources and a link back to God & Christian Faith.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet from the right, in the Berean.

## Colour

History orange, because the name is given in Exodus 3 and proclaimed in Exodus 34, the passage the whole Old Testament keeps returning to, and the study closes there. A darker orange carries text in the light theme. The site's gold marks the concepts, the title's turn and the care notes. Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice, in the same category, also uses History orange, so this page's difference rests on its shapes: an orbit of words, and one passage open at a time.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article II: one God, revealed as Father, Son and Holy Spirit; His knowledge extends to the future decisions of His free creatures; He reigns with providential care. Where Baptists differ, both are shown and neither is chosen: the age of the earth (part 06), and how God's rule fits with human choice, Calvinist and non-Calvinist (part 09). The two readings of "I AM WHO I AM" and of "Us" in Isaiah 6:8 are shown as the text gives them. The care notes stay where the text puts them: the crisis lines first in part 09, forgiveness that is not trust and not a return to danger, "perfect love drives out fear" as no rebuke to the anxious, Isaiah 6:9–10 never a verdict on the Jewish people. No drawing shows God or any person. The text's own "check" notes are shown on the page as "Not yet confirmed", and one misquotation found by the build (Exodus 3:1, "on" for "to") is shown with the Berean's words beside it until the text is corrected; see the record.
