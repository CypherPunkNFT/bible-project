## Interpretive decisions and limits

- **The name:** use the KJV's I AM wording and the narrative's continuity with the fathers, presence, and deliverance. Do not assert an exhaustive Hebrew etymology, preferred pronunciation, or philosophical definition without separate research.
- **The bush:** Exodus 3 speaks of the angel of the LORD appearing and God calling. The draft does not settle the angel's identity or declare a specific later doctrinal identification as explicit in this passage.
- **Holiness:** explain through Isaiah's encounter rather than an unsupported one-word lexical definition. The triple “holy” is not here treated as a direct enumeration of Father, Son, and Spirit. The plural “us” in 6:8 is not made an isolated proof of a later formulation.
- **Cleansing and commission:** the coal scene is narrated as Isaiah's particular encounter, not a ritual readers are instructed to reproduce. Read beyond 6:8 to keep the commission's hardening and judgment language visible. Full treatment of 6:13's imagery is deferred rather than reduced to a confident detailed prediction.
- **Mercy and truth:** faithfulness is the outline's thematic description; the reader-facing passage retains the KJV's goodness and truth. The draft does not present that thematic heading as a word-for-word quotation from Exodus 34.
- **Generations:** preserve Exodus 34:7's severity alongside forgiveness. Do not diagnose a modern person's suffering as an ancestor's punishment or infer an exhaustive doctrine of inherited guilt from this text alone. Further development should compare relevant passages explicitly.
- **Justice:** the fatherless, widow, and stranger in Deuteronomy provide concrete textual content. Psalm 96 looks toward righteous judgment. Neither is converted into a promise that every present dispute is already resolved fairly.
- **Wisdom:** Romans 11's doxology follows the discussion of Israel and Gentiles. The draft neither settles the meaning of “all Israel” nor treats a universal-salvation conclusion as agreed. Read the surrounding argument for a fuller treatment. Mystery does not mean avoiding the text's actual claims.
- **Love:** 1 John grounds love in the sending of the Son for sins. Retain “propitiation” as the KJV's term without making this introductory section an exhaustive atonement theory; item 25 develops the cross's language.
- **Fear:** 1 John 4 places fear alongside judgment and torment. The application does not make every fear response evidence of spiritual failure or claim to diagnose an emotional condition.
- **Seeing God:** Isaiah's vision and 1 John's statement that no one has seen God appear in different kinds of discourse. The draft retains both rather than supplying an unargued harmonisation or treating either as meaningless. A fuller account of divine revelation belongs with further reading on the Son and God's self-disclosure.
