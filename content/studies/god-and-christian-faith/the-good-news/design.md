## Direction

Teach one habit: read the gospel as news before you read it as advice. Ask first what God has done, then what it calls for. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the four steps in gold (who God is, what went wrong, what Jesus did, how to respond), the title *The good news* with *How a person is made right with God* under it, the lead, John 3:16, and a drawing of a house with its door open, lamplight across the road, and running footprints going out to meet a smaller set coming home. No face or figure of God, of Christ or of the father. Four computed figures, each with what it means, and a row of links to every part.
- **00 News first, then the response.** The frame paragraph, then a ledger of seven verses: what God has done on the left, an arrow, what it calls for on the right, both in the verse's own words. One row is left open on purpose: the father pleads with the older son, and Jesus does not say what the son did. On a phone the two sides stack.
- **01–06 The road.** Six parts down one dotted thread, the number, a small drawing, the title and the main readings held beside the reading as it scrolls. Inside each part: the text, the passages printed in full, notes marked "With care" and "Its limits", and Consider and Reflect side by side at the end. Part 03 adds Luke 15 as three fields of dots: one lost out of a hundred, out of ten, out of two, with the older son drawn dashed. Part 06 sets its three next steps (a church, baptism, the Bible) side by side.
- **Questions readers ask.** Six questions that open in place. The first, "Is it too late for me?", is open from the start, because it carries the crisis lines.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question for the reader, and Luke 15:11–32 printed in full beside them, then the open door again, the sources and a back link.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Gospels' colour, because the page reads Luke and Acts and its message is the gospel. The site's gold marks the care notes.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: salvation by grace through faith in Christ alone (article IV), believer's baptism by immersion as obedience, not as what saves (article VII), and the call to make disciples of all nations (article XI). The Lutheran view of baptism is shown in Luther's words and named as theirs. No prayer is treated as what saves. The guilt for Christ's death is placed on all of us, never on the Jewish people as a people. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
