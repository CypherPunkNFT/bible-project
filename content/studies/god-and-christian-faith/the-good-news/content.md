# The good news: how a person is made right with God

Concepts: Who God is · What went wrong · What Jesus did · How to respond

Motto: A father running. / A door left open.

Lead: This study is for you if you are not a Christian, or are not sure what Christians actually believe. It reads a few passages to answer one question: how can a person be put right with God? It teaches one habit for reading them: before you ask what you must do, ask what God has done.

> For God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son, that everyone who believes in Him shall not perish but have eternal life.

John 3:16 · BSB

Frame: The word *gospel*, which Christians use often, means "good news". News is a report of something that has already happened. Advice tells you what to do. The Bible does give commands, but the message at its centre is news: God has acted, in history, through Jesus. So as you read, look first for what God has done. Ask what you should do only after that. The passages below are written in this order, and so is this study.

Selection: This is a selection, not a census. The Bible says far more about God, sin and salvation than one page can hold. This study walks two passages fully. The first is three stories Jesus told in Luke 15. The second is the first public sermon about Jesus after His death, in Acts 2. It quotes a few other verses along the way and links to the studies that read them fully. Six questions readers often ask are answered at the end.

## 01 Who God is

**Main readings:** Romans 1:20; John 3:16; Genesis 18:25

The Bible does not open with a proof that God exists. It opens with God already at work, making the world (Genesis 1:1). Paul says the world itself points to its Maker:

> For since the creation of the world God’s invisible qualities, His eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly seen, being understood from His workmanship, so that men are without excuse.

Romans 1:20 · BSB

Creation shows that God is powerful. It does not show what He is like toward us. For that the Bible gives His words and actions, and two things stand out.

God is **just**. Abraham, pleading for a city, asks: "Will not the Judge of all the earth do what is right?" (Genesis 18:25). God does not shrug at wrong. A judge who ignored cruelty would not be good.

God is also **loving**, and His love is not a feeling held at a distance. It acts: "God so loved the world that He gave His one and only Son" (John 3:16). The Son is Jesus. Christians believe in one God, who is eternally Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Read more in the site's articles on the Trinity [check: title and link of the Trinity articles].

Its limits: this part does not try to prove God's existence. If that is your question, the site's apologetics answers take it up [check: title and link of the apologetics answer on God's existence].

**Consider:** What can creation tell you about God, and what can it not tell you?

**Reflect:** When you picture God, do you picture someone just, someone loving, or neither? Which is harder for you to believe?

## 02 What went wrong

**Main readings:** Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Isaiah 53:6

The Bible's word for what went wrong is *sin*. Sin is not only breaking rules. It is turning away from God to run our lives our own way. The breaking of rules follows from that.

> for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God,

Romans 3:23 · BSB

"All" includes the religious and the irreligious, the decent and the notorious. Nobody is ranked here. The verse puts every reader in the same place.

Isaiah describes the same thing with a picture from farm life. Jewish readers, from the medieval commentator Rashi onward, usually take the suffering "servant" of these chapters to be the people of Israel. Isaiah himself calls Israel God's servant (Isaiah 41:8; 49:3). Christians, following the New Testament (Acts 8:30–35), read the servant as Jesus. Either way, the verse's description of us is plain:

> We all like sheep have gone astray, each one has turned to his own way; and the LORD has laid upon Him the iniquity of us all.

Isaiah 53:6 · BSB

(*Iniquity* means wrongdoing.) Read more in *Christ in the Old Testament*.

The cost is serious: "For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 6:23). Look at the two halves. Death is earned, like a wage. Life is a gift. *Eternal life* in the Bible means more than living forever. It is life with God, beginning now and never ending.

Its limits: this part names the problem. It does not ask you to feel a certain amount of guilt before you may go on reading. Read more in *Humanity, sin and renewal*.

**Consider:** In Romans 6:23, which half is earned, and which is given?

**Reflect:** Where in your own life have you "turned to your own way"? Name it honestly, to yourself.

## 03 Three lost things

**Main readings:** Luke 15:1–32

Jesus told these stories because people were complaining about the company He kept. *Tax collectors* were Jews who collected taxes for the Roman occupiers, and many people saw them as traitors and cheats. The *Pharisees* were a movement of devout Jews who took God's law seriously in daily life, and the *scribes* were teachers of that law. Their complaint was a reasonable-sounding one: "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them" (Luke 15:2). Jesus answers it with three *parables*, short stories that make one picture carry a truth.

### The sheep and the coin

A shepherd loses one sheep out of a hundred and goes after it "until he finds it" (Luke 15:4). A woman loses one coin out of ten, lights a lamp and sweeps "until she finds it" (Luke 15:8). In both stories the one who owns the lost thing goes looking for it. The sheep does not find its own way home. And both stories end in a party: "Rejoice with me" (Luke 15:6, 9).

> In the same way, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of God’s angels over one sinner who repents.

Luke 15:10 · BSB

### The son who came home

The third story is longer. A younger son asks for his inheritance early, leaves, and spends it all. In a famine he ends up feeding pigs, which was a bitter disgrace for a Jewish listener. Then "he came to his senses" (Luke 15:17). He plans a speech: he has sinned, he is not worthy, he will ask to be a hired servant (Luke 15:18–19). This is what the Bible means by *repent*. It means to turn: to change your mind about yourself and about God, and then to head back home.

> So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still in the distance, his father saw him and was filled with compassion. He ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him.

Luke 15:20 · BSB

Look closely at what the son says when he arrives. He begins his speech, but the request to be made a hired servant never comes out (compare Luke 15:19 with 15:21). The father interrupts with a robe, a ring, sandals and a feast. "For this son of mine was dead and is alive again! He was lost and is found!" (Luke 15:24).

### The son who stayed outside

Then the story turns to the older brother, who "became angry and refused to go in" (Luke 15:28). He had stayed and worked, and he resents the welcome. His father "came out and pleaded with him" (Luke 15:28). The story ends with the father's plea, and we never learn whether the older son went in. Jesus told it to the grumblers of verse 2. It leaves them, and us, at the door.

Its limits: a parable is a picture, not a complete account. This one shows God's welcome. It does not explain how God can welcome sinners and still be just; Acts 2 and Romans 3 take that up below. Read the parables more widely in *The parables of Christ*.

**Consider:** In each of the three stories, who does the searching or the running? How does the father answer each son?

**Reflect:** Are you more like the son far away, or the son who stayed and is angry outside? Both are invited in.

## 04 Who Jesus is, and what He did

**Main readings:** Acts 2:22–24; Acts 2:36

About seven weeks after Jesus was put to death, Jerusalem was full of pilgrims for *Shavuot*, the Feast of Weeks. This Jewish festival falls fifty days after Passover, and Greek-speaking Jews called it Pentecost. On that day Jesus' followers were filled with the Holy Spirit (Acts 2:1–4; read more in *The Holy Spirit*). Peter, a Jew speaking to his fellow Jews, explained what had happened.

> Men of Israel, listen to this message: Jesus of Nazareth was a man certified by God to you by miracles, wonders, and signs, which God did among you through Him, as you yourselves know.

Acts 2:22 · BSB

Peter says three things about Jesus in a few lines. God showed who Jesus was through His works. Jesus' death was not an accident: "He was delivered up by God’s set plan and foreknowledge" (Acts 2:23). And death could not hold Him: "God raised Him from the dead … because it was impossible for Him to be held in its clutches" (Acts 2:24). The *resurrection* means that Jesus came back to bodily life, and did not merely survive as a memory.

Peter then reads Israel's own Scriptures, the Psalms of David, as pointing to this:

> Foreseeing this, David spoke about the resurrection of the Christ, that He was not abandoned to Hades, nor did His body see decay. God has raised this Jesus to life, to which we are all witnesses.

Acts 2:31–32 · BSB

(*Hades* is the place of the dead.) Read more in *The book of Psalms*. Peter's sermon reaches its point:

> Therefore let all Israel know with certainty that God has made this Jesus, whom you crucified, both Lord and Christ!”

Acts 2:36 · BSB

*Christ* is the Greek form of the Hebrew *Messiah*, "anointed one": the King God had promised. *Lord* is the title of the one who has the right to rule.

[care] Peter said "you crucified" as a Jew to a Jerusalem crowd, and he named the Romans as "the hands of the lawless" (Acts 2:23). Weeks later the first believers prayed that "Herod and Pontius Pilate conspired with the Gentiles and the people of Israel against Your holy servant Jesus" and that "they carried out what Your hand and will had decided beforehand would happen" (Acts 4:27–28). Isaiah says the LORD laid on Him "the iniquity of us all" (Isaiah 53:6). Scripture places the guilt for Christ's death on all of us. It never places it on the Jewish people as a people, and this site never does.

Its limits: this part does not explain how Jesus' death takes away sin; read more in *What the cross accomplishes*. If you want the historical evidence, see the site's apologetics answers on Jesus [check: link], on the cross [check: link] and on the resurrection [check: link]. Read more on who Jesus is in *Who do you say that I am*.

**Consider:** What three things does Peter say God did through, to and for Jesus?

**Reflect:** If Jesus really is "both Lord and Christ", what claim does that make on you?

## 05 Turn and trust

**Main readings:** Acts 2:37–41; Romans 10:9–13

The crowd did not hear Peter's sermon as interesting information. "They were cut to the heart and asked Peter and the other apostles, 'Brothers, what shall we do?'" (Acts 2:37). The *apostles* were the people Jesus personally sent out with His message.

> Peter replied, “Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

Acts 2:38 · BSB

The promise is wide: "to you and your children and to all who are far off" (Acts 2:39). That includes people far from Jerusalem and far from God. "Those who embraced his message were baptized" (Acts 2:41): they embraced the message first, and the water came after. The question about baptism is answered below.

Paul puts the same response in other words:

> that if you confess with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.

Romans 10:9 · BSB

To *believe*, or have *faith*, here means to trust, the way you trust a bridge by putting your weight on it. To be *justified* (Romans 10:10) means to be declared right by God, as a judge declares someone in the right. That is how a person is made right with God. It is not earned. It is *grace*, God's kindness given to people who do not deserve it. "Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved" (Romans 10:13). In the prophet Joel, "the Lord" in those words is the LORD, Israel's God (Joel 2:32). Paul applies them to Jesus.

[care] No form of words saves anyone. A prayer can express faith. You may tell God, in your own words, that you have sinned, that you believe Jesus died and rose, and that you are trusting Him as Lord. But it is God who saves, through faith in Christ, and not the words. If you have trusted Him, you do not need to wonder whether you said it correctly.

Read more in *Grace, faith and salvation*.

**Consider:** What did the crowd ask, and what two things did Peter answer?

**Reflect:** Is there anything you are still trying to earn from God that Romans 10 offers as a gift?

## 06 What happens next

**Main readings:** 1 John 5:11–13; Acts 2:41

The new believers in Acts 2 did not stay alone. They were baptized and "added to the believers" (Acts 2:41). Three things follow for anyone who trusts Christ.

**A church.** A *church* is not a building. It is a gathering of people who trust Jesus, who meet to worship, learn and care for one another. Look for one that preaches the Bible and Jesus plainly. The site's church finder can help [check: link to the church finder]. Read more in *The church and its calling*.

**Baptism.** *Baptism* is going under water, in the name of the Father, Son and Holy Spirit, as a public sign that you belong to Christ. Baptists call it an *ordinance*, a practice Jesus ordered His followers to keep. With the Baptist Faith and Message, this site holds that baptism is "the immersion of a believer in water", and "an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith" (article VII, baptism and the Lord's Supper). Read more in the baptism study [check: title of the baptism and Lord's Supper study].

**The Bible.** Begin with one of the Gospels, the four accounts of Jesus' life. Mark is short, and John was written so that readers would believe (John 20:31). This suggestion is the site's, not a rule.

And you can know where you stand:

> And this is that testimony: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in His Son. Whoever has the Son has life; whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life. I have written these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, so that you may know that you have eternal life.

1 John 5:11–13 · BSB

John writes "so that you may *know*". This kind of confidence, called *assurance*, rests on God's word about His Son, not on how strong your feelings are today. Read more in *Chosen and kept*.

The word *everyone* has no exceptions.

## Question: What about my family's religion?

Turning to Christ does not mean despising the people who raised you. God's own commandments tell us to honour father and mother (Exodus 20:12). If your family is Jewish, remember that Peter was a Jew speaking to Jews, and that Jesus is presented to them as Israel's own promised Messiah (Acts 2:36). Paul writes, "there is no difference between Jew and Greek: The same Lord is Lord of all, and gives richly to all who call on Him" (Romans 10:12).

Faith cannot be inherited or forced. The Baptist Faith and Message holds that God alone is Lord of the conscience (article XVII, religious liberty). Take your time, ask your questions, and speak to your family with respect. The site's apologetics answers treat other religions in their own words [check: link].

## Question: Must I be baptized to be saved?

Baptists answer no. People are saved by grace through faith in Christ (Ephesians 2:8). The man on the cross was promised Paradise, and Luke records no baptism (Luke 23:43). In Acts 10:43–47, the Spirit came on believers before any water. Baptists read "for the forgiveness of your sins" in Acts 2:38 as joining faith and its first public act, because in Acts baptism followed belief closely. Baptism does not save. It is obedience, and it shows in public what has already happened. That does not make it optional for a believer, because Jesus commanded it.

Other Christians read it differently. Luther's Small Catechism, a Lutheran teaching, says of baptism that "It worketh forgiveness of sins". It is shown here as theirs.

## Question: Why would a loving God judge anyone?

A God who never judged would be a God who did not care about the abused, the cheated and the murdered. His justice is part of His love. The question is how a just God can forgive at all, and the cross is where both are kept:

> He did this to demonstrate His righteousness at the present time, so as to be just and to justify the one who has faith in Jesus.

Romans 3:26 · BSB

God "presented Him as the atoning sacrifice" (Romans 3:25). An *atoning sacrifice* is one that takes away guilt and makes peace with God. God does not ignore sin. In Christ He bears it Himself. Read more in *What the cross accomplishes*.

## Question: Is Jesus the only way?

This is not a claim Christians invented to feel superior. Jesus made it about Himself:

> Jesus answered, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.

John 14:6 · BSB

Peter, on trial, said the same: "Salvation exists in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given to men by which we must be saved" (Acts 4:12). With the Baptist Faith and Message, this site holds that "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord" (article IV, salvation). That is said humbly. No Christian found the way by their own goodness. Like the sheep in Luke 15, they were found.

## Question: What about those who never heard?

Scripture gives us solid ground here, not every detail. The Judge of all the earth will do what is right (Genesis 18:25). Everyone has some light. Creation shows God's "eternal power and divine nature" (Romans 1:20), and conscience bears witness, "their thoughts either accusing or defending them on the day when God will judge men’s secrets through Christ Jesus" (Romans 2:15–16). Paul does not say that this light saves. He says it leaves no one without witness.

This site will not promise more than Scripture says. What it does say is why Christians go: Paul goes on to ask how people can call on someone they have never heard of (Romans 10:14–15). The Baptist Faith and Message calls every believer to "endeavor to make disciples of all nations" (article XI, evangelism and missions). If you have now heard, the question is not only about them. Read more in *Bearing witness*.

## Close: Ask what God has done

Way: Here is the habit to carry to the next passage. Read the gospel as news before you read it as advice. Ask first what God has done: in the shepherd who searches, the father who runs, the Jesus God raised. Then ask what it calls for. Turn, trust, and come in.

Invite: If you have not yet trusted Christ, none of this begins with you getting yourself in order. It begins with Him. The father in Luke 15 ran before his son finished speaking. Turn from your sin and trust Jesus as Lord, because "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord". Tell Him so, in your own words. Then find a church, tell someone there, and ask to be baptized.
