# Angels, demons and spiritual powers

**Study 53 · God and Christian Faith · First content draft · 2026-11-06**

An army had surrounded the town in the night. Elisha's servant went out early, saw the horses and chariots, and ran back: "Oh, my master, what are we to do?" (2 Kings 6:15 · BSB). Elisha did not tell him the danger was small. He told him he was not seeing everything:

> "Do not be afraid," Elisha answered, "for those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

2 Kings 6:16 · BSB

Then he prayed, "O LORD, please open his eyes that he may see," and the young man saw "that the hills were full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha" (6:17 · BSB).

Scripture speaks of a world we do not usually see: angels who serve God, a fallen adversary and his spirits, and powers that Christ has overcome. It says enough for us to live without fear. It does not say everything we would like to know, and much that is said about angels and demons today goes far past it. This study reads what the text says, and marks plainly where it stops.

Moses gave the rule this study follows:

> The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but the things revealed belong to us and to our children forever, so that we may follow all the words of this law.

Deuteronomy 29:29 · BSB

Three neighbouring studies are not repeated here. Study 18, The signs and works of Jesus, reads the man with the legion of spirits in Mark 5. Study 15, The kingdom he announced, reads Jesus' answer that he drives out demons "by the finger of God". Study 25, What the cross accomplishes, reads Christ's victory as one of six pictures of the cross. Carry three questions into every passage here:

1. **What does the text actually say?** Read it before you read anything about it.
2. **Where does it stop?** Notice what it does not tell you, and leave it there.
3. **Where does it point?** In Scripture the unseen world is never the main subject. God is.

## 01 Made by him and for him

**Main readings:** Colossians 1:16; Nehemiah 9:6; Psalm 148:2–5; Job 38:7; Revelation 5:11; 22:8–9; Colossians 2:18.

Paul lists every power he can think of, and puts all of them on the same side of one line.

### What the text shows

"For in Him all things were created, things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities. All things were created through Him and for Him" (Colossians 1:16 · BSB). The "Him" is the Son. Every invisible ruler is something he made.

The Old Testament says the same. "You created the heavens, the highest heavens with all their host ... and the host of heaven worships You" (Nehemiah 9:6 · BSB). The psalm calls the angels to praise along with sun, moon and stars, "for He gave the command and they were created" (Psalm 148:5 · BSB). When God laid the earth's foundations, "all the sons of God shouted for joy" (Job 38:7 · BSB). Their number is beyond counting: John heard "myriads of myriads and thousands of thousands" round the throne (Revelation 5:11 · BSB).

### What it means

Angels are creatures. They are great, and they are not God. So they may not be worshipped. When John fell at an angel's feet, the angel stopped him: "Do not do that! I am a fellow servant with you and your brothers the prophets ... Worship God!" (Revelation 22:9 · BSB). Paul warned the Colossians against "the worship of angels" and "speculation about what he has seen" (Colossians 2:18 · BSB). The Second London Baptist Confession (1677/1689) says worship is due to God alone, "not to Angels, Saints, or any other Creatures" (chapter 22, paragraph 2).

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

Scripture does not say on which day angels were made, how many there are, or how they are ranked. It names only two of the holy angels, Gabriel and Michael. The Eastern Orthodox Longer Catechism (1839) teaches that "the invisible was created before the visible, and the angels before men" (question 103); that is its teaching, drawn from Job 38. Later writers built a ladder of nine orders of angels on a book called *The Celestial Hierarchy*, written under the name of Dionysius. John Calvin answered that "the greatest part of it" was "mere babbling", and that "we must forsake the vain imaginations ... concerning the nature, orders, and multitude of angels" (*Institutes* I.14.4).

**Consider:** Colossians 1:16 names "thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities". What does it change to know that each of them was created "for Him"?

**Reflect:** Is there anything unseen you fear as if it were equal to God?

## 02 Sent to serve

**Main readings:** Hebrews 1:4–14; Psalm 103:20–21; Luke 1:19; 15:10; Acts 12:7–15; Matthew 18:10; Psalm 91:11; Hebrews 13:2; 1 Peter 1:12.

Peter was asleep in chains between two soldiers when "an angel of the Lord appeared and a light shone in the cell" (Acts 12:7 · BSB). The chains fell off, and Peter walked out thinking he was dreaming.

### What the text shows

Hebrews gives the plainest description of what angels are for: "Are not the angels ministering spirits sent to serve those who will inherit salvation?" (Hebrews 1:14 · BSB). They are "mighty in strength", and they "carry out His word" (Psalm 103:20 · BSB). Gabriel said of himself, "I stand in the presence of God, and I have been sent" (Luke 1:19 · BSB). They rejoice: "there is joy in the presence of God's angels over one sinner who repents" (Luke 15:10 · BSB). And they do not know everything; the gospel is a thing that "even angels long to look into" (1 Peter 1:12 · BSB).

The same chapter of Hebrews sets them below the Son: "to which of the angels did God ever say: 'You are My Son'?" Instead, "Let all God's angels worship Him" (1:5–6 · BSB).

### What it means

Angels are servants, sent by God, for the good of his people. They are never offered as someone to pray to or call on. Every prayer in Scripture goes to God, and the angels come when he sends them. Hebrews adds a gentle surprise: "some people have entertained angels without knowing it" (13:2 · BSB). Their help is often unseen and unannounced.

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

Does each believer have one guardian angel? Jesus said of the little ones, "their angels in heaven always see the face of My Father" (Matthew 18:10 · BSB), and the believers at Mary's house, hearing Peter at the gate, said, "It must be his angel" (Acts 12:15 · BSB). Christians read these verses differently:

- **Yes.** The Orthodox Longer Catechism asks, "Has each one of us his guardian angels?" and answers, "Without doubt," from Matthew 18:10 (question 106).
- **Perhaps, but it does not matter.** Calvin wrote, "I know not whether this justifies the conclusion, that every one of them has his particular guardian angel," and held it certain "that they all with one consent watch for our salvation" (*Institutes* I.14.7).
- **No.** James Petigru Boyce, a founder of the Southern Baptist Theological Seminary, judged that for one angel to each person "the scriptural authority for them is wanting"; Acts 12:15 reports what the disciples said, not what God teaches (*Abstract of Systematic Theology*, chapter 18).

The Baptist Faith and Message does not address the question, and this study does not choose. All three agree on what the text makes plain: God sends his angels to serve his people.

**Consider:** In Acts 12, who sent the angel, and what were the believers doing at the time (12:5)?

**Reflect:** Do you thank God for help you never saw coming?

## 03 The angel who speaks as God

**Main readings:** Genesis 16:7–13; Exodus 3:2–6; Judges 13:18–22; Psalm 34:7.

Hagar ran away into the desert, and "the angel of the LORD found Hagar by a spring of water" (Genesis 16:7 · BSB). By the end of the conversation she was not talking about an angel.

### What the text shows

The angel of the LORD speaks to Hagar and promises, "I will greatly multiply your offspring" (16:10 · BSB), something only God can promise. Then "Hagar gave this name to the LORD who had spoken to her: 'You are the God who sees me'" (16:13 · BSB). At the bush, "the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a blazing fire," and a few lines later "God called out to him from within the bush" and said, "I am the God of your father" (Exodus 3:2, 4, 6 · BSB). Manoah asked this angel's name and was told, "Why do you ask my name ... since it is beyond comprehension?" Afterward he said, "We are going to die ... for we have seen God!" (Judges 13:18, 22 · BSB).

### What it means

This figure is unlike other angels. He speaks as God, in the first person, and people who meet him say they have met God. Two readings have been held since the early church:

- **The Son before his birth.** Justin Martyr, in the second century, argued from Mamre that the one who appeared was "another God and Lord subject to the Maker of all things; who is also called an Angel" (*Dialogue with Trypho* 56). Calvin agreed that "the same chief angel was the Word of God", who "though he was not yet incarnate ... descended, as it were, in a mediatorial capacity" (*Institutes* I.13.10).
- **A created angel speaking in God's name.** Augustine held that these appearances "were wrought by angels", speaking "in the person of God, as ... the prophets also were wont to do" (*On the Trinity* III.11.27).

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

No New Testament passage names the angel of the LORD as Christ, and none says he was not. Baptists hold both readings. This study shows both and does not choose. Either way, the passages teach what Hagar learned: God came to an outcast servant in the desert and saw her.

**Consider:** Why might the God who speaks from the bush also be called "the angel of the LORD"?

**Reflect:** Hagar named God "the God who sees me". Where do you need to hear that?

## 04 The adversary

**Main readings:** Job 1:6–12; 2:6; Zechariah 3:1–2; Genesis 3:1, 15; Revelation 12:9–10; John 8:44; 2 Corinthians 11:14; 1 Peter 5:8–9; 2 Peter 2:4; Jude 6; 1 Timothy 3:6.

"One day the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came with them" (Job 1:6 · BSB). Even the adversary has to report.

### What the text shows

In Job, Satan accuses ("Does Job fear God for nothing?" 1:9 · BSB) and can act only within the limit God sets: "you must not lay a hand on the man himself" (1:12 · BSB); "you must spare his life" (2:6 · BSB). In Zechariah he stands "to accuse" the high priest, and the LORD rebukes him (3:1–2 · BSB). The New Testament names the serpent of Eden: "that ancient serpent called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world" (Revelation 12:9 · BSB). Jesus called him "a murderer from the beginning ... a liar and the father of lies" (John 8:44 · BSB). He "masquerades as an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14 · BSB), and "prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour" (1 Peter 5:8 · BSB).

He is not alone. Peter writes of "the angels when they sinned" (2 Peter 2:4 · BSB), and Jude of angels who "abandoned their proper dwelling" (Jude 6 · BSB).

### What it means

Satan is real, personal and hostile, but he is not God's opposite number. He is a creature on a leash. His work is to accuse, to deceive and to destroy, and the Baptist Faith and Message traces the fall of the human race to him: "Through the temptation of Satan man transgressed the command of God" (Article III).

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

Scripture does not tell the story of Satan's fall in a single plain account. Readers have looked in three places:

- **Isaiah 14 and Ezekiel 28.** Isaiah's "song of contempt against the king of Babylon" (14:4 · BSB) cries, "How you have fallen from heaven, O day star, son of the dawn!" (14:12 · BSB); Ezekiel's lament for the king of Tyre says, "You were in Eden ... You were anointed as a guardian cherub" (28:13–14 · BSB). Augustine read Isaiah's words, "under the figure of the king of Babylon", as spoken "of the devil" (*On Christian Doctrine* III.37).
- **The kings themselves.** The commentary of Jamieson, Fausset and Brown (1871) reads Isaiah 14 as applying "to the Babylonian king primarily", and through him to the final Antichrist, not to Satan's first fall.
- **Pride.** Boyce, without leaning on either prophet, took Paul's warning that a new believer "may become conceited and fall under the same condemnation as the devil" (1 Timothy 3:6 · BSB) to make it "probable that pride was the sin of Satan" (*Abstract*, chapter 18).

Scripture says that angels sinned and fell. It does not give the date, or explain how a good creature first chose evil.

**Consider:** In Job 1–2, who sets every limit? What does that say about Satan's power?

**Reflect:** Satan's first words to Eve were, "Did God really say?" (Genesis 3:1 · BSB). Where do you hear that question?

## 05 Demons and the authority of Jesus

**Main readings:** Mark 1:23–27, 34; Matthew 12:28–29; Luke 10:17–20; Matthew 4:24; James 2:19; Acts 19:13–16.

The first miracle Mark tells is in a synagogue. A man with "an unclean spirit" cries out, "I know who You are—the Holy One of God!" (Mark 1:23–24 · BSB).

### What the text shows

Jesus answers with two commands: "Be silent!" and "Come out of him!" (1:25 · BSB). There is no ritual, no long struggle, no conversation. The crowd says, "He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him!" (1:27 · BSB). The demons know him, and he "would not allow the demons to speak, because they knew who He was" (1:34 · BSB). James adds, "Even the demons believe that—and shudder" (James 2:19 · BSB). Jesus explains what his deliverances mean: no one can plunder "a strong man's house ... unless he first ties up the strong man" (Matthew 12:29 · BSB).

When the seventy-two came back thrilled that "even the demons submit to us in Your name," Jesus turned their joy elsewhere: "do not rejoice that the spirits submit to you, but rejoice that your names are written in heaven" (Luke 10:17, 20 · BSB).

### What it means

In the Gospels demons are real and Jesus is never in doubt against them. They recognise him before most people do. Knowing who he is does not save them; it makes them tremble. And Jesus would not let his followers make power over spirits the centre of their joy.

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

Scripture gives no method. Matthew lists "the demon-possessed" beside those with "various diseases" and "seizures" (4:24 · BSB), so not every illness is a demon. The sons of Sceva used the right words, "I bind you by Jesus, whom Paul proclaims", and were beaten and stripped (Acts 19:13, 16 · BSB): the name of Jesus is not a spell. Christians also differ on whether a believer can be troubled by a demon from within. Scripture says that God "has rescued us from the dominion of darkness" (Colossians 1:13 · BSB) and that "the evil one cannot touch him" who is born of God (1 John 5:18 · BSB); it also warns believers, "do not give the devil a foothold" (Ephesians 4:27 · BSB). The Baptist Faith and Message does not speak to this, and this study does not choose.

**Consider:** Why did Jesus silence demons who were telling the truth about him?

**Reflect:** What makes you rejoice more: what God does through you, or that your name is written in heaven?

## 06 Disarmed at the cross

**Main readings:** Colossians 2:13–15; Hebrews 2:14–15; 1 John 3:8; Genesis 3:15; John 12:31; Ephesians 1:20–21; 1 Peter 3:22; Revelation 12:10–11; 20:10; Matthew 25:41.

The first promise in the Bible is about this fight. God told the serpent that the woman's seed "will crush your head, and you will strike his heel" (Genesis 3:15 · BSB).

### What the text shows

The New Testament says where the head was crushed. God "forgave us all our trespasses" and nailed our debt to the cross; "and having disarmed the powers and authorities, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them by the cross" (Colossians 2:13–15 · BSB). Christ shared our flesh "so that by His death He might destroy him who holds the power of death, that is, the devil, and free those who all their lives were held in slavery by their fear of death" (Hebrews 2:14–15 · BSB). "This is why the Son of God was revealed, to destroy the works of the devil" (1 John 3:8 · BSB). Before the cross Jesus said, "now the prince of this world will be cast out" (John 12:31 · BSB). After it, God seated him "far above all rule and authority, power and dominion" (Ephesians 1:21 · BSB), "with angels, authorities, and powers subject to Him" (1 Peter 3:22 · BSB).

### What it means

The victory and the forgiveness are one act. The accuser's weapon was our guilt; at the cross the debt was cancelled, so the accusation has nothing left to hold. That is why Revelation says the saints "conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (12:11 · BSB). Study 25 sets this victory beside the cross's other pictures.

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

The powers are disarmed but not yet gone: Peter still calls the devil a roaring lion. The end is fixed, though. The eternal fire was "prepared for the devil and his angels" (Matthew 25:41 · BSB), and at the last "the devil who had deceived them was thrown into the lake of fire" (Revelation 20:10 · BSB). How the years between are to be mapped is a question Baptists answer differently; Study 30, The hope to come, lays out their views.

**Consider:** In Colossians 2:13–15, what comes first, the forgiveness or the triumph? Why does the order matter?

**Reflect:** Hebrews says Christ frees those "held in slavery by their fear of death". Is that fear still holding you?

## 07 Standing firm

**Main readings:** Ephesians 6:10–18; James 4:7; 1 Peter 5:8–9; 1 John 4:1–4; Deuteronomy 18:10–12; Daniel 10:12–13, 20–21; Jude 9; Romans 8:38–39.

Near the end of Ephesians, Paul names the struggle: it is "not against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the powers of this world's darkness" (Ephesians 6:12 · BSB).

### What the text shows

His command is to stand: "be strong in the Lord ... so that you can make your stand against the devil's schemes" (6:10–11 · BSB). The armour is truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, "the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God", and prayer "at all times" (6:14–18 · BSB). James: "Submit yourselves, then, to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you" (4:7 · BSB). Peter: "Resist him, standing firm in your faith" (1 Peter 5:9 · BSB). John: "test the spirits to see whether they are from God", by what they confess about Jesus Christ, "because greater is He who is in you than he who is in the world" (1 John 4:1, 4 · BSB). And the law shuts the door on the occult: no divination, sorcery or spells, no one who "consults a medium or spiritist, or inquires of the dead" (Deuteronomy 18:10–11 · BSB).

### What it means

The weapons are ordinary: the truth, a right life, the gospel, faith, God's word and prayer. Resisting the devil begins with submitting to God. Even the archangel Michael, disputing with the devil, "did not presume to bring a slanderous charge against him, but said, 'The Lord rebuke you!'" (Jude 9 · BSB). The believer stands in Christ's strength, not his own.

### What Scripture leaves unsaid

Daniel 10 lifts the curtain once: an angel was opposed by "the prince of the kingdom of Persia" until "Michael, one of the chief princes, came to help" (10:13 · BSB). Calvin took this to mean "certain angels are appointed to preside over kingdoms and provinces" (*Institutes* I.14.7); Boyce doubted that guardian angels over nations can be proved from it (*Abstract*, chapter 18). The text does not say how many such powers there are or where. Daniel did not name or address them: he humbled himself before God and prayed, and was told, "your words were heard" (10:12 · BSB). Scripture gives no command to map spirits or to speak to them.

**Consider:** Which pieces of the armour in Ephesians 6 are things you already have in Christ, and which are things you do?

**Reflect:** Is there a practice in your life, even one that seems harmless, that Deuteronomy 18 would close?

## The question for your life

Elisha's servant saw the chariots of fire for a moment, and then the hills looked ordinary again. You may never see an angel, and you need not go looking for a demon. What has been revealed is enough: the powers were made by Christ, were disarmed by Christ, and are subject to Christ. Paul names them in his list of things that cannot reach you:

> For I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor principalities, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38–39 · BSB

Do you fear the unseen world more than you trust the One who rules it, and what would standing firm in him look like for you this week?

Read the passage again before you answer.
