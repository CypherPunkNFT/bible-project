## Direction

Teach one habit: read the text as far as it goes, and stop where it stops. Ask three questions of every passage: what does the text actually say, where does it stop, and where does it point. The shape comes from Deuteronomy 29:29, which the study itself takes as its rule: "the secret things belong to the LORD our God, but the things revealed belong to us". The page draws that sentence as a line, and every part crosses it once. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The study trail, the four subjects in gold capitals (angels · the adversary · demons · the powers), the title *Angels, demons and* with *spiritual powers* in gold italic, the lead, and 2 Kings 6:16 printed in full. Beside it, the hills round Dothan full of chariots of fire (2 Kings 6:17): ridges, wheels and slow flames round a small walled town, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, with the motto "Open his eyes / that he may see." No horse riders, no faces, no figure of God. Under the hero, four counts made by script from the site's Berean, each with its counting rule and what it means, and a row of links to every part.
- **00 The line Moses drew.** Deuteronomy 29:29 split across a vertical line: the secret things on one side, the things revealed on the other. Under it the three questions, each with its own small mark (an open eye, a dashed edge, an arrow that points up), which come back as the labels of the three sub-heads in every part. Then **the edge chart**: seven rows, one per part, with what is revealed (a phrase from its verse) on the revealed side, the dashed edge, and what is left unsaid on the other side, with the number of views the part shows. Each row opens its part.
- **01–07 The parts.** Each part is one band across the page: its number, title, hook and main readings; then "What the text shows" and "What it means" side by side in full light; then a dashed rule marked "Here the text stops"; then "What Scripture leaves unsaid" on a quieter, veiled ground. Where a part shows more than one view, the views stand side by side, each joined by a fine line to the question, under the words "Shown, not chosen". The part closes with Consider and Reflect under the arrow that points up.
  - Part 01 adds **one line**: the Son named above it in words, and below it everything Colossians 1:16, Nehemiah 9:6, Psalm 148 and Job 38:7 call created (thrones, dominions, rulers, authorities, the host of heaven, sun, moon and stars, the sons of God). Each word is checked in its verse.
  - Part 06 adds **the order in Colossians 2:13–15**: forgiveness, the debt cancelled, nailed to the cross, then the powers disarmed and the triumph. The build proves the phrases come in that order in the verses.
- **Close: the question for your life.** The hills again, ordinary now, as the servant saw them afterwards. Romans 8:38–39 printed in full, with the things it names that cannot separate us set out as words on fine rules around "the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord". The question, then "Read the passage again before you answer." Sources with what was read, and a link back to God & Christian Faith.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

On a phone the two light columns and the views stack; the edge chart drops its middle drawing and keeps the words.

## Colour

The Prophets' blue. The page's opening and its two glimpses behind the curtain come from the prophets: Elisha's prayer that his servant's eyes be opened (2 Kings 6, among the Former Prophets in the Hebrew order), Zechariah 3 and Daniel 10. The site's gold marks the concepts and the title's turn, as everywhere.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, which names Satan as the tempter in the fall (Article III) and says no more about angels. Where it is silent the page shows each view with who holds it and does not choose: guardian angels (three views), the angel of the LORD (two readings), where Satan's fall is told (three places), whether a believer can be troubled by a demon from within (the texts only), and angels over nations (Calvin and Boyce). The page says plainly that angels are creatures, not to be worshipped or prayed to (the Second London Confession, 22.2). The Orthodox Longer Catechism is labelled as Orthodox teaching. No drawn face or figure of God, of Christ, of an angel or of a demon: the unseen world is drawn as fire, light and lines, never as beings. Nothing on the page names a method for dealing with spirits, because Scripture gives none.
