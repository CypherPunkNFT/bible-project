## Direction

Teach one habit: before you ask whether a verse is about you, ask what it was written to do. The shape comes from the study itself. It names four purposes a passage can have (to praise, to comfort, to warn, to answer), and its close gives each of its passages one sentence saying which: Ephesians 1 was written to make you praise; Romans 8, John 6 and 10 and First John to comfort; Hebrews 6 to warn drifters so that they stay; Romans 9 to answer the charge that God is unfair; Mark 3 warns of one sin and promises forgiveness for all the rest. So the page sorts its eight passages by purpose before it reads any of them, carries each passage's purpose at the head of its part, and ends each part on the close's sentence for it. Its picture is the study's motto, "Two hands. One grip. / No one snatches." (John 10:28–29): sheep asleep in a fold, and tracks that come near and turn away. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The standard heading: the study trail (Study / God & Christian Faith / Chosen and kept); the three concepts in gold capitals (Chosen · Kept · Known); the title *Chosen and kept:* with *can I be sure?* as the gold italic turn; the lead; 1 John 5:13 printed in full. Beside it, inside a faint circle and dotted orbit, a small flock in a low stone fold at night, one sheep standing with its head lifted to listen, and a line of paw tracks that comes along the outer ring and turns away. The motto beneath. No hand, face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father.
- **Four counts**, each computed on the build: the eight passages the study walks (five from the letters, three from the Gospels); the four purposes; the five fears the study names; the verses printed in full.
- **00 What was it written to do?** The study's frame, with the reading key straight after the habit: four quarters, Praise, Comfort, Warning and Answer, each with its own small mark, a line on what it does, a line on how it is misread ("A song is not a sorting machine." "A promise is not a riddle." "A warning is not a verdict."), and the passages written for it. Pointing at a passage shows the close's sentence for it.
- **01–07 The passages.** Each part opens with its purpose mark, "Written to …" in gold capitals, the number, the title with its turn in gold italic, and the main readings. The reading follows in one column; paragraphs that open with the study's own labels (the setting, what it does not say, the site's reading, what both readings hold) carry a small mark. Each part has one figure of its own, drawn from its own verses:
  - 01: Ephesians 1:3–14 as one sentence of twelve verses: a dot over each verse with "in Him" or its like (nine of the twelve), the three refrain verses in gold, and the three movements, the Father chooses, the Son redeems, the Spirit seals and guarantees.
  - 02: the five links of Romans 8:29–30 as joined rings, asking which depends on you holding on; then the love of God in Christ in the centre of a ring, with the seventeen things Paul names around it and none inside.
  - 03: Romans 9's three questions, each beside Paul's answer; the two Baptist readings side by side.
  - 04: John 6:37 in its two halves, God's side and yours; then the Son's hand inside the Father's hand as two rings round the flock (John 10:28–29), so that no hand is drawn.
  - 05: two fields under one rain (Hebrews 6:7–8), grain and thorns, then the writer's turn, "we are convinced of better things in your case" (6:9).
  - 06: where to look first: what God has said (1 John 5:11), then the Spirit and a changed life confirming it, "not the price of it".
  - 07: what Jesus says first in Mark 3:28–30 (all, one, why), then Peter and Paul restored. The crisis note opens the part, before anything else.
  Each part ends with **Written to**, the close's sentence for it, then Consider and Reflect.
- **Close.** "Held, and knowing it": the close paragraphs beside John 10:27–30 printed in full; then the five fears in the study's own words, each joined to the promise its part sends you to (two of them to John 6:37); the reader's question; "Read the passage again before you answer."; the fold again at morning; the sources; and a back link to God & Christian Faith.
- **Marks.** Every note the study still has to check is a small gold four-point mark with the note in its title, so an unread source is never shown as read. Two quotations that are not word for word the Berean carry the same mark with the Berean's words.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean, and goes in the address.

## Colour

The site's gold for the page and its marks. Each part takes the colour of the part of the Bible its passage is in: the letters for parts 01, 02, 03, 05 and 06, the Gospels for 04 and 07 and for the close's passage. Every colour repeats something the words already say, so nothing depends on telling colours apart.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article V: election is "the gracious purpose of God", "consistent with the free agency of man", and "All true believers endure to the end"; article IV: "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord." Every phrase of the confession the study quotes is found by script in the copy held on this PC. On election itself the page shows the two Baptist readings side by side under one heading, Calvinist and non-Calvinist, as the study does, with what both hold; it does not choose between them, because the confession does not. The Wesleyan and Arminian view of Hebrews 6 is shown as another tradition's, in its own words, under its own mark. The page treats the fear in its readers as the reason for the study: each fear is named in the study's own words and sent to a promise in its own passage, and the part on the unforgivable sin opens with the crisis lines before anything else. Nothing on the page draws God, Christ or a hand: the two hands are two rings. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
