# Passage notes: Chosen and kept: can I be sure? (58)

Folder: god-and-christian-faith/chosen-and-kept. Doctrinal anchor: Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article V (God's purpose of grace); also article II (God's foreknowledge), article IV (salvation), article XI (evangelism and missions). All Scripture is quoted from the BSB text supplied for this job. Verses not supplied are referred to without being quoted.

## Ephesians 1:3–14 (owned)

**Context.** The opening blessing of the letter, a single sentence in the Greek, with a threefold refrain "to the praise of His glory" (1:6, 12, 14). It moves from the Father's choosing (1:3–6) to the Son's redeeming (1:7–12) to the Spirit's sealing (1:13–14). Paul's prayer that follows (1:15–23) belongs to 04, *Prayer across Scripture*.

**Main readings.** (1) God's choosing of particular persons "in Him" before creation: the Calvinist Baptist reading (Mohler, Schreiner, Ascol; the Abstract of Principles). (2) Corporate election: God chose a people in Christ, and individuals share in that choice by faith, with weight laid on "having heard and believed" (1:13). This is a common non-Calvinist reading (the 2012 Traditional Statement; Allen and Lemke; Keathley). Both readings agree that election aims at holiness (1:4) and rests on grace (1:6–7).

**Links.** 04 (1:15–23); the cross study quotes 1:20–23; 53 quotes 1:20–21; *The Holy Spirit* for sealing.

## Romans 8:28–39 (owned)

**Context.** The close of Romans 8, after life in the Spirit (8:1–17, owned by *The Holy Spirit*) and creation's groaning and hope (8:18–25, owned by 30, *The hope to come*). 45 keeps 8:18–26 as a pair.

**Main readings.** The "golden chain" (8:29–30) is read by both Baptist groups as unbroken: no one justified fails to be glorified. On "foreknew": as God's prior love and choice (Calvinist), or as His foreknowledge of faith (non-Calvinist). Both are consistent with article II. On 8:28, the good is defined by 8:29, and the verse is no promise that all things are good. 8:36 quotes Psalm 44:22.

**Links.** *The Holy Spirit*; *The hope to come*; 45; 03 *Faith under pressure* (for suffering).

## Romans 9:6–24 (owned)

**Context.** Paul's grief over Israel (9:1–5) and his question whether God's word has failed (9:6), within Romans 9 to 11, which ends in hope for Israel (11). The study states plainly that the chapter lays no blame on the Jewish people and describes Israel's election in Jewish covenant terms first. Old Testament citations: Genesis 21:12 (9:7), Genesis 18:10, 14 (9:9), Genesis 25:23 (9:12), Malachi 1:2–3 (9:13), Exodus 33:19 (9:15), Exodus 9:16 (9:17).

**Main readings.** (1) Individual, unconditional election to salvation: Calvinist Baptists (R. Albert Mohler Jr.; Thomas R. Schreiner, Romans commentary; Tom Ascol; historically the Abstract of Principles, 1858). (2) God's freedom to choose the line and instruments of His purpose in history and to set faith as the condition of mercy (9:30–32), with Jacob and Esau read partly as nations: non-Calvinist Baptists (Eric Hankins and the 2012 Traditional Statement; David L. Allen and Steve W. Lemke, *Whosoever Will*; Kenneth Keathley, *Salvation and Sovereignty*; Herschel H. Hobbs). The site does not choose. On 9:22, the "what if" and God's patience are noted; article V speaks of no decree of condemnation, and the study leaves the verse's further explanation open.

**Links.** *Saved by grace* (Romans 3:21–4:8); the apologetics answer on election.

## John 6:35–44 (owned)

**Context.** The bread-of-life discourse after the feeding of the five thousand, in Galilee. The study notes that "the Jews" in 6:41 means that crowd, and that Jesus and His disciples were Jewish.

**Main readings.** 6:37 and 6:39 are read by both Baptist groups as promising that none who come are lost. On "draws" (6:44): effectual drawing of those the Father gives (Calvinist), or a drawing of all, read with 12:32, which can be resisted (non-Calvinist).

**Links.** *Who do you say that I am?* (the "I am" sayings); *The good news*.

## John 10:27–30 (owned)

**Context.** The Good Shepherd discourse, spoken at the Festival of Dedication (10:22). The study walks only 10:27–30.

**Main readings.** Both Baptist groups read the passage as a central text for the security of the believer (article V). The Wesleyan reading takes "follow" as a continuing condition. 10:30 is read by *Who do you say that I am?* for Christ's oneness with the Father; this study only points there.

**Links.** 11 *Who do you say that I am?*

## Hebrews 6:4–12 (owned)

**Context.** One of the warning passages in Hebrews, written to believers under pressure who were tempted to drift (5:11–6:3; 10:32–34). The image of two fields (6:7–8) is followed at once by "better things in your case" (6:9–12).

**Main readings.** (1) People who came near Christ without true new birth: Calvin and John Owen on Hebrews; many Calvinist Baptists. (2) A real warning used by God as a means of keeping believers: Schreiner and Caneday, *The Race Set Before Us*. (3) A hypothetical warning. (4) A real fall by a true believer: the Wesleyan and Arminian view (the Methodist Articles of Religion, article 12, quoted as theirs), held also by Free Will Baptists outside the convention, many of whom treat such a fall as irremediable. The site holds with article V that true believers will never finally fall away.

**Links.** *A new creation* (links here for "Can I lose my salvation?"); *Life together* (Hebrews 10:23–25); 47 and 41 (other Hebrews passages); 1 John 2:19 is quoted.

## 1 John 5:1–13 (owned)

**Context.** The close of John's first letter, written to churches after some members had left (2:19). Its stated purpose is in 5:13. 1:5–2:2 belongs to *Life together*; 4:1–6 to 06; 4:7–21 to 21, the God study.

**Main readings.** Assurance grounded in God's testimony that life is in the Son (5:9–12), confirmed by the Spirit (5:6, 10) and by love and obedience (5:1–3), the last two read as evidences, not grounds. "Water and blood" (5:6) is most often read as Jesus' baptism and death. Other readings exist; the study leaves this open.

**Links.** *The good news* (quotes 5:11–13); *Life together*; 06 *Learning to discern*; 21 *God: who He is and what He does*.

## Mark 3:28–30 (owned)

**Context.** Teachers of the law from Jerusalem attribute Jesus' exorcisms to Beelzebul (3:22). Mark states the reason for Jesus' words in 3:30. Parallels are Matthew 12:31–32 and Luke 12:10, referred to only.

**Main readings.** (1) A settled, knowing attribution of the Spirit's evident work in Jesus to Satan. (2) Final impenitence, unbelief kept up until death (Augustine [check]). Pastoral reading for the anxious believer: 3:28's "all sins and blasphemies" comes first, and distress over this sin is the opposite of the accusers' hardness. That last point is offered as a pastoral observation, not as Scripture. Crisis help is placed first, under safe-messaging rules.

**Links.** *Fear, anxiety and despair* (56); *A new creation*; John 6:37 (part 04).
