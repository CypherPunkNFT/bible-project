# Father Son and Holy Spirit

**Study 22 · God and Christian Faith · First content draft · 2026-10-13**

Scripture confesses one God and speaks of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Jesus prays to the Father, speaks of the Spirit whom the Father will send, and promises the Spirit's witness concerning himself. The apostles name them together in the life of the church.

Begin with what the passages say before introducing the terms used to explain their relationship. This study distinguishes three kinds of statement: the biblical wording, observations about its setting, and doctrinal formulations that bring passages together. The last section introduces a Trinitarian formulation and identifies two differing readings through their own published statements.

## 01 The LORD our God is one LORD

**Main readings:** Deuteronomy 6:4–9; 1 Corinthians 8:4–6.

### Hear, O Israel

> Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD:

Deuteronomy 6:4 · KJV

The declaration leads immediately into the command to love the LORD with all the heart, soul, and might. These words are to be kept in the heart and taught to children, spoken of at home and along the way, and remembered in the ordinary movements of the day.

God's oneness is therefore proclaimed within a call to undivided love and allegiance. It is not introduced merely as an abstract counting problem. Israel's life is to be ordered by the identity of the God addressed and worshipped.

Read the declaration in its own setting. It does not contain the later phrase “one essence, three persons.” Neither should a later study of Father, Son, and Spirit set this confession aside as though it belonged to a discarded faith. The question is how the passages are read together while retaining this declared oneness.

### One God, the Father; one Lord Jesus Christ

Paul discusses food offered to idols in 1 Corinthians 8. Within that setting, he says there is no other God but one. He distinguishes the many beings called gods and lords from the confession he gives the community: one God, the Father, from whom are all things, and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things.

Both the distinction and the shared scope of “all things” deserve attention. Paul names the Father and Jesus Christ together while rejecting the world of competing gods. This verse belongs among the passages a doctrinal account must explain; it should not be shortened so that either the one God or the one Lord disappears.

**Consider:** What response follows the confession in Deuteronomy? What setting prompts Paul's statement in Corinthians?

**Reflect:** Consider how confessing one God should affect the loyalties and practices that organise everyday life.

## 02 Shew us the Father

**Main readings:** John 14:1–14, 23–31; 17:1–5.

### He that hath seen me hath seen the Father

Jesus speaks to troubled disciples about his departure and the Father's house. Thomas asks about the way; Philip asks to be shown the Father. Jesus answers Philip by directing attention to himself, his words, and his works. He speaks of being in the Father and the Father in him.

Read this answer together with the distinction maintained throughout the conversation. Jesus goes to the Father, is sent by the Father, and seeks the Father's glory. His revelation of the Father does not make those statements unnecessary. The passage requires attention both to the closeness it declares and to the relation it describes.

Knowing the Father is not presented as a task that bypasses Jesus. He names himself the way, the truth, and the life, and says that coming to the Father is through him. The teaching answers the disciples' uncertainty by directing them to the one already speaking among them.

### Father, glorify thy Son

John 17 begins with Jesus lifting his eyes and addressing the Father. He speaks of the Father's gift of authority, the work given to him, and the glory he had with the Father before the world existed. The prayer includes the statement that eternal life is knowing the Father, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom he sent.

John 14 also records Jesus saying that the Father is greater than he. These sayings belong in the reading alongside the statements about shared glory and mutual indwelling. A responsible account cannot select only one group of statements and act as though the other were absent.

At this stage, observe what the passages actually give: prayer, sending, love, work, glory, and revelation. The doctrinal question is how to understand those relations without losing the claims made elsewhere about the Son.

**Consider:** How does Jesus answer Philip through his words and works? Which details in the prayer distinguish the one praying from the one addressed?

**Reflect:** Read the whole conversation before using one sentence as a summary of everything John says about the Father and Son.

## 03 The Word was made flesh

**Main reading:** John 1:1–18.

### With God, and was God

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

John 1:1 · KJV

The opening holds two statements together: the Word was with God, and the Word was God. It then says that all things were made through him, excluding from created things anything made without him. Life and light follow within this description.

John the witness is carefully distinguished from the light to which he bears witness. The prologue then tells of the world's failure to recognise the one through whom it was made, of rejection, and of reception by those who believe. These are not only statements about what existed in the beginning; they lead into the world's encounter with the one being described.

Do not remove “with God” to erase relation, or remove “was God” to avoid the force of the KJV's wording. Begin by retaining the whole sentence and its account of creation.

### He hath declared him

The Word becomes flesh and dwells among people. John speaks of witnessed glory, grace, and truth, and names Jesus Christ. The prologue concludes by saying that the only begotten Son, in the bosom of the Father, has declared the God whom no one has seen.

This connects the Son's identity with making God known. The passage does not describe a human teacher who simply supplies opinions about a distant deity. Its account reaches from the beginning and creation to flesh, dwelling, reception, and revelation.

The distinction between Father and Son remains in the language of relationship. At the same time, the opening's claim about the Word must continue to inform the reading. The later doctrinal vocabulary is intended to address precisely such combinations of statements; it should be tested against them rather than substituted for reading them.

**Consider:** What does each clause of John 1:1 contribute? How does the closing statement about declaring God connect with the opening?

**Reflect:** Consider how John's movement from creation to flesh changes what it means to say that God makes himself known.

## 04 Another Comforter

**Main readings:** John 14:15–26; 15:26–27; 16:5–15.

### Whom the Father will send in my name

Jesus promises to ask the Father, who will give another Comforter to remain with the disciples. He identifies this Comforter as the Spirit of truth and speaks of his presence with and in them. The promise answers the concern that Jesus' departure will leave them alone.

> But the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you.

John 14:26 · KJV

The teaching and remembrance concern what Jesus has told these disciples. The Father sends in the Son's name; the Spirit's work is connected to the Son's words. Retain this setting before applying the promise to later readers. It is not a declaration that every impression a reader experiences is infallible instruction.

### He shall testify of me

John 15 speaks of the Comforter whom Jesus will send from the Father, the Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father. The Spirit will testify of Jesus; the disciples will also bear witness because they have been with him from the beginning.

In John 16, the Spirit reproves the world, guides the disciples into truth, speaks what he hears, and glorifies Jesus. Jesus then connects what is his with what belongs to the Father. The passage describes a coordinated work of revelation rather than unrelated messages from competing sources.

These actions are central to the question of how the Spirit is understood. Read the verbs and relationships, not only pronouns treated in isolation. A fuller account of the Spirit's work follows in the next study; here the focus is how Jesus speaks of the Spirit in relation to himself and the Father.

**Consider:** Who asks, who sends, and what does the Comforter do? How is the Spirit's witness connected with the disciples' witness?

**Reflect:** Consider how this promise directs attention back to Jesus' words rather than away from them.

## 05 In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost

**Main readings:** Matthew 3:13–17; 28:16–20; 2 Corinthians 13:11–14.

### At the baptism of Jesus

Matthew narrates Jesus being baptized, the Spirit of God descending like a dove, and a voice from heaven identifying the beloved Son. Keep the description concrete: the Son is baptized, the Spirit descends, and the heavenly voice speaks about the Son.

This is a narrative in which the relations are displayed together. It is not itself a paragraph defining substance or person. Its contribution to a doctrinal reading is what it shows and says; later explanations must account for those details rather than replace the scene with an analogy.

Matthew's concluding commission likewise names Father, Son, and Holy Ghost in the instruction to baptize. Jesus gives that instruction between declaring his authority and promising his continuing presence. Teaching obedience belongs to the same commission. The threefold naming is part of the church's commanded practice.

### Grace, love, and communion

Paul closes 2 Corinthians with an appeal to peace and a blessing:

> The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all. Amen.

2 Corinthians 13:14 · KJV

The blessing joins all three in addressing the community. It does not give a detailed metaphysical explanation, but neither is it incidental to the letter's spiritual life. Grace, love, and communion are invoked for all the readers.

Taken with Matthew, this shows why a study of the relationship concerns more than terminology. Baptism, blessing, teaching, and shared life are already places where these names appear together. The task is to understand their relation faithfully within the confession of one God.

**Consider:** What does the baptism scene show that a bare list of names would not? What purposes do the threefold naming and blessing serve?

**Reflect:** Notice how passages central to doctrinal discussion also concern belonging, obedience, peace, and the life of a community.

## 06 Bringing the passages together

**Read again:** Deuteronomy 6:4; John 1:1–18; 14:16–28; 17:1–5; Matthew 28:16–20.

### One God, three persons

In Trinitarian doctrine, unity of divine being is distinguished from the personal relations of Father, Son, and Spirit. An explicit Catholic formulation teaches one God in three really distinct persons, each fully divine rather than a portion of God. It uses “substance” for divine unity and “person” for distinction. These are explanatory terms, not a verbatim sentence from the passages above. See the [Catechism, paragraphs 251–255](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_2_the_father.index.html).

The interpretive task is to retain God's oneness, the claims about the Word, the Son's relation to the Father, and the Spirit's described activity together. Questions remain to be explained, including the Son's prayer and obedience, the Father's greatness, and the Spirit's sending. Naming the doctrine does not remove the need to read these passages carefully.

### Where readings differ

Two examples show why “non-Trinitarian” does not identify a single alternative. The United Pentecostal Church International describes one God revealed as Father, through the Son in redemption, and as the Holy Spirit; its statement also identifies Jesus as God manifested in flesh, both God and man. See [UPCI, Our Beliefs](https://upci.org/our-beliefs/).

Jehovah's Witnesses distinguish Jesus from Almighty God and deny his equality with the Father. They describe the holy spirit as God's active power rather than a divine person. See their [statement of beliefs](https://www.jw.org/en/library/tracts/What-Do-Jehovahs-Witnesses-Believe/What-Do-Jehovahs-Witnesses-Believe/) and [explanation of the holy spirit](https://www.jw.org/en/bible-teachings/questions/what-is-the-holy-spirit/).

These are attributed summaries, not interchangeable descriptions or an exhaustive survey. Compare what each account does with the same passages: how it preserves oneness, understands the Word, explains Jesus' prayer, and reads the Spirit's work. Fair comparison requires the actual teaching of those represented, as well as the whole biblical context. A familiar analogy or an opponent's slogan cannot do that work.

**Consider:** Which statements in this section are doctrinal formulations rather than Bible quotations? What must any proposed explanation account for in the selected readings?

**Reflect:** Choose one passage and distinguish its wording, your observations, and the conclusions you draw. Identify where further reading is needed.

The passages lead through confession, revelation, prayer, promise, baptism, and blessing. Keep their words visible as you study the doctrine: the unity confessed, the relationships described, and the life to which their hearers are called.
