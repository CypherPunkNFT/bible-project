## Official doctrinal sources

Consulted 2026-10-13. Use these as evidence of what the named communities teach, not as independent proof of every historical or exegetical assertion made on their pages.

- [Catholic Catechism, The Father](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_2_the_father.index.html), especially 251–255: source for the attributed formulation and its vocabulary. This Catholic source does not by itself represent every detail of Orthodox or Protestant teaching.
- [UPCI, Our Beliefs](https://upci.org/our-beliefs/): source for its account of one God revealed as Father, through the Son, and as Spirit, and Jesus as God manifested in flesh. Do not equate its reading with the Witness account.
- [Jehovah's Witnesses, What Do Jehovah's Witnesses Believe?](https://www.jw.org/en/library/tracts/What-Do-Jehovahs-Witnesses-Believe/What-Do-Jehovahs-Witnesses-Believe/): source for its distinction between Jesus and Almighty God and denial of equality with the Father.
- [Jehovah's Witnesses, What Is the Holy Spirit?](https://www.jw.org/en/bible-teachings/questions/what-is-the-holy-spirit/): source for its active-power interpretation. Its textual-history and linguistic arguments are not independently adopted here.

## Interpretive decisions

1. **Three levels:** Bible wording, contextual observation, and doctrinal formulation remain identifiable. Do not present “one essence, three persons” as a full-verse quotation or suppose that a later term settles the interpretation of every passage merely by being named.
2. **One God:** Deuteronomy is read with love and teaching, and Corinthians with the idol-food context. No Hebrew claim that the word for one inherently proves a compound unity is made.
3. **Father and Son:** preserve John 14:28 and 17:3 alongside 1:1, 14:9–11, and 17:5. The draft identifies the interpretive task rather than hiding passages that different traditions emphasise.
4. **Word:** the main text follows the KJV and makes no unsupported Greek-grammar claim about John 1:1. A translation comparison would require original-language sources and an explicitly labelled treatment.
5. **Spirit:** actions and relations in the discourse are read together. No proof rests on English pronoun gender alone. The teaching/remembrance promise is first addressed to Jesus' disciples; later application does not make individual impressions infallible.
6. **Proceeding and sending:** John 15:26 is retained without treating it as an exhaustive settlement of the later East–West procession dispute. A fuller comparison needs official sources from both traditions and its own scope.
7. **Baptism:** Matthew narrates Son, Spirit, and heavenly voice together. The commission and blessing jointly name them without themselves spelling out the complete later terminology. Avoid claiming that the singular “name” alone proves every aspect of a doctrinal system.
8. **KJV scope:** this draft does not use 1 John 5:7 as a shortcut. If that passage is later introduced, its textual-history question needs explicit sourced treatment; no claim about it is developed here.
9. **Alternatives:** the two selected institutional statements illustrate distinct disagreements, not all forms of non-Trinitarian belief. Summaries should be checked against the linked statements and not expanded into claims about adherents' motives or standing before God.
10. **Analogies:** none is required. Do not add water/ice/steam, three parts of an object, or three independent people as if any supplied an exact account of the doctrine under discussion.
