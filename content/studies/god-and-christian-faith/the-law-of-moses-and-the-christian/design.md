## Direction

Teach one habit: read each law of Leviticus twice, first where it stood in Israel, then with what the New Testament does with it, and only then ask what it asks of you. The study's three questions are the habit; the page draws them as one plan of the camp read in two ways, and as two columns side by side in every part. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

The shape comes from the book itself (stage 2 of the study prompt, "Scripture's own structure"). Leviticus is set in places: the altar at the entrance to the Tent of Meeting (1:3), the entrance where the priests are ordained (8:3–4), the camp and the place outside it where the man with a skin disease lives (13:46), the Most Holy Place behind the veil that one man enters once a year (16:2, 16:34), the field with its edges left for the poor (19:9), and the land that "is Mine" (25:23). Its time is counted in sevens: the seventh day, the seventh month, the seventh year, and after seven sevens the fiftieth day and the fiftieth year (23:3, 23:16, 23:24, 25:4, 25:8–10). A reader can open the chapters and see both.

- **Opening.** Study trail (Study / God & Christian Faith / Leviticus), five concepts in gold capitals (Offerings · Priests · Clean and unclean · Atonement · Holiness), the title *Leviticus:* in ink with *holiness and sacrifice* in gold italic, and the lead: Exodus ends with a closed door; Leviticus begins on the other side of it. Beside it, a line drawing of the court and the Tent of Meeting with the cloud settled over it and smoke rising slowly from the altar, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, with the motto from Leviticus 1:1, "The LORD called / from the Tent of Meeting." No face or figure of God. Leviticus 1:1 is printed under the lead. A bar of four counted figures, each saying what it means: the 27 chapters read in order, the 8 parts, the number of New Testament verses that repeat "love your neighbor as yourself" (counted from the Berean), and the verses printed in full. Then the three questions the study carries into every part, numbered, and a row of links to every part.
- **00 The camp, read twice.** The signature graphic: a plan of the camp drawn as nested outlines (the land, the camp, the court with the altar, the Holy Place, the Most Holy Place behind the veil) with the wilderness outside. Each part of the study is a numbered point where its chapters happen. A switch reads the same plan twice: *In Israel* (the verse that puts the part there) and *As the New Testament reads it* (the verse that answers it: the one sacrifice at the altar, the touch at the camp's edge, the way opened through the curtain, the suffering outside the gate). Point at or choose a point to read its line below; the line opens the verse and the part. The question it answers is stated beside it: "Where does each part stand, and how near could Israel come?" Its one sentence: only one man, once a year, goes behind the veil; every other part happens further out. On a phone the plan is left out and its points are shown as a list.
- **01–08 The parts.** Each part opens with a wide head: a large numeral, a miniature of the same plan with that part's place lit, the title, the hook in italics and the main readings. Below it, the habit in two columns side by side: *In Israel* on the left and *How the New Testament reads it* on the right, with the passages printed in full where the study prints them. Under both, *Where the reading stops*, set apart by a dashed rule, the edge of what can be said. Consider and Reflect close each part side by side.
  - **Part 06** adds *Holy time counts in sevens*: five rows (day, month, year, fifty days, fifty years), each in the words of its verse, with the seventh step marked and the fiftieth after seven sevens drawn as one step beyond. It answers "How does Leviticus count holy time?"
  - **Part 08** lays out its three Baptist families as three columns of equal weight, in the study's order, each with "Baptists who hold it". Then *Two verses, three routes*: choose Leviticus 19:18 or 19:19, and each family's route to it is drawn in the study's own words, ending in the same place. The caption is the study's own sentence: they differ more in how they reach a command than in where they arrive, though the Sabbath is one place they arrive differently. The page does not choose.
- **Close.** *Read Leviticus again*: Leviticus 26:12 and 2 Corinthians 6:16 side by side, the same promise to Israel and to a church, then the study's question and "Read the passage again before you answer", with Leviticus 19:9–18 printed in full beside it (the field, the courtroom and the neighbour's house the question names). The sources, each saying what was read, and a back link to God & Christian Faith.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean. The writer's five open citation checks are shown in place as muted "to check" notes, so the page does not pretend they are settled.

## Colour

History orange, the site's colour for the books from Genesis to Esther, because the page reads Leviticus. The site's gold marks the concepts and the title's turn. The two readings of the plan are told apart by line (solid for Israel, dashed for the opened way) and by words, never by colour alone.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: Christ's one sacrifice and substitutionary death (Article II), salvation not by keeping the law (Article IV), and the first day as the Lord's Day (Article VIII). Where Baptists differ, on how the law of Moses applies today, the page gives the three families equal columns and the same diagram, and does not choose; theonomy is named only so the reader recognises it. The forgiveness the offerings gave is shown as real and as pointing beyond itself. Readings without a holder in the text (the food laws' other proposed reasons, Azazel, the autumn feasts as a timetable) stay labelled as the study labels them. No drawn face or figure of God; the cloud and the veil stand for His presence. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
