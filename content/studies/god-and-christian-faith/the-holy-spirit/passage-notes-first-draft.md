# The Holy Spirit — passage and editorial notes

**Item 23 · First-draft notes · 2026-10-13**

## Primary readings

Read local KJV: John 3:1–8; 14:15–26; Acts 2:1–21, 32–42; Romans 8:1–27; 1 Corinthians 12–13; 14:1–5, 26–33, 39–40; Galatians 5:13–6:10. The selected Acts ranges support the event and concluding explanation; this draft does not claim to expound every intervening quotation in Peter's sermon.

Five exact full-verse block quotations: John 3:6; Romans 8:16; Galatians 5:22; Galatians 5:25; Galatians 6:9. Galatians 5:22 ends mid-sentence in verse numbering; the following prose explicitly continues the list from verse 23. Exposition and reflection are editorial writing.

## Attributed doctrinal sources

Consulted 2026-10-13:

- [Assemblies of God, Statement of Fundamental Truths](https://ag.org/Beliefs/Statement-of-Fundamental-truths), sections 7–8. Supports the attributed distinction between new birth and Spirit baptism, tongues as initial evidence, and the distinction of purpose/use from the congregational gift. Do not describe this as the position of every Pentecostal or continuationist.
- [Orthodox Presbyterian committee report on baptism and gifts](https://opc.org/GA/giftsHS.html). Supports the report's conclusion that prophecy and tongues ceased with the apostolic age. Identify it as a committee report, not a quotation from Scripture or a universal Reformed statement.

These sources document the named positions. Their wider theological arguments are not all reproduced or independently established here. The draft offers examples of disagreement, not an exhaustive denominational survey.

## Interpretation and claim limits

1. **Promise:** John 14's remembrance promise is addressed first to disciples who heard Jesus. It does not establish that every modern inward impression is inspired or beyond examination.
2. **Pentecost:** retain Acts' comparisons, sound as wind and tongues like fire. The crowd recognises languages, and Peter explains the event through Joel and the risen Jesus. No modern language phenomenon is declared identical solely from resemblance.
3. **Acts 2:38–39:** retain repentance, baptism, gift, and the Lord's calling. A complete account of sacramental efficacy, reception sequence, and baptismal subjects requires further texts and attributed interpretations; the introductory retelling does not settle all these issues.
4. **John 3:5:** the draft identifies the interpretive question of water without assigning unsourced denominational positions. If expanded, source both baptismal and cleansing-imagery readings directly. No universal emotional sequence is prescribed.
5. **Assurance:** read Romans 8:16 within belonging, putting sinful deeds to death, suffering, inheritance, and prayer. The KJV's “itself” is retained in quotation; no conclusion about the Spirit's personhood is drawn from English pronoun choice.
6. **Groaning:** Romans 8 includes creation, believers, and the Spirit's help. Do not equate the intercession of 8:26 automatically with tongues; the passage does not name tongues there.
7. **Gifts:** diversity, common benefit, Spirit's will, body, love, intelligibility, and order all matter. The question about whether all speak in tongues should be read with the Pentecostal distinction of uses when representing that position, rather than claiming advocates simply overlook the verse.
8. **Cessation:** no date is inferred directly from the phrase “that which is perfect” without argument. The attributed committee conclusion is a synthesis, not Paul's explicit use of the words “apostolic age.” Cessation of particular gifts should not be rewritten as denial of all present divine help.
9. **Scope of Corinthians:** the full section on gifts is larger than the selected reading in chapter 14. The draft makes no claim to settle every question of speech and order, including verses 34–38. Expand the reading transparently if that subject is added.
10. **Fruit and walk:** they are distinct reading emphases, not separate spiritual ranks. The fruit's relational setting continues into restoration in Galatians 6. Do not use the list as a simplistic personality diagnosis.
11. **Sowing:** the context concerns life, good, responsibility, and sharing. No financial-return formula or guaranteed healing follows from this imagery.
