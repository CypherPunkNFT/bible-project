# The Holy Spirit (23): passage notes

## John 14:15–26 (owned)

**Context.** Jesus' farewell words in the upper room (John 13–17), the night before the crucifixion, after the foot-washing and Judas's departure. The promise of the Spirit is framed by love and obedience (14:15, 21, 23) and by Jesus' departure and return.

**Main readings.**
- *The Advocate (paraklētos)* as a helper called alongside, legal overtones; "another" read as another of the same kind as Jesus. Broadly shared across Protestant, Catholic and Orthodox commentary.
- *Trinitarian reading:* Father gives, Son asks, Spirit comes; Father and Son "make Our home" (14:23) where the Spirit dwells. Historic Christian reading, held by the site (BFM article II).
- *14:26 as the apostles' promise:* the Spirit reminds the apostles of Jesus' words, read by many evangelical and Baptist teachers as underlying the New Testament's inspiration. Some readers apply it directly to all believers' illumination; the study allows a secondary application (illumination) but warns against treating impressions as revelation.

**Links.** What the Bible says about itself (27); Trinity articles; Who do you say that I am? (11).

## John 16:7–15 (owned)

**Context.** Same discourse. Jesus explains why His going is for the disciples' benefit, then describes the Spirit's work toward the world (16:8–11) and toward the disciples (16:12–15).

**Main readings.**
- *Conviction of the world:* the Spirit shows the world's guilt (sin = not believing in Jesus), the righteousness vindicated by Jesus' going to the Father, and the judgement of "the prince of this world". BFM article II echoes 16:8 ("convicts men of sin, of righteousness, and of judgment").
- *"Guide you into all truth":* first to the apostles (with 16:12), applied secondarily to the Spirit's work of illumination in all believers. Baptists, with most evangelicals, deny it licenses new revelation contrary to Scripture.
- *16:14 "He will glorify Me":* the study's governing verse.

**Links.** What the Bible says about itself (27); Angels, demons and spiritual powers (53) could be linked for 16:11 (not linked in content; editor's choice).

## Acts 2:1–21 (owned)

**Context.** The Jewish festival of Shavuot (Feast of Weeks; Leviticus 23:15–21; Deuteronomy 16:9–12), a pilgrimage feast, fifty days from Passover. Described in Jewish terms first in the study. The believers, all Jews, are together in Jerusalem; Peter interprets the event from Joel 2.

**Main readings.**
- *Tongues as known languages* (2:6, 8, 11): plain reading, widely held.
- *Babel reversed:* languages that divided now declare God's wonders; read in Genesis 1–11 (50), which quotes 2:5–11.
- *Pentecost as unrepeatable inauguration* (Baptist and most Reformed readings) versus *Pentecost as the pattern of a subsequent Spirit baptism with tongues* (Classical Pentecostal; Assemblies of God Statement of Fundamental Truths, 7 and 8). Shown labelled in Part 07.
- *Sinai link:* some Christian readers connect Pentecost with the giving of the law; the study mentions only the Jewish liturgical link and flags its date.

**Links.** The good news (54) for 2:22–41 and 2:38; Genesis 1–11 (50) for 2:5–11; The church and its calling (28) for women's prophecy and the pastoral office. Psalms study (48) keeps 2:29–36 (not quoted here).

**Care taken.** No guilt for Christ's death laid on the Jewish people; Peter's later "you crucified" (2:36) is not quoted.

## Ezekiel 36:25–27 (owned)

**Context.** Ezekiel, priest-prophet in Babylonian exile, after the fall of Jerusalem (36:16–38 as a unit). God acts for His name's sake to restore Israel, cleanse them and give a new heart and His Spirit.

**Main readings.**
- *Jewish reading (given first):* God's promise to Israel of cleansing, inner renewal and return to the land; read as the haftarah of Shabbat Parah, beside Numbers 19 (red heifer, water of purification).
- *Christian reading:* fulfilled through the Spirit's regenerating work in Christ, heard in John 3:5 ("water and the Spirit"); linked with the new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31–34, not quoted).
- *Israel and the church:* dispensational, progressive dispensational, covenantal and progressive covenantal Baptists relate the promise to Israel and the church differently; the study does not choose and links Covenants (26).

**Links.** Encounters with Jesus (14) for John 3; A new creation (01); Baptism and the Lord's Supper (59); Covenants and promises (26).

## Romans 8:1–17 (owned)

**Context.** Follows Romans 7's struggle with sin and the law. Paul's account of life in the Spirit: freedom from condemnation, the Spirit's indwelling, the obligation to put sin to death, adoption.

**Main readings.**
- *8:9 every believer has the Spirit:* central to the Baptist reading that the Spirit is given at conversion (BFM article II).
- *8:13 synergy of effort and grace:* believers act "by the Spirit"; broadly shared Reformed and Baptist reading.
- *8:15–16 the Spirit's witness:* read by some (Puritan and Wesleyan traditions differently) as a distinct inner witness; by others as the witness given through crying "Abba" and a changed life. The study refuses to make it a demand for one feeling.
- *8:17 suffering:* the transition to 8:18–25 (owned by The hope to come, 30).

**Links.** The cross, the empty tomb and the throne (25); The hope to come (30); Chosen and kept (58); What Scripture promises the sick (45) keeps 8:18–26 (not linked in content; editor may add for 8:26).

## Galatians 5:16–26 (owned)

**Context.** Galatian churches disputing whether Gentile believers must keep the law of Moses; quarrelling (5:15). Paul answers with life by the Spirit, not new law.

**Main readings.**
- *Flesh and Spirit* as two powers in conflict in the believer (most Protestant readings); some read 5:17 as the Spirit overcoming the flesh rather than describing ongoing stalemate. The study names the struggle without settling the fine point.
- *5:21 warning:* read by Baptists (BFM article V) as describing a settled way of life inconsistent with belonging to Christ, and as a means God uses to keep believers; Wesleyan-Arminian readers see a possibility of final apostasy (not developed here; covered in Chosen and kept, 58).
- *Fruit singular:* one harvest, not a menu.

**Links.** Life together (02) for Galatians 6:1–10; A new creation (01), which quotes 5:16, 22–23; Chosen and kept (58).

## 1 Corinthians 12:1–11 (owned)

**Context.** Opens Paul's section on spiritual gifts (chapters 12–14) to a church prizing dramatic gifts, many from pagan backgrounds. Answers a question the Corinthians had raised ("Now about…").

**Main readings.**
- *12:3 confession test;* *12:4–6 Trinitarian pattern;* *12:7 common good* — widely shared.
- *The list (12:8–10):* cessationist and continuationist readings differ on whether the miraculous items continue (open question; BFM silent). Classical Pentecostal readings link "tongues" here with the sign of Spirit baptism while distinguishing purpose (AG, section 8).

**Links.** The church and its calling (28) for 12:12–31 and chapter 14; What Scripture promises the sick (45) for healing; 1 Corinthians 12:13 quoted from 28's section.

## Romans 12:3–8 (owned)

**Context.** After 12:1–2 (owned by A new creation, 01), Paul turns to humility and the use of gifts in one body.

**Main readings.**
- *"In proportion to his faith" (12:6):* either the prophet's own measure of trust, or "the faith" as the body of truth against which prophecy is checked; commentators divide. The study does not develop it.
- *Ordinary and extraordinary gifts side by side:* no ranking; no list complete.

**Links.** A new creation (01); Faith in daily life (05) for 12:9 onward; The church and its calling (28).
