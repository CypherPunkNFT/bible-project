# The Holy Spirit

**Study 23 · God and Christian Faith · First content draft · 2026-10-13**

Jesus promises the Spirit to disciples facing his departure. Acts describes the Spirit's coming at Pentecost and Peter's explanation of what the crowd sees and hears. The letters speak of new life, belonging to Christ, help in prayer, gifts for the body, and a changed way of living.

Read these themes together. The Spirit's work is not confined to a striking experience, nor reduced to an inward feeling. These passages connect his presence with the witness to Jesus, the life of God's children, and the good of others. Where Christians differ over how passages apply today, distinguish the text's statements from the interpretation being proposed.

## 01 The promise of the Holy Ghost

**Main readings:** John 14:15–26; Acts 2:1–21, 32–42.

### He shall teach you

Jesus promises to ask the Father for another Comforter who will remain with the disciples. He names the Spirit of truth and speaks of his dwelling with and in them. The promise is given within teaching about loving Jesus, keeping his commandments, and not being left comfortless.

John 14:26 identifies the Comforter as the Holy Ghost, sent by the Father in Jesus' name. His work includes teaching and bringing Jesus' words to the disciples' remembrance. The immediate hearers have received those words from Jesus himself. Attend to that setting before treating the promise as a guarantee that any later person's impression must be God's instruction.

The promise keeps the disciples' attention on Jesus and the Father. The Spirit's presence is not introduced as a rival source of teaching that makes Jesus' words unnecessary.

### This Jesus hath God raised up

At Pentecost, Acts describes a sound like a mighty wind, divided tongues like fire, and speech in other tongues as the Spirit gives utterance. The gathered hearers recognise their own languages and hear God's wonderful works. Amazement and mockery prompt Peter's explanation.

Peter cites Joel's promise of the outpouring and then identifies the exalted Jesus as the one who has poured out what the crowd sees and hears. The event is explained through Scripture and the proclamation of Jesus' resurrection and lordship. The listeners are called to repent and be baptized, with the gift of the Holy Ghost promised; those receiving the word continue in teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, and prayers.

Keep the event and its explanation together. The sounds and speech do not exhaust Pentecost's meaning. Peter directs the crowd to Jesus and calls for a response that leads into a shared life.

**Consider:** How does the promise in John concern Jesus' words? How does Peter explain the event rather than merely describe its effects?

**Reflect:** Consider whether your attention to the Spirit's work is leading you toward the witness to Christ and the shared practices named in Acts.

## 02 Born of the Spirit

**Main reading:** John 3:1–8.

### Ye must be born again

Nicodemus comes to Jesus acknowledging him as a teacher whose signs indicate God's presence. Jesus answers by speaking of the birth necessary to see God's kingdom. Nicodemus asks how a grown person can be born; Jesus speaks of water and the Spirit.

> That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.

John 3:6 · KJV

The distinction challenges the assumption that standing, knowledge, or natural descent already supplies everything needed. Jesus does not tell Nicodemus simply to add another lesson to his accomplishments. He names a birth that must come from the Spirit.

The conversation gives a claim before it supplies an explanation that Nicodemus can master. His question is taken seriously, but his existing categories are not allowed to determine the limits of Jesus' answer.

### The wind bloweth where it listeth

Jesus compares the person born of the Spirit with the wind, whose sound can be heard though its origin and destination are not grasped by the hearer. The comparison points to an activity that is real without being at the listener's command. It is not an instruction for identifying new birth by an outward display that the passage itself never specifies.

The phrase “of water and of the Spirit” should remain visible. A baptismal reading and readings that understand water as cleansing imagery require explanation; this introductory paragraph does not settle their differences by silently omitting water or inserting a complete sacramental account. The explicit demand is new birth, and the Spirit is named as its source.

Read the scene as instruction to Nicodemus before turning it into a description of how every person's experience must feel. The text gives no required emotional sequence or age at which this birth must occur.

**Consider:** How does Jesus' answer go beyond Nicodemus's opening recognition of a teacher? What does the wind comparison explain, and what does it leave undescribed?

**Reflect:** Consider whether you have confused familiarity with religious language with receiving the life Jesus says is needed.

## 03 The Spirit of adoption

**Main reading:** Romans 8:1–27.

### Abba, Father

Romans connects the Spirit's indwelling with belonging to Christ. It speaks of the Spirit of God and the Spirit of Christ, of life, and of the future quickening of mortal bodies. The response is not to live as debtors to the flesh, but through the Spirit to put its deeds to death.

Being led by the Spirit appears in that context of life and conduct. Paul then contrasts bondage and fear with adoption, in which believers cry to God as Father.

> The Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God:

Romans 8:16 · KJV

The assurance belongs to a larger account of belonging, living, and inheritance. It should not be detached into a demand to produce one distinctive feeling on command. Paul immediately connects being heirs with suffering with Christ and future glory.

### Helpeth our infirmities

The chapter does not treat possession of the Spirit as the end of every present difficulty. Believers who have the firstfruits of the Spirit still groan while awaiting bodily redemption. Hope includes patient waiting for what is not yet seen.

The Spirit also helps when believers do not know how to pray as they ought. Paul speaks of intercession and of God knowing the Spirit's mind, with intercession according to God's will. Weakness in prayer is therefore named within the account of divine help, not as proof that the believer has been abandoned.

The passage holds assurance, changed conduct, suffering, hope, and prayer together. Its comfort is greater than a promise of constant emotional confidence: it speaks of God's work amid need and of a future that includes the body.

**Consider:** What surrounds the statement about the Spirit's witness? What difficulties remain even for those who have the firstfruits of the Spirit?

**Reflect:** Consider how this passage permits honest acknowledgement of weakness while directing you toward the Father's care and promised future.

## 04 Diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit

**Main readings:** 1 Corinthians 12–13; 14:1–5, 26–33, 39–40.

### To profit withal

Paul begins with the confession of Jesus as Lord, then describes different gifts, services, and workings associated with the same Spirit, Lord, and God. The manifestation of the Spirit is given for benefit beyond the recipient. The Spirit distributes as he wills.

The body image explains why difference is necessary. A foot need not become a hand to belong, and an eye cannot declare that it needs no hand. Members regarded as weaker remain necessary. The intended result is shared care, with suffering and honour affecting the whole body.

The questions at the chapter's end resist uniformity: not all have the same function or gift. The following chapter makes love indispensable even where impressive speech, knowledge, faith, or giving is claimed. Gifts cannot excuse the absence of the very love the community is called to practise.

### Let all things be done unto edifying

Chapter 14 prioritises the building up of the church. Paul instructs that tongues in the assembly be interpreted, that speakers take turns, and that prophecy be weighed. He calls for order while also telling the Corinthians not to forbid tongues. Desire and discernment appear together.

Christians differ over the continuation and application of these gifts. The Assemblies of God teaches Spirit baptism as distinct from and subsequent to new birth, with tongues as its initial physical evidence; it distinguishes that use from the congregational gift. See its [Statement of Fundamental Truths, sections 7–8](https://ag.org/Beliefs/Statement-of-Fundamental-truths). An Orthodox Presbyterian committee report instead argues that prophecy and tongues belonged with the apostolic foundation and ceased with the apostolic age. See its [report on the baptism and gifts of the Holy Spirit](https://opc.org/GA/giftsHS.html).

These are two attributed positions, not all Christian views. The shared reading task remains to explain the actual passages, including love, diversity, intelligibility, judgment of what is said, and the community's good. A claimed gift does not make those instructions irrelevant.

**Consider:** How does the body image address both inferiority and self-sufficiency? What does chapter 14 require other people to be able to understand and assess?

**Reflect:** Consider whether the way you value a contribution is shaped more by its visibility or by the good it does for others.

## 05 The fruit of the Spirit

**Main reading:** Galatians 5:13–26.

### By love serve one another

Paul addresses people called to liberty, warning against using it as an occasion for the flesh. Freedom is directed toward serving one another in love. The warning about biting and devouring makes the community's destructive conduct part of the setting for what follows.

The works of the flesh include sexual wrongdoing and idolatry, but also hatred, wrath, strife, divisions, and envy. The warning cannot be confined to the faults a reader most readily sees in outsiders. Conduct that damages relationships within the community also belongs to the list.

Paul places the flesh and Spirit in opposition and warns about the kingdom of God. Read the seriousness of this warning alongside the positive account of what the Spirit produces. The passage is not merely a personality checklist for deciding which qualities feel natural to someone.

### Love, joy, peace

> But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith,

Galatians 5:22 · KJV

The sentence continues with meekness and temperance, and the declaration that there is no law against such things. These qualities give concrete content to life shaped by the Spirit. Patience, gentleness, and self-control matter alongside what may be more immediately celebrated.

The chapter returns to the danger of vainglory, provoking, and envying. The fruit is therefore relevant to the conflict with which the paragraph began. It is not an ornamental list detached from how people speak to and treat one another.

The passage names this fruit as the Spirit's, while still addressing commands to the readers. Dependence and responsibility are not alternatives. The next reading follows how the call to walk becomes visible when someone fails, when a burden is heavy, and when doing good becomes tiring.

**Consider:** Which listed works concern damaged relationships? How do the closing warnings connect the fruit with the paragraph's opening?

**Reflect:** Choose one named quality and consider what it would look like in an interaction where you are usually impatient or defensive.

## 06 Let us also walk in the Spirit

**Main reading:** Galatians 5:24–6:10.

### Restore such an one

> If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit.

Galatians 5:25 · KJV

The appeal joins received life to continuing conduct. Paul immediately warns against rivalry, then describes how those who are spiritual should respond to a person overtaken in a fault: restore with meekness while considering their own vulnerability to temptation.

This is a searching description of spiritual conduct. The person helping is not given permission to be harsh on the ground of being spiritually superior. Meekness, self-watchfulness, and the restoration of the other person shape the task.

Bearing one another's burdens is followed by a warning against self-deception and an instruction to examine one's own work. Mutual help does not erase personal responsibility; personal responsibility does not excuse indifference to another person's burden. Both appear in the argument.

### Be not weary in well doing

Paul uses sowing and reaping to distinguish investing life in the flesh from sowing to the Spirit. He then encourages perseverance:

> And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not.

Galatians 6:9 · KJV

The next verse gives the activity a clear direction: doing good to all as opportunity allows, with particular attention to the household of faith. The sowing image is not presented as a technique for obtaining financial returns through a gift. Its context is a life of responsibility, generosity, and continued good.

Walking by the Spirit thus reaches into how believers restore, carry, examine, share, and persevere. It cannot be measured solely by the intensity of an experience or the prominence of a claimed gift. The commands ask what kind of life follows the claim to live in the Spirit.

**Consider:** What conduct does Paul expect from those called spiritual? How do mutual help and personal responsibility fit together?

**Reflect:** Identify a modest act of restoration, burden-bearing, or good that you can continue without needing it to attract attention.

The promise leads toward witness to Jesus, the new birth toward life, adoption toward prayer and hope, and gifts toward the body's good. The fruit and the walk bring these themes into daily relationships. Keep the Spirit's work and the passages' calls to respond together.
