# The Holy Spirit

Concepts: Promised · Poured out · Living within · Given for others

Motto: Wind you cannot see. / A light that falls on Jesus.

Lead: On the night before He died, Jesus promised His friends that someone would come to stand beside them in His place. This study reads that promise, the day it was kept, and what the letters say the Holy Spirit does in an ordinary believer's life. It teaches one habit: when a passage speaks of the Spirit, ask where He is pointing.

> He will glorify Me by taking from what is Mine and disclosing it to you.

John 16:14 · BSB

Many people come to the Holy Spirit looking for an experience to have, a feeling to wait for, or a power to use. Jesus describes Him differently. The Spirit does not speak "on His own" (John 16:13). He takes what belongs to Jesus and shows it to us. The Anglican writer J. I. Packer pictured this as a floodlight. A floodlight is not meant to be looked at. It is meant to light up a building so you see the building. [check: wording and page of Packer's floodlight picture in *Keep in Step with the Spirit*, 1984]

So this study asks one question of every passage: **where is the Spirit pointing?** In these readings He points to Jesus, to new life in a person who was dead toward God, and outward to other people. If someone's account of the Spirit points mainly at itself, that is worth noticing.

First, the name. The Bible teaches that there is one God, and that this one God is Father, Son and Holy Spirit. Christians call this the Trinity. The Holy Spirit is not a force, a mood or a part of God. He is God, present and at work. The confession this site writes under, the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 (the Southern Baptist Convention's statement of faith), says so in article II (God): the Spirit is "the Spirit of God, fully divine". [check: wording of article II C at bfm.sbc.net] The site's article [The Trinity](/study/theology-articles/the-trinity) teaches this fully.

This is a selection, not a census. These eight passages were chosen because each gives a full paragraph to one part of the Spirit's work: the promise (John 14 and 16), the day He came (Acts 2), new birth (Ezekiel 36), His home in the believer (Romans 8), the life He grows (Galatians 5) and the gifts He gives (1 Corinthians 12 and Romans 12). Much is left out. The Spirit at creation (Genesis 1:2), the Spirit coming on Israel's judges and kings, the rest of Acts, the Spirit as a seal in Ephesians, and John's tests of what is true are not walked here. Other studies read some of them, and they are linked where they matter. This study reads Scripture. It is not medical or professional guidance.

## 01 Another Advocate: the Spirit promised

**Main readings:** John 14:15–26; John 16:7–15

Jesus is leaving. He tells His friends this is good news.

The setting is the last evening before the cross. Jesus has washed His disciples' feet and told them He is going away (John 13–17). They are frightened. Into that fear He makes a promise: the Father "will give you another Advocate to be with you forever" (John 14:16).

An *Advocate* is someone called to stand beside you and speak for you, as a helper does in a court. The word *another* matters. Jesus has been their helper for three years. The Spirit will be another of the same kind, not a lesser replacement. And He will not leave: "to be with you forever".

### At home in you

Jesus calls Him "the Spirit of truth" and says, "He abides with you and will be in you" (John 14:17). Then comes one of the tenderest lines in the Gospels: "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you" (John 14:18). A few verses later the promise widens. Of the one who loves Him, Jesus says, "My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make Our home with him" (John 14:23).

Look at who is speaking and who is promised: the Father gives, the Son asks, the Spirit comes, and where the Spirit makes His home, Father and Son are at home too. This is one of the places where the teaching of the Trinity comes from. It was not invented later; it was spoken at a supper table.

The promise also sits inside a sentence about love and obedience: "If you love Me, you will keep My commandments" (John 14:15). The Spirit does not replace following Jesus. He comes to those who follow.

### He will remind you

> But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in My name, will teach you all things and will remind you of everything I have told you.

John 14:26 · BSB

Notice who was in the room. Jesus said this first to the apostles, the men who had heard Him teach. The Spirit would remind *them* of everything He had told them. Christians have long seen in this verse, and in John 16:13, the promise behind the New Testament itself: the apostles would remember and write truly. The Bible says the same of the older prophets.

> For no such prophecy was ever brought forth by the will of man, but men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit.

2 Peter 1:21 · BSB

The confession says it plainly in article II: the Spirit "inspired holy men of old to write the Scriptures". To *inspire* here means more than to encourage. It means God the Spirit so moved the writers that what they wrote is what God said. This site holds that, with article I (the Scriptures), and the study *What the Bible says about itself* reads it in full.

The limit of the passage matters here. John 14:26 is not a promise that every thought that comes into a Christian's head is the Spirit teaching. The Spirit reminds of what Jesus *told*. So any impression, dream or "word" a person believes they have received is to be tested against what Jesus said, which we now have in Scripture. A message that contradicts Scripture does not come from the Spirit who wrote it.

### Why leaving was better

"It is for your benefit that I am going away" (John 16:7). The disciples must have found this hard to believe. While Jesus was on earth He was in one place. The Spirit would be with every believer, everywhere, at once.

Jesus names what the Spirit will do toward people who do not yet believe: "He will convict the world in regard to sin and righteousness and judgment" (John 16:8). To *convict* means to show someone the truth about their guilt, so they see it and cannot argue it away. The sin is named exactly: "because they do not believe in Me" (John 16:9). When a person who never cared about Jesus suddenly sees their need of Him, this passage says who is at work.

Then the Spirit's work with believers: "He will guide you into all truth. For He will not speak on His own, but He will speak what He hears" (John 16:13). And the verse this study opens with: "He will glorify Me" (John 16:14). Here is the habit in Jesus' own words. The Spirit points to Jesus.

**Consider:** What does the word *another* in John 14:16 tell you about the Spirit? According to John 14:26 and 16:14, what does the Spirit teach, and to whom does He draw attention?

**Reflect:** Is there an impression or feeling you have treated as God's voice without testing it against what Jesus actually said? What would testing it look like?

## 02 Poured out at Pentecost

**Main readings:** Acts 2:1–21

Pilgrims from many nations hear Galileans speaking their own languages. Some ask what it means. Some laugh.

### The festival first, in Jewish terms

*Pentecost* is the Greek name, meaning "fiftieth", for a Jewish festival called Shavuot, the Feast of Weeks. The law of Moses told Israel to count seven weeks from the time of Passover and then to hold a feast of the first fruits of the wheat harvest (Leviticus 23:15–21; Deuteronomy 16:9–12). It was one of the three festivals when Jewish men came up to Jerusalem, which is why the city was full of Jews from abroad. Jewish prayer today also calls Shavuot "the season of the giving of our Torah", remembering the law given at Sinai; how early that link was made is a matter scholars discuss. [check: earliest source linking Shavuot with the giving of the Torah] Jewish communities still keep Shavuot, reading the book of Ruth and the giving of the Ten Commandments. [check: synagogue readings for Shavuot]

That is the day on which Acts tells its story. The first Christians were all Jews, and they were in Jerusalem keeping their own festival.

### What happened

"They were all together in one place" (Acts 2:1). Then "a sound like a mighty rushing wind", and "tongues like flames of fire" coming to rest on each of them (Acts 2:2–3). Luke writes *like*. He describes what it seemed to be, without claiming more.

> And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit enabled them.

Acts 2:4 · BSB

*Tongues* here means languages. The next verses make that plain: "each one heard them speaking his own language", and the visitors list their homelands, from Parthia to Rome to Arabia, and say, "we hear them declaring the wonders of God in our own tongues!" (Acts 2:6, 9–11). The study *Genesis 1–11: the questions readers bring* reads these verses beside the tower of Babel, where languages divided people.

Then come the two reactions people still have to God's work: "What does this mean?" and "They are drunk on new wine!" (Acts 2:12–13).

### What it means

Peter answers from Scripture. These men are not drunk; "It is only the third hour of the day!", about nine in the morning (Acts 2:15). "This is what was spoken by the prophet Joel" (Acts 2:16). God had promised, "I will pour out My Spirit on all people" (Acts 2:17).

*All people* is the surprise. In Israel's history the Spirit came on chosen leaders: prophets, judges, kings. Joel promised Him to "sons and daughters", young and old, and "Even on My menservants and maidservants" (Acts 2:17–18). No one is too young, too old, too poor or too low.

> And everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.’

Acts 2:21 · BSB

Peter goes on to preach Jesus, risen and exalted, as the one who has poured out the Spirit. When the crowd asks what to do, he answers:

> Peter replied, “Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

Acts 2:38 · BSB

To *repent* is to turn: from sin and from going your own way, to God. *Baptism* is being put under water as a public sign of belonging to Jesus. The rest of Peter's sermon, and how a person is made right with God, are read in *The good news: how a person is made right with God*. Read more there.

Where is the Spirit pointing at Pentecost? The wind and fire draw a crowd, but the Spirit's gift of languages is used to declare "the wonders of God", and Peter's explanation leads to Jesus.

[care] Two limits. First, Pentecost happened once, as the fulfilment of Joel's promise at the start of the church; Acts nowhere tells readers to expect the same wind and fire. What later believers should expect is taken up in the questions below. Second, Joel's "your daughters will prophesy" shows women speaking God's word, and the New Testament honours that. It does not address the office of pastor, which the confession limits to men (article VI). *The church and its calling* takes that question up.

**Consider:** Why were people from so many nations in Jerusalem that day? How does Peter explain what the crowd saw and heard?

**Reflect:** Joel's promise reaches "all people". Is there anyone you have quietly assumed God would not want to fill with His Spirit, including yourself?

## 03 A new heart: being born again

**Main readings:** Ezekiel 36:25–27

A prophet promises exiles a new heart. Centuries later a teacher of Israel hears it again.

### A promise to Israel in exile

Ezekiel was a priest carried off to Babylon with his people. Jerusalem had fallen. In chapter 36 God promises to bring Israel home, for the sake of His own name, and to change them from within.

> I will also sprinkle clean water on you, and you will be clean. I will cleanse you from all your impurities and all your idols. I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit within you; I will remove your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh. And I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk in My statutes and to carefully observe My ordinances.

Ezekiel 36:25–27 · BSB

Jewish readers hear this first as God's promise to Israel: a people cleansed, given a new heart and returned to their land. The passage is read in synagogues on the Sabbath before Passover season called Shabbat Parah, beside the law of the red heifer whose ashes made the water for cleansing (Numbers 19). [check: the haftarah range for Shabbat Parah and its customs] Sprinkled water, in Ezekiel's world, means being made clean enough to come near God.

A plain-words note: *ordinances* in verse 27 means God's rules for living. It is not the church word *ordinance*, which Baptists use for baptism and the Lord's Supper.

Look at the order in verse 27. God does not say, "Obey, and then I will give you My Spirit." He says, "I will put My Spirit within you and cause you to walk." The obedience comes from the gift.

### Born of water and the Spirit

The Christian reading hears this promise taken up by Jesus. A respected teacher named Nicodemus comes to Him at night, and Jesus tells him he must be born again.

> Jesus answered, “Truly, truly, I tell you, no one can enter the kingdom of God unless he is born of water and the Spirit.

John 3:5 · BSB

Nicodemus was "the teacher of Israel" (John 3:10), and Jesus expected him to understand. Many readers think he was meant to hear Ezekiel: water that cleanses and a Spirit who gives a new heart. Being *born again*, or *regeneration*, means that God gives new life to a person who was spiritually dead. It is not self-improvement. You did not arrange your first birth, and you do not arrange this one. The confession says the Spirit "effects regeneration" (article II). [check: wording]

> The wind blows where it wishes. You hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit.”

John 3:8 · BSB

You cannot see wind, steer it or schedule it. You see what it moves. The new birth works the same way. No feeling, age or sequence of steps is required in the passage. What can be seen is its effect: a person who now trusts Jesus and begins to walk differently.

The full conversation belongs to *Encounters with Jesus*. Read more there.

[other] What does *water* mean in John 3:5? Readers differ. Many Baptists hear Ezekiel's cleansing water, so that "water and the Spirit" describes one new birth. Others take water as natural birth. The Roman Catholic Church and Lutheran churches teach that the water is baptism, and that God gives new birth through it; that teaching is theirs. This site holds with article VII (baptism and the Lord's Supper) that baptism is "an act of obedience symbolizing the believer's faith": it pictures the new birth and does not cause it. *Baptism and the Lord's Supper: the two things Jesus told His church to do* sets out the reasons. What changes after new birth is read in *A new creation: what changes when you believe*.

[open] Ezekiel's promise was spoken to Israel, and the New Testament speaks of a new *covenant* (a binding promise God makes with His people). How God's promises to Israel relate to the church is a question on which Baptists differ, and this page does not choose. *Covenants and promises* shows the main Baptist views.

**Consider:** In Ezekiel 36:27, which comes first, the Spirit or the obedience? What does Jesus' picture of the wind explain, and what does it leave unexplained?

**Reflect:** Have you been trying to give yourself a new heart by effort? What would it mean to ask God for one?

## 04 The Spirit who lives in you

**Main readings:** Romans 8:1–17

No condemnation. A new obligation. A new name to call God.

### Free, and belonging

Paul has spent seven chapters on sin, God's law and the death of Jesus. Then:

> Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

Romans 8:1 · BSB

*Condemnation* is the judge's sentence of guilt. For those joined to Christ, it is gone. Paul gives the reason: God sent "His own Son ... as an offering for sin", and "the law of the Spirit of life set you free from the law of sin and death" (Romans 8:2–3). The Son deals with our guilt; the Spirit brings us life. *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* reads what the death of Jesus did.

Paul then sets two ways of living side by side. *The flesh*, in his letters, does not mean the body as such. It means human nature turned away from God and living for itself. "The mind of the flesh is death, but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace" (Romans 8:6).

### Every believer has the Spirit

> You, however, are controlled not by the flesh, but by the Spirit, if the Spirit of God lives in you. And if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Christ.

Romans 8:9 · BSB

This is the heart of what is called the *indwelling* of the Spirit: God the Spirit making His home in a believer. Read the verse slowly. Paul does not divide Christians into those who have the Spirit and those who do not yet. To belong to Christ *is* to have His Spirit. Notice also the two names in one verse: "the Spirit of God" and "the Spirit of Christ". One Spirit, belonging to Father and Son.

And the Spirit's presence reaches the body: God "will also give life to your mortal bodies through His Spirit, who lives in you" (Romans 8:11). The Spirit who lives in a believer now is the promise of the resurrection to come. *The hope to come* reads Romans 8:18–25 and the groaning of creation; read more there.

### An obligation, and a name

"We have an obligation, but it is not to the flesh" (Romans 8:12). Paul does not say the Spirit makes effort unnecessary. He says, "if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live" (Romans 8:13). You do the putting to death, and you do it by the Spirit. Depending on God and working hard are not opposites here.

> For you did not receive a spirit of slavery that returns you to fear, but you received the Spirit of sonship, by whom we cry, “Abba! Father!” The Spirit Himself testifies with our spirit that we are God’s children.

Romans 8:15–16 · BSB

*Sonship*, often called adoption, means being brought into God's family with the full rights of a child. In Paul's world a son was the heir; here every believer, man or woman, is made an heir (Romans 8:16–17). *Abba* is the Aramaic word a child used for a father, the word Jesus Himself prayed in Gethsemane (Mark 14:36). The Spirit teaches a frightened person to call God by that name.

[care] Verse 16 is not a demand to produce a certain feeling. Some believers feel God's nearness strongly; others, for long seasons, hardly at all. The Spirit's witness here is joined to crying out to God as Father, and to a life no longer ruled by the flesh. Paul goes straight on to "if indeed we suffer with Him, so that we may also be glorified with Him" (Romans 8:17). Having the Spirit does not end hardship. A few verses on, Paul says the Spirit helps us in our weakness when we do not know how to pray (Romans 8:26). *Chosen and kept: can I be sure?* takes up assurance at length.

**Consider:** According to Romans 8:9, who has the Spirit? In verse 13, what does the believer do, and what does the Spirit do?

**Reflect:** When you pray, do you come to God more like a slave afraid of punishment, or a child calling a father? What would change if you believed verse 15?

## 05 The fruit of the Spirit

**Main readings:** Galatians 5:16–26

A church is biting itself. Paul shows it what the Spirit grows instead.

The churches in Galatia were arguing about whether non-Jewish believers had to keep the law of Moses. The quarrel had turned ugly: Paul warns them that if they "bite and devour" one another they will be consumed (Galatians 5:15). His answer is not a new list of rules.

"Walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh" (Galatians 5:16). The flesh and the Spirit "are opposed to each other" (Galatians 5:17). Paul does not pretend the struggle is over. He names it.

### What the flesh produces

"The acts of the flesh are obvious" (Galatians 5:19). The list begins where readers expect: sexual immorality, idolatry, sorcery, drunkenness. But look at the middle: "hatred, discord, jealousy, and rage; rivalries, divisions, factions, and envy" (Galatians 5:20–21). Most of the list is about how people treat one another, and much of it can happen inside a church. A reader cannot keep this warning for outsiders.

Paul adds: "those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom of God" (Galatians 5:21). The word is *practice*: a settled way of life, not a single failure. This site holds with article V (God's purpose of grace) that true believers "will never fall away from the state of grace", and that they can still fall into sin and grieve the Spirit. The warning is real, and it is one of the ways God keeps His people from settling into what they were.

### What the Spirit grows

> But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. Against such things there is no law.

Galatians 5:22–23 · BSB

*Fruit* is singular. Paul does not offer nine options to choose from, as if one person could take patience and another joy. It is one harvest with nine sides. And fruit is grown, not manufactured. It takes time, and it comes from what is planted in you, not from straining.

Look at which qualities stand out in a quarrelling church: patience, kindness, gentleness, self-control. The fruit answers the very problem the paragraph began with.

"Those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires. Since we live by the Spirit, let us walk in step with the Spirit" (Galatians 5:24–25). The life is given; the walking is ours, step by step, keeping pace with Him. The paragraph ends where it began, with a warning about life together: "Let us not become conceited, provoking and envying one another" (Galatians 5:26).

The next verses, on restoring a believer who falls and carrying one another's burdens (Galatians 6:1–10), are read in *Life together: belonging to a church*. Read more there.

Where is the Spirit pointing? Toward other people. Every quality on the list is shown toward someone.

**Consider:** How many items on the list of the flesh's acts are about how people treat each other? Why might it matter that *fruit* is singular?

**Reflect:** Choose one of the nine and picture the person you find hardest to be with. What would that quality look like in your next conversation with them?

## 06 Gifts given for the common good

**Main readings:** 1 Corinthians 12:1–11; Romans 12:3–8

Corinth prized the gifts that impressed. Paul asks whom they serve.

A *spiritual gift* is an ability the Spirit gives a believer to serve God and other people. The confession says the Spirit "bestows the spiritual gifts by which they serve God through His church" (article II).

### One Spirit, many gifts

The church in Corinth was rich in gifts and poor in love. Many had come from temples where they were "led astray to mute idols" (1 Corinthians 12:2), and some seem to have valued dramatic experiences for their own sake. Paul begins with a test: "no one can say, “Jesus is Lord,” except by the Holy Spirit" (1 Corinthians 12:3). The first sign of the Spirit is not a display. It is a person who confesses Jesus as Lord.

> There are different gifts, but the same Spirit. There are different ministries, but the same Lord. There are different ways of working, but the same God works all things in all people.

1 Corinthians 12:4–6 · BSB

Spirit, Lord, God: Paul cannot speak about gifts without naming Father, Son and Spirit together.

> Now to each one the manifestation of the Spirit is given for the common good.

1 Corinthians 12:7 · BSB

Two words carry the passage: *each one* and *common good*. Every believer is given something, and no one is given it for themselves. Paul lists a range: a message of wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy (speaking a message from God), distinguishing between spirits, tongues and their interpretation (1 Corinthians 12:8–10). All of them come from the Spirit, "who apportions them to each one as He determines" (1 Corinthians 12:11). The gifts are His to give, not ours to demand. *What Scripture promises the sick* takes up the gifts of healing.

Paul goes on to picture the church as one body with many parts (1 Corinthians 12:12–31), read in *The church and its calling*. Then comes his great chapter on love (1 Corinthians 13), without which, he says, even the greatest gift is nothing.

### Ordinary gifts, used well

Romans 12 makes the same point in a quieter key. It begins with humility: "think of yourself with sober judgment" (Romans 12:3). The church is one body in which "each member belongs to one another" (Romans 12:5). "We have different gifts according to the grace given us" (Romans 12:6). *Grace* means God's undeserved kindness; the gifts are grace, so no one can boast of them.

Then the list: prophecy, serving, teaching, encouraging, giving, leading, "if it is showing mercy, let him do it cheerfully" (Romans 12:8). Prophecy sits beside serving tables and giving money. Paul does not rank them. The gifts no one notices are as much the Spirit's work as the ones that fill a room. No list in the New Testament claims to be complete.

### Built up, and weighed

Paul's longer instructions on gifts in worship come in 1 Corinthians 14, read in *The church and its calling*. Four lines show their direction. "Earnestly pursue love and eagerly desire spiritual gifts" (1 Corinthians 14:1). In the gathering, "All of these must be done to build up the church" (1 Corinthians 14:26). When someone prophesies, "the others should weigh carefully what is said" (1 Corinthians 14:29), because "God is not a God of disorder, but of peace" (1 Corinthians 14:33).

> So, my brothers, be eager to prophesy, and do not forbid speaking in tongues. But everything must be done in a proper and orderly manner.

1 Corinthians 14:39–40 · BSB

Where is the Spirit pointing? Toward the common good. A gift that serves only its owner's reputation has lost its direction.

**Consider:** What two words in 1 Corinthians 12:7 say who receives gifts and what they are for? Which gifts in Romans 12 would rarely be noticed in public?

**Reflect:** What has God given you that could serve someone else this week, even if no one ever thanks you for it?

## 07 Questions readers bring

**Main readings:** 1 Corinthians 12:13; Romans 8:9; John 16:13–14

Five questions, each answered from the passages, with other views shown in their own words.

### Is the Holy Spirit a person, or a power?

The passages speak of Him as a person. He teaches and reminds (John 14:26), hears and speaks (John 16:13), testifies (Romans 8:16), and gives gifts "as He determines" (1 Corinthians 12:11). A force does not decide. The Berean writes *He* throughout, following the way Jesus speaks of Him. And He is named beside the Father and the Son as their equal (John 14:23, 26; 1 Corinthians 12:4–6).

[other] Jehovah's Witnesses teach that the holy spirit is not a person but God's "active force". [check: wording and source in a Watch Tower publication] That teaching is theirs. This site holds with article II that the Spirit is fully God. The site's article [The Trinity](/study/theology-articles/the-trinity) answers this question at length.

### Is being baptized in the Spirit a second experience after becoming a Christian?

[read] This site holds with article II of the Baptist Faith and Message: "At the moment of regeneration He baptizes every believer into the Body of Christ." [check: wording] The plainest verse is this:

> For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, slave or free, and we were all given one Spirit to drink.

1 Corinthians 12:13 · BSB

Paul says *all*, writing to a church with many faults, not to its most spiritual members. With Romans 8:9 ("if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Christ"), Baptists conclude that every believer receives the Spirit at conversion, the moment a person turns from sin and trusts Christ. Believers may later be *filled* with the Spirit many times (Acts 4:31 and Ephesians 5:18 speak of this), but that is not a second entry into belonging. Baptists read Acts 2, and the moments in Acts 8, 10 and 19 when the Spirit came on new groups, as steps in the gospel reaching Jews, Samaritans and the nations, as Jesus said it would (Acts 1:8).

[other] Classical Pentecostal churches teach otherwise, and their teaching should be heard in their own words. The Assemblies of God, the largest of them, teaches in its Statement of Fundamental Truths that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is an experience "distinct from and subsequent to the experience of the new birth" (section 7), and that it is witnessed by "the initial physical sign of speaking with other tongues" (section 8). They read the pattern in Acts as the normal pattern for believers today, and they distinguish this sign from the gift of tongues used in the gathering. [check: current wording of sections 7 and 8 at ag.org] That teaching is theirs. Many Pentecostal believers love Christ deeply, and they are brothers and sisters in Him.

[care] Classical Pentecostal teaching is its own tradition. It is not the same as the "Word of Faith" or prosperity movement, which is a different set of teachers. The two should never be grouped together.

### Do the miraculous gifts continue today?

[open] This is the one question this study leaves open, because the Baptist Faith and Message does not settle it. Article II says the Spirit gives gifts; it does not say whether tongues, prophecy and healing gifts continue as they did in the first churches. Baptists hold more than one view.

Some are *cessationists* (from *cease*, to stop). They hold that those gifts marked the apostles' time, when the New Testament was being given, and stopped when it was complete; God still heals and answers prayer, but not through such gifts. Thomas Schreiner, a Southern Baptist teacher, argues a form of this view in *Spiritual Gifts: What They Are and Why They Matter* (2018). [check: Schreiner's exact position and the publisher] An Orthodox Presbyterian Church committee report, from outside the Baptist family, argues that prophecy and tongues ceased with the apostolic age. [check: title and date of the OPC report]

Others are *continuationists*. They hold that all the gifts may still be given, under Paul's rules in 1 Corinthians 14: love, order and weighing what is said. John Piper, a Baptist pastor, holds this view. [check: a published source to cite for Piper] The Southern Baptist Convention's International Mission Board stopped excluding missionary candidates who practise a private prayer language in 2015. [check: date and wording of the policy change]

No named source is quoted directly here, and this page does not choose. Both sides agree on what the passages require: every gift serves the common good; anything claimed as from God is weighed against Scripture; and love matters more than any gift.

Jesus spoke of blasphemy against the Holy Spirit that will not be forgiven (Matthew 12:31–32; Mark 3:28–30). Read where He said it: to religious leaders who had watched Him free a man from an evil spirit and declared that He did it by the power of Satan (Mark 3:22, 30). It was a settled, open rejection of the Spirit's work in Jesus, calling God's good evil.

Pastors have long observed that people who fear they have committed this sin are almost never the people Jesus was describing, because the fear itself shows a heart that still cares about God. The Spirit's work is to convict and to draw a person to Jesus (John 16:8–9). If you want to come to Christ, He has not shut the door. "Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved" (Acts 2:21). Talk to a pastor you trust. *Fear, anxiety and despair* has more help.

### How can I know the Spirit lives in me?

Not by one feeling, and not by one gift. The passages give signs you can look for: you confess that Jesus is Lord (1 Corinthians 12:3); you cry to God as Father (Romans 8:15); you are at war with the flesh rather than at peace with it (Galatians 5:17); and the fruit, however small, is growing (Galatians 5:22–23). Every one of those points to Jesus, to new life, or to other people. Read more in *Chosen and kept: can I be sure?*

## Following the light

Here is the habit to carry to the next passage. When the Bible speaks of the Spirit, ask where He is pointing. In John He points to Jesus and His words. At Pentecost He points the nations to the wonders of God. In Ezekiel and John 3 He points to a new heart no one can give themselves. In Romans He points a frightened person to a Father. In Galatians He points to the person you find hard to love. In Corinth and Rome He points to the common good.

The Spirit does not ask to be the centre of attention. He asks to show you Jesus.

If you have not yet trusted Christ, you do not need to work up an experience first. The Spirit is already doing His work if you see your need. Turn from your sin and trust Jesus as Lord, because, in the words of article IV (salvation), "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord." Call on His name. Then find a church, and ask to be baptized.
