# The church and its calling passage notes

**Item 28 · First-draft notes · 2026-10-13**

## Primary readings

Read local KJV: 1 Corinthians 12:12–31; Ephesians 4:1–16; Ephesians 2:11–22; 1 Peter 2:4–12; 1 Corinthians 14:1–19, 26–40; 1 Timothy 3:1–16; 1 Peter 5:1–4; Matthew 28:16–20; 1 Corinthians 11:17–34; Acts 1:6–8. The accepted outline's wider 1 Corinthians 11–14 anchor is developed through these selections; gifts receive fuller attention in item 23.

Six full-verse quotations: 1 Corinthians 12:27; Ephesians 2:19; 1 Corinthians 14:40; 1 Peter 5:3; 1 Corinthians 11:26; Acts 1:8. Exposition and applications are editorial writing.

| Division | Context retained |
| --- | --- |
| Body | Different members, both exclusion and self-exclusion challenged, shared suffering and honour; gifts serve maturity under Christ. |
| Household | Gentiles brought near, reconciliation and access; cornerstone, temple, spiritual house, mercy and declaration of praise. |
| Gathering | Understanding, learning and edification guide gifts and order; divisions can make assembly harmful. |
| Leadership | Qualifications address conduct, teaching, testing and reputation; God's flock is served through willing oversight and example. |
| Practices | Baptism within the commission; Supper within correction of humiliating divisions, remembrance and proclamation. |
| Witness | Mercy produces proclamation and honourable conduct; Acts' original commission receives its geographical and narrative setting. |

## Primary doctrinal sources

Consulted 2026-10-13:

- [Baptist Faith and Message 2000](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/), VI–VII: local autonomy, congregational processes, believer's immersion and symbolic remembrance at the Supper. Article VI on the live source records a 2023 amendment. The draft does not treat this statement as every Baptist's position.
- [Westminster Confession, OPC edition](https://opc.org/wcf.html), 28, 29.7 and 31: baptism of professing believers and believers' infants, covenant sign and seal, spiritual feeding on Christ in the Supper, and synods/councils with acknowledgement of their fallibility. The draft summarises the edition consulted.

These examples establish real differences while leaving broader comparison open. No hostile historical wording from either confession is adopted as the editorial voice. Neither comparison represents Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran or other sacramental accounts by implication.

## Interpretive limits

1. **Body:** 1 Corinthians 12 does not make all gifts identical or require every member to have the same manifestation. A full continuation/cessation discussion remains in item 23.
2. **Ministry:** Ephesians 4's list is read for its stated purpose of equipping and growth. The draft does not resolve which offices continue or reconstruct a complete modern staffing plan from the list.
3. **Household and temple:** the images here refer to people joined in Christ. That observation is not a blanket judgment about the usefulness of physical places of worship.
4. **Priesthood:** retain the spiritual-house language without implying it alone settles every tradition's distinction between shared priesthood and ordained ministry.
5. **Gathering:** 1 Corinthians 14's order and intelligibility are not equated with one modern liturgy. Instructions about women in verses 34–35 are acknowledged. Detailed treatment requires the broader context including 1 Corinthians 11 and representative sources; no universal permission/prohibition is silently inferred.
6. **Leadership:** retain bishop, deacon and elder as the passages use them. Do not assume the KJV office word alone establishes a later episcopal, presbyterian or congregational system. 1 Timothy 3:11's wives/women question and other disputed qualification details are not settled.
7. **Non-domination:** 1 Peter's prohibition is part of the account of authority. It must not be weakened into the claim that a title excuses mistreatment.
8. **Baptism:** Matthew's commission names Father, Son and Holy Ghost. Mode, recipients and sacramental efficacy require wider arguments. The two attributed examples are not an exhaustive account.
9. **Supper:** retain Paul's concrete concern for the hungry and humiliated alongside his warnings and institution narrative. Unworthy participation is not paraphrased as needing to earn grace. The full meaning of discerning the body and sacramental presence is not settled by one application.
10. **Acts:** the original addressees are the disciples in the narrative. The draft connects their witness with the church's calling without pretending every later reader was physically present for the resurrection or receives an identical apostolic office.
11. **Reputation and response:** 1 Peter urges good conduct despite accusation. Do not promise that faithful conduct prevents all hostility or guarantees conversions.
