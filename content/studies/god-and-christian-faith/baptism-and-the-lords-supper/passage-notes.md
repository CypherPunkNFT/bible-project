# Passage notes: Baptism and the Lord's Supper (59)

## Passages this study owns

### The Acts baptisms panel: Acts 8:12; 9:17–18; 10:44–48; 16:13–15; 16:25–34; 18:8; 19:1–7 (with 2:41, 8:13 and 8:36–38 quoted or referred to)
- **Context.** Luke's account of the gospel spreading from Jerusalem to Samaria, to a Gentile household in Caesarea, and into Macedonia, Corinth and Ephesus. These are the ten narrated baptisms in Jesus' name (Acts 22:16 is Paul's retelling and is not counted). Carried across from draft 28's water panel.
- **Main readings.** *Believers' baptism* (Baptists; Second London Confession 29.2; New Hampshire Confession 1833; site view under article VII): in each account, hearing, believing or receiving the Spirit comes first. *Household baptism as including infants* (Westminster Confession 28.4; Albert Barnes on Acts 16:15). *Undecided from these texts* (Jamieson, Fausset and Brown on Acts 16:15: "not explicitly stated"; "must be determined on other grounds").
- **Acts 16:25–34 (walked).** The Philippian jailer. Order: the word spoken to the whole household (16:32), wounds washed, baptism "without delay", then the whole household rejoicing that they had come to believe (16:34). Carries a [care] block with crisis lines because of 16:27. The study quotes 16:28, not 16:27, and gives no method detail.
- **Links.** The good news (Acts 2); Christ in the Old Testament (Acts 8:30–35); The church and its calling (draft 28, from which the panel comes).

### Colossians 2:11–12
- **Context.** Paul answering pressure at Colossae to add rules, circumcision among them, to Christ (2:8–23). Verses 13–15 belong to the cross study, and 16–17 to Christ in the Old Testament.
- **Main readings.** *Reformed covenant reading:* baptism replaces circumcision as the covenant sign, so it is given to the infants of believers (Westminster Confession 28.4; Col 2:11–12 is thought to be among its proof-texts, see the open questions). *Baptist reading:* the circumcision meant is "performed by Christ and not by human hands", the inward change; baptism pictures it; the rising is "through your faith" (Second London Confession 29; site view).
- **Links.** A new creation (Romans 6:3–4); The cross, the empty tomb and the throne (Col 2:13–15); Christ in the Old Testament (2:16–17).

### 1 Peter 3:18–22
- **Context.** Suffering for doing right (3:13–17), grounded in Christ who suffered "once for all"; Noah's eight saved through water as a picture of baptism.
- **Main readings of 3:21.** *Baptist:* the water symbolizes; what saves is the pledge of a clear conscience, through the resurrection, received by faith (site view). *Sacramental:* baptism is a means of grace that forgives and regenerates (Catechism of the Catholic Church 1213; Luther's Small Catechism; Orthodox teaching).
- **3:19, "spirits in prison" (open).** Christ preaching in Noah's day through Noah (Augustine, Letter 164; Wayne Grudem). Christ proclaiming victory to fallen spirits (William Dalton). Other readings exist. Not settled here.
- **Links.** Saved by grace; Angels, demons and spiritual powers; The cross, the empty tomb and the throne.

### Mark 14:12–26
- **Context.** The Passover meal in the upper room on the night of the betrayal. Owned here; the cross study drops draft 19's reading of it.
- **Main readings.** Mark presents the meal as a Passover (14:12). "Blood of the covenant" echoes Exodus 24:8. The hymn (14:26) is commonly taken as the Hallel psalms (113–118), and the study marks that as an inference. John's apparently different timing is noted as an open question. The Passover is described first in Jewish terms (Exodus 12, the seder, Mishnah Pesachim 10:5); the Christian reading of Christ as the Passover lamb (1 Corinthians 5:7, referred to, not quoted) is labelled as Christian. No guilt for Jesus' death is laid on the Jewish people.
- **Links.** Covenants and promises (Luke 22:14–20); Christ in the Old Testament (Exodus 12).

### 1 Corinthians 11:23–34
- **Context.** Paul's correction of the divided meal at Corinth. Verses 17–22 belong to draft 28; this study walks 23–34.
- **Main readings.** *Memorial that looks forward* (article VII; site view). *"Unworthy manner"* read in the light of the shaming of the poor (11:21–22, 33). *"Recognizing the body"* (11:29): the Supper told apart from an ordinary meal (Albert Barnes); also the church as the body (this study's reading, with 10:17, labelled as its own). *11:30*: a [care] note that illness is not to be read as punishment, and no medical advice.
- **Presence views shown:** Roman Catholic (Council of Trent, session 13, 1551); Lutheran (Small Catechism; Formula of Concord); Reformed (Westminster Confession 29.7); Orthodox (manner not defined). Baptist answer from "remembrance", "this bread" (11:26) and "once for all" (1 Peter 3:18).
- **Links.** The church and its calling (11:17–22); What Scripture promises the sick; Life together; Chosen and kept.

### 1 Corinthians 10:16–17
- **Context.** A warning against sharing in idol feasts (10:14–22).
- **Main readings.** "Participation" (koinōnia, fellowship or sharing): some Baptists, and the Second London Confession 30.7, speak of a spiritual feeding by faith; others hold to remembrance alone; article VII says only "memorialize" and "anticipate". Verse 17, one loaf and one body, supports the study's reading of 11:29.
- **Links.** The church and its calling.
