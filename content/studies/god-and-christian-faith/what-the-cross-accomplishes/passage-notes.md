# Passage notes: The cross, the empty tomb and the throne (25)

Rewrite of draft 25 (What the cross accomplishes), taking in draft 19 (The cross and resurrection). All quotations re-quoted from the Berean Standard Bible text supplied. None of draft 19's readings of the Last Supper (Mark 14:12–31, Luke 22:14–20), Gethsemane (Mark 14:32–52), the trial and death (Mark 14:53–15:41, John 19), the burial (Mark 15:42–16:8, John 19:38–20:10), Emmaus (Luke 24:13–35, 44–49) or John 20 is carried over. Draft 25's readings of Hebrews 9:1–10:25, 1 Peter 1:13–21, 2 Corinthians 5:14–17, Ephesians 2:11–22, Isaiah 53:1–12, Colossians 2:8–12 and 1 John 4:7–12 are narrowed to the verses this study owns or quotes.

## Hebrews 9:15–28 (part 01)
**Context.** The centre of Hebrews' argument comparing the first covenant's sanctuary with Christ's priesthood (Hebrews 8–10). Verses 7–14 (the Day of Atonement and the conscience) belong to *Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice* (47).
**Readings.** The Greek *diathēkē* is read as "covenant" throughout by some interpreters, and as "will" in verses 16–17 by most English versions, the BSB included. "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness" is read by Protestant and Catholic interpreters alike as grounding forgiveness in Christ's death. The Jewish observance of Yom Kippur is described first, in Jewish terms (fasting, prayer, *teshuvah*), before the Christian reading.
**Links.** *Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice* (47); *Covenants and promises* (26), for Exodus 24; *Grace, faith and salvation* (24), for Romans 3:25–26; *Christ in the Old Testament* (41).

## Mark 10:35–45 (part 02)
**Context.** The third prediction of the Passion (10:32–34) is followed by the request of James and John; Bartimaeus (10:46–52) follows and belongs to *Encounters with Jesus* (14).
**Readings.** The ransom saying is read as a substitutionary payment (in the Reformed and Baptist line), as a liberation from bondage (in the victory tradition) and, by some early writers, as a price paid to the devil. The text names no recipient, and the study does not supply one. "Many" is read by Calvinist Baptists as the elect, and by others as a Semitic way of saying "all", over against the one.
**Links.** *Covenants and promises* (26), for Galatians 3:13; *Following Jesus* (16).

## 2 Corinthians 5:18–21 (part 03)
**Context.** Paul's defence of his ministry; 5:14–17 belongs to *A new creation* (01).
**Readings.** Verse 21 is read by Reformation and Baptist interpreters as a double imputation (our sin counted to Christ, his righteousness counted to us). Some read "to be sin" as "to be a sin offering". N. T. Wright reads "the righteousness of God" as Paul's apostolic embodiment of God's covenant faithfulness [check: source before citing]. The study presents the exchange reading in the site's voice and does not cite Wright.
**Links.** *A new creation* (01); *The church and its calling* (28), for Ephesians 2:14–16.

## 1 Peter 2:21–25 with Isaiah 53:4–6 (part 04)
**Context.** Instructions to household slaves (2:18–20). Peter quotes or echoes Isaiah 53:9, 5 and 6. Isaiah 52:13–53:12 as a whole belongs to *Christ in the Old Testament* (41) (pair: 41 reads how the apostles read the servant; this study reads Christ in our place).
**Readings.** The Jewish reading (the servant as Israel; the "we" as the nations' kings; Rashi and others) is named first. The Christian reading follows the New Testament (Acts 8; 1 Peter 2). Substitution is taught in the site's voice (BFM 2000 article II, "substitutionary death"; Second London Confession 8.5). On healing "in the atonement", the Assemblies of God's Statement of Fundamental Truths is quoted as a classical Pentecostal view, with no grouping with any other movement. The site's view is that there is no promise of healing in this life.
**Links.** *Christ in the Old Testament* (41); *What Scripture promises the sick* (45), which quotes 53:4–5; *Faith under pressure* (03); the apologetics answer on the cross.

## Colossians 2:13–15 (part 05)
**Context.** Follows the baptism image (2:11–12, owned by the baptism study) and precedes 2:16–17 (41).
**Readings.** The "debt ascribed to us in the decrees" is read as the law's charge against sinners (most Protestant interpreters), or as a record of debt in general. The "powers and authorities" are read as demonic powers (most) or as human and structural powers too. Aulén's *Christus Victor* (1931) is named as the classic statement of the victory theme.
**Links.** *Angels, demons and spiritual powers* (53), which quotes 2:13–15.

## Hebrews 2:9–18 (part 05)
**Context.** Psalm 8 is read of Jesus (2:5–9); the Son's solidarity with his "brothers".
**Readings.** "Taste death for everyone" (2:9) is central to the extent-of-the-atonement question (part 06). Verse 17's "make atonement" (BSB) is the *hilaskomai* word family, read as propitiation by some and as expiation by others.
**Links.** *Who do you say that I am* (11), which quotes 2:14–18; *Angels, demons and spiritual powers* (53), which quotes 2:14–15.

## Romans 5:6–11 (part 06)
**Context.** Between 5:1–5 (*Faith under pressure*, 03) and 5:12–21 (*Humanity, sin and renewal*, 29).
**Readings.** Love and justification are held together. Abelard is named for the moral-influence emphasis, with a check on the source. The extent question is shown without choosing (Calvinist and non-Calvinist Baptists; BFM articles IV and V).
**Links.** *Knowing God* (21), for 1 John 4:9–10; *Faith under pressure* (03); *Humanity, sin and renewal* (29); *Chosen and kept*.

## Luke 24:1–12 and 24:36–43 (part 07)
**Context.** Luke's resurrection chapter. 24:13–35 and 44–49 belong to *Scripture and its witness* (27).
**Readings.** A bodily resurrection is read by all historic Christian traditions. Differences of detail between the Gospels (one young man or two men) are noted, with no harmonised timetable imposed. Verse 12 is absent from a few Western witnesses [check: whether to mention this; the BSB prints it].
**Links.** *Women of the Bible* (46), for John 20:1–18; *Scripture and its witness* (27); the apologetics answers on the resurrection.

## 1 Corinthians 15:1–11 (part 08)
**Context.** Opens Paul's resurrection chapter. 15:12–58 belongs to *The hope to come* (30) (the editor makes 30's narrowing). *Humanity, sin and renewal* (29) quotes 15:22.
**Readings.** Verses 3–5 are widely read as a summary Paul received from earlier believers. The appearances list is read as testimony that could be checked. "If you hold firmly" is read alongside BFM article V.
**Links.** *The hope to come* (30).

## Philippians 2:9–11 (part 09)
**Context.** The second half of the hymn-like passage. 2:5–8 belongs to *Who do you say that I am* (11).
**Readings.** The passage echoes Isaiah 45:23, applying to Jesus the honour God claims for himself. The study notes that "every knee" does not teach universal salvation (BFM article X).
**Links.** *Who do you say that I am* (11); *The hope to come* (30).

## Hebrews 4:14–16 (part 09)
**Context.** It bridges the warnings of chapters 3–4 and the priesthood argument of chapters 5–10.
**Readings.** "Without sin": Christ was truly tempted and did not sin (this is common to all orthodox traditions). The study links it to the present intercession of 7:25 (owned by 41; quoted here).
**Links.** *Christ in the Old Testament* (41); *Prayer in the letters* (04), for Ephesians 1:20–23; *The hope to come* (30), for Acts 1:9–11.
