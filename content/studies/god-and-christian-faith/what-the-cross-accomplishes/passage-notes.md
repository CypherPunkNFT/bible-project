## Doctrinal sources

Consulted 2026-10-13:

- [Westminster Confession, chapter 8.4–8, published by the Orthodox Presbyterian Church](https://opc.org/wcf.html): willing obedience and offering, satisfaction of divine justice, reconciliation and inheritance for those given to Christ, application of purchased redemption. The draft summarises 8.5, not all Protestant positions.
- [Catholic Catechism, Jesus Died Crucified, paragraphs 613–616](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_two/article_4/paragraph_2_jesus_died_crucified.html): sacrifice, reconciliation, freely offered obedience, substitution, satisfaction and love. This is an attributed Catholic formulation, not a claim that all traditions use these terms identically.

Both sources use several descriptions together. Do not manufacture a simple contrast of a supposedly loveless judicial Protestant account against a supposedly non-sacrificial Catholic account. This introductory comparison is not an exhaustive catalogue of theories. A later expansion on named penal substitution, satisfaction, Christus Victor or moral influence models requires dedicated primary sources and clear definitions rather than attaching the labels mechanically to individual verses.

## Interpretive limits

1. **Sacrifice:** Hebrews' own contrast is about effectiveness, repetition, conscience and access. Do not infer that every Israelite misunderstood worship or that Judaism can be dismissed as an attempt to purchase salvation. Do not settle Eucharistic disagreements from the phrase once for all without reading representative sources.
2. **Propitiation:** retain KJV wording in Romans 3 and 1 John 4. The draft follows their arguments without pretending to settle lexical debates about propitiation, expiation or mercy-seat associations. Any future lexical expansion needs specialist primary research.
3. **Ransom:** Mark 10:45 does not identify a recipient. Do not silently introduce payment to Satan or turn the image into a fully specified commercial transaction.
4. **Scope:** preserve many, all and world within the passages where they occur. This study does not adjudicate definite/unlimited atonement or universal salvation. The appeal to be reconciled is retained alongside God's reconciling action.
5. **Made sin:** 2 Corinthians 5:21 explicitly describes Christ as knowing no sin. The paraphrase must not imply he committed evil. A claim that the phrase simply means sin offering would require further argument and has not been asserted.
6. **Isaiah:** the comparison follows Peter's application of servant language to Christ. It is not a survey of Jewish and Christian readings of Isaiah 53 or a claim that every disputed question about the servant has been resolved.
7. **Suffering and healing:** 1 Peter addresses servants under unjust masters. Keep the setting visible; do not make its exhortation permission for abuse or a demand that victims stay in danger. Its healing language is explained through this paragraph's sins and shepherd imagery, without promising immediate physical recovery.
8. **Victory:** avoid a speculative map of spiritual ranks or an unsourced grammatical resolution of every pronoun in Colossians 2:15. The paragraph connects forgiveness and triumph; Hebrews connects victory through death with help for people still tempted.
9. **Father and Son:** Romans 5 names God's love; 1 John names God's sending of his Son; Hebrews describes Christ offering himself. Do not depict the Father and Son as having opposed saving purposes.
10. **Cross and resurrection:** the scope is Christ's saving death, but the readings themselves include resurrection and present help. Preserve those connections.
