# What the cross accomplishes working notes

**Item 25 · God and Christian Faith · First draft · 2026-10-13**

## Purpose and central question

What do the biblical writers say Christ's death accomplishes, and how do their descriptions relate? Help readers follow the passages before adopting a single explanatory formula. Preserve the accepted six themes and use scriptural wording for their headings.

## Content divisions

1. One sacrifice for sins — Christ's offering, conscience, forgiveness, access; Hebrews 9–10 and Romans 3.
2. A ransom for many — his life given, costly redemption, service and holiness; Mark 10 and 1 Peter 1.
3. Reconciled to God — God's initiative, trespasses, shared access and belonging; 2 Corinthians 5 and Ephesians 2.
4. He bare our sins — saving action and example in the same paragraph; 1 Peter 2 with Isaiah 53.
5. Triumphing over them — forgiveness, hostile powers, death and compassionate help; Colossians 2 and Hebrews 2.
6. Herein is love — God acts for sinners, and the saved are called to love; Romans 5 and 1 John 4.

Each section has two subdivisions, readings, a consideration question and a reflection. These are content divisions, not a site layout.

## Editorial boundaries

The reader-facing draft is in [content.md](content.md); sources and interpretive limits are in [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md). Keep the comparison of named doctrinal sources subordinate to the biblical readings. It illustrates both shared language and differences; it is not a complete history of atonement theology.

Item 19 holds the death/resurrection narrative. Item 24 holds repentance, faith and justification in broader arguments. Item 26 will follow covenants. Avoid duplicating those studies or suggesting that this draft resolves the extent of the atonement, all sacramental questions, or all interpretations of Isaiah's servant.
