## Direction

Teach one habit: every picture of the cross has a home. Ask where the word comes from, what it says Christ did, and where the writer stops pressing it. At the empty tomb the habit changes, because the resurrection is not a picture but a report: ask who saw what, and what they said. The shape is Scripture's own: Paul's four lines in 1 Corinthians 15:3–5 (died, buried, raised, appeared), carried to the throne by Philippians 2:9. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the eight concepts in gold, the title with "and the throne" as its gold italic turn, *What Jesus' death and rising did* under it, the lead, 1 Corinthians 15:3–4, and a drawing of a plain cross on a low hill, a tomb with its stone rolled aside and a throne on its steps, joined by one rising dotted thread. No face or figure of God, of Christ or of anyone. Four computed figures, each with what it means.
- **The way up the page.** The four lines and the throne as five rising steps, each opening its parts. This is the page's contents.
- **01–06 One cross. Six pictures.** The frame, then a window of six circles (temple, slave market, broken friendship, law court, battlefield, family) around one centre, "In our place, for our sins". Part 04's line to the centre is solid. Choosing a picture shows its home, what it says Christ did and where it stops, and opens its whole reading below, one at a time and in the address. "Where the picture stops" is a dashed rule in the reading. On a phone the window becomes a grid of six under the centre line.
- **The turn.** "Not a picture. A report." The three picture questions give way to the two report questions.
- **07–08 An empty tomb. Who saw what.** A register of Luke 24 beside Paul's list, a line joining the same people (Simon and Cephas, the Eleven and the Twelve), and the note that Paul names no women while Luke names them first. Then the two parts, with quotations in the margin on a wide screen.
- **09 The throne.** Isaiah 45:23 beside Philippians 2:10–11 with the shared words marked, then the reading, ending at the throne of grace and the prayer that has not ended.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer" beside 1 Corinthians 15:1–11, a line-art foot, the sources, the way back to God & Christian Faith.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Epistles' gold: seven of the nine parts read the letters, and the page's spine is 1 Corinthians 15. The site's accent gold marks the care notes. Because the two golds are close, the page's distinctness rests on its shapes: the rising steps, the six-circle window, the turn, the register and the echo.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: Christ's substitutionary death (article II) is stated plainly as the centre among the pictures, and the window ties part 04 to it. The other pictures are shown as true and partial, never set against substitution. Where Baptists differ, on for whom Christ died, the page shows both views under "Where Baptists differ" and does not choose. The Jewish reading of Isaiah's servant is given first and in its own terms; the guilt for Christ's death is laid on "us all", never on the Jewish people. "Every knee" is captioned as not teaching that everyone is saved (article X). The care note on 1 Peter 2 (no one is told to stay where they are harmed) and the note on healing in the atonement stay as the study wrote them. Fourteen of the study's own "to check" notes remain visible on the page until they are checked. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.

## What makes it unlike the other studies in this category

The rising steps of the four gospel lines, the six-circle window with one solid line to its centre, the dashed "where it stops" rule, the turn from picture to report, the joined witness register and the Isaiah–Philippians echo. The good news uses a ledger and a road; The hope to come cards, a table and maps; Covenants and promises cards, a table and arcs; Humanity, sin and renewal a line and a body band; The church and its calling rings of points.
