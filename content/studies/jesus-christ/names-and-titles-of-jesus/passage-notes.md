## Scope and sources

A selective introduction using the six approved working themes. Matthew's naming account supplies the introduction. Speaker, genre and surrounding argument remain visible. No original-language lexical study or exhaustive count of titles is claimed.

## Interpretive boundaries

- **Messiah:** the explicit equivalence in John 1:41 supports the draft. A fuller etymological and anointing study would require additional sources; it is not implied by this draft. No single uniform set of expectations is attributed to all first-century Jews.
- **Son:** announcement, confession and discourse are not flattened into one speaker's fully developed theology. Divine sonship is not presented as sexual generation. John's claims about works and honour remain alongside the Father's sending and the Son's relation to him.
- **Son of Man:** do not reduce all uses to humanity alone or assume each occurrence explicitly cites Daniel. The connection in Mark 14 to Daniel 7 and Psalm 110 is marked as an interpretive comparison grounded in shared wording. Daniel's direction of movement toward the Ancient of Days is retained.
- **Lord:** the draft uses two strongly contextualised confessions rather than assigning the same force to every address translated Lord. It makes no unsupported lexical claim that every occurrence is a direct invocation of the divine name.
- **Philippians:** humility, death and exaltation are read together. The draft does not settle the meaning of every disputed phrase in 2:6–7, claim that Christ ceased to be divine, or reduce the passage to a free-standing title list.
- **Lamb:** the texts explicitly connect imagery with sin, blood, redemption and worthiness. Specific claims that John 1:29 derives exclusively from Passover, Isaiah 53 or another sacrificial source are not asserted. Revelation's visionary form is retained; it is not a literal anatomy of Jesus.
- **Firstborn and image:** Colossians 1:15 is interpreted with verses 16–20, including creation through and for Christ and preeminence. The study does not quietly paraphrase firstborn as first-created, nor claim that the English word alone resolves every doctrinal question.
- **Word:** both “with God” and “was God” are retained along with becoming flesh. The narrator's prologue is distinguished from a direct self-designation spoken by Jesus in a scene.
- **KJV and variants:** quotations follow the local KJV, including John 1:34 and Revelation 5:9. The draft does not claim to adjudicate textual variants or translation choices.

## Reuse and connections

Item **11 Who do you say that I am** already develops Peter and Thomas in narrative detail. This study keeps only what clarifies a title and reuses the same local primary texts rather than creating a competing account. **13 The life of Jesus across the Gospels** and **14 Encounters with Jesus** will develop the narratives more fully.

**18 The signs and works of Jesus**, **19 The cross and resurrection** and **20 Jesus in the letters** will expand the work connected with these names. **22 Father Son and Holy Spirit** and **25 What the cross accomplishes** will develop theological synthesis. Existing site title/name collections are not replaced or modified by this local draft; no new structured catalogue is created.

Possible later addition: a supplementary index for titles outside these six units, such as Son of David and Saviour. The present introduction and six units are complete, with readings, explanation, questions and reflections. No site design or implementation is included.
