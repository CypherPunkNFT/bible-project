# Names and titles of Jesus

What he is called. Who speaks. What the passage reveals.

Matthew records the instruction to Joseph to name the child Jesus, connecting that name with saving his people from their sins. It also gives the explanation of Emmanuel: God with us. The names are presented within an account of who the child is and what he will do. — Matthew 1:20–23

Other passages call Jesus the Christ, the Son of God, the Son of Man and Lord. John speaks of the Word and records the Baptist's witness to the Lamb of God; Colossians describes the image of the invisible God. These expressions belong to particular sentences, speakers and arguments. Reading them in context gives them more substance than a list of interchangeable labels.

## 01 Messiah and Christ

**Read:** John 1:35–51; Mark 8:27–38.

### A title the Gospel explains

> He first findeth his own brother Simon, and saith unto him, We have found the Messias, which is, being interpreted, the Christ.

John 1:41 · KJV

Andrew speaks to Simon after spending time with Jesus. John supplies the equivalence between Messias and Christ within the verse itself. The expression identifies the one Andrew believes they have found; it is not presented as a second personal name.

The following encounters place several descriptions alongside one another. Philip identifies Jesus through what Moses and the prophets wrote. Nathanael addresses him as Rabbi, Son of God and King of Israel. Jesus then speaks of greater things to be seen and uses Son of Man. These speakers are responding within a developing story; their words should not be flattened into a single definition supplied by every speaker equally. — John 1:43–51

### The Christ who must suffer

In Mark, Peter confesses that Jesus is the Christ. Jesus then teaches that the Son of Man must suffer, be rejected, be killed and rise again. Peter objects and is rebuked. The confession is followed by instruction that challenges the disciple's expectations. — Mark 8:27–33

Jesus continues with the call to take up the cross and follow him. The title must therefore be read with the mission the narrative describes. It cannot be allowed to conceal the suffering and resurrection that Jesus teaches immediately afterward. — Mark 8:34–38

**Consider:** Who uses Messias in John, and what explanation does the narrator provide? What does Peter still need to learn after naming Jesus as Christ?

**Reflect:** When you use Christ, consider whether you are attending to the person and mission presented in the surrounding story. What does this reading add to your understanding?

## 02 Son of God

**Read:** Luke 1:30–35; John 1:29–34, 49–51; 5:19–23.

### Announcement and confession

Luke records the angel telling Mary that her child will be called the Son of the Highest, receive David's throne and reign without end. In response to her question, the angel speaks of the Holy Spirit and the power of the Highest, then identifies the child as the Son of God. The description belongs to the birth announcement, with both kingship and the child's conception in view. — Luke 1:30–35

In John, the Baptist testifies after describing the Spirit descending and remaining upon Jesus:

> And I saw, and bare record that this is the Son of God.

John 1:34 · KJV

Nathanael also uses Son of God, placing it alongside King of Israel. The shared wording occurs within different testimonies. Ask what each speaker has seen or heard and how the narrative develops the identification. — John 1:49–51

### The Son and the Father

John 5 gives extended content to the relationship. Jesus speaks of doing what the Father does, the Father's love for the Son, giving life and exercising judgment. He says that all should honour the Son as they honour the Father. The passage joins the Son's relation to the Father with extraordinary claims about his works and authority. — John 5:19–23

These statements should be read together. The Gospel does not describe Son merely as a term of affection, nor does it erase the distinction between the Father who sends and the Son who is sent. The passages supply the language that a fuller study of Father, Son and Holy Spirit must consider.

**Consider:** What accompanies the title in Luke's announcement and John's testimonies? How does Jesus' discourse develop the relationship beyond the title alone?

**Reflect:** Note one statement about the Son's relation to the Father and one about his work. Keep both in view when explaining what this reading says about him.

## 03 Son of Man

**Read:** Mark 2:5–12; 8:27–38; 10:42–45; 14:60–64. Compare Daniel 7:9–14 and Psalm 110:1.

### Authority, suffering and service

Jesus uses Son of Man in several kinds of setting. In Mark 2 he claims authority on earth to forgive sins and commands the paralysed man to rise. In Mark 8 he speaks of suffering, death and resurrection. In Mark 10 he uses the expression while teaching the disciples about service:

> For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many.

Mark 10:45 · KJV

The title is not confined to a statement that Jesus is human. These passages associate it with authority to forgive, the path of suffering and a life given for others. The surrounding verbs show what the speaker says the Son of Man does.

### Clouds and the right hand

Before the high priest, Jesus speaks of the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of power and coming with the clouds of heaven. Compare the wording with Daniel's vision of one like a son of man coming with the clouds to the Ancient of Days and receiving enduring dominion, and with the invitation to sit at the right hand in Psalm 110. — Mark 14:60–64; Daniel 7:9–14; Psalm 110:1

The shared language supports reading these as scriptural echoes in Jesus' answer. The comparison is an interpretive connection, not a separate explanatory sentence supplied by Mark. Daniel's figure comes to the Ancient of Days in the vision; the passage should not be retold as though it simply describes descending from heaven to earth.

Read the authority and glory passages with the suffering and service passages. The Gospel presents these together in Jesus' speech. Choosing only one group would narrow the account the reader is given.

**Consider:** What actions are associated with Son of Man in these settings? Which features of Daniel and the psalm illuminate the council scene?

**Reflect:** Compare one saying about suffering with one about authority. What would be lost if either were excluded from your understanding of the title?

## 04 Lord

**Read:** John 20:24–31; Philippians 2:5–11.

### The risen Jesus addressed

Thomas's confession is spoken to Jesus after the resurrection appearance recorded in John:

> And Thomas answered and said unto him, My Lord and my God.

John 20:28 · KJV

The surrounding scene concerns testimony, Thomas's refusal to believe without seeing, and Jesus' call to believe. Lord belongs here to a confession that also says my God. Its force should be read from that setting rather than inferred only from the presence of the word Lord.

When reading other uses, ask the same questions: who is speaking, to whom, and with what understanding at this point in the story? Repeated wording need not show that every speaker already grasps all that a later confession expresses.

### Confessed by every tongue

Philippians places the confession that Jesus Christ is Lord at the end of an account of humility, obedient death and exaltation by God. Every knee bows and every tongue confesses, to the glory of God the Father. — Philippians 2:5–11

The passage begins as an instruction about the mind believers should have. It speaks of Christ's form of God, taking the form of a servant and obedience to the cross before his exaltation. Lordship is not detached from this self-giving movement.

The confession thus has consequences for how the letter addresses its readers. The one honoured as Lord is also the one whose humility they are called to consider. The title does not provide an excuse for his followers to pursue status without service.

**Consider:** What accompanies Lord in Thomas's confession? How does Philippians connect the final confession with the passage's opening instruction?

**Reflect:** Read the whole Philippians paragraph before repeating its final declaration. What does the passage ask you to notice about the Lord you confess?

## 05 Lamb of God

**Read:** John 1:29–37; 1 Peter 1:18–21; Revelation 5:1–14.

### The Baptist's witness

John the Baptist identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. Later he repeats the designation in the hearing of two disciples, who follow Jesus. The title belongs to testimony directing people toward him. — John 1:29–37

First Peter speaks of redemption through Christ's precious blood, comparing him to a lamb without blemish or spot. The paragraph also speaks of his being made manifest, raised by God and given glory, so that faith and hope are in God. The lamb imagery is tied to redemption, not simply gentleness. — 1 Peter 1:18–21

### The Lamb who was slain

In Revelation's vision, an elder announces the Lion of Judah and Root of David; the seer then beholds a Lamb standing as though slain. The Lamb takes the sealed book and receives praise. The song links his worthiness with his death and the redemption of people from every kindred, language, people and nation. — Revelation 5:1–10

> Saying with a loud voice, Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing.

Revelation 5:12 · KJV

The vision holds being slain and being worthy of honour together. Its imagery should remain recognisable as vision, rather than being turned into a literal physical description of Jesus. John, First Peter and Revelation use related language in different forms: testimony, exhortation and apocalyptic vision.

**Consider:** What does each passage connect with the lamb imagery? How does the contrast between the announced Lion and the seen Lamb shape Revelation's scene?

**Reflect:** Follow the references to sin, blood, redemption and praise in these readings. What would be missing if Lamb were explained only as a picture of harmlessness?

## 06 Word and image

**Read:** John 1:1–18; Colossians 1:15–20.

### The Word made flesh

John opens with the Word in the beginning, with God and described as God. All things are made through him; life and light are associated with him. The prologue then speaks of the Word becoming flesh and dwelling among us. — John 1:1–14

> And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

John 1:14 · KJV

The narrator connects grace and truth with Jesus Christ and says that the Son declares the Father. Read with the beginning, Word concerns the one through whom creation came to be and who entered the life the Gospel narrates. It is not merely a label for Jesus' spoken sentences. — John 1:15–18

### The image of the invisible God

Colossians describes Christ as the image of the invisible God and the firstborn of every creature. The next statements speak of all things created by him and for him, his being before all things, and all things holding together in him. The description of firstborn must be read with these claims, not isolated from them. — Colossians 1:15–17

The paragraph also names him head of the body, the church, and firstborn from the dead, so that he has preeminence in all things. It proceeds to fullness, reconciliation and peace through the blood of his cross. Creation, the church and reconciliation belong to the same account. — Colossians 1:18–20

Word and image are distinct expressions from distinct texts. Both passages connect Christ with making God known, but their wording and arguments deserve separate attention before being brought together. Neither should be reduced to a decorative synonym for the other.

**Consider:** What does John's prologue say the Word does and becomes? How do the sentences after Colossians 1:15 guide the reading of its descriptions?

**Reflect:** Write one sentence about each passage using its own language. Then identify a connection between them without losing what is particular to each.

## Read the title with the passage

These names and titles direct attention to Jesus' identity, relationship to the Father, authority, service and saving work. Keep asking who speaks and what the surrounding account reveals. Let the words lead into the passage rather than stand in place of reading it.
