## Direction

Teach one habit: of any meeting in the Gospels, ask who moved first, what was at stake, and where the record stops. People are read through to their recorded end, and what the record leaves unsaid is left unsaid. Only one direction was built, as with the earlier mock-ups, because this was an existing draft.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the three concepts, the title, the lead, Luke 19:10, and a drawing in thin line: two dotted paths coming to a well, and a water jar left beside it. A visible button pauses the paths. The motto, the figures with their meanings, and the note on what was selected.
- **01 Who moved first, where the record stops.** The signature. All fifteen meetings on one plate, in three rows and four columns, each with its drawing, name and last line in words. "What are you carrying?" chips light the meetings that held the same thing, and hide none. A meeting opens its record in the sheet: how it began, what was asked, what Jesus says, how it ended, what the record does not say, what was at stake and where it is seen again. Each row and column shows its count.
- **02 Seven groups.** A rail: Called to follow, Those called sinners, The sick, Seekers and questioners, Opponents, Two men on one road, In grief and in doubt. Each opens in a form its own passage gives: a path with what was left behind, a ladder of who calls him what, one story folded inside another, two conversations with two last lines, who gives the reason, one line turning off, and one sentence said twice with terms handed back.
- **03 After the last line.** Only the six seen again run on to their verses. The nine others are named once, as not seen again as far as the record lets us tell. Then five rows of older Scripture, each saying who draws the link: Jesus, the Gospel writer, or this study.
- **04 Said, or supplied?** Nine things often said of these people, each with three plain choices and then the passage's answer. No score, no right-or-wrong colour, and nothing in the address.
- **05 Five hard questions,** on the one sheet.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, the passage again, links back, a far land, and the sources.

## Choices made, and why

- **The plate is the argument.** The same start does not settle the end. The widest column holds people as unlike as a healed woman and a teacher asking how, and the page says so.
- **Every line is written under its drawing.** Pointing adds nothing a phone cannot read, and a screen reader hears the same words.
- **Chips light and never filter.** A reader who came for grief should still see the rest on the plate.
- **Where the record stops is not a verdict.** "No further word" means only that. "Not seen again" says "as far as the record lets us tell".
- **Forms come from the text.** Mark 5 is a fold because Mark folds it. The two sisters share a sentence because John gives them one.
- **No faces and no people drawn.** The drawings are objects and places: a net, a tree, a door. A face would claim what the page says it cannot.
- **Colour never carries meaning alone.** The Gospels' violet, with words and borders carrying every state.
- **Unlike the sibling studies.** Prophets in their times is rows and a rule, and Rulers and their choices is beams and strings. This is a plate of meetings. The Signs and works of Jesus, next on the list, should not reuse it.
