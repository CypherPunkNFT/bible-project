## Direction

Teach the text's one habit: keep the words attached to the person who said them. Before deciding what a passage says about Jesus, ask who is speaking, what exactly they say, to whom, and what they do next. The page is written for someone who does not yet believe. It shows what people said about Him, friendly and hostile, leaves His question open, and says plainly where this site stands. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail; the four concepts in gold (The question · The answers · The Word made flesh · Yours); the title *Who do you say that I am?* with *I am?* in gold italic and "Who Jesus really is" under it; the lead; Matthew 16:15. Beside it, a ring with one tick for each line on the wall, in the wall's groups, with one gap and a ruled blank at the centre. A slow dot goes round, and a visible button pauses it. Then the motto, the figures, the Words list of three titles (Christ, Son of God, Son of Man), where this site stands, and what the study selected.
- **01 The answers, and a blank.** The Habit leads the section. Then the signature: 47 lines under ten kinds of speaker, set as serif type on fine rules. The type shows how a line was said: upright if stated, italic if asked, small capitals if dared, mocked or charged. Pointing or tabbing tries a line in the blank. Choosing keeps it, and Read it opens the passage. The blank's fields are labelled with the Habit's own questions, "Who is speaking?" and "What exactly do they say?", then "What came next". Eight words light the lines that use them, hide none, and draw a fine rule down the margin.
- **02 The readings, read whole.** Six ruled entries that open where they stand. Each holds its passages, the form its own text has, what the passage says, what this study reads, and Consider and Reflect. The forms are: three accounts side by side (the road); the steps from "Blessed" to "Get behind Me" (the turn); the council's record beside the witnesses of John 5; the voice's two sentences, word above word; for part 05, a ledger, "Who says it, and to whom"; and for Thomas, his terms against Jesus' words, then John's answers in order, ending on an empty stop.
- **Part 05, the Word made flesh,** is the long one at the centre. Its ledger gives one row per speaker, each in their own verse: John, the angel to Joseph, the angel to Mary, Isaiah with Matthew after him, Paul to the Philippians, Hebrews, Paul to the Colossians. *Became* and *taking* are in gold. Under a rule, "Later, the church. Not Scripture", come Chalcedon's and the Baptist Faith and Message's words. It ends on the text's own sentence that these passages do not explain *how* God and man are one person. Its six subsections open one at a time.
- **03 Five questions, faced,** as a plain ruled list. Each opens the sheet: the passage, this study's reading, then what others have written, labelled as theirs.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, a ruled blank that is not a form, the passage again, links, a drawing of a road going into hills, and the sources.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Gospels' violet, because the page reads the Gospels and asks the Gospels' question. The site's gold marks links, what is lit, and the turn of the title. How a line was said is carried by the type, never by colour alone.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, quoted with its articles where they bear: 1 (the Scriptures), 2 (God the Son, "fully God, fully man", in part 05), 3, 4, 6 and 17. Article 17, on religious liberty, stands beside Article 4's "no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord". The Nicene Creed and the Council of Chalcedon are quoted as the church's later words and marked as not Scripture. On the rock in Matthew 16, Catholic teaching is given in the First Vatican Council's own words, and each other reading is named as its holders'; the page does not choose. Hostile words stay on the wall in their speakers' mouths ("He is out of His mind"), and the legend says each line is its speaker's words, not this page's. A question is never counted as a claim. The page does not say Thomas touched anything, and the build checks that the record does not say so. No face or figure of Christ or of God is drawn.

## Choices made, and why

- **The title is the text's own,** *Who do you say that I am?*. The first design said the page used the Berean's wording; that is withdrawn. The Berean's "Who do you say I am?" stays in the quoted verse.
- **The blank is never filled for the reader.** It does not cycle, and only the reader's own pointing puts words in it. The ruled line at the end cannot be typed in, so no one's answer about Jesus is kept.
- **The part 05 ledger is sorted by speaker, not into God and man columns,** because the text says these passages do not explain how the two are one person, and two columns would claim to.
- **The way-in strip, the pairs and "The answer that changed" are gone,** because the rewritten text no longer has their words.
- **Nothing is hidden by a word.** The other lines only fade, so a reader still sees who did not use it.
- **Unlike the sibling studies.** Encounters with Jesus is a plate of meetings, and The signs and works of Jesus is cords on a brown band. This is type on rules, with no cards and no pills.
