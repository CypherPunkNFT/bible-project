# Passage notes: Who do you say that I am? Who Jesus really is (11)

Merge of draft 11 (Who do you say I am?) with draft 12 (Names and titles of Jesus). All King James quotations from draft 12 are dropped. Material taken from draft 12 has been re-quoted from the Berean or reduced to references. That material is: Christ and Messiah, Son of Man with Daniel 7, Thomas' "Lord", Philippians 2, John 1 and Colossians 1. Draft 12's Lamb of God part (John 1:29–37; 1 Peter 1:18–21; Revelation 5) is not carried. It is left to the built Names page and to the cross study.

## Owned passages

### Matthew 16:13–28, with Mark 8:27–38 and Luke 9:18–27 (parts 01, 02; question "rock")
- **Context:** This is the turning point of the Synoptic Gospels. It comes after the Galilean ministry, and Jesus' first plain prediction of His death follows straight on. In Matthew and Mark it happens near Caesarea Philippi. Luke sets it after the feeding of the five thousand, at prayer.
- **Readings:** The confession is read as the disciples' first clear recognition of the Messiah, which is at once corrected by the teaching about suffering. *The rock* has three readings. (1) Peter himself (Maclaren; Barnes). (2) Peter as confessor, with his faith (Jamieson, Fausset and Brown). (3) Christ, or the confession about Him (reported by Barnes, not adopted). Roman Catholic teaching (Vatican I, *Pastor Aeternus*) adds that the primacy passes to Peter's successors in Rome. Baptists reject any successor; the confession does not fix the identity of the rock. *Matthew 16:28* is read of the transfiguration, of the resurrection and Pentecost, or of AD 70. The study does not choose.
- **Links:** *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount* (16), which quotes Mark 8:34–35; *The church and its calling* (28); *Encounters with Jesus* (14).

### Mark 14:53–65 (part 03)
- **Context:** Jesus' night hearing before the high priest and the Sanhedrin. Peter's denial is set around it.
- **Readings:** Jesus' answer joins Psalm 110:1 and Daniel 7:13. Most commentators take it as His clearest public claim in Mark. "I am" in 14:62 is read by some as a simple yes, by others as an echo of the divine name. The study draws nothing from it in Mark. Care is taken that the scene is the action of particular leaders, and that no collective guilt is laid on the Jewish people.
- **Links:** *Reading Daniel and Revelation* (49) for Daniel 7; the cross study (25).

### John 5:16–47 (part 03; questions "teacher" and "claim")
- **Context:** A Sabbath healing at the pool of Bethesda leads to a dispute and a long speech about the Son and the Father, followed by a list of witnesses.
- **Readings:** Christian readers take the passage as showing the Son as equal to the Father and yet distinct from Him and obedient to Him. Arian readers stress 5:19 and 5:30. The study presents the Sabbath in Jewish terms first.
- **Links:** *The hope to come* (30) keeps 5:24–29 as a pair, and is linked from part 03.

### Matthew 3:13–17 (part 04)
- **Context:** Jesus' baptism by John, at the opening of His public ministry.
- **Readings:** The scene is read as Jesus identifying with sinners ("fulfill all righteousness") and as His anointing with the Spirit. It is classically cited for Father, Son and Spirit together. Here it is read as a scene and not as a proof-text. Believer's baptism as practised by the church is a separate question and is not argued here.
- **Links:** the built Trinity articles; *Baptism and the Lord's Supper* (59) is not needed.

### Matthew 17:1–9 (part 04)
- **Context:** Six days after the confession; three disciples; Moses and Elijah.
- **Readings:** Moses and Elijah are commonly read as standing for the Law and the Prophets. Some read Matthew 16:28 as fulfilled here.
- **Links:** none required.

### John 1:1–18 (part 05; question "claim")
- **Context:** The prologue to John's Gospel.
- **Readings:** The Word as eternal, divine and creator, made flesh. Arian readings are answered from 1:1–3. "Made His dwelling" is linked by many commentators to the tabernacle, which the study marks as an interpretation. 1:18 has a textual variant: "the one and only Son, who is Himself God" against "the only Son". The Berean follows the first.
- **Links:** *Christ in the Old Testament* (41) quotes 1:14; *Saved by grace* (24) quotes 1:12–13.

### Matthew 1:18–25 (part 05)
- **Context:** Joseph's account of the virgin conception and the naming of Jesus.
- **Readings:** Isaiah 7:14 is read in two ways. The Jewish reading comes first in the study: *almah*, a young woman, and a sign to Ahaz fulfilled in his day. The Christian reading follows: the Septuagint's *parthenos*, fulfilled in Jesus. Within Christian reading there are two views: a double fulfilment, or a direct messianic prophecy. The study does not choose between these two. It affirms the virgin conception with the Baptist Faith and Message, Article 2. "Until" in 1:25 is read by Baptists and most Protestants as allowing a normal marriage afterwards. Catholic and Orthodox teaching holds Mary's perpetual virginity. This point is not raised in the content.
- **Links:** *Women of the Bible* (46), which owns Luke 1:26–56 and from which Luke 1:35 is quoted; the site's apologetics answer on miracles.

### Philippians 2:5–8 (part 05)
- **Context:** Paul's appeal for humility, with the hymn to Christ.
- **Readings:** "Emptied Himself" is read by the historic church as Christ adding a servant's nature, not giving up His divine nature. Kenotic theories (nineteenth century onwards), which hold that He laid aside divine attributes, are mentioned without names. Verses 9–11 belong to the cross study.
- **Links:** *Life together* (02) for 2:1–4; the cross study (25) for 2:9–11; Hebrews 2:14 and 2:17 are quoted from the cross study's passage.

### Colossians 1:15–20 (part 05; question "claim")
- **Context:** The hymn to Christ as creator and reconciler, written against false teaching at Colossae.
- **Readings:** "Firstborn" is read by the historic church as rank and heir. Arian readings, and the readings of some groups today, take it as "first created". The study answers from 1:16.
- **Links:** *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* (25).

### John 20:24–29 (part 06; question "unsure")
- **Context:** The second resurrection appearance to the disciples. Ownership passed to this study from *Encounters with Jesus* (14).
- **Readings:** "My Lord and my God" is read as a confession addressed to Jesus. Some (rejected by Barnes) read it as an exclamation. 20:29 is read as a blessing on later believers who trust the witness.
- **Links:** *The signs and works of Jesus* (18), which owns 20:30–31 (quoted here); *Women of the Bible* (46) for 20:1–18.
