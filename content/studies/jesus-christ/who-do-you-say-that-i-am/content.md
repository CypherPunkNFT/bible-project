# Who do you say that I am? Who Jesus really is

Concepts: The question · The answers · The Word made flesh · Yours

Motto: The answers were many. / The question is yours.

Lead: The people who met Jesus did not agree about Him, and the Gospels kept what they said. They called Him a prophet, a blasphemer and the Son of God. Then, on a road in the north, He turned to His own friends and asked the question this study is named for. You do not have to believe anything to read on.

> “But what about you?” Jesus asked. “Who do you say I am?”

Matthew 16:15 · BSB

Habit: This study teaches one reading habit. **Keep the words attached to the person who said them.** When a passage tells you who Jesus is, ask four things before you decide what it means. Who is speaking? What exactly do they say? How does Jesus answer? What do they do next? The Gospels often give the right words to the wrong people, and a hesitant answer to someone who later gives his life. You only see this if you keep each line with its speaker.

Stance: Where this study stands. This is a Christian site. Its guide is the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, the statement of faith of the Southern Baptist Convention. The site holds that the Gospels are true, and that Jesus is who Thomas said He was in John 20:28. It says so here so that you can read with your eyes open. Nothing below asks you to start there. It asks you to read what was said, who said it and what they did next, and then to answer for yourself.

Selection: This is a selection, not a census. Six scenes were chosen because each gives a different kind of answer: a friend's, a council's, a voice from heaven, the Gospel writers' own, and a doubter's. A seventh reading sits at the centre: how the Son of God came to be a man at all. The Gospels hold many more answers than these. The meetings Jesus had with single people are read in *Encounters with Jesus*, and His miracles are read in *The signs and works of Jesus*. This study does not list every name and title Jesus is given. The site's page on the names of Jesus does that [check: exact title and link of the built Names page]. This study reads the titles that its own passages give, in the sentences where they are said.

Words: Three titles come up in almost every part, so here they are first.
- **Christ** is the Greek word for the Hebrew *Messiah*. Both mean "anointed one". Israel's kings were anointed with oil when they were made king, and the prophets promised a coming king from David's family. "The Christ" is not Jesus' surname. It is a claim about what He is.
- **Son of God** can mean several things in the Bible, from a king of Israel to a whole people. Watch what each passage fills it with.
- **Son of Man** is the name Jesus most often uses for Himself. In ordinary Hebrew and Aramaic speech it can simply mean "a human being". Part 03 shows where Jesus takes it further.

## 01 The question on the road

**Main readings:** Matthew 16:13–20; Mark 8:27–30; Luke 9:18–21

Hook: Other people's opinions took one verse. Then came a question no one could answer for anyone else.

### What is said

Three Gospels tell this one moment, and they do not use the same words. Matthew and Mark place it near Caesarea Philippi, a town in the far north, near the source of the Jordan. Luke gives no place. He says only that Jesus "was praying in private and the disciples were with Him" (Luke 9:18).

The first question is about other people, and the answers are reports.

> They replied, “Some say John the Baptist; others say Elijah; and still others, Jeremiah or one of the prophets.”

Matthew 16:14 · BSB

Only Matthew adds Jeremiah. Mark has "one of the prophets" (Mark 8:28). Luke has "a prophet of old has arisen" (Luke 9:19).

The second question is the same in all three Gospels: "Who do you say I am?" (Mark 8:29). Peter's answer is not:

- Mark: "You are the Christ." (Mark 8:29)
- Luke: "The Christ of God." (Luke 9:20)
- Matthew: "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God." (Matthew 16:16)

You do not need to melt three accounts into one sentence to hear what they agree on. They agree that Peter called Jesus the Christ, the promised king.

### What this study reads

[read] Every report in the first answer is respectful. Each places Jesus among the great men of Israel's past. The passage does not call these people foolish. Jesus simply asks again, and this time He asks the people closest to Him.

[source:maclaren] Alexander Maclaren, the nineteenth-century Baptist preacher of Manchester, puts it in one line: "We cannot take refuge from it in the general opinion." [check: confirm wording and locate in Maclaren's *Expositions of Holy Scripture*, on Matthew 16]

[care] Knowing what other people think about Jesus, including what a church or a parent told you, is not the same as answering. It is not nothing either. The disciples could report these views, and they were asked to.

**Consider:** Which opinions are reported, and in which Gospel? What changes when the question turns to the disciples?

**Reflect:** Write down what you have always assumed about Jesus, and where you got it. Is it something you read, or something you were told?

## 02 Blessed, and then rebuked

**Main readings:** Matthew 16:17–28; Mark 8:31–38; Luke 9:22–27

Hook: "Blessed are you" (Matthew 16:17). Six verses later: "Get behind Me, Satan!" (Matthew 16:23). It is the same man.

### What is said

In Matthew, Jesus answers Peter's confession with a blessing.

> Jesus replied, “Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah! For this was not revealed to you by flesh and blood, but by My Father in heaven.

Matthew 16:17 · BSB

Then He tells them to keep it to themselves (Matthew 16:20). "From that time on" He begins to show them that He must suffer, be killed, and on the third day be raised to life (Matthew 16:21). Peter takes Him aside to correct Him: "Far be it from You, Lord! … This shall never happen to You!" (Matthew 16:22).

> But Jesus turned and said to Peter, “Get behind Me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to Me. For you do not have in mind the things of God, but the things of men.”

Matthew 16:23 · BSB

Satan means "accuser" or "adversary". It is the Bible's name for the enemy of God. Mark records the rebuke too, and adds that Jesus said it "turning and looking at His disciples" (Mark 8:33). The others were meant to hear. Luke does not record the rebuke.

Then the call goes out to everyone:

> Then Jesus called the crowd to Him along with His disciples, and He told them, “If anyone wants to come after Me, he must deny himself and take up his cross and follow Me. For whoever wants to save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for My sake and for the gospel will save it.

Mark 8:34–35 · BSB

The *gospel* means "good news": the message about Jesus and what He came to do. Only Luke has the word "daily" (Luke 9:23). The cross is to be taken up every day, not once. How that call is lived is read in *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount*.

### What this study reads

[read] Peter had the title right and the meaning wrong. He wanted a Christ who would not die. So the Gospels do not let the title stand alone. They fill it in, from Jesus' own mouth, with suffering, death and rising, and with a cross for the one who follows.

[read] Why tell them to keep quiet? The Gospels give no full reason, and this study will not invent one. The order comes just before Jesus begins teaching that He must die. Peter's reply then shows what the disciples would have announced: a Christ with no cross.

[source:barnes] Albert Barnes, a nineteenth-century Presbyterian commentator, gives one suggestion: "The reason of this seems to be that his time had not fully come" [check: confirm wording in Barnes' *Notes on the New Testament*, on Matthew 16:20]

[open] "Some who are standing here will not taste death before they see the Son of Man coming in His kingdom" (Matthew 16:28). Readers take this in more than one way. Some read it of the mountain scene that comes six days later (part 04). Others read it of Jesus' rising and the Spirit's coming, or of the judgement on Jerusalem in AD 70. This study does not choose. Luke's form of the saying speaks of seeing "the kingdom of God" (Luke 9:27).

[care] Christians differ over what the rock and the keys in Matthew 16:18–19 mean for Peter and for the church. That is one of the hard questions at the end, so that it does not take over this scene.

**Consider:** How does Jesus answer Peter's confession, and then Peter's objection? Who else was meant to hear the rebuke?

**Reflect:** If Jesus is who Peter said, what part of what He says next would you most like to take Him aside about?

## 03 Before the council

**Main readings:** Mark 14:53–65; John 5:16–47

Hook: The witnesses could not agree. Then the prisoner was asked outright, and He did not stay silent.

### What is said in Mark

Jesus is taken at night to the high priest. The *Sanhedrin* was the ruling council of the Jewish people in Jerusalem, under Roman rule, and the high priest led it. Mark says its leaders were looking for testimony to put Jesus to death, "but their testimony was inconsistent" (Mark 14:56). Jesus says nothing in His own defence (Mark 14:61). Then the high priest asks Him directly.

> Again the high priest questioned Him, “Are You the Christ, the Son of the Blessed One?” “I am,” said Jesus, “and you will see the Son of Man sitting at the right hand of Power and coming with the clouds of heaven.”

Mark 14:61–62 · BSB

"The Blessed One" is a respectful way of saying "God" without using His name. The high priest tears his clothes and calls Jesus' answer *blasphemy*, which means dishonouring God by what one says. "And they all condemned Him as deserving of death" (Mark 14:64).

Jesus' words take up two older passages. "At the right hand" echoes Psalm 110:1. "Coming with the clouds of heaven" echoes the vision in Daniel 7:

> In my vision in the night I continued to watch, and I saw One like the Son of Man coming with the clouds of heaven. He approached the Ancient of Days and was led into His presence. And He was given dominion, glory, and kingship, that the people of every nation and language should serve Him.

Daniel 7:13–14 · BSB

Read more in *Reading Daniel and Revelation*.

[read] In Daniel the figure comes *to* the Ancient of Days and receives an everlasting kingdom. That is why the title Son of Man is more than "a human being" in Jesus' mouth. Jewish readers have often taken Daniel's figure as standing for the holy people who receive the kingdom later in the chapter (Daniel 7:18, 27), and that reading has its place. Before the council, Jesus applies the vision to Himself. The high priest understood Him to do so.

### What is said in John 5

John 5 is another day and another dispute. The *Sabbath* is the seventh day, which God gave Israel as a day of rest. Jewish people keep it from Friday evening to Saturday evening as a holy gift and a sign of the covenant, God's binding promise to His people. Jesus had healed a man on that day and told him to carry his mat. When He was challenged, He answered:

> But Jesus answered them, “To this very day My Father is at His work, and I too am working.” Because of this, the Jews tried all the harder to kill Him. Not only was He breaking the Sabbath, but He was even calling God His own Father, making Himself equal with God.

John 5:17–18 · BSB

Jesus does not take it back. In the same breath He says "the Son can do nothing by Himself, unless He sees the Father doing it" (John 5:19). Both halves belong to the speech. The Son is not a rival god, and He is not less than the Father in honour:

> so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent Him.

John 5:23 · BSB

Then Jesus names His witnesses, because He will not stand on His own word: "If I testify about Myself, My testimony is not valid" (John 5:31). He calls on John the Baptist (5:33), the works the Father gave Him (5:36), the Father Himself (5:37), and the Scriptures:

> You pore over the Scriptures because you presume that by them you possess eternal life. These are the very words that testify about Me,

John 5:39 · BSB

He ends with Moses, "because he wrote about Me" (John 5:46). The middle of the speech, on the dead hearing His voice and the coming judgement (John 5:24–29), is read more fully in *The hope to come*.

### What this study reads

[read] Until this point in Mark, Jesus often silences talk of who He is. Before the council, with nothing left to gain, He answers. A reader may think the answer false. It is harder to think it a misunderstanding, since it is what He was condemned for.

[care] Keep the voices apart. Blasphemy is the high priest's judgement in the scene, and the Gospel does not agree with it. "The Jews" in John 5:16 and 5:18 means particular opponents in that dispute, and not a people. Jesus and all His first followers were Jews, and John tells of many Jews who believed in Him (John 11:45). The New Testament teaches that Jesus went to His death willingly, for the sins of people of every nation. The guilt of His death is never to be laid on the Jewish people. This site holds with its confession that every person of every race "possesses full dignity and is worthy of respect and Christian love" (Article 3, man).

**Consider:** What does Jesus say before the council, and why does the high priest tear his clothes? Who are the witnesses John 5 names?

**Reflect:** Choose one of those witnesses, and ask what it would take for you to hear it fairly.

## 04 The voice from heaven

**Main readings:** Matthew 3:13–17; Matthew 17:1–9

Hook: The first time, the voice speaks at a river. The second time, on a mountain, three terrified men are told what to do about it.

### What is said

John the Baptist was calling Israel to *repent*, to turn away from sin and back to God. He baptized, dipping people in the Jordan as a sign of that turning. When Jesus comes to be baptized, John tries to stop Him: "I need to be baptized by You, and do You come to me?" (Matthew 3:14). Jesus answers, "It is fitting for us to fulfill all righteousness in this way" (Matthew 3:15).

> As soon as Jesus was baptized, He went up out of the water. Suddenly the heavens were opened, and He saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and resting on Him. And a voice from heaven said, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased!”

Matthew 3:16–17 · BSB

Later, on a high mountain, Peter, James and John see Jesus *transfigured*, which means changed in appearance: "His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light" (Matthew 17:2). Moses and Elijah appear, talking with Him. Peter offers to build three shelters. Then:

> While Peter was still speaking, a bright cloud enveloped them, and a voice from the cloud said, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Listen to Him!”

Matthew 17:5 · BSB

They fall facedown in terror. "Then Jesus came over and touched them. 'Get up,' He said. 'Do not be afraid.' And when they looked up, they saw no one except Jesus" (Matthew 17:7–8). On the way down He tells them to say nothing "until the Son of Man has been raised from the dead" (Matthew 17:9).

### What this study reads

[read] At the river, the Father speaks, the Son stands in the water, and the Spirit comes down on Him. Christians confess one God who is eternally Father, Son and Holy Spirit. This is called the *Trinity*. This scene does not explain the Trinity, but it shows all three together. Read more in the site's articles on the Trinity [check: title and link of the built Trinity articles].

[read] A reader of Matthew has known the answer since chapter 3. The people in the story have not. That gap lets you watch them come slowly to what you were told at the start, and it lets you ask where you stand.

[read] "Listen to Him!" is the only instruction the voice gives. It does not tell the disciples to admire Him. Moses gave Israel the law, and Elijah stands for the prophets. When the disciples look up, both are gone, and the one left is the one to listen to. The mountain is also tied to the death and rising Jesus has just begun to speak of, because He tells them to keep silent until then.

[care] Jesus also says the Father "has Himself testified about Me" (John 5:37). John does not say he means either of these scenes, and this study does not fill in John from Matthew.

**Consider:** What does the voice say each time, and what does the second time add?

**Reflect:** Find one thing Jesus says in these chapters that you would rather not listen to, and read it twice.

## 05 The Word became flesh: how God became a man

**Main readings:** John 1:1–18; Matthew 1:18–25; Philippians 2:5–8; Colossians 1:15–20

Hook: Every other answer in this study is about a grown man. This part asks where He came from, and the answer goes back before the first day.

Two church words carry this part. The **incarnation** (from the Latin for "in flesh") means that the eternal Son of God became a real human being, without ceasing to be God. The **virgin birth** means that Mary conceived Jesus by the Holy Spirit, with no human father. Strictly it is a virgin *conception*, but "virgin birth" is the usual name.

### Before the beginning

John's Gospel does not begin in Bethlehem. It begins where Genesis begins.

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was with God in the beginning. Through Him all things were made, and without Him nothing was made that has been made.

John 1:1–3 · BSB

The Word was *with* God, so He is distinct from the Father. The Word *was* God, so He is not a second, lesser god. And He is not part of what was made, because "all things were made" through Him. Then comes the sentence the whole Gospel turns on:

> The Word became flesh and made His dwelling among us. We have seen His glory, the glory of the one and only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth.

John 1:14 · BSB

*Grace* means God's kindness given to people who have not earned it. "Made His dwelling" translates a Greek verb related to the word for a tent. A reader who knows Exodus remembers the tent where God's glory lived among Israel in the wilderness. The prologue closes with a claim no one else in Scripture makes:

> No one has ever seen God, but the one and only Son, who is Himself God and is at the Father’s side, has made Him known.

John 1:18 · BSB

[read] "Word" is not only a name for Jesus' sayings. It names the one through whom God made the world and through whom God speaks. That Word did not visit humanity in disguise. He *became* flesh. The same Gospel goes on to show Him tired, thirsty and weeping.

[read] John's opening tells the reader what the people in his scenes do not yet know. You read every chapter knowing that "the Word was God", and you watch the people in the story arrive at it. Part 06 shows one of them doing so.

### A child with no human father

Matthew tells the birth from Joseph's side.

> This is how the birth of Jesus Christ came about: His mother Mary was pledged in marriage to Joseph, but before they came together, she was found to be with child through the Holy Spirit.

Matthew 1:18 · BSB

In Jewish custom of the time, being pledged in marriage was a binding promise that only a divorce could end. Joseph, "a righteous man", decides to end it quietly, so as not to shame her (Matthew 1:19). Then the angel speaks to him:

> “Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to embrace Mary as your wife, for the One conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. She will give birth to a Son, and you are to give Him the name Jesus, because He will save His people from their sins.”

Matthew 1:20–21 · BSB

*Jesus* is the Greek form of the Hebrew *Yeshua*, which means "the LORD saves". The name is the mission. Joseph obeys, takes Mary as his wife, has "no union with her until she gave birth to a Son", and names Him Jesus (Matthew 1:24–25). Luke tells the same conception from Mary's side:

> The angel replied, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. So the Holy One to be born will be called the Son of God.

Luke 1:35 · BSB

Read more in *Women of the Bible*.

### Isaiah's sign: the Jewish reading first

Matthew says all this took place to fulfil what the Lord said through the prophet (Matthew 1:22–23). He means Isaiah:

> Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: Behold, the virgin will be with child and will give birth to a son, and will call Him Immanuel.

Isaiah 7:14 · BSB

These are first the words of Jewish Scripture, and Jewish readers have read them in their own setting. Isaiah spoke them to Ahaz, king of Judah, when the kings of Aram and Israel were threatening Jerusalem [check: date commonly given, about 735–734 BC]. The Hebrew word here, *almah*, means a young woman of an age to marry. It does not by itself say "virgin", and Hebrew has another word, *betulah*, for that. In Jewish reading, the sign was for Ahaz in his own day. Before the boy is old enough to choose good and refuse evil, the two threatening kings will be gone (Isaiah 7:16). The child was a boy born then. Jewish commentators have named him as a son of Isaiah, a son of Ahaz such as Hezekiah, or a child born to a young woman present at the scene [check: which commentators hold which identification; Rashi is often cited for Isaiah's wife]. This is a careful reading of the chapter, and it is held in good faith.

The Christian reading follows Matthew. Some two centuries before Jesus, Jewish translators rendered Isaiah into Greek, in the translation called the Septuagint. They used *parthenos* here, a word that normally means virgin. Matthew quotes that wording and adds Isaiah's meaning for the name: "God with us" (Matthew 1:23). Christians differ on how the sign worked. Some, including many Baptists, hold that it had a first meaning in Ahaz's day and its full meaning in Jesus. Others hold that Isaiah looked straight to the Messiah, because the Lord calls it a sign, and because Isaiah goes on to speak of the child to come in chapters 8, 9 and 11. The Berean Standard Bible translates "virgin". This study does not choose between those two Christian readings. It does hold, with Matthew, that Isaiah's words were fulfilled in Jesus.

The site's own voice is plain here. The Baptist Faith and Message says that Christ "was conceived of the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary" (Article 2, God the Son) [check: wording against bfm.sbc.net]. Christians of every great tradition confess the same. The Apostles' Creed says it in its second article.

[read] Is a virgin conception simply impossible? It is a miracle, and Matthew and Luke tell it as one. They do not tell it as a symbol. If "all things were made" through the Word (John 1:3), then the Maker taking flesh by His own Spirit is not beyond Him. The question underneath is whether miracles can happen at all. Read more in the site's answer on miracles [check: exact title and link of the apologetics answer on miracles].

### He emptied Himself

Paul tells the church at Philippi to be humble, and he gives them a picture of Christ.

> Let this mind be in you which was also in Christ Jesus: Who, existing in the form of God, did not consider equality with God something to be grasped, but emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in human likeness. And being found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself and became obedient to death— even death on a cross.

Philippians 2:5–8 · BSB

[read] The Son was "existing in the form of God" before He became a man. "Emptied Himself" has sometimes been read to mean He stopped being God. The sentence itself says what the emptying was: "taking the form of a servant". He did not give up being God. He took on being a servant, down to a cross. Historic Christian teaching reads it this way, and Baptists with it. How God raised Him and gave Him the name above every name (Philippians 2:9–11) is read in *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne: what Jesus' death and rising did*.

Hebrews gives the reason He had to be truly human:

> Now since the children have flesh and blood, He too shared in their humanity, so that by His death He might destroy him who holds the power of death, that is, the devil,

Hebrews 2:14 · BSB

> For this reason He had to be made like His brothers in every way, so that He might become a merciful and faithful high priest in service to God, in order to make atonement for the sins of the people.

Hebrews 2:17 · BSB

*Atonement* means making peace between God and people by dealing with sin. Read more in *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne: what Jesus' death and rising did*.

### The image of the invisible God

Paul writes to the Colossians as if he were singing.

> The Son is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. For in Him all things were created, things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities. All things were created through Him and for Him. He is before all things, and in Him all things hold together.

Colossians 1:15–17 · BSB

[read] "Firstborn" here cannot mean the first thing made. The next verse says why: "in Him all things were created". In Israel the firstborn son held the first rank and the inheritance. God calls Israel His firstborn in Exodus 4:22, and David is made "firstborn" over the kings in Psalm 89:27. The word speaks of rank and of being heir.

> For God was pleased to have all His fullness dwell in Him, and through Him to reconcile to Himself all things, whether things on earth or things in heaven, by making peace through the blood of His cross.

Colossians 1:19–20 · BSB

To *reconcile* is to bring two who were at odds back together. The one through whom everything was made is the one through whom everything is made right.

### What these passages do and do not say

They say that the Son was God before He was born, that He truly became a man, that He was conceived by the Holy Spirit of a virgin, and that He became man in order to save. They do not explain *how* God and man are one person. The church has always been careful not to say more than Scripture says. The Council of Chalcedon (451) confessed Christ as "truly God and truly man", "in two natures, inconfusedly, unchangeably, indivisibly, inseparably" [check: wording against the standard English translation of the Definition of Chalcedon, e.g. Schaff, *Creeds of Christendom*]. Southern Baptists say it in four words: "fully God, fully man" (Article 2) [check: wording against bfm.sbc.net].

**Consider:** What does John say the Word was before He became flesh? Which word in Philippians 2:7 tells you what the emptying was?

**Reflect:** If God came near in a body, tired and hungry, what does that say about how near He is willing to come to you?

## 06 Thomas sets his terms

**Main readings:** John 20:24–29

Hook: He named what it would take, in detail. Eight days later Jesus came back and went through his list.

### What is said

Thomas was not with the others when the risen Jesus first came. They tell him, "We have seen the Lord!" He will not take their word for it:

> But he replied, “Unless I see the nail marks in His hands, and put my finger where the nails have been, and put my hand into His side, I will never believe.”

John 20:25 · BSB

Eight days later the doors are locked again, and Thomas is there. Jesus stands among them and says "Peace be with you" (John 20:26). Then He answers each of Thomas' terms in Thomas' own words:

> Then Jesus said to Thomas, “Put your finger here and look at My hands. Reach out your hand and put it into My side. Stop doubting and believe.” Thomas replied, “My Lord and my God!”

John 20:27–28 · BSB

The record does not say Thomas touched anything. It says what he said, and to whom he said it. Jesus does not correct him.

> Jesus said to him, “Because you have seen Me, you have believed; blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed.”

John 20:29 · BSB

### What this study reads

[read] Thomas' words meet John's first words. The reader was told on the first page that "the Word was God" (John 1:1). Now a man in the room says as much to Jesus' face, and Jesus accepts it.

[read] "Stop doubting and believe" is a real rebuke, and it comes with what Thomas asked for. Not everyone who demanded a sign was met this way. Jesus refused a sign to those who came only to test Him (Matthew 16:1–4).

[read] The blessing is not on believing without any reason. The very next verses point to what was written down, as witness, for people who were not in the room:

> Jesus performed many other signs in the presence of His disciples, which are not written in this book. But these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in His name.

John 20:30–31 · BSB

Read more in *The signs and works of Jesus*. Thomas had his eyes. You have his testimony, and the testimony of the others.

[source:maclaren] Maclaren on the scene: "The object of the Christian's faith is not a proposition; it is not a dogma nor a truth, but a Person." [check: confirm wording and locate in Maclaren's *Expositions*, on John 20]

**Consider:** To whom does Thomas speak? What does the writer leave out about touching?

**Reflect:** What are your terms? Say them plainly, and ask whether they are about evidence or about something else.

## Question teacher: Wasn't He simply a great teacher?

Text: John 5:21–23

[read] He was a great teacher, and people said so. What the Gospels do not give you is a teacher who kept to teaching. In John 5 He says the Son "gives life to whom He wishes", that the Father "has assigned all judgment to the Son", and that all should "honor the Son just as they honor the Father" (John 5:21–23). In Mark 2:5–7 He tells a paralysed man his sins are forgiven. Some scribes, the experts in the law, ask the fair question of who can forgive sins but God. Before the council He says the council will see Him at the right hand of Power (Mark 14:62).

[read] So the Gospels leave a reader with a harder choice. You may decide the accounts are wrong about what He said. You may decide He was wrong. What is hard to keep, once you have read them, is a wise teacher who claimed nothing unusual. The site's own answer is at *Was Jesus simply a great teacher?* [check: exact title and link of this apologetics answer].

## Question claim: Did Jesus ever say He was God?

Text: John 5:17–18

[read] The exact sentence "I am God" is not in the four Gospels, and this study will not pretend it is. Here is what is there. He called God His own Father, and John tells us His hearers took it as "making Himself equal with God" (John 5:18). He did not take it back. He said the Son is to be honoured "just as they honor the Father" (John 5:23). In John 8:58 He said He existed before Abraham was born, and His hearers picked up stones. In John 10:30–33 He said He and the Father are one, and they picked up stones again and said why. He let Thomas say "My Lord and my God!" to His face (John 20:28), and He did not correct him.

[read] The same Gospel has Him say "the Son can do nothing by Himself" (John 5:19), and in John 14:28 He calls the Father greater. Christians read these together. There is one God. The Son is truly God with the Father, and He took on human nature and did His Father's will as a man.

[source:nicene] The Nicene Creed, confessed by Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant churches alike, says the Son is "begotten, not made, being of one substance with the Father" [check: wording against the English translation used in the site's creeds collection].

[source:jfb] Jamieson, Fausset and Brown, nineteenth-century Scottish commentators, on the stones in John 10: "In that sense the Jews plainly understood Him" [check: confirm wording and verse].

[source:barnes] Barnes on Thomas: "There is no evidence that this was a mere expression, as some have supposed, of surprise or astonishment." [check: confirm wording, on John 20:28]

[source:boyce] James P. Boyce, first president of the Southern Baptist Theological Seminary: "though a servant, he was still the Lord" [check: confirm wording and locate, probably in Boyce's *Abstract of Systematic Theology*].

[care] A real alternative exists. Arius, a church elder of the fourth century, taught that the Son was the first and highest thing God created, and others since have read "firstborn" (Colossians 1:15) and John 14:28 that way. This site rejects that view, with the historic church. Colossians says "in Him all things were created" (Colossians 1:16), and John says "Through Him all things were made" (John 1:3). The Maker cannot be one of the things made. Christians read John 14:28 of the Son as He came among us, "taking the form of a servant" (Philippians 2:7).

[care] Do not lean on the words "I am" in Mark's scene (Mark 14:62). In English they simply answer the high priest's question, and this study draws nothing more from them there. Some readers, this study among them, also hear God's name "I AM" from Exodus 3:14 behind John 8:58. The Gospel does not draw that link in words.

## Question rock: Who is the rock in Matthew 16?

Text: Matthew 16:18–19

> And I tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades will not prevail against it.

Matthew 16:18 · BSB

[read] The name *Peter* means "rock". *Hades* is the place of the dead, and its gates stand for death's power. Christians give different answers about the rock. The passage says Jesus is the builder, that the church is His, and that this comes straight after a confession. It names no successor to Peter. The keys stand for authority in the kingdom, and what they cover is part of the dispute.

[source:vatican] Roman Catholic teaching, from the First Vatican Council (1870), in its own words: "the primacy of jurisdiction over the universal Church of God was immediately and directly promised and given to blessed Peter the Apostle by Christ the Lord." *Primacy of jurisdiction* means authority over the whole church. The council teaches that it continues in the bishop of Rome. [check: wording against the English text of *Pastor Aeternus*, chapter 1]

[source:maclaren] Maclaren, a Baptist, reads the rock as Peter: "Unquestionably, the apostle is the rock on which the Church is built." He goes straight on to say the promise belongs to Peter's faith, and so to all who share it. [check: confirm wording, on Matthew 16:18]

[source:jfb] Jamieson, Fausset and Brown read it as the man and his faith together: "not on the man Simon Bar-jona; but on him as the heavenly-taught confessor of a faith" [check: confirm wording].

[read] What Baptists hold, from Scripture. A New Testament church is, in their statement of faith, "an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers" (Article 6, the church), with no one office over all churches. That statement does not decide who the rock is, and Baptists are not bound to one reading. None of these readings gives Peter a successor. Scripture calls the apostles and prophets together the foundation, with Christ as the cornerstone (Ephesians 2:20). Binding and loosing are given to the disciples again in Matthew 18:18. And Peter himself calls Jesus the living stone (1 Peter 2:4). Read more in *The church and its calling*.

## Question trust: These were written by His followers to persuade. Can I trust them?

Text: John 20:30–31

[read] It is right to ask. John says he wrote "so that you may believe" (John 20:31), and quoting a Gospel to prove the Gospel proves nothing. Here is what can fairly be said. The writers say they are giving testimony. John says an eyewitness testified (John 19:35). Luke says he investigated carefully from the beginning (Luke 1:3). A letter under Peter's name denies following clever myths (2 Peter 1:16).

[read] A witness who cares about the outcome is not for that reason lying. People who have seen something usually do care. And notice what these writers kept. Jesus' own family thought He was out of His mind (Mark 3:21). Peter was called Satan (Matthew 16:23). Thomas refused his friends' word (John 20:25). None of this proves the accounts true. It is not what you would expect from people polishing a story.

[read] This site's own position, plainly: it holds with its confession that Scripture is "truth, without any mixture of error" (Article 1, the Scriptures). You do not have to start there. If you want to weigh the copies and how they survived, read more in *The surviving witnesses*. What Scripture says about itself is read in *What the Bible says about itself*.

## Question unsure: What if I do not know what I think yet?

Text: John 20:29

[read] Then you are in good company in these pages. John the Baptist sent from prison to ask whether Jesus was the one to come, and Jesus told the messengers to report what they saw (Matthew 11:2–6). A father told Jesus he believed and asked for help with his unbelief (Mark 9:24). Thomas named his terms. None of these honest askers was turned away.

[read] When Nathanael sneered, his friend Philip gave him the only invitation you need here: come and see (John 1:46). You could read one Gospel through. Mark is the shortest, and John says why he wrote. You could also put the question to the one it is about, even if you are not sure anyone is listening.

[care] This site does hold that the question cannot stay open for ever, because its statement of faith says "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord" (Article 4, salvation). It also holds that "God alone is Lord of the conscience" (Article 17, religious liberty). No one can answer for you, and no one should press you to say what you do not mean.

## Close: Your answer

Passage: Mark 8:27–30

Close: You have read the answers of people who opposed Him, feared Him, respected Him, misunderstood Him and worshipped Him. You have read how the Son came to be among them at all: the Word who was God, made flesh, born of a virgin, emptied into a servant's form. Many in these pages had the right words. Having the words was not the same as trusting Him.

Way: Take the habit to any passage about Jesus. Keep the words attached to the person who said them. Who is speaking? What exactly do they say? How does He answer? What do they do next? And what has the writer told you that they do not yet know?
