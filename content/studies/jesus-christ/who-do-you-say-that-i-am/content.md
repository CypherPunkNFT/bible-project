# Who do you say I am?

Concepts: The question · The answers · Yours
Motto: The answers were many. / The question is yours.
Lead: The people who met Jesus did not agree about Him, and the Gospels kept what they said: out of His mind, a deceiver, a prophet, the Son of God. Then, on a road in the north, He turned to His own friends and asked the question this page is named for. You do not have to believe anything to read on.

> “But what about you?” Jesus asked. “Who do you say I am?”

Matthew 16:15 · BSB

Stance: Where this page stands. This is a Christian site, with the Baptist Faith and Message, the Southern Baptist statement of faith, as its guide. It holds that the Gospels are true and that Jesus is who Thomas said He was in John 20:28, and it says so here so that you can read with your eyes open. Nothing below asks you to start from there. It asks you to read what was said, who said it and what they did next, and then to answer for yourself.
Selection: This is a selection, not a census. These {{answers}} answers were chosen to show every kind of speaker the Gospels record. Most sayings that come more than once are given only once, so nothing here counts how often a thing was said. Which group a speaker sits in is this study's own sorting.

WayInLead: Begin where He began. The first question was about other people.
WallLead: What was said about Him? Every line here is the speaker's own words in the Berean, set under the kind of speaker. Point at one to try it in the blank. Choose it to keep it there, and read who said it and what came next. Choose a word to see who else used it.
WallReads: Look at who shares the same words. {{Holy}} speakers here call Jesus "the Holy One of God": an unclean spirit in Mark 1:24 and Simon Peter in John 6:69. Having the right words does not tell you who was for Him.
WallCare: A line on this wall is what its speaker said, and not what this page says. The type shows how it was said: upright if stated, italic if asked, small capitals if thrown as mockery or a charge. A question is never counted here as a claim.
WordSon: {{Son}} lines say "Son of God" or words close to it, and {{sonGroups}} of the {{groups}} groups use them. {{SonClaim}} of the lines state it, and the others dare Him or ask.
WordChrist: Christ is the Greek word for Messiah, the anointed king Israel was waiting for, as John 1:41 explains. {{Christ}} lines use one or the other.
WordKing: {{King}} lines say King. In this selection, {{kingClaim}} of them states it, and the others ask, mock or charge.
WordLord: {{Lord}} lines say Lord. The word can be polite in the Gospels, as "Sir" is in John 9:36. Read the line beside it to see which it is here.
WordProphet: {{Prophet}} lines say prophet. Each is said with respect. It is a kind answer, and Jesus still asked the second question.
WordHoly: {{Holy}} lines, and they are the same words. One speaker is afraid of Him, and the other has just refused to leave Him.
WordTeacher: {{Teacher}} lines, each from a man who came to Him with respect. One came at night, and one went away sad.
WordDavid: {{David}} lines say Son of David, Israel's name for its promised king.

PairsLead: The same words, in two mouths, with what the record says happened next.
PairsReads: The words do not divide these people. Having the words was not the same as trusting Him. James says so even of a true sentence about God: "Even the demons believe that" (James 2:19). And Jesus says so to people who already use the right name: "Why do you call Me 'Lord, Lord,' but not do what I say?" (Luke 6:46).

ChangedLead: People in this story changed their answer, and later Scripture remembers it. Read each line across: what they said first, and what they said after.
ChangedReads: None of these answers is said to have changed over a better argument. In each case the record names something that happened: a meeting, a question, a death and an empty tomb. You may not accept that. It is what they say changed them.
ChangedCare: The James who wrote the letter does not call himself the Lord's brother there. This study reads him as the James of Galatians 1:19, as most readers have. Others in the story are not seen again: the man who went away sad in Mark 10:22, and Nicodemus, of whom John 19:39 tells what he did and not what he believed. Silence is not a verdict on either.

## 01 The question on the road

Slug: road
Read: Matthew 16:13–20; Mark 8:27–30; Luke 9:18–22
Ask: What people say, and what you say.
Hook: Other people's opinions took one verse. Then came the question nobody could answer for anyone else.

### What is said

Three Gospels tell this one moment, and they do not tell it in the same words. Matthew and Mark put it near Caesarea Philippi. Luke says only that Jesus "was praying in private and the disciples were with Him" (Luke 9:18). The first question is about other people, and the answers are reports: John the Baptist, Elijah, a prophet. Matthew alone adds Jeremiah, in Matthew 16:14.

The second question is the same in all three: "Who do you say I am?" (Mark 8:29). Peter's answer is not. A reader does not need to melt three accounts into one sentence to hear what they agree on.

### What this study reads

[read] Every report in the first answer is respectful, and each puts Jesus among the great men who came before. The passage does not call these people foolish. He simply asks again.

[source:maclaren] Alexander Maclaren, a Baptist preacher: "We cannot take refuge from it in the general opinion."

[care] Knowing what other people think about Jesus, including what a church or a parent told you, is not the same as answering. It is not nothing either. The disciples could report these views, and they were asked to.

Consider: Which opinions are reported, and by whom? What changes when the question turns to you?
Reflect: Write down what you have always assumed about Jesus, and where you got it. Is it something you read, or something you were told?

## 02 Blessed, and then rebuked

Slug: turn
Read: Matthew 16:13–28; Mark 8:27–38; Luke 9:18–27
Ask: The right title, and what it did not yet mean.
Hook: "Blessed are you" (Matthew 16:17). Then, {{turnGap}} verses on: "Get behind Me, Satan!" (Matthew 16:23). It is the same man.

### What is said

Peter answers, and in Matthew Jesus says this was revealed to him "by My Father in heaven" (Matthew 16:17). He tells them not to say it publicly. "From that time on" (Matthew 16:21) He begins to tell them He must suffer, be killed and be raised. Peter takes Him aside to correct Him, and in Matthew and Mark, Jesus rebukes him. Luke does not record the rebuke.

Then comes the call, "he must deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow Me" (Luke 9:23). Only Luke has the word "daily" (Luke 9:23).

### What this study reads

[read] Peter had the title right and the meaning wrong. He wanted a Christ who would not die. So the Gospels do not let the title stand alone. They fill it in, from Jesus' own mouth, with suffering, death and rising, and a cross for the one who follows.

[source:maclaren] Maclaren on Peter's words: "True, the speaker did not know all which lay in His words. Do we?"

[read] Why tell them not to tell anyone? The Gospels give no full reason, and this study will not invent one. The order comes just before He begins to teach that He must die, and Peter's reply shows what they would have announced: a Christ with no cross.

[source:barnes] Albert Barnes, a Presbyterian commentator: "The reason of this seems to be that his time had not fully come"

[care] Christians differ over what the rock and the keys mean for Peter and for the church. That is one of the hard questions at the end, so that it does not take over this scene.

Consider: How does Jesus answer Peter's confession, and then Peter's objection?
Reflect: If Jesus is who Peter said, what part of what He says next would you most like to take Him aside about?

## 03 Before the council

Slug: council
Read: Mark 14:53–65; John 5:16–47
Ask: What He said when it would cost Him His life.
Hook: The witnesses could not agree. Then the prisoner was asked outright, and He did not stay silent.

### What is said

Mark says the leaders were looking for testimony to put Him to death, and "their testimony was inconsistent" (Mark 14:56). Jesus says nothing until the high priest asks Him directly. He answers, and He adds words about the Son of Man at the right hand of Power, coming with the clouds of heaven. The high priest tears his clothes and calls it blasphemy, and the council condemns Him.

John 5 is another day and another dispute, over the Sabbath and over Jesus calling God His Father. John tells us how the hearers took it: "making Himself equal with God" (John 5:18). Jesus does not take it back, and in the same breath He says "the Son can do nothing by Himself" (John 5:19). Both belong to the speech. Then He names His witnesses, because He will not stand on His own word: "If I testify about Myself, My testimony is not valid" (John 5:31).

### What this study reads

[read] Until now in Mark He has often silenced talk of who He is. Here, with nothing left to gain, He answers. A reader can think the answer false. It is harder to think it was a misunderstanding, since it is what He was condemned for.

[care] Keep the voices apart. Blasphemy is the high priest's judgment in the scene, and the Gospel does not agree with it. "The Jews" in John 5:16 means particular opponents in that dispute, and not a people. Jesus and all His first followers were Jews, and John 11:45 tells of many Jews who believed. This site holds that "every person of every race possesses full dignity and is worthy of respect and Christian love" (Article 3).

Consider: What does Jesus say in Mark's scene, and how do others react? Who are the witnesses John 5 names?
Reflect: Choose one of those witnesses, and ask what it would take for you to hear it fairly.

## 04 The voice from heaven

Slug: voice
Read: Matthew 3:13–17; Matthew 17:1–9; John 5:37
Ask: The same sentence twice, and three words more.
Hook: The first time, it is said at a river. The second time, on a mountain, three frightened men are told what to do about it.

### What is said

At His baptism a voice from heaven says who He is. Later, on a high mountain, Peter, James and John see Him changed, with Moses and Elijah talking with Him, and the voice says the same thing and adds to it. They fall down in terror. Jesus touches them and says "Do not be afraid" (Matthew 17:7), and when they look up they see "no one except Jesus" (Matthew 17:8).

On the way down He tells them to say nothing until the Son of Man has been raised. So the mountain is tied to the death and rising He has just begun to speak of.

### What this study reads

[read] A reader of Matthew has known the answer since chapter 3. The people in the story have not. That gap lets you watch them come slowly to what you were told at the start, and it lets you ask where you stand.

[read] "Listen to Him!" (Matthew 17:5) is the only instruction the voice gives. It does not say to admire Him. When they look up, Moses and Elijah are gone, and the one left is the one to listen to.

[care] John 5:37 also speaks of the Father's testimony, and it does not say it means either of these scenes. This study does not fill in John from Matthew.

Consider: What does the voice say each time, and what does the second time add?
Reflect: Find one thing Jesus says in these chapters that you would rather not listen to, and read it twice.

## 05 Thomas sets his terms

Slug: thomas
Read: John 20:24–29
Ask: A man who refused, and what he was offered.
Hook: He named what it would take, in detail. Eight days later Jesus came back and went through his list.

### What is said

Thomas was not there, and his friends tell him "We have seen the Lord!" (John 20:25). He will not take their word for it, and he says what it would take. Eight days later Jesus comes, says "Peace be with you" (John 20:26), and answers each term in Thomas' own words.

Then the record says what Thomas said, and it does not say he touched anything. He speaks to Jesus: "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28). Jesus does not correct him. He says "Because you have seen Me, you have believed" and turns to everyone who has not seen: "blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed" (John 20:29).

### What this study reads

[read] Thomas said "I will never believe" (John 20:25), and he is told "Stop doubting and believe" (John 20:27). That is a real rebuke, and it comes with what he asked for. Not everyone who demanded a sign was met this way: Matthew 16:4 refuses one to those testing Him.

[read] The blessing is not on believing without any reason. The very next verses point to what was written down, as witness, for those who were not in the room. Thomas had his eyes, and you have his testimony and the others'.

[source:maclaren] Maclaren on this scene: "The object of the Christian's faith is not a proposition; it is not a dogma nor a truth, but a Person."

Consider: To whom does Thomas speak? What does the writer leave out about touching?
Reflect: What are your terms? Say them plainly, and ask whether they are about evidence or about something else.

## 06 Written for you

Slug: written
Read: John 20:30–31; John 1:1–18
Ask: The Gospel that says it was written so that you may believe.
Hook: For twenty chapters, people have been answering Him. The end of chapter 20 turns round and speaks to you.

### What is said

John says plainly that he left things out: "many other signs" (John 20:30) were done that he did not write. Then he says why he kept what he kept, and it has two halves: that you may believe who Jesus is, and that "by believing you may have life in His name" (John 20:31).

He opened the book the same way, telling the reader what the people in the scenes do not yet know: "the Word was God" (John 1:1), and "The Word became flesh" (John 1:14). So you read every scene knowing more than they do, and you watch them arrive.

### What this study reads

[read] John sets answers along the whole road, and the line does not stop at the last one. Its next stop is you. Thomas' words meet John's opening words: the reader was told "the Word was God" (John 1:1), and a man in the room has now said as much to His face.

[care] John says he wrote to persuade. He does not hide it. Whether that makes him easier or harder to trust is one of the hard questions below.

Consider: Why does John say these things were written? How does his opening change the way you read the people in his scenes?
Reflect: Read one scene in John with John 20:31 beside you. What does it show about Jesus, and what is it asking of you?

## Question teacher: Wasn't He simply a great teacher?

Text: John 10:30–33

[read] He was a great teacher, and people said so, as in John 3:2. What the Gospels do not give you is a teacher who kept to teaching. He told a paralysed man "your sins are forgiven" (Mark 2:5). Some scribes there thought it blasphemy, and they asked a fair question: "Who can forgive sins but God alone?" (Mark 2:7). In this passage a crowd with stones says what the stones are for: "You, who are a man, declare Yourself to be God."

[read] So the Gospels leave a reader with a harder choice. You may decide the accounts are wrong about what He said. You may decide He was wrong. What is hard to keep, once you have read them, is a wise teacher who claimed nothing unusual. The site's own answer to this question is at [Was Jesus simply a great teacher?].

[care] When a man calls Him "Good Teacher" (Mark 10:17), Jesus asks "Why do you call Me good?" (Mark 10:18). He does not say He is not good. He makes the man think about the word, and then says "come, follow Me" (Mark 10:21). That is this study's reading, and the verse does not spell it out.

## Question claim: Did Jesus ever say He was God?

Text: John 8:56–59

[read] The exact sentence "I am God" is not in the four Gospels, and this page will not pretend it is. Here is what is there. He said "before Abraham was born, I am!" (John 8:58), and at once they picked up stones. He said "I and the Father are one" (John 10:30), and in John 10:33 they say why they picked up stones again. He let Thomas say "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28) to His face, and He did not correct him.

[read] The same Gospel has Him say "the Son can do nothing by Himself" (John 5:19), and "the Father is greater than I" (John 14:28). Christians read these together: one God, the Son truly God with the Father, who took on human nature and did His Father's will. The Baptist Faith and Message says "fully God, fully man" (Article 2).

[source:jfb] Jamieson, Fausset and Brown, on the stones: "In that sense the Jews plainly understood Him"

[source:barnes] Barnes, on Thomas: "There is no evidence that this was a mere expression, as some have supposed, of surprise or astonishment."

[source:boyce] James P. Boyce, first president of Southern Baptist Theological Seminary: "though a servant, he was still the Lord"

[care] A real alternative exists. Arians in the early church, and others since, have held that the Son is a created being, and they read verses such as John 14:28 that way. The note by Jamieson, Fausset and Brown names them and argues against them. This site rejects that view: John says "the Word was God" (John 1:1) and "Through Him all things were made" (John 1:3). Christians read John 14:28 of the Son's coming as a man.

[care] Do not lean on the words "I am" (Mark 14:62) in Mark's scene. In English they simply answer the question put to Him, and this study draws nothing more from them there. Some readers, this study among them, also hear "I AM" (Exodus 3:14) behind John 8:58. The Gospel does not draw that link in words.

## Question rock: Who is the rock in Matthew 16?

Text: Matthew 16:17–19

[read] Christians give different answers. The passage says Jesus is the builder, that the church is His, and that this comes straight after a confession. It names no successor to Peter. The keys stand for authority in the kingdom, and what they cover is part of the dispute.

[source:vatican] Catholic teaching, from the First Vatican Council in 1870, labelled as theirs: "the primacy of jurisdiction over the universal Church of God was immediately and directly promised and given to blessed Peter the Apostle by Christ the Lord." Primacy of jurisdiction means authority over the whole church, and the council says it continues in the bishop of Rome.

[source:maclaren] Maclaren, a Baptist, reads the rock as Peter: "Unquestionably, the apostle is the rock on which the Church is built." He goes straight on to say the promise belongs to Peter's faith, and so to all who share it: "wherever that faith is, there are these gifts".

[source:jfb] Jamieson, Fausset and Brown read it as the man and his faith together: "not on the man Simon Bar-jona; but on him as the heavenly-taught confessor of a faith".

[source:barnes] Barnes reports a third answer, that Christ meant Himself or the truth about Him, and he does not take it. He reads the rock as Peter: "This is the obvious meaning of the passage".

[read] What Baptists hold, from Scripture. A New Testament church is, in their statement of faith, "an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers" (Article 6), with no one office over all churches. That statement does not decide who the rock is, and Baptists are not bound to one of these readings. None of them gives Peter a successor. Scripture calls the apostles and prophets together the foundation, "with Christ Jesus Himself as the cornerstone" (Ephesians 2:20). Binding and loosing are spoken of again in Matthew 18:18, in a talk to the disciples that begins at Matthew 18:1. And Peter himself writes of Jesus as "the living stone" (1 Peter 2:4).

## Question trust: These were written by His followers to persuade. Can I trust them?

Text: John 20:30–31

[read] It is right to ask. John says he wrote so that you may believe, and quoting a Gospel to prove the Gospel proves nothing. Here is what can fairly be said. The writers say they are giving testimony: "The one who saw it has testified to this" (John 19:35). Luke says he worked "having carefully investigated everything from the beginning" (Luke 1:3). And a letter under Peter's name denies the charge outright: "we did not follow cleverly devised fables" (2 Peter 1:16).

[read] A witness who cares about the outcome is not for that reason lying. People who have seen something usually do care. And notice what these writers kept. His own family said "He is out of His mind" (Mark 3:21). Peter was called Satan in Matthew 16:23. Thomas refused the others' word. None of this proves the accounts true. It is not what you would expect from people polishing a story.

[read] This site's own position, plainly: it holds that Scripture has "truth, without any mixture of error" (Article 1). You do not have to start there. If you want to weigh the copies and how they survived, the [Surviving witnesses] study lays out what is held and what is inferred.

## Question unsure: What if I do not know what I think yet?

Text: Matthew 11:2–6

[read] Then you are in company on these pages. John the Baptist, in prison, sent to ask "Are You the One who was to come, or should we look for someone else?" (Matthew 11:3), and Jesus told the messengers to report what they saw. A man who had been blind asked "Who is He, Sir?" (John 9:36). A father said "I do believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24). These honest askers were not turned away.

[read] When Nathanael sneered, a friend gave him the only invitation you need here: "Come and see" (John 1:46). You could read one Gospel through. Mark is the shortest, and John says why he wrote. You could also put the question to the one it is about, even if you are not sure anyone is listening.

[care] This site does hold that the question cannot stay open for ever, because the statement of faith it stands with says "There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Jesus Christ as Lord" (Article 4). It also holds that "God alone is Lord of the conscience" (Article 17). No one can answer for you, and no one should press you to say what you do not mean.

## Close: Your answer

Passage: Mark 8:27–30
Close: You have now read the answers of people who hated Him, feared Him, respected Him, misunderstood Him and worshiped Him. Many of them had the words. Having the words was not the same as trusting Him.
Way: Take this to any passage about Jesus. Who is speaking? What exactly do they say? How does He answer? What do they do next? And what has the writer told you that they do not yet know?
Invite: This site's own answer is Thomas' answer, and it is what John wrote his book for. Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God. He "died for our sins" (1 Corinthians 15:3) and was raised. His first call was "Repent and believe in the gospel!" (Mark 1:15), and Paul gives the response in one line: "believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved" (Romans 10:9). If you have come to that, tell someone, and find a church that opens this book.
Question: The first answer was about other people. The second one never was. Who do you say He is?
Again: Read the passage again before you answer.
