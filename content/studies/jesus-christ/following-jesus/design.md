## Direction

Teach one habit: read the line inside the whole. Before you take a saying away, ask who is listening, what came just before, and where the passage is going. The shape is already in the text. The Sermon on the Mount is one speech of 111 verses with a frame Matthew built himself: the disciples come to Him on the mountain (Matthew 5:1–2) and the crowds are astonished when He has finished (7:28–29), and inside that, "the Law and the Prophets" at 5:17 and again at 7:12 holds the middle together. So the page draws the whole sermon as one band of 111 verses, with those two frames arched over it, and every famous line is shown in its place on that band. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail (Study / Jesus Christ / Following Jesus), the four concepts in gold capitals (Blessing · Hearing · Doing · Remaining), the title *Following Jesus* with *and the Sermon on the Mount* as its gold italic turn, the lead, and Matthew 7:24 printed in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: two houses on one shore under the same slow rain, one on layered rock and one on sand, the second drawn in a broken line because "it fell" (Matthew 7:27). The motto beneath: "Hear the words. / Build on the rock." No person is drawn. A bar of four computed figures, each with what it means: the sermon's 111 verses and the 100 this study reads, the nine parts, the six times "But I tell you" in Matthew 5, and the verses printed in full. Links to every part follow.
- **00 The line inside the whole.** The signature. The sermon drawn as one band of 111 verse marks in three chapters, with the six reading parts named beneath it, the prayer (6:5–15) shown hatched as read in its own study, and two arches above: the listeners' frame (5:1–2 and 7:28–29) and the Law-and-Prophets frame (5:17 and 7:12). Above the band sit ten famous lines as plain words in two groups, "In the sermon" and "After the sermon". Choose one and the band labels it at its own verse with a fine rule, and marks three places for it: who is listening (a square), what came just before (a ring) and where it is going (an arrow). Each place is answered under the band in the Berean's own words with its verse, followed by a one-sentence reading from the study. Only the chosen line is labelled on the band, so ten labels never crowd it. Three of the ten lines come from the passages read after the sermon (Luke 14, Matthew 11, John 15); for those the band redraws as that passage's own verses. Pointing at any mark shows that verse's first words under the band, and choosing it opens the verse. The chosen line is kept in the address, so a shared link opens on it. On a phone the words wrap as a list and the band keeps only its marks.
- **01–06 The sermon, read through.** While the reader is in these six parts, a thin copy of the band stays at the top of the screen and lights the part's own verses, so the reader always sees where the passage sits in the whole (left out on phones). Each part opens with its number, its title split at the colon into ink and gold italic, its main readings, and a small copy of the band with its range filled. Each part carries one drawing taken from the shape of its own verses:
  - 01: the nine "Blessed are" lines set as a column, who is blessed in ink and the promise in violet italic (5:3–12).
  - 02: six sayings on a ground line, "it was said" above the line and "But I tell you" below it, because each one goes past the act to its root.
  - 03: three practices side by side with the same refrain, "your Father, who sees what is done in secret, will reward you" (6:4, 6, 18); the middle one, prayer, marked as read in its own study.
  - 04: the rich man's own words (Luke 12:17–19) with every "I", "my" and "myself" marked, counted by script, then God's answer.
  - 05: the band again with the Law-and-Prophets arch closing at 7:12.
  - 06: four pairs, each with a small drawing: two gates, two trees, two who say "Lord, Lord", two builders.
- **Can anyone actually live this?** The study's own question, set between the sermon and what follows. Two voices side by side and labelled: what this site says with the confession, and what other Christians hold, reported as theirs.
- **07 and 08 side by side.** "Count the cost" and "Come to Me" are set as two columns on a wide screen, because the study says to read part 08 "beside part 07, not instead of it". A thin rule joins their two key verses at the top: the same Lord says both. On narrower screens they stack.
- **09 Remain in Me.** A vine drawn from the text: one leaf for each verse of John 15:1–17 that says "remain", sized by how often it says it, each leaf growing from one stem and opening its verse. The leaves' verse numbers are left out on phones; each leaf still names its verse to a screen reader.
- **Close.** The closing paragraphs, Matthew 28:19–20, and the three examples from the study's close ("Do not judge", "Do not worry", "Count the cost") each opening its line in the map at the top. The invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Matthew 7:24–29 printed in full beside them. A line-art landscape of a mountain in Galilee at the foot, since Matthew ends on a mountain as the sermon began on one. Sources, the notes still to be checked, and a link back to Jesus Christ.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Gospels' violet, because every passage the study reads is in the Gospels and its subject is what Jesus taught His followers. The site's gold marks the concepts, the title's turn, the care notes and the notes still to be checked.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: salvation by grace through personal faith in Christ, not by keeping the sermon (article IV); the perseverance of true believers, with the Wesleyan and Arminian reading of John 15:6 shown and labelled as theirs (article V); stewardship, not riches as the reward of faith (article XIII); peace sought "on principles of righteousness", with the just-war and pacifist views both shown and neither chosen (article XVI). The Anabaptist, Lutheran, dispensational and Bonhoeffer readings of the sermon are reported as theirs. Every care note in the text is kept and drawn in the site's gold: the crisis lines beside the eye-and-hand saying, "But I tell you not to resist an evil person" (Matthew 5:39) never meaning to stay where you are harmed, anxiety as an illness, no prosperity promise, assurance after "I never knew you", and the misuse of "hate his father and mother" to control someone. The ten famous lines, their three answers and the drawings are this page's reading aids, taken from the study's own words and checked against the verses, not claims the text makes.
