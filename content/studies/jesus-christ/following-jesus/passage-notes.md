# Passage notes: Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount (16)

## Owned passages

### Matthew 5:1–16 (Beatitudes; salt and light)
- **Context:** Follows the call of the first disciples (Matthew 4:18–22) and the summary of Jesus' Galilean ministry. Disciples are taught, crowds listen (5:1–2; 7:28).
- **Main readings:** (1) The Beatitudes describe the character and blessed state of those who belong to the kingdom, given as grace before command (the common evangelical and Baptist reading, held here). (2) Entrance requirements or virtues to be achieved (rejected here as contrary to article IV). Luke 6:20–26 has its own wording ("poor", woes). It is referred to but not quoted or blended.
- **Links:** The kingdom he announced (15).

### Matthew 5:17–48 (the Law fulfilled; six antitheses)
- **Context:** Thesis statement (5:17–20), then six "You have heard… But I tell you" units.
- **Main readings:** On 5:17–18, how the Law applies to Christians is left open by the confession. The three Baptist views (three kinds of law, as in the Second London Confession; new covenant theology / progressive covenantalism; dispensational readings) are shown in Leviticus (47), which keeps 5:17–18 as a pair. On 5:39, there are just-war and pacifist readings (article XVI does not choose), and the Anabaptist (Schleitheim, 1527) reading forbidding the sword. On 5:33–37, Anabaptists and Quakers forbid all oaths; the Second London Confession allows lawful oaths. On 5:38, the Jewish reading of "eye for eye" as monetary compensation (Mishnah Bava Kamma 8). On 5:29–30, read as hyperbole (safe-messaging care note included).
- **Links:** Sexuality, singleness and divorce (43, pair on 5:27–32); Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice (47, pair on 5:17–18); The Ten Commandments (61); The Christian and the state (64).

### Matthew 6:1–4, 6:16–18 (giving and fasting in secret)
- **Context:** Three acts of Jewish devotion (almsgiving, prayer, fasting), described in Jewish terms first. The prayer (6:5–15) is owned by the prayer study; 6:9–13 is quoted only.
- **Main readings:** Read with 5:16. The issue is the desired audience, not visibility. Reward is the Father's gift, not merit.
- **Links:** Prayer across Scripture: learning to pray (04).

### Matthew 6:19–34 and Luke 12:13–34 (treasure, two masters, worry; the rich fool, the little flock)
- **Context:** Luke's version is prompted by an inheritance dispute. Matthew's sits within the sermon.
- **Main readings:** Greed and anxiety are treated as one root (trust misplaced). Luke 12:33 is read as addressed to the disciples, with obedience today worked out in conscience; some traditions (mendicant orders, some Anabaptist communities) take it as a call to give up private property, which is not adopted here. The "good eye" is read as generosity, an interpretation resting on a Hebrew idiom. Anti-prosperity care note; anxiety care note (not medical advice). The fear study quotes 6:25–34.
- **Links:** Fear, anxiety and despair (56); Money, possessions and generosity (63).

### Matthew 7:1–12 (judging, asking, golden rule)
- **Context:** The inclusio "the Law and the Prophets" (5:17; 7:12) frames the sermon's central body.
- **Main readings:** 7:1 forbids hypocritical condemnation, not discernment (7:6, 15–16). 7:6: do not press holy things on those who scorn them (common reading; the text is difficult). The golden rule is compared with Hillel's negative form (b. Shabbat 31a).
- **Links:** none required.

### Matthew 7:13–29 (two ways, false prophets, "Lord, Lord", two builders)
- **Main readings:** Obedience as the evidence and shape of real faith, not its ground (Baptist reading, article IV). 7:23 "never knew" is read with article V (assurance care note). The storm is read as final judgement first.
- **Links:** Chosen and kept: can I be sure? (58); Who do you say that I am? (11).

### Luke 14:25–35 (counting the cost)
- **Main readings:** "Hate" is read as a Semitic idiom of priority (compare Matthew 10:37), not malice. "Give up everything" is read as total allegiance, not one property arrangement (Zacchaeus, Luke 19:8–9, referred to). Care note on the misuse of the passage for control or abuse.
- **Links:** Who do you say that I am? (11), which owns Mark 8:27–38; Mark 8:34–35 is quoted only.

### Matthew 11:25–30 (rest for the weary)
- **Context:** Follows the woes on the unrepentant towns (11:20–24).
- **Main readings:** Rest is found in relation to Jesus and His teaching, not apart from it. The yoke is compared with the rabbinic "yoke of the kingdom of heaven".
- **Links:** none required.

### John 15:1–17 (the vine and the branches)
- **Context:** Farewell discourse, the night before the crucifixion. Old Testament vine imagery for Israel (Psalm 80; Isaiah 5) referred to.
- **Main readings:** On 15:6, there is (1) the Baptist/Reformed reading (article V): outward attachment without true union (Judas as example); (2) the Wesleyan/Arminian reading: a true believer can finally fall away (labelled as theirs; no source read); (3) some read it as loss of fruitfulness or usefulness rather than salvation (not set out on the page; the editor may add it). 15:7 is read as conditioned by "My words remain in you".
- **Links:** Chosen and kept (58); The cross, the empty tomb and the throne (25), which owns Mark 10:35–45; Mark 10:45 is quoted only.
