## Interpretive and editorial questions

1. **Luke 14:26:** the draft reads the severe family saying alongside Matthew 10:37–39 as a demand for supreme allegiance. This is a contextual interpretive comparison, not an unverified claim about a Greek word or a replacement translation. Keep Luke’s reference to one’s own life visible too.
2. **Forsaking possessions:** preserve Luke 14:33 without making it merely a change of feeling. Specific applications and traditions’ accounts of renunciation require further treatment if expanded; the draft does not prescribe one property arrangement as explicitly narrated here.
3. **Cross-bearing:** distinguish allegiance to Jesus amid costly consequences from treating all suffering, arbitrary human demands, or deliberate pursuit of pain as commands from Jesus.
4. **Rest and cost:** the draft places Matthew 11 beside Mark 8 and Luke 14 as complementary readings. It does not claim Matthew’s invitation is an explicit commentary on those particular scenes.
5. **John 15:** Christian interpretations differ over branches, perseverance, and the warning’s relationship to salvation. Retain the command and warning without resolving those systems in this overview. Avoid making fruit solely conversions, performance totals, or feelings; the passage itself develops love and obedience.
6. **Prayer in John 15:** preserve abiding, Jesus’ words, and asking in his name as the setting of the promises. Do not detach the promises into guarantees for arbitrary desires.
7. **Matthew 28:** retain the KJV’s “teach all nations” and the following instruction to observe Jesus’ commands. No claim about Greek grammar is needed for the draft’s point. Baptism’s significance and subjects remain outside the present study’s detailed scope.
8. **Worship and doubt:** Matthew reports that some doubted; the draft does not identify which disciples or invent their internal explanation.
