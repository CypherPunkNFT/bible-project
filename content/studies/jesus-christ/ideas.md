# Jesus Christ study ideas

Develop studies that help readers encounter Jesus through Gospel narratives, his teaching and the later witness of the New Testament. Preserve each writer’s emphasis and distinguish direct statements from interpretive connections.

These are content proposals for review. Subcards are working content divisions, not site components. Passage lists are starting points for later reading and verification, not completed research.

## 11 Who do you say that I am

**Purpose:** Explore the questions and claims surrounding Jesus’ identity.

**General direction:** Organize around named questions, confessions and responses in their narrative settings.

**Possible subcards:** The question to the disciples; Peter’s confession; Jesus before his opponents; the Father’s testimony; Thomas’s confession; the purpose of John’s Gospel.

**Starting passages or sources:** Matthew 16:13–20; Mark 14:53–65; John 5:16–47; John 20:24–31.

## 12 Names and titles of Jesus

**Purpose:** Study what Jesus is called, by whom and in what setting.

**General direction:** Distinguish titles used by Jesus, narrators, followers and opponents. Explain meaning without treating every use as interchangeable.

**Possible subcards:** Messiah and Christ; Son of God; Son of Man; Lord; Lamb of God; Word and image.

**Starting passages or sources:** Mark 8:27–38; John 1:1–51; Philippians 2:5–11; Colossians 1:15–20.

## 13 The life of Jesus across the Gospels

**Purpose:** Follow the major movements of Jesus’ life through the four accounts.

**General direction:** Build on the existing Gospel study; separate shared events, unique material and uncertain chronology.

**Possible subcards:** Birth and beginnings; baptism and wilderness; Galilee; the journey to Jerusalem; the final week; resurrection appearances.

**Starting passages or sources:** Matthew 1–4; Mark 1; Luke 9:51–19:48; John 12–21.

## 14 Encounters with Jesus

**Purpose:** Read individual encounters as complete stories.

**General direction:** Ask who approaches whom, what is said, what changes and what the narrator leaves unresolved.

**Possible subcards:** Called to follow; the excluded; the sick; seekers and questioners; opponents; those who turn away.

**Starting passages or sources:** Mark 1:16–20; Mark 5; Mark 10:17–31; Luke 19:1–10; John 3–4.

## 15 The kingdom he announced

**Purpose:** Explore Jesus’ proclamation of the kingdom of God.

**General direction:** Compare announcement, entry, growth, present signs and future expectation without forcing them into a single slogan.

**Possible subcards:** The kingdom near; receiving the kingdom; kingdom parables; signs of the kingdom; wealth and status; watchfulness.

**Starting passages or sources:** Mark 1:14–15; Matthew 13; Luke 11:14–23; Luke 18:15–30; Matthew 25.

## 16 Following Jesus

**Purpose:** Study what Jesus asks of those who follow him.

**General direction:** Keep the cost, dependence, grace and community of discipleship together.

**Possible subcards:** Come and follow; take up the cross; count the cost; learn from me; abide in me; sent to others.

**Starting passages or sources:** Mark 8:34–38; Matthew 11:25–30; Luke 14:25–35; John 15:1–17; Matthew 28:16–20.

## 17 The teaching of Jesus

**Purpose:** Explore the arguments and settings of Jesus’ teaching.

**General direction:** Develop full teaching units, linking to the existing parables study rather than producing a duplicate catalogue.

**Possible subcards:** The Sermon on the Mount; prayer; enemies and forgiveness; possessions; greatness and service; hearing and doing.

**Starting passages or sources:** Matthew 5–7; Luke 6:20–49; Luke 12:13–34; Mark 10:35–45.

## 18 The signs and works of Jesus

**Purpose:** Study how the Gospel writers present Jesus’ works and the responses to them.

**General direction:** Build on the existing miracles collection; preserve narrative purposes rather than grouping only by spectacle.

**Possible subcards:** Healing; deliverance; provision; authority over creation; raising the dead; belief and opposition.

**Starting passages or sources:** Mark 4:35–5:43; Mark 6:30–52; John 2:1–12; John 9; John 11.

## 19 The cross and resurrection

**Purpose:** Follow the accounts of Jesus’ death and resurrection and their interpretation.

**General direction:** Separate the narrated events, witness claims and theological explanations while showing their connections.

**Possible subcards:** The final meal; Gethsemane; trial and crucifixion; burial and the empty tomb; appearances; why the apostles call it good news.

**Starting passages or sources:** Mark 14–16; Luke 22–24; John 18–21; 1 Corinthians 15:1–28.

## 20 Jesus in the letters

**Purpose:** Explore how the epistles speak of Christ’s person, work and continuing significance.

**General direction:** Focus on the witness of the letters; connect to Christian Life where that teaching grounds an instruction.

**Possible subcards:** Christ before all things; incarnation and humility; reconciliation; our high priest; intercession; the returning Lord.

**Starting passages or sources:** Philippians 2:5–11; Colossians 1:15–23; Hebrews 1–2; Hebrews 4:14–5:10; Romans 8:31–39; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18.
