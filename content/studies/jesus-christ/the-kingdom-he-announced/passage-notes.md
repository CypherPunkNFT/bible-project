# The kingdom he announced (15): passage notes

Rewrite of draft 15 (jesus-christ/the-kingdom-he-announced). Every quotation re-taken from the supplied Berean Standard Bible text; all King James wording in the old draft, including its part headings ("Repent ye, and believe the gospel", "Come ye after me", "How hardly shall they that have riches enter", "Watch therefore"), has been removed. The draft's walks of Mark 1:16–20, John 3:1–8, Luke 18:9–30 and Matthew 25:31–46 are dropped to references or quotations, since this row does not own them.

## Passages owned

### Isaiah 52:7–10
**Context.** Isaiah 40–55, comfort to Judah after the fall of Jerusalem and the exile to Babylon. A herald runs to ruined Jerusalem announcing God's reign; the watchmen take up the cry; the LORD returns to Zion and bares his arm before the nations. The passage sits just before the servant song of 52:13–53:12, which this study does not read.
**Readings.** (1) Jewish reading: God's promised return to Zion and redemption of Israel, a hope still awaited, expressed in the kingdom language of Jewish prayer (Kaddish; the Shema as accepting "the yoke of the kingdom of heaven"); at Qumran, 11QMelchizedek quotes 52:7. Given first in the study. (2) Christian reading: the good news of God's reign fulfilled in Jesus; Paul applies 52:7 to gospel preachers (Romans 10:15, referred to, not quoted). Many note that "gospel" in Mark echoes Isaiah's "good news".
**Links.** God: who He is and what He does (Psalm 96); Reading Daniel and Revelation (Daniel 2, 7); Christ in the Old Testament (41, owner of 52:13–53:12, not touched).

### Mark 1:14–15
**Context.** The opening of Jesus' public preaching in Mark, after John's arrest, in Galilee.
**Readings.** "Near": arrived (C. H. Dodd's emphasis) or approaching and arriving (most readers); the study leaves the word's range open and lets the whole set of passages answer. Repent and believe follow the announcement, not the other way round.
**Links.** Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount (Mark 1:16–20); Leviticus: holiness and sacrifice (Luke 4:18–19, Jubilee); The good news.

### Matthew 13
**Context.** Matthew's third great block of teaching: seven parables by the sea and in the house, Jesus' explanation of why he speaks in parables (quoting Isaiah 6), and the rejection at Nazareth (13:53–58), read as the sower enacted.
**Readings.** "Kingdom of heaven" as reverent Jewish wording for "kingdom of God" (common explanation, labelled). Weeds: the field is the world (Jesus' own explanation), not the church; patience until the end of the age. Leaven: growth (majority, followed) or spreading evil (Scofield Reference Bible note, shown as an alternative, needs checking). Treasure and pearl: worth and joy, not a purchase price.
**Links.** The site's parables study; Saved by grace (not purchasable).

### Luke 11:14–23
**Context.** A deliverance from a mute demon, the Beelzebul accusation, and Jesus' answer: divided kingdom, the finger of God, the strong man and the stronger one, no neutrality. Parallel in Matthew 12:22–30 (referred to only; 12:28 "Spirit of God").
**Readings.** The present reign of God breaking in against Satan's. "Finger of God" echoes Exodus 8:19. Care note against diagnosing illness as demonic and against reading it as a promise of present healing for all.
**Links.** The signs and works of Jesus (18); Angels, demons and spiritual powers (53); What Scripture promises the sick (45).

### Luke 17:20–21
**Context.** Pharisees ask when the kingdom will come; followed at once by teaching to disciples about the days of the Son of Man to come (17:22 onward, referred to).
**Readings.** "In your midst" (Berean, fits an audience of opponents) or "inside you" (older translations). The study follows the Berean and gives the reason; both keep the point that the kingdom is present in Jesus.
**Links.** The hope to come (the future days of the Son of Man).

### Luke 18:15–17
**Context.** Babies brought for blessing; disciples rebuke; Jesus welcomes them; receiving like a child. Placed after the Pharisee and tax collector (18:9–14, owned by Saved by grace) and before the rich ruler (18:18–30, referred to only).
**Readings.** Receiving as dependence, not innocence. Article IX's "trustful, childlike commitment". Some traditions have used this passage in support of infant baptism; the study does not take that up, and article VII (baptism and the Lord's Supper) governs the site's view (see Baptism and the Lord's Supper). Question on children who die handled with care, not as a promise the verse makes.
**Links.** Saved by grace; Encounters with Jesus (John 3:3–5); Grief and dying; Children and the home.

### Matthew 25:1–30
**Context.** The Olivet discourse (Matthew 24–25), answering the question of Jesus' coming and the end of the age (24:3, referred to). Ten virgins; talents.
**Readings.** Readiness that cannot be borrowed; the date unknowable; delay built into the stories. The oil not allegorised. Talents as money, with applications marked as applications; no prosperity reading (article XIII). Outer darkness and eternal punishment stated plainly under article X. Care note on assurance under article V.
**Links.** The hope to come (25:31–46); Chosen and kept; Fear, anxiety and despair; Money, possessions and generosity.
