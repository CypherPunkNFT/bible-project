## Direction

Teach one habit: listen for the tense. Before you decide what a kingdom saying means, hear whether it says the kingdom *is near*, *has come upon you*, *is in your midst* or *will be like*. The shape is already in the text: Jesus' own verbs change tense from passage to passage, and the study's frame and close name those verbs. The page sets them out the way a grammar book sets out a verb, one line per tense, and every part of the page carries its line. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail (Study / Jesus Christ / The kingdom he announced), the four concepts in gold capitals (Reign · Nearness · Seed · Delay), the title *The kingdom* with *he announced* as its gold italic turn, the proposed subtitle, the lead, and Mark 1:15 printed in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: a mustard tree grown from a seed, birds in its branches, and on the other side a closed arched door with an oil lamp burning at its threshold. The motto beneath: "A small seed. A shut door. / Near now, coming still." No person is drawn. A bar of four computed figures, each with what it means: the seven passages walked, the kingdom sayings set by tense, the verses of Matthew 13 that name the kingdom, and the verses printed in full.
- **00 The kingdom in its tenses.** The study's frame paragraph, then the signature: a table of six lines, one for each part, set like a verb in a grammar book. Each line gives the verb in large italic (*reigns*, *is near*, *is like*, *has come upon you* with *is in your midst*, *belongs to*, *will be like*), the clause it stands in, its verse, a plain name for the line (always, come close, like a seed, already here, received, still to come), labelled as this page's reading aid and the part that reads it. Choosing a line prints its verse beneath the table with the verb marked, so the reader sees the tense in the Bible's own sentence. The study's selection note sits under it.
- **01–06 The parts.** Each part opens with its number, its saying in large italic, the rest of its title, its line from the table, its hook and its main readings. The reading runs in one column. The study's own readings ("This study reads"), the places where readers differ ("Readers differ") and the alternative reading of the leaven ("Another reading") stand in a margin beside the paragraph they answer, as notes in a study Bible stand beside the text; on a phone they fall in under it. "With care" notes stay in the main column, in the site's gold. Consider and Reflect close each part side by side.
- **Part 03, Matthew 13 in order.** The chapter drawn as the reader meets it: the seven parables placed by verse along the chapter, with the line at 13:36 where Jesus leaves the crowd and goes into the house. They sit in three rows taken from the part's own question: hearing; growth or worth; the end. A ring marks the three Jesus explains himself (the sower, the weeds, the net). The chapter ends in his hometown (Matthew 13:54 does not name the town), which the study reads as "hard ground", where the seed of the first parable falls; the page labels that as the study's reading. Pointing at a parable reads its opening line; choosing it opens the verse. On a phone the same seven are a list in chapter order.
- **Questions readers ask.** Four questions set as words on one fine thread, not boxes, each answer opening in place. The question about children who die begins with the crisis lines and is open from the start, so care is never folded away. The question whether Jesus expected the end at once carries "Waiting, in Jesus' own stories": three phrases in his words where the waiting is long ("until the harvest", "the bridegroom was delayed", "After a long time"), each checked in its verse. Each outside view is labelled by name: Augustine and Roman Catholic teaching, Walter Rauschenbusch, and the three scholars Schweitzer, Dodd and Ladd. 
- **Close.** The way of reading, then its three asks in the study's own words: trust what God has already done, turn toward what is near, be ready for what is coming. The invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Mark 1:14–15 printed in full beside them. Then the seed and the door drawn again, a closed list of the study's notes still to be checked (each numbered in the text), the sources, and a link back to Jesus Christ.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Gospels' violet, because every passage the study walks after part 01 is in the Gospels and its subject is what Jesus preached. The site's gold marks the concepts, the title's turn, the care notes and the notes still to be checked.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: the kingdom as God's reign, entered now by trustful, childlike commitment to Christ, with its full consummation at his return (article IX); the church as distinct from the kingdom and living under it (article VI); Christ's personal, visible return and the final judgment (article X); stewardship, not riches as the reward of faith (article XIII); the perseverance of true believers (article V). Where Baptists differ, on the millennium, the page names the question and points to *The hope to come* without choosing. Other views (Augustine, Roman Catholic teaching, Rauschenbusch, Schweitzer, Dodd, the Scofield note on the leaven) are shown as theirs and labelled. Jewish readings of Isaiah 52 are given first and in their own terms. The care note in part 04 forbids diagnosing illness as demonic; the question on children who die carries the crisis lines and makes no promise the verse does not make. The tense names and the three kinds of parable are this page's reading aids, taken from the study's own words, not claims the text makes. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
