## Direction

Teach one habit: do not read only the deed. The Gospel writers tell what Jesus did, and around it they set what He said, what they say it shows, and who believed or turned away. The page lets a reader see that shape first, then read the accounts whole. Only one direction was built, as with the earlier mock-ups of existing drafts.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the three concepts, the title, the lead, John 20:31, and a thin-line drawing of a boat on the water under a cord with one star. A visible button pauses the moving cord. The motto, "The deed is short. The account is not.", the figures with their meanings, and what this page chose.
- **01 Where the deed falls.** The signature. A warm-brown band, the same in both themes, holding one cord for each of eight accounts. Each verse is one touching 24-pixel length of cord, so a longer cord is a longer account, at one scale on every screen. The stroke says what the verse mostly does: dotted thin for the setting, thin for others, thick for Jesus, double for the writer, and a four-point star for the deed. Each cord has its tally: before, the deed, after. A sticky readout shows the verse. Five lenses fade everything but one kind and say what that kind shows. Under the band, the rule for sorting, then a fold where the reader marks three unmarked accounts from Luke, with no score.
- **02 Six readings.** A three-by-two grid of cards, with no sideways scroll. Each reading opens with its own cord and a form its own passage gives: the steps after the woman's healing, five who begged, what Mark counts and does not show, two crossings row by row, whom He speaks to in John 11, and two stairs going opposite ways in John 9.
- **03 The same deed. Not the same response.** One row for each account and three columns, in the account's own words. "Not recorded" is written where the account gives nothing.
- **04 Scripture reads these works too.** Three voices: Jesus on His works, older Scripture with a line saying who draws each link, and the apostles.
- **05 Five hard questions,** on the one sheet.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, the passage again, links back and the sources.

## Choices made, and why

- **The cords are the argument.** A reader sees before reading that the star is small and the cord is long. The page then says that this does not make the deed small, because everything else stands on it.
- **A rule for sorting, stated on the page.** Many verses do two things, so one order decides, and any account opens to show how each verse was sorted.
- **Not a score.** No percentage is drawn and no account is ranked. Verse numbers are named as a ruler laid on the text.
- **Pointing is never the only way.** Keyboard, touch and pointer all reach the same readout. On a touch screen the first tap reads the verse and the readout offers the whole account.
- **Forms come from the text.** The word beg in Mark 5, Mark's counts, the same words at the end of two crossings.
- **"Not recorded" is said.** The feeding has a row of it, because Mark gives no response there.
- **The band holds the same colours in both themes.** Stroke and shape carry each kind, so colour never carries it alone. There is no glow and nothing drawn as a sparkle of wonder.
- **No faces and no people drawn.**
- **Unlike the sibling studies.** Encounters with Jesus is a light plate of meetings, and Rulers and their choices is beams and strings. This is cords measured in verses.
