## Interpretive and editorial cautions

- **Firstborn:** interpret Colossians 1:15 with 1:16–18, not in isolation. The draft observes creation by/for him, priority, coherence, resurrection, and stated preeminence. It does not invent a lexical proof or imply the verse explicitly says “first created.”
- **Philippians 2:** retain the KJV’s form-of-God and servant language. The draft makes no claim that Jesus ceased to be divine, supplies no speculative account of surrendered attributes, and does not claim verified pre-Pauline hymn origins. Translation and doctrinal comparisons require separate treatment if added.
- **Hebrews:** do not attribute the book to Paul as an established fact. This draft refers to Hebrews without asserting authorship. Its use of scriptural quotations is observed without adding unverified source reconstructions.
- **Reconciliation:** distinguish the cosmic language in Colossians 1:20, the direct address in 1:21–22, and the continuation condition in 1:23. No automatic universal salvation conclusion or settled account of perseverance is inferred from a single phrase.
- **Learning obedience and perfection:** retain Hebrews 4:15’s sinlessness when reading 5:8–9. Completion through suffering in the priestly argument must not be rewritten as correction of prior sin. A full discussion of “made perfect” belongs to expanded Hebrews notes.
- **High priest:** no attempt is made to explain all of Melchisedec or replace Hebrews’ larger argument with a name definition. The scope here is why readers may hold fast and draw near.
- **Intercession:** distinguish the Spirit in Romans 8:26–27 from Christ in 8:34. Do not picture the Father as reluctant and Christ as introducing a compassion the Father lacks. Hebrews’ enduring priesthood is not a repeated death or repeated self-offering.
- **Assurance:** Romans names actual suffering and death. Do not turn its promise of inseparability into immunity from adversity or a prosperity guarantee.
- **Return:** the KJV’s “prevent” in 1 Thessalonians 4:15 is expressed as precede, supported by the stated order in verses 16–17. Retain the text’s public descent, rising, gathering, and lasting presence without imposing a complete end-times scheme or predicted date.
- **Sleep:** chapter 4 addresses believers who have died; chapter 5 also uses sleep in an exhortation to watchfulness. Do not assume every occurrence in these paragraphs has an identical function or settle all intermediate-state questions here.
- **Grief:** Paul contrasts sorrow with hopeless sorrow. The reflection does not condemn bereavement or demand immediate emotional resolution.
