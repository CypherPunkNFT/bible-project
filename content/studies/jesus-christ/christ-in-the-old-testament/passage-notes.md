## Context, readings and limits

1. **Luke 24.** Jesus names "the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms" (24:44). Luke gives the scope of what He
   explained, not the list of passages. The draft does not claim that the five figures were the ones He used; it says
   only that the rest of the New Testament keeps returning to them. Study 27 already walks Luke 24 as a passage about
   Scripture; this study uses it only as the doorway and links there.
2. **The lamb.** Exodus 12:27 calls the Passover "the Passover sacrifice to the LORD"; Exodus does not call it a sin
   offering. The draft says so, and says that the New Testament's "lamb who takes away sin" gathers several pictures
   (Passover, sacrifice, Isaiah 53:7). Which lamb John the Baptist chiefly meant in John 1:29 (Passover lamb, Isaiah's
   lamb, the daily offering, or the ram of Genesis 22) is an interpretive question; the draft says interpreters differ
   and does not choose. John 19:36: the Berean's own footnote reads "Psalms 34:20; see also Exodus 12:46 and Numbers
   9:12", so the draft names both possible sources. The title "Lamb of God" itself belongs to Study 12.
3. **The temple.** John 1:14: the Berean footnote gives "Or and tabernacled among us". The draft's sentence that John's
   verb recalls the tent rests on that footnote; the Greek word is not discussed further. John 2:22 is used to show that
   understanding followed the resurrection, matching Luke 24. The draft keeps the line running through Christ to the
   church (1 Corinthians 3:16) and to the new creation (Revelation 21:22), so that none of the three is flattened.
4. **The priest.** Only Hebrews calls Jesus high priest, so this part rests on one book; that is stated in the text.
   The argument is drawn from contrast as well as likeness ("Unlike", 7:27). Melchizedek: Hebrews 7:3 says "like the Son
   of God". The view that Melchizedek was an appearance of the Son Himself is mentioned as held by some readers, **without
   a named holder; find and read a named source before publishing, or cut the sentence.**
5. **The king.** Solomon's own words (1 Kings 8:20) show the first fulfilment of 2 Samuel 7:12–13. Hebrews 1:5 quotes
   2 Samuel 7:14a and not 7:14b ("When he does wrong, I will discipline him"); the draft uses this to show that the New
   Testament applies part of a promise to Christ, not every line. The draft does not argue why Hebrews stops where it
   does beyond the plain observation. Acts 2 is left to Study 37, Long Memory (part 05), to avoid repeating it.
6. **The servant.** Isaiah 49:3 names the servant "Israel"; 49:5–6 sends the servant to restore Israel. The draft
   states the Jewish reading of the servant as the people of Israel, naming Rashi. **Rashi's commentary on Isaiah was
   not read for this draft: check his comments on Isaiah 52:13 and 53:3 before publishing.** The Christian reading is
   argued from the song's own words (53:6, 53:9) and from Acts 8:34–35. Matthew 8:17 is included so the song is not
   read only for the cross. The Berean footnote on Isaiah 53:4 notes the Greek Old Testament wording and its citation in
   Matthew 8:17 and 1 Peter 2:24; the draft does not discuss it.
7. **The method.** Romans 5:14's "pattern" is the Greek *typos*, the source of the English "type". The same word family
   stands behind "examples" in 1 Corinthians 10:6 and 10:11 and behind "symbolizes" in 1 Peter 3:21; the draft names
   only Romans 5:14's word. 1 Corinthians 10:4 ("that rock was Christ") is in the context read but not discussed; it is
   a harder verse and could be added in revision. Galatians 4:24 ("These things serve as illustrations") is left to the
   Academic study on how the church has read the Bible, which is built around Galatians 4.

## Doctrine

- Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Article I: Scripture "is a testimony to Christ". The study's whole frame rests on it,
  quoted as a witness, argued from Luke 24 and John 5:39, 46.
- Article IV (salvation) stands behind parts 01, 03 and 05: Christ's once-for-all sacrifice (Hebrews 7:27; 10:12).
- Where Baptists differ: how far typology may go beyond the types the New Testament names. Part 06 shows the two
  positions (the narrower rule reported by Fairbairn from Marsh; Fairbairn's wider principle) and does not choose.
  **The sentence that Baptist teachers today stand on both sides needs a named Baptist source on each side before
  publishing.** The confession does not address the question.
- No allegory or number symbolism is offered in the site's voice. First Clement's reading is shown as an example of a
  reading beyond the text, labelled as such.
