## Interpretive limits and future review

- This is a selected reading study. Section 01 introduces the sermon’s movement; it does not fully expound every saying concerning divorce, oaths, retaliation, or judgment. Do not describe it as a comprehensive commentary.
- Matthew’s poor in spirit and Luke’s poor are retained separately. No hypothesis about sermon chronology, literary dependence, or reconstructed original wording is required.
- The account of fulfilling law and prophets begins with Jesus’ explicit statement. Detailed disputes over the law’s continuing application remain outside this overview; avoid implying that the sermon simply repudiates the Old Testament.
- Matthew 6’s vain repetitions warning gives its own reason: reliance on much speaking. The draft does not equate all repeated or written prayers with the condemned practice.
- The KJV prayer includes its received doxology. A later translation comparison should identify textual differences explicitly rather than silently mix versions; none is attempted here.
- Enemy-love and forgiveness are treated as concrete commands. Detailed questions about reconciliation, trust, civil justice, and nonresistance require fuller readings if added. Do not collapse them into one unqualified instruction or dilute the actual commands.
- Luke 6:38 is read in its mercy, giving, and judgment context. No guaranteed financial return is inferred.
- The rich fool’s harvest is not called dishonest. His misplaced security and failure to be rich toward God are the textual focus. Provision teaching is not converted into clinical claims about anxiety or a formula guaranteeing wealth.
- Mark 10’s cup and baptism are interpreted in the nearby suffering context. The draft does not reconstruct each apostle’s later death or treat the metaphors here as a complete sacramental discussion.
- The ransom saying is retained with Jesus’ service. Detailed accounts of atonement belong particularly to item 25; its significance is not reduced to an illustration of useful leadership.
- The two builders concern hearing and doing, within a warning that includes judgment. Application to present trials is secondary and cannot justify judging a sufferer’s faith from hardship.
