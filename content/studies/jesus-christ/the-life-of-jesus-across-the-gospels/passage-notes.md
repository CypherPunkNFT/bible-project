## Interpretive boundaries

- **Chronology:** these are broad reading movements, not a dated biography. Do not infer an exact ministry duration, birth year or complete travel itinerary from the outline. John supplies earlier Jerusalem visits; Luke's journey does not negate them.
- **Openings:** shepherds and wise men are not forced into the same moment. Matthew's house and Luke's manger are retained without asserting a precise interval. The birth narratives' differences are not resolved by invented travel details.
- **Wilderness:** Matthew and Luke's order differs on the page. No conjecture about the writer's motive or original historical order is presented as fact.
- **Distinct material:** one Gospel's omission is not treated as an explicit denial. Conversely, comparison does not justify attributing another writer's details to the passage being read.
- **Harmony:** the existing Robertson-derived boundaries are editorial aids. The portraits already warn against reading their diagram as a calendar; that distinction is reused here.
- **Final week:** no exact inter-Gospel Passover chronology is settled. The reader route follows Mark continuously while noting selected Johannine material. A fuller meal and passion comparison belongs in item 19.
- **Anointing:** this draft reports narrative placement as the existing portraits do. It does not resolve the date of the act or silently identify every Gospel anointing story as one event.
- **Mark's ending:** the comparison deliberately selects 16:1–8 and calls it a selected passage, not the end of the local KJV chapter. It makes no manuscript-history claim about 16:9–20. A later textual discussion would need appropriate primary evidence and belongs with item 19 or 35.
- **Resurrection and ascension:** no complete interleaving of the appearances is claimed; Luke's ending is not used to assign a one-day resurrection-to-ascension interval. Acts would need to be considered for a fuller account.
- **Narrative claims:** statements describe what the Gospel accounts report. The study is not an independent historical demonstration of every event or a resolution of every apparent tension.

## Connections and completion

**11 Who do you say that I am** and **12 Names and titles of Jesus** provide identity-focused readings. **14 Encounters with Jesus**, **15 The kingdom he announced**, **16 Following Jesus**, **17 The teaching of Jesus**, **18 The signs and works of Jesus** and **19 The cross and resurrection** will develop individual material more fully. Existing portraits remain the reusable comparison resource rather than a second catalogue being built here.

All six units have readings, exposition, questions and reflection. A later revision may add a reading checklist linked to the completed companion content, but no new event database is needed. No site design or implementation is included.
