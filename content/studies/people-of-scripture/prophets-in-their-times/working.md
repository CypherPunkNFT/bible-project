# 38 — Prophets in their times

First content draft ready for owner review · 2026-10-13 · Not final-copy approval.

## Purpose and developed scope

Connect named prophets with their setting and message. [The draft](content.md) develops six sections with two subdivisions each: calling, setting, message, symbolic actions, response and later remembrance. Examples: Jeremiah, Haggai, Amos, Huldah and Elijah, with Joel's opening illustrating less precise dating evidence.

## Decisions and boundaries

- Reuse existing records as passage leads; do not adopt every short biography or era label as a verified claim.
- Distinguish the prophet in an account, the voice of a book and claims about the book's entire composition. A conventional book title does not settle authorship questions.
- Dates explicitly stated in a passage differ from proposed modern dates or a broad chart era.
- Amos names rulers of both kingdoms; Jeremiah's opening spans several reigns. A single king field is not an exhaustive ministry chronology.
- Prophetic messages include present accusation, instruction and response, not only future prediction.
- Symbolic actions retain their commanded setting and accompanying explanation. They are not instructions for readers to reproduce them.
- Huldah demonstrates prophetic speech preserved in narrative without a book bearing her name.
- James's remembrance is read as exhortation, not as a complete biography or a guarantee of identical experience.
- No site changes, corrected records, new timeline, builds or design.

## Supporting material and review

[Passage notes](passage-notes.md) record sources and limits; [existing-material.md](existing-material.md) documents the bounded inventory. Detailed authorship, compositional history and absolute chronology remain separately sourced research tasks. The draft does not claim to audit all 64 prophet records.

Study 33 introduces the prophetic collection; 36 explains person evidence; 37 explains later remembrance. Study 39 will develop rulers' responses and decisions in greater depth. This study keeps its focus on the prophet's setting and message.
