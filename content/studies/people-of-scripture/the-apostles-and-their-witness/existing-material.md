## Useful distinctions to preserve

The dossiers distinguish Scripture, inferences from the text, early-church material, tradition and scholarly views. They contain calling passages, moments, Acts material, places, companions, endings, writings, questions and statements of what is not said. A category label or short preview should not replace the supporting passage.

The Matthias record keeps his explicit appointment separate from subsequent collective references. Peter's material separates Gospel encounters, Acts scenes, letter references and later claims about Rome. James's record gives the explicit Acts 12 death. Paul's record contains both narrated events and proposed later chronologies; those are not interchangeable. The study uses directly read biblical passages to make its claims.

The existing fourteen-profile collection is the Twelve plus Matthias and Paul. Acts 14:14 also names Barnabas an apostle. The collection's scope must not be converted into an exhaustive biblical count of everyone called an apostle.

## What this work does not certify

This inventory does not audit every dossier sentence, identification, scholarly citation, date or later tradition. It does not endorse the broad introductory sentence in `apostles-1.json` that what became of the other six after Acts 1 comes only from later writers: Peter and John have explicit later Acts material in the same resource. The study uses the narrower supported distinction between particular biblical records and later accounts of journeys or deaths. No existing person data is changed during this content-only pass.

The existing biography and presentation files remain the reuse base. This new study supplies a connected reading sequence across them. See [content](content.md) and [passage notes](passage-notes.md).
