# The apostles and their witness working notes

Study 40 · People of Scripture · Content draft ready · 2026-10-13

## Purpose and reader

Follow selected apostles through the Gospels, Acts and the letters, asking how their calling, formation, witness and care for churches relate. Intended for readers who know some names but need more than a directory or travel itinerary. Central question: What do these accounts tell us about the people Jesus sent and the witness they bore?

## Content decisions

The accepted six divisions remain: calling; learning from Jesus; witness; journeys; letters; what the sources do not say. Each has two explanatory subdivisions, a main reading, a full KJV anchor verse, a consideration question and a reflective question. These are content divisions only.

- Calling distinguishes the Twelve, Matthias's appointment and Paul's own account; it does not impose Acts 1's appointment conditions on every use of apostle.
- Learning keeps ambition, correction and dependence on Jesus in view.
- Witness attends to the content of the proclamation as well as courage under opposition.
- Journeys uses Barnabas and Paul together; Acts 14:14 names both apostles. Travel includes return visits and care for churches.
- Letters starts with named senders, readers and the communication's purpose. First Thessalonians supplies an integrated example rather than isolated sayings.
- Limits uses John 21's correction of a circulating claim. Contrasting narrative endings illustrates differing evidence without adopting later death traditions.

## Boundaries and review questions

This is a selective study, not fourteen complete biographies. Do not equate all disciples with the Twelve, give every apostle a surviving letter, or silently identify people with shared names. No new absolute chronology, reconstructed route, book attribution argument or later martyrdom claim is introduced. The beloved disciple remains designated as the passage designates him.

Historical authorship questions and later traditions remain separate research subjects. The draft describes letters' self-presentation without claiming to settle those debates. It does not decide later ecclesial questions about apostolic office or succession. Owner review can determine whether a subsequent expansion should compare more individual lives.
