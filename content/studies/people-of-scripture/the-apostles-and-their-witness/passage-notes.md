## Context and limits

1. **Calling:** Mark 3 gives being with Jesus and being sent together. Luke explicitly distinguishes twelve chosen from disciples. Acts 1 concerns replacing Judas in that company; the draft does not universalize the conditions or suggest Matthias's appointment was invalid. Paul's own retrospective account supplies his distinct place. The original Twelve, Matthias and Paul are a useful profile collection, not an exhaustive definition of every New Testament use of apostle.
2. **Learning:** Keep James and John's request within Mark's wording; do not silently replace it with another Gospel's telling. Luke's prayer and prediction concerning Peter and John's charge to feed the sheep remain attributed to their respective accounts. Reflection concerns service and dependence, not guaranteed restoration to any particular present-day office.
3. **Witness:** Acts 4's refusal answers an order against speaking in Jesus' name; it is not blanket immunity for claims made with personal certainty. First Corinthians 15's appearances belong to Paul's argument about resurrection. The draft neither manufactures independent evidence nor claims this short study proves every historical question.
4. **Journeys:** Acts 13 names a praying church and the Spirit's sending. Acts 14:14 explicitly calls Barnabas and Paul apostles. Distinguish the Antioch in the return itinerary from the sending church's Antioch when developing future detailed routes; no new map or precise chronology is proposed. The Lystra crowd's deification is corrected by the messengers themselves.
5. **Letters:** First Thessalonians names Paul, Silvanus and Timotheus; the plural voice should not be automatically narrowed to Paul. This observation does not assign every writer an identical compositional role. The KJV's `feebleminded` in 5:14 is paraphrased as fainthearted in explanation, not presented as a quotation or clinical diagnosis. First Peter's opening is described as the letter's stated address; historical authorship debates are not settled here.
6. **Limits:** John 21 explicitly corrects the inference that the beloved disciple would not die. The draft does not equate him with a named person as though this paragraph named him. John explains Jesus' saying about Peter's death without narrating an execution. Acts 12 names the murdered James as John's brother. Acts 28 stops short of narrating Paul's death. These are observations about what these passages report, not arguments that later events did not occur.

## Source use and further work

The local KJV is the primary text for this draft. Selected existing apostle records provided a reuse inventory and reference leads; their later traditions and scholarly reconstructions are not adopted here. No Josephus material is used. A future expansion on extra-biblical traditions would require reading the named original sources and assessing their claims separately.

See [existing material](existing-material.md) for the inspection boundary and [working notes](working.md) for scope and handoff. All six readings remain distinct rather than stitched into a single continuous life.
