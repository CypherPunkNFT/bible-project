## Direction

Teach one habit: of any reign, ask who says so, who paid, and what came after. A verdict is short, and the choices behind it are not. Only one direction was built, as with the earlier mock-ups, because this was an existing draft.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the three concepts, the title, the lead, Deuteronomy 17:18–20, and a drawing in thin line: a crown on an open scroll, with a line letting itself down and drawing back. A visible button pauses it. The motto, the figures with their meanings, and what the page chose.
- **01 Two beams, and a line for every ruler.** The signature. Israel's 19 and Judah's 20 hang from two beams in the order Kings tells them. A string is as long as the reign the text states. Its end is the writer's verdict by shape: a charge, did right, both said, or no line. Threads above the beam run back to Jeroboam son of Nebat or to David, who is a peg and is not counted. A tick marks a note under the line. Pointing reads the verdict; choosing opens a record in the sheet.
- **02 Six readings.** A rail: Solomon, Rehoboam, Jeroboam, Ahab, David, Manasseh. Each opens in a form its own text gives: two forks, three voices on one decision, four claims on one plot, a story and whose story it was, and two accounts in their own order. Then the same three questions, and the reading itself.
- **03 Beyond Kings.** Six kings by switch, David, Solomon, Abijah, Asa, Joash and Uzziah, with stops running down two threads that join where both accounts tell it and part where one does. Then three royal letters, one row for each hand: David to Joab, Jezebel to the elders, Haman under Xerxes, and Esther's turn.
- **Six hard questions,** on the one sheet.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, the passage again, links back, a far land, and the sources.

## Choices made, and why

- **The string is the stated reign, and nothing else.** No years or dates anywhere. The two beams do not line up, and the caption says so. Six reigns under two years are drawn longer than scale and marked.
- **The verdict's shape comes from its words.** The build reads the shape from the verse. Where Kings speaks differently, as with Rehoboam, Abijam and Elah, the record gives the real words and says so.
- **Two names come back most, and do different work.** Only those two have threads, and other names are on the rulers' records. The page does not offer a theory of why the writer chose them.
- **A fork only where the text names two ways.** A reading does not get a choice its text does not give.
- **Colour never carries meaning alone.** History's orange, with shapes and words carrying the verdict.
- **Unlike the sibling People pages.** Prophets in their times is rows, a rule and a record. This is hanging lines and forks. On a phone, where the beams become rows, they hang as cords from a spine with the verdict's words.
