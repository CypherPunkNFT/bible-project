# Rulers and their choices working notes

**Item 39 · People of Scripture · Rewritten to the study standard · 2026-10-30.** The first draft of 2026-10-13, in the King James, is replaced here.

## Purpose and central question

Rulers are told through what they chose and what the accounts say of it. What does each writer actually say of a reign, who paid for it, and what came after? Ask those three things of any reign, and let no one's beginning, or one verdict, stand for the whole.

## Content divisions

1. Accession: Solomon's request and his later turn.
2. Counsel: Rehoboam, the people's request, two sets of advisers.
3. Worship: Jeroboam, what he said in his heart, and the verdict.
4. Justice: Ahab, Jezebel and Naboth's vineyard.
5. Correction: David, Nathan, forgiveness and consequences.
6. The verdict: Manasseh in Kings and in Chronicles.

## Decisions

- **Widened as [the to-do list](../../../../../Pages/Topics/TODO.md) asked:** the Chronicler's retelling is in section 03, with six kings followed through both accounts. The Persian court of Esther is in the third of three royal letters.
- **All 39 rulers are drawn, not only the six worked examples.** The draft's selection stays as the readings. The beams give the rest.
- **The habit is three questions:** who says so, who paid, what came after. The draft's rule to keep narrator, ruler and prophet as distinct voices became the first of them.
- **The people harmed are named as paying.** Naboth, Uriah, Bathsheba and the child are not props in a ruler's development.
- **Kings and Chronicles are read each in its own order.** A stop told once is an addition, never a correction, following Article 1 of the Baptist Faith and Message.
- **David stays the measure and his sin stays on the page.** 1 Kings 15:5 says both, in one verse.
- **Hard questions show views by name.** Rehoboam, the census and Ahab each give two commentaries, labelled as not Baptist, with this study's reading marked as such.
- **Authority.** Romans 13 and 1 Timothy 2 are given, with Acts 5:29 and Article 17, and no comparison with any present leader.
- **The "Try three reigns" section was cut after review.** It repeated the frame a third time. Hezekiah, Josiah and Jehu remain reachable on the beams.
- **The King James is gone.** Every verse, phrase and link is now in the Berean.

## Boundaries kept from the first draft

- A wise beginning does not summarize a reign, and a later humbling does not erase harm.
- Ahab's response in 1 Kings 21:27–29 stays beside the condemnation.
- Jeroboam's stated motive is reported, and it is not made a general psychology of rulers.
- No modern political scoring system, comparison with a present leader, absolute chronology or legal guidance.
