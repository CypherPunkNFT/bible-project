# 39 — Rulers and their choices

First content draft ready for owner review · 2026-10-13 · Not final-copy approval.

## Purpose and developed scope

Study rulers through their actions and the accounts' judgments, preserving each biblical writer's perspective. [The draft](content.md) develops six accepted units, each with two subdivisions: accession, decisions, worship, justice, prophetic encounters and verdict.

Examples: Solomon's request and later departure; Rehoboam's counsel; Jeroboam's worship arrangements; Ahab and Naboth; David and Nathan; Manasseh in Kings and Chronicles. These kings are a worked selection, not a replacement for the existing broader collection of rulers, judges, governors and emperors.

## Editorial boundaries

- Retain narrator, ruler and prophet as distinct voices.
- A wise beginning does not summarize an entire reign; a later humbling does not erase harm.
- Include Ahab's response in 1 Kings 21:27–29, not only the condemnation.
- Preserve the people harmed; do not turn Naboth or Uriah into mere props for a ruler's character development.
- Report the stated motive in Jeroboam's case; do not generalize speculative psychology across all reigns.
- Kings and Chronicles are read in their own sequences before consolidation.
- No modern political scoring system, present-day leader comparison, absolute chronology or legal guidance is introduced.
- No website, dataset, design, build or deployment changes.
