# 37 — Long Memory

First content draft ready for owner review · 2026-10-13 · Not final-copy approval.

## Purpose and developed scope

Follow how a person is named, speaks, acts and is remembered across Scripture. [The draft](content.md) develops mentions, speech, moments, remembrance, passage context and counting limits, with two subdivisions per section. The first four preserve the existing feature's study methods; the final two explain their responsible use.

Examples use existing selections: Moses at the bush and in Hebrews/Revelation; Peter in John 21; David in Acts 2; Joshua in Hebrews 4. No replacement set of ten people or new ranking is proposed.

## Content decisions

- Distinct indexed verses, name occurrences and selected passage verses are different quantities.
- Words spoken selections may contain other speakers and narrative; retain attribution.
- Pivotal moments and later remembrance are editorial selections, currently two passages per lens per person.
- Later remembrance is not a composition-date claim. One current Paul selection is a recollection in his own letter, so do not define the view as exclusively other people remembering him.
- Explicit interpretation in Acts 2 differs from an editorial suggested cross-reference.
- KJV Hebrews 4:8 requires explaining Jesus as Joshua; do not use raw string matching as identity evidence.
- No theological ranking follows from reference totals.
- No website changes, data regeneration, design, builds or deployment.

## Reuse and open questions

[Existing-material.md](existing-material.md) records the ten people, checked local counts, selected lens coverage and build rules. [Passage notes](passage-notes.md) document sources and interpretive limits. The audit checks consistency of stored counts, not every upstream person assignment.

Review whether a later publication should move snapshot counts into a maintained note so they cannot drift from generated data. Detailed treatment of Moses' departure in Hebrews 11:27 remains outside this orientation study; no preferred reconciliation is asserted.
