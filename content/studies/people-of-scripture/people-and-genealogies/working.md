# 36 — People and genealogies

First content draft ready for owner review · 2026-10-13 · Not final-copy approval.

## Purpose and developed scope

Explore lives, relationships and family connections named in Scripture. [The draft](content.md) develops six sections, each with two subdivisions: individual lives, families, shared names, relationships, narrative setting and limits of identification. It explains how to use existing person/family information without duplicating the directory or redesigning its charts.

Worked examples: Ruth and Naomi; Matthew's genealogy; the two Simons in Acts 10; Timothy's family and companions; Moses in Exodus and Stephen's telling; Matthew and Luke read separately.

## Boundaries

- Person summaries and relationship links are research aids, not additional Scripture.
- Retain the kind of relationship: named parent, listed ancestry, spouse, household connection, host or companion.
- Do not infer complete biographies or immediate biological links from every chart edge.
- Gospel genealogies remain separate accounts before any harmonization is attempted. No preferred reconciliation is asserted.
- Shared names require identification evidence. Unknown parents and uncertain connections remain visible.
- Later traditions require their own attribution and sources; this draft uses biblical passages only.
- No website changes, data corrections, designs, builds or deployment.
