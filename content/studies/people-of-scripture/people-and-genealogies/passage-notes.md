## Context and limits

1. **Ruth 1:1–18; 4:13–22.** The speech is to Naomi after bereavement; the concluding household and genealogy widen the account. No motives beyond what speech/actions support are supplied. The draft directs readers to intervening chapters rather than pretending two excerpts exhaust the life.
2. **Ruth 4:17–22; Matthew 1:1–17.** Named links through Ruth, Obed, Jesse and David are visible. Matthew's wording in 1:16 differs from preceding formulas; keep Joseph's marriage to Mary and Jesus' birth of Mary explicit. Verse 17 gives Matthew's grouping; the draft does not infer a complete chronological census from it.
3. **Acts 10:1–8.** Verses 5–6 explicitly distinguish Simon Peter from Simon the tanner, his host. No uncertain association with another Simon is needed. This is a worked identification example, not a complete list of people called Simon.
4. **Acts 16:1–3; 2 Timothy 1:3–7.** Acts describes Timothy's mother and father; Second Timothy names Lois and Eunice. The father is unnamed in these passages. Do not add his beliefs, later fate or a complete family reconstruction. Family faith is not treated as inherited automatically.
5. **Exodus 2:1–15; Acts 7:20–29.** Separate the original narrative from Stephen's speech. Acts supplies education, age and Moses' supposition about deliverance. Exodus reports becoming Pharaoh's daughter's son. No legal system of adoption is reconstructed and no Egyptian royal name supplied.
6. **Matthew 1:1–17; Luke 3:23–38.** The different directions, Jacob/Heli and Solomon/Nathan are explicit. Luke does not name Mary in the genealogy. The draft identifies rather than settles harmonization questions. No contemporary scholarly consensus, source-language grammatical proof or traditional legal mechanism is claimed.
