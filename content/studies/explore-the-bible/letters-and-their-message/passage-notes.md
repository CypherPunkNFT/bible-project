## Context and claim limits

1. **Philemon 1–7; 1 Corinthians 1:10–13; Philippians 1:1–11.** Philemon's address includes others and a house church; the main appeal addresses Philemon. Avoid treating every singular request as a universal communal duty. Corinthians' divisions are explicitly named; no reconstruction of factions beyond those verses is necessary.
2. **1 Corinthians 1:11; 7:1–7; Galatians 1:6–12; Jude 3–4; Philemon 18.** Distinguish reports from questions sent in writing. Do not assume every later “Now concerning” reproduces a specific question verbatim. Jude's words establish an urgent reason for writing, not necessarily a recoverable prior draft. Galatians names troubling teaching but does not preserve a full opposing document. Philemon 18 is conditional.
3. **All 25 verses of Philemon.** The movement from the saints being refreshed (7) to Paul's request for refreshing (20) supports the relational reading. The appeal includes willingness (14), brotherhood (16), reception (17) and debt (18–19). Do not add a theft, a manumission record or Philemon's eventual response. Applying the letter to reconciliation must not erase coercion or imply all present relationships share its circumstances.
4. **Romans 3:21–31; 6:1–4; Galatians 5:1–6; James 2:14–26.** Include James's Abraham/Rahab argument and 2:24, not just the easier example about food. Include Paul's rejection of continuing in sin. The draft proposes a reading method, not an exhaustive reconciliation of differing uses of justification, faith and works. Any later theological synthesis must state its interpretive steps.
5. **Philemon; 1 John 1:1–4; Hebrews 1:1–4; 13:20–25; Colossians 4:7–18.** Varied forms are observed from these openings and endings. First John's conventional title is not a claim that its opening names its author. Colossians explicitly sends messengers and describes Epaphras's prayer; avoid expanding the named relationships into unsupported biographies.
6. **Colossians 4:16; 1 Thessalonians 5:25–28; 2 Peter 3:15–18.** Communal reading and exchange are explicit. The epistle from Laodicea is not identified here. Second Peter compares distortion of Paul's letters with distortion of the other Scriptures; do not infer an exact complete list of letters known to its readers or the full later history of the New Testament collection.
