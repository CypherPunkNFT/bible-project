# 32 — Letters and their message

First content draft ready for owner review · 2026-10-13 · Not final-copy approval.

## Purpose and scope

Explore the 21 New Testament letters as complete communications. Central question: how do audience, occasion, argument and form shape the response a letter seeks? The [reader-facing draft](content.md) supplies six developed studies; the [coverage map](letter-map.md) connects all 21 letters to the existing collection. It does not create a competing set of historical introductions.

## Developed sections

1. Recipients: personal and congregational address.
2. Occasions: stated reasons, reports, questions and limits of reconstruction.
3. Arguments: Philemon read from greeting through appeal to closing words.
4. Shared themes: faith, works and love read within their separate arguments.
5. Letter form: varied openings, prayers, messengers and greetings.
6. Reception: explicit instructions for communal reading and the caution about difficult passages.

Two content subdivisions per section; no decision about visual rows, menus or interactions.

## Decisions and boundaries

- Existing letter outlines and occasion fields were inspected for all 21 letters. Reuse their passage leads; do not import their external historical claims without source review.
- Philemon is the complete-letter worked example. Other passages demonstrate methods; they are not substitutes for complete reading of those letters.
- Faith and works comparison identifies textual questions rather than claiming to settle denominational accounts of justification.
- Philemon's conditional debt statement does not establish theft, and his response is not recorded. No application should make the letter a demand that a person return to an abusive situation.
- Reception here means the reading and sharing attested within the letters. Later collection, canon and historical reception require separately sourced development.
- Authorship and date debates remain in the existing research trail. Grouping letters for inventory does not settle disputed authorship.
- No website changes, build or design work.
