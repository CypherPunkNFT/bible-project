## Context and boundaries

1. **Exodus 19:1–6; 20:1–3; Deuteronomy 6:4–12.** The order of deliverance and instruction is explicit in these passages. Do not turn that observation into an unqualified account of every covenant question. Deuteronomy names Israel and warns against forgetting amid received benefits.
2. **Joshua 1:1–9; 2 Kings 17:7–15; Ezra 1:1–4.** Joshua's charge names Moses and the law. Do not detach its success language into a universal guarantee. Kings explains wrongdoing in relation to exodus, covenant and prophets; it is the narrator's evaluation, not merely a character's claim. Ezra explicitly mentions Jeremiah and Cyrus; no precise chronology or external identification is added.
3. **Psalm 78:1–8; Psalm 13; Proverbs 26:4–5; Job 42:7–9.** Psalm 78 states why the past is taught to children. Psalm 13 is read in full as lament, petition and trust. The paired proverbs give distinct reasons for the two instructions; the discernment reading is an editorial explanation, not extra wording in the verses. Job's closing rebuke concerns Eliphaz and his two friends; do not expand it to every speaker or conclude that every individual sentence they utter is false.
4. **Isaiah 1:10–20; Jeremiah 31:31–34; 2 Kings 17:13–15.** Isaiah's worship critique and commands to cease evil belong together. Do not present it as rejection of all worship or as a denunciation of every Israelite in every time. Jeremiah names Israel and Judah and recalls a broken covenant. Later theological application must keep those words visible.
5. **Luke 24:44–49; Acts 1:1–11.** Luke explicitly names law, prophets and psalms concerning Jesus; the draft does not use the wording to assert a fully specified ancient table of contents. Acts' address to Theophilus and recollection of a former treatise support reading it with Luke. Do not infer that the order of books supplies a complete chronology of every letter.
6. **Ephesians 4:1–6; Revelation 1:1–11; 21:1–5; 22:1–5; Genesis 2:8–10.** Ephesians links a shared confession to conduct. Revelation identifies churches, prophecy, visions, tribulation and keeping the words. River/tree comparisons with Genesis are editorial comparisons of evident imagery, not claims that Revelation here explicitly introduces a quotation with a source name. No scheme of end-time chronology is proposed.

## Evidence types to keep visible

- Explicit reference: Joshua to Moses' law; Ezra to Jeremiah; Jesus to earlier Scripture.
- Recollection within another form: Psalm 78's account of what the fathers told.
- Editorial comparison: Genesis 2 and Revelation 22's river and tree of life.
- Reading aid: grouping books or measuring their lengths. This is not itself an interpretation of a passage.
