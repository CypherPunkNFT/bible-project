# Passage notes: The story of the Bible: from the garden to the city (55)

## What this study owns

**Nothing.** By the plan's row, this study is the shelf's map and owns no passage. It walks no passage at length (no reading of five verses or more is explained line by line as an owner would). Each movement quotes a few supplied verses and links the study that owns that strand. The longer block quotations (2 Samuel 7:12–16, Jeremiah 31:31–34, Revelation 21:1–5) are each quoted once, with a short note on the key words only, and the reader is sent to the owning study for the walk. The editor may wish to confirm this stays on the right side of "walking" (see open questions).

## Passages quoted, their context, readings and links

### Genesis 3:15 (owner: Humanity, sin and renewal, 29)
- Context: God's words to the serpent after the fall, before the judgements on the woman and the man.
- Readings: Jewish readers commonly read it of the lasting struggle between humanity and the serpent / evil; some Targums connect it with the days of the Messiah. Christian readers since Irenaeus read the woman's offspring as Christ ("first gospel", protoevangelium). The text names no individual; the New Testament echoes it (Romans 16:20) but does not quote it.
- Links: *Humanity, sin and renewal*; *Genesis 1–11: the questions readers bring*.

### Genesis 12:1–3 (owner: Covenants and promises, 26, which owns 12:1–7)
- Context: God's call to Abram immediately after Babel (Genesis 11).
- Readings: All traditions read it as the founding promise to Abraham. Paul reads it as the gospel announced in advance (Galatians 3:8). 50 quotes 12:1–3; this study quotes it as allowed by the row.
- Links: *Covenants and promises*.

### Exodus 19:4–6 (owner: Covenants and promises, 26)
- Context: God's message at Sinai before the covenant is given; rescue precedes command.
- Readings: In Jewish tradition, the Sinai covenant and the giving of Torah (remembered at Shavuot). Christian readers note 1 Peter 2:9 applying the phrases to the church (owned by *The church and its calling*, 28). How Israel and the church relate is left open among Baptists.
- Links: *Covenants and promises*; *The Ten Commandments*; *Christ in the Old Testament* (owner of Exodus 12, Passover).

### Joshua 21:45 (supplied 21:43–45; only 21:45 quoted)
- Context: Summary at the end of the land distribution. Set beside Joshua 13:1 and Judges 1 (referred to, not quoted).
- Gives Joshua its place on the shelf ("place, not home").

### Judges 21:25
- Context: The closing verdict of Judges. Gives Judges its place. Ruth referred to (4:17) without quotation.

### 2 Samuel 7:12–16 (owner: Covenants and promises, 26; Christ in the Old Testament, 41, keeps 7:12–16 as a pair)
- Context: Nathan's oracle in answer to David's wish to build a temple; the "house" wordplay.
- Readings: Fulfilled first in Solomon; verse 14 expects discipline of erring sons. Jewish hope of a Davidic king remains (Amidah). New Testament: Jesus as son of David.
- Links: *Covenants and promises*; *Rulers and their choices*.

### Jeremiah 31:31–34 (owner: Covenants and promises, 26)
- Context: The "Book of Consolation" (Jeremiah 30–33), spoken in the time of exile.
- Readings: Jewish readers read it of Israel's future renewal; Christians read it with Luke 22:20 and Hebrews 8:8–12 (referred to only).
- Links: *Covenants and promises*; *Prophets in their times*.

### Isaiah 49:6 (no owner named in the passage table)
- Context: The second Servant song (Isaiah 49:1–13). Isaiah 49:3 addresses the Servant as "Israel".
- Readings: Jewish: the Servant is Israel, or a faithful remnant, called to be a light to the nations. Christian: applied to Jesus (Luke 2:32) and to the apostolic mission (Acts 13:47). Not Isaiah 53, but the Jewish reading is still named first, in keeping with the site's rule.
- Links: none owned; see *Christ in the Old Testament* for the Servant songs generally.

### 2 Chronicles 36:22–23 and Nehemiah 8:8
- Context: The last verses of Chronicles (the last book of the Hebrew Bible in the Jewish order); the same decree opens Ezra (1:1–3, referred to). Nehemiah 8 is the public reading of the law.
- Gives Chronicles and Ezra–Nehemiah their place on the shelf.
- Links: *Prophets in their times*; *Reading Daniel and Revelation*.

### Galatians 4:4 (owner: Saved by grace, 24)
- Links: *Saved by grace: how God forgives and changes us*.

### Mark 1:14–15 (no owner named in the passage table; theme belongs to *The kingdom he announced*, 15)
- Links: *The kingdom he announced*.

### John 1:14 (owner of John 1:1–18: Who do you say that I am?, 11; 41 quotes 1:14)
- The tabernacle echo of "made His dwelling" is a widely held reading of the Greek word, labelled as what "many readers hear".
- Links: *Who do you say that I am? Who Jesus really is*.

### Acts 1:8 (owner of Acts 1:6–8: The church and its calling, 28; witness study quotes 1:8)
- Readings: first spoken to the disciples; taken by the church as its own task (article XI).
- Links: *The church and its calling*; *Witness and the nations: telling others about Jesus*; *The Holy Spirit* (owner of Acts 2:1–21).

### Revelation 21:1–5 (row names The hope to come, 30, as the strand's owner)
- Readings: echoes of Eden, tabernacle and covenant formula; "the sea was no more" labelled as an interpretation of the image; millennial order left open, as the confession is silent.
- Links: *The hope to come*; *Reading Daniel and Revelation*; *Grief and dying* (care note).
