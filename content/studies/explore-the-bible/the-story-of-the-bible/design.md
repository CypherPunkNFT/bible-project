## Direction

Teach one habit: before you read any verse, ask where you are in the story. The study gives three questions for it (where am I, what has God promised by this point, what is still waiting) and calls itself "the shelf's map". So the page is a map with a *you are here* pin: choose any point in the story and the page answers the three questions for that point. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

The shape comes from the text (stage 2 of the study prompt, "Scripture remembering itself" and "a whole-Bible thread"). The study's last movement says the Bible's last pages answer its first ones, and it names the promises that run between them: the woman's offspring, the blessing for every family, a land, God living among His people, a king from David's line, and "they will be My people". Each is made at one point in the story and kept, or still waiting, at a later one. The page draws those six threads under one road, and only where the study itself draws them.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail (Study / Explore the Bible / the title), the eight movements in gold capitals (Garden · Promise · People · King · Exile · Christ · Nations · City), the title *The story* with *of the Bible* as the gold italic turn, *From the garden to the city* under it, the lead, and Genesis 12:3 printed in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: a garden at the lower left with one tree and a river, a road climbing from it, and at the upper right a walled city with its gates open and a tree beside a river inside. No person is drawn: no face or figure of God, of Christ or of anyone. The motto: "A garden lost. A city given. / One promise, kept."
- **Figures.** Four computed counts, each with what it means: the eight movements, the books the map cites out of sixty-six, the six promises followed, and the verses printed in full.
- **00 Where am I?** The frame paragraph, then the map. The eight movements stand as stations on one road from the garden to the city, each coloured by the part of the Bible its readings come from (History, the Prophets, the Gospels, Acts, Revelation), and each also named in words. Under the road run six threads. A filled mark is where a promise is kept, an open ring where it is made, a dashed stretch where it waits, a cross-bar where it is lost from view, and the woman's offspring is dashed all through, labelled as a Christian reading, because the verse names no person. Choose a station and the pin moves to it, and three lines below answer the study's three questions for that point. Point at a mark to read its verse on one line; choose it to open the verse. Every mark is also listed in words beneath. Then the study's selection paragraph, with a small figure of two maps of the same country: the four-part summary (creation, fall, redemption, new creation) set against the eight movements, the alignment labelled as this page's.
- **01–08 The movements.** On wide screens a thin strip of the eight stations stays above the reading, the pin on the part being read; on a phone it is left out. Each part opens with its number, a small drawing, its title, its hook and line, and its main readings. Then the text, with the passages printed in full. Where the study gives a Jewish reading and then a Christian one together, the two stand side by side as two ways of hearing the verse. Notes are labelled by kind: "In Jewish tradition", "This study reads", "Where Baptists differ", "With care". Each part ends with where the map stands at this point (promised so far, still waiting), its signposts to the studies that read this part closely, and Consider and Reflect side by side.
- **Close.** Finding your place in the story: the way of reading, the three questions once more, Nehemiah 8:8, the invitation, the question for the reader's own life, "Read the passage again before you answer.", and Revelation 21:1–5 printed in full beside them. Then the city drawing, the notes still to be checked, the sources, and a link back to Explore the Bible.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean, and puts the reference in the address. The chosen station is in the address too (`?at=05`).

## Colour

The site's fixed section colours carry *where in the Bible you are*: History orange for the first four movements (Genesis to 2 Samuel), the Prophets' blue for the exile, the Gospels' violet for Christ, Acts' colour for the church sent out, Revelation's rose for the city. The build checks that each movement has a main reading in the books of its colour. The site's gold carries the concepts, the title's turn and the thread of the nations, the one thread the study asks the reader to follow from the first page to the last. Colour is never the only carrier: every station is named and every mark has a shape.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: human dignity (article III), salvation only by personal faith in Christ (article IV), Christ's return and judgement (article X), and the call to make disciples of all nations (article XI). Where Baptists differ (the days of creation, how Israel and the church relate, the order of the last things), the page shows the views and does not choose. Genesis 3:15 is marked as a Christian reading, and the Jewish reading is named first, as the study does. The page never says a promise was kept unless the study says it. Christ's death is said to be for the sins of the whole world, never laid on the Jewish people as a people. The care note on grief stays open, not folded away.
