## Direction

Teach one habit: read a vision from the throne outward. Ask first who is on the throne and what the vision explains about itself, then where Scripture has used the picture before, then what it was for. The page is built on two things the text itself notices: Daniel was told to seal his book and John was told not to (Daniel 12:4; Revelation 22:10), and the two books put the throne and the beasts in opposite order. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail (Study / Explore the Bible), the four concepts in gold (visions, pictures, numbers, the throne), the title *Reading Daniel* with *and Revelation* in gold italic, and the lead. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, Daniel 7 as one picture: the churning sea below, with only the backs, wings and horns of the four beasts above the waves, and above them the throne of fire with its wheels and river of fire. The motto is "As I continued to watch, / thrones were set in place." (Daniel 7:9). No face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father; the seat holds only light. Five computed counts, each with what it means: the two books' chapters (12 and 22), Daniel's 200 verses in Aramaic, Revelation's 37 verses that name a throne (the first at 1:4, before any beast), its 31 verses that say "seven", and the page's 226 references.
- **Sealed, then open.** The opening paragraph, then Daniel 12:4 and Revelation 22:10 side by side, the first in the Prophets' blue under a whole seal, the second in Revelation's rose with the seal broken. Then the study's three questions (does the vision explain itself; where has Scripture used this picture before; what was it for), which come back as small tags in the parts that teach them.
- **The two books, chapter by chapter.** One column per chapter, sized by its verses, with a dot for every verse that has the word "throne" above the line and "beast" below it, and the study's own marks on it (the beasts from the sea and the thrones set in Daniel 7, the book sealed, the seven churches, the door open in heaven, the beast from the sea, the book left open). Choosing a chapter lists its verses with either word. A note says plainly that this counts words, not visions: Daniel 4's beasts and Revelation 6:8's "wild beasts" are animals.
- **01–07 down a scroll of seven seals.** On wide screens a rod at the left holds seven seals, each a link to its part, each breaking as its part is reached; on narrower screens it is left out. Each part: its number in a seal, title, hook and main readings, then "What the text shows", "What it means" and "Where the reading stops", told apart by label and rule, with the places readers differ marked in gold, and Consider and Reflect to close. Each part has one figure from its own text: what Revelation calls itself and Daniel's two languages drawn to scale (01); the book's own key to its pictures, heard then seen, and two pictures left unexplained (02); eight echoes of older passages, solid where the Berean names the source and dashed where the two were only read side by side (03); three and a half years three ways, and the seventy weeks read two ways without choosing (04); Nebuchadnezzar's statue and the stone, the two lists of empires, and what the beast "was given" (05); one picture, four ways of reading it, and what all four share (06); Patmos and the seven churches as points, in the order of the letters, each with its promise to the one who overcomes (07).
- **Close.** The way of reading, the reader's question, "Read the passage again before you answer.", the two seals again, both open, the sources and a back link to Explore the Bible.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

Two of the site's fixed section colours, one for each book: the Prophets' blue for Daniel and Revelation's rose for Revelation, so the reader can tell at a glance which book a quotation, a chart column or a seal belongs to. The site's gold marks the concepts and the places where readers differ.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000. Both books are read as God's word (article I): Daniel's setting in the sixth century before Christ is taken as the book gives it, and the later date is reported as others' view. Christ's personal and visible return is stated plainly (article X); the confession names no way of reading Revelation, so the four ways are shown with their holders round one picture and none is chosen. The view that Christ's return has already happened is outside the confession and is not shown as an option. The 1689 confession's line on the papacy is shown as that confession's reading, with the note that the 2000 confession names no such figure. No date for Christ's return is set or hinted at: part 04 closes with Mark 13:32 and Acts 1:7. The figures draw only phrases that are in their verses and only readings and kingdoms the study itself names, so no picture says more than the text.
