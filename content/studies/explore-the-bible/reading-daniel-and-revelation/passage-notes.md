# Passage notes — Reading Daniel and Revelation

Study 49 · Explore the Bible · 2026-11-06 · First content draft. Written as step 12 of the
[next-20 sequence](../../../../../Pages/Topics/NEXT_20_DRAFTS.md). The
[redundancy review](../../../../../Pages/Topics/REDUNDANCY_REVIEW.md) kept this study because how this kind of writing
works is covered nowhere on the site, with one boundary: Study 30, The hope to come, keeps Christ's return.

**Scripture source.** Every Bible quotation in [content.md](content.md) is from the Berean Standard Bible, copied from
the site's own text in [data/text/bsb/](../../../../data/text/bsb/) and checked by script on 2026-11-06: each quoted
piece (a quotation broken by an ellipsis counts as two) was matched word for word, ignoring case, punctuation and
quote marks, against the Berean's verse text and then its footnotes. Of 111 pieces, 91 match Bible text; 4 match the
Berean's own footnotes and are labelled as notes in the draft (the Aramaic note at Daniel 2:4, "Seventy sevens" at
Daniel 9:24, "some manuscripts" read 616 at Revelation 13:18, "Or suddenly or quickly" at Revelation 1:1). The rest are

the Second London Baptist Confession (one), the Baptist Faith and Message (two phrases), Criswell (the single word

"looked and", and now reads "I looked and saw a door standing open in heaven". The check proves the words are the
Berean's; it does not prove that each reference is the right verse (checked by hand when each was copied) or that the
reading of it is right.

**Counts, and how they were made.** From the site's Berean text, by script, 2026-11-06.
- Revelation has 22 chapters and 404 verses.

  Daniel 12:1–13 and Daniel 12:1 with Revelation 1:1–3; Revelation 13:1 with Daniel 7:1–8 and Daniel 7:1 with

  with the counting rule (part 03 says so).
- 42 months of 30 days is 1,260 days: arithmetic, stated as such in part 04.

**Scope.** How apocalyptic writing works, taught through Daniel and Revelation together: what kind of writing it is
(01); visions that explain their own pictures, and the heard-then-seen pattern (02); the Old Testament pictures inside
Revelation (03); the numbers, with two readings of Daniel's seventy weeks and Irenaeus on 666 (04); beasts and thrones,
the heart of both books (05); the four ways of reading Revelation, shown without choosing (06); and the comfort the
books were written to give (07). The way of reading taught is three questions: does the vision explain itself; where
has Scripture used this picture before; what was it for.

**Boundaries kept.** Study 30, The hope to come, owns Christ's return, the resurrection, the judgment, hell, the new
creation and the maps of the thousand years (Criswell, Piper, Akin, Waldron, the golden-age view, Boyce): this draft
names Revelation 20 only to point there, quotes nothing from Revelation 20–21 except the number "12,000 stadia" and the
twelve gates and foundations (21:12–17) as an example of twelve, and closes with Revelation 22:20, which Study 30 also
uses; the two can share it. Study 12, Names and titles of Jesus, owns the Son of Man of Daniel 7:13–14 and the Lion and
the Lamb of Revelation 5: part 02 uses the Lion and the Lamb only to show the heard-then-seen pattern and links there;
part 05 names the Son of Man in one clause. Angels and spiritual powers (Michael, Gabriel, the "prince of the kingdom of
Persia" in Daniel 10:13) are left to Study 53, Angels, demons and spiritual powers, now being drafted in this sequence.
Daniel 11, the kings of the north and the south, is not walked: it belongs with the coming Academic study Between the
Testaments.

## Context, readings and limits

1. **Sealed and unsealed (opening).** Daniel 12:4 and 9 ("the words are closed up and sealed until the time of the
   end") stand against Revelation 22:10. The contrast is in the text; that John's book opens what Daniel's closed is a
   reading many draw from the pairing, offered in the opening as a way in, not as a claim the text makes in so many
   words. The Berean itself pairs Daniel 12 with Revelation 1:1–3.
2. **Daniel's two halves and two languages (01).** Stories in the third person (1–6) and visions in the first person
   (7–12) is a plain description; chapter 7 begins in the third person (7:1) and turns to "I, Daniel" (7:15). The
   Aramaic section (2:4b–7:28) follows the Berean's note. An earlier wording called Aramaic "the language of the
   empire"; it was cut because no source was read for it.
3. **The date of Daniel (01, 05).** The book's setting runs from the third year of Jehoiakim (about 605 BC, the
   late seventh century) to the third year of Cyrus (10:1). The site's voice takes the book's own setting (1:1, 1:21, 10:1) and Jesus' words
   (Matthew 24:15) with Article 1 of the Baptist Faith and Message. The late date is reported, not argued: Porphyry's
   view is known through Jerome (Preface to Daniel; and the editor's note in the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, second
   series, vol. 6, that Porphyry "took the predictions in the Book of Daniel as relating to the time of Antiochus
   Epiphanes and the Maccabees, and written near that date", adding "like most modern critical scholars"). That
   Porphyry took the fourth kingdom to be Alexander's successors is reported by Calvin's translator, Thomas Myers, in
   his preface, describing Maldonatus' commentary. Jerome's Preface also says "Daniel in Hebrew is not found among the
   prophets, but amongst the writers of the Hagiographa" (the Writings): a possible fact for part 01, not used.
4. **The 144,000 and the multitude (02).** Two readings shown: the same people heard and then seen (no named holder
   read for this draft; **needs a named source**, see open questions), and Criswell's two visions, Israel then the
   Gentiles (sermon "The One Hundred Forty-Four Thousand", 1962). Not chosen.
5. **Symbol and plain sense (02).** Warfield's principle of symbolism is quoted; Criswell's objection to
   "spiritualizing" is told in the site's words with one word quoted. Both views agree that the book's own
   explanations come first; that agreement is the site's summary, not a statement either man makes.
6. **Old Testament pictures (03).** Each echo given is one where the wording is close (lion, calf, man, eagle; sweet as
   honey; olive trees and lampstands; water to blood; the song of Moses). The Ezekiel 3:3 and Zechariah 4:14 links are
   in the Berean's own notes; the others are from reading the passages side by side. "Revelation never once says 'as it
   is written'": checked by script; the phrase does not occur in the Berean text of Revelation.
7. **Three and a half (04).** "Three and a half" is the reader's sum for "a time, and times, and half a time", taken as
   one, two and a half; it is the common reading, and part 04 treats Revelation 12:6 and 12:14 as one flight because
   both describe the woman's place in the wilderness. Whether the spans are literal years is left open.
8. **Seventy weeks (04).** Two readings shown without choosing: fulfilled at Christ's first coming (Calvin) and a
   seventieth week still to come (Criswell, "The Times of the Gentiles", 1967; also "Measuring the Temple of God",
   1962, and "Understanding Prophecy", 1985). A third reading, that the weeks end in the second century BC,
   belongs with the late date and is not shown.
9. **666 (04).** Irenaeus is quoted for his caution. His Greek names are given as the translation spells them, except
   that the translation writes "Evanthas" in the text and "EUANThAS" in capitals; the draft gives "Euanthas". The
   sentence in Irenaeus that blames copyists for 616 is bracketed in the Ante-Nicene Fathers and called an
   interpolation in its footnote 4701, so the draft does not use it; it uses only his statement that 666 stood in "all
   the most approved and ancient copies".
10. **Which empires (05).** Daniel names Babylon (2:38), Media and Persia, and Greece (8:20–21); the fourth is not
    named. Calvin's four (Babylon, Persia, Greece, Rome) and the late-date ending in the Greek kingdoms are both shown. That Media
    and Persia make one kingdom is itself part of the traditional reading; late-date readers count them as two, so that
    their fourth is Greece, which 8:21 names. "Like Jerome before him" rests on Myers' preface, which reports that Jerome
    took the fourth beast of chapter 7 as the Roman empire; Jerome's own commentary was not read.
    The draft does not say which Baptists hold which; Criswell's identification was not checked.
11. **Four ways of reading (06).** Shown without choosing, with holders read directly where possible: preterist
    (Moses Stuart, **through Barnes's summary only**); historicist (the 1689 confession, on 2 Thessalonians 2, and the
    Reformers as Criswell describes them); futurist (Criswell); recapitulation or idealist (Warfield). Criswell's
    descriptions of the other three are an opponent's, and the draft quotes him only for his own view and his liking
    the last least. The school he rejected he described as allegory tied to no event; the draft says so, and notes that
    Warfield's recapitulation reading is of real history, so that Criswell's objection is not read as aimed at Warfield.
    Irenaeus is described as leaning toward Teitan (he calls it the name with "a strong degree of probability") while
    refusing to pronounce "positively". Criswell also called the preterist view mostly Roman Catholic; the draft does not repeat that.
12. **The date of Revelation (06).** Irenaeus, "towards the end of Domitian's reign" (Domitian reigned AD 81–96, as
    Criswell's sermon also gives), is the main early witness for a date in the 90s; Barnes reports that Stuart dated
    the book before the fall of Jerusalem. The draft states both and does not choose.
13. **Comfort (07).** The reading that the books were written to sustain God's people under empire is drawn from the
    text (Revelation 2:10; 13:10; Daniel 3; 6) and is held across all four ways of reading; Calvin says of Daniel 2
    that the dream was "for the consolation and support of the remnant of the faithful".

## Doctrine

- **Article I (the Scriptures):** both books are treated as God's word. Daniel's setting (late seventh and sixth centuries BC) is taken as the
  book gives it; the second-century date is reported as others' view, not as a correction. Revelation's authorship by
  John (1:1, 4, 9; 22:8) is stated as the book states it; which John (the apostle, as Irenaeus held, or another) is
  not raised.
- **Article X (last things):** Christ's personal and visible return is stated plainly in the site's voice (06). The
  confession names no way of reading Revelation and no millennial view: the four ways are shown with holders and none
  is chosen, as Study 30 does for the thousand years. The view that Christ's return has already happened (sometimes
  called full preterism) is outside the confession and is not shown as an option; part 06 says that those who read
  Revelation mostly as past "within the Baptist Faith and Message still look for Christ's personal return".
- **Other traditions:** the 1689 confession's identification of the papacy is quoted as that confession's reading,
  with the note that the Baptist Faith and Message 2000 names no such figure. No Catholic teaching is described.
  Criswell's claim that preterists are mostly Roman Catholic is left out as an opponent's account.
- **Date-setting:** part 04 closes with Mark 13:32 and Acts 1:7. That no number is given to fix the date of Christ's
  return is the site's voice, and is held across all the views shown.
