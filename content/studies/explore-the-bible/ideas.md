# Explore the Bible study ideas

Give the neighbouring category a content workspace while keeping the immediate development priority on Christian Life, Jesus Christ and God and Christian Faith. Begin by inventorying existing material.

These are content proposals for review. Subcards are working content divisions, not site components. Passage lists are starting points for later reading and verification, not completed research.

## 31 Books in their settings

**Purpose:** Introduce each book through its setting, form and unfolding argument.

**General direction:** Inventory existing introductions before drafting additions.

**Possible subcards:** Writer and audience; setting; literary form; structure; central questions; reading the whole book.

**Starting passages or sources:** Candidate passages will be selected book by book.

## 32 Letters and their message

**Purpose:** Explore the 21 New Testament letters as complete communications.

**General direction:** Consolidate the existing letters content instead of starting a competing collection.

**Possible subcards:** Recipients; occasions; arguments; shared themes; letter form; reception.

**Starting passages or sources:** The New Testament epistles, selected letter by letter.

## 33 The shape of Scripture

**Purpose:** Explore connections across books without losing their differences.

**General direction:** Build on the existing structure study.

**Possible subcards:** Law; history; poetry; prophets; Gospels and Acts; letters and Revelation.

**Starting passages or sources:** Passages to be chosen for each literary collection.

## 34 Reading a passage in context

**Purpose:** Develop practical questions for careful reading.

**General direction:** Keep observations, interpretation and application distinguishable.

**Possible subcards:** Paragraph; speaker and audience; genre; argument; earlier Scripture; application.

**Starting passages or sources:** Worked examples to be chosen during content development.

## 35 Versions and translation

**Purpose:** Explain what readers are comparing when they use different Bible editions.

**General direction:** Reuse existing versions material and reserve manuscript-history details for the academic path.

**Possible subcards:** Translation choices; language; edition history; notes; comparison; choosing a reading edition.

**Starting passages or sources:** Passage comparisons to be selected after checking the available editions.
