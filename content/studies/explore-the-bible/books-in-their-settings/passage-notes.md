## Passage decisions

1. **Luke 1:1–4; Philippians 1:1–11; Hebrews 1:1–4; 13:22–25.** Luke names Theophilus and states a purpose. The opening does not itself name Luke: the draft uses the book's conventional name without claiming its preface supplies authorship. Philippians names Paul and Timothy in the greeting. Hebrews' closing identifies relationships without naming the writer. No inference about who wrote Hebrews is offered.
2. **Ruth 1; 4:13–22; Philippians 1:7–30.** The judges period, famine, deaths, return and harvest are explicit. David in the genealogy is not an exact composition date. Philippians speaks of bonds and possible life or death; the draft assigns no prison city or date.
3. **Psalm 13; Jonah 1–2; Hebrews 13:22.** Complaint, petition and trust are a reading of the psalm's sequence, not an asserted universal psalm structure. No invented intervening rescue is supplied. Jonah contains both narrative and prayer. Hebrews names its work a word of exhortation and a letter.
4. **Philippians 1:12–2:11; 4:1–9.** Trace conduct, one mind, lowliness and Christ's mind. Euodias and Syntyche are named in 4:2; their labour in 4:3. The connection is an editorial observation, not proof that their disagreement accounts for every section of the letter.
5. **Jonah 1–4.** Jonah 4:2 gives his own explanation for flight. The king's proclamation calls for turning from violence (3:8); 3:10 reports God's response. The final question has no subsequent answer in the book. Do not invent Jonah's final decision, identify the unnamed king or equate all the people in 4:11 with children without argument.
6. **Ruth 1–4.** The field, threshingfloor and gate are narrative locations, not a prescribed formula for personal guidance. Ruth 4:7 explains a custom within the account; no added legal reconstruction is required. The final genealogy reaches David. The summary is a whole-book reading exercise, not a promise that every bereaved household receives the same temporal outcome.

## Review before any later publication

Keep proposed historical information sourced and visibly distinct from the text. If expanding into individual introductions, give each book its own passage-based scope and unresolved questions; do not fill every author/date field with a guess. Review canon coverage separately from the number of labels present in a code registry. The present draft is ready for content review, not a finished book-introduction library.
