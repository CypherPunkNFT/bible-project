# 31 — Books in their settings

Status: first content draft ready for owner review, 2026-10-13. Not final-copy approval. Content pass only.

## Purpose and deliverables

Introduce books through their setting, form and unfolding argument. [The draft](content.md) develops six worked studies rather than claiming to supply complete introductions to every biblical book. [Passage notes](passage-notes.md) record evidence and boundaries; [the inventory](existing-material.md) identifies reusable work before new introductions are commissioned.

## Developed sequence

1. Writer and audience: Luke's purpose, Philippians' greeting and Hebrews' unnamed writer.
2. Setting: Ruth's narrative period and circumstances; Paul's bonds in Philippians.
3. Literary form: Psalm 13's movement; prayer within Jonah and exhortation in Hebrews.
4. Structure: the appeal for one mind and the mind of Christ in Philippians.
5. Central questions: Jonah's response to mercy and the unanswered final question.
6. Whole-book reading: Ruth from loss to the genealogy; a repeatable introduction exercise.

Each section has two content subdivisions. These are editorial groupings, not commitments to cards, rows, tabs or scrolling behavior.

## Boundaries

- Keep the narrative's period separate from a proposed date of composition.
- Separate textual self-description, traditional attribution and modern reconstruction.
- No historical authorship/date claim imported simply because it appears in existing data.
- Psalm 13 illustrates reading one psalm; it is not an introduction to all Psalms.
- This inventory is a bounded review of named resources, not certification that every introduction in the repository has been audited.
- Broader canon and arrangement belong to 33; detailed passage practice to 34; translation to 35. Letter-specific development continues in 32.
- No site implementation or design decisions in this pass.
