## Direction

Teach one habit: **read the verse before the view.** Every question is laid out the same way, in three tiers that are the study's own three questions: what the text says, the readings Christians give, and where the text stops. The text is set on a solid rule with a filled mark; the readings stand side by side at equal width with open rings, and none is lit; the place where the text stops is a dotted edge. The study's own close names the habit: "Hold the readings with an open hand, and what the text plainly says with a closed one." One direction was built, because the text already existed.

The shape comes from the text (stage 2 of the study prompt, "a question real readers bring"): the questions follow the eleven chapters in order, and the eleven chapters end by narrowing, in one verse, to one man (Genesis 12:1). So the page is laid on the chapters of Genesis themselves.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail (Study / Explore the Bible / the title), the three concepts in gold capitals (the text · later Scripture · the readings), the title *Genesis 1–11* with *the questions readers bring* in gold italic, the opening paragraph as the lead, and Genesis 12:1 printed in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, a line drawing of the whole world in these chapters: the lights of day four over a wide horizon, the ark resting on a ridge of mountains, an unfinished brick tower, and one thin road leaving to the right. The motto: "Eleven chapters. Eight questions. / Read the verse before the view." No person and no figure of God is drawn.
- **Figures.** Four counts, each with what it means: the questions, the readings shown, the verses in Genesis 1–11, and the references outside these chapters where the rest of Scripture takes the questions up.
- **The chapters and the questions.** Genesis 1–11 as eleven blocks, each as wide as its verses, then Genesis 12:1 as one thin verse at the end. Each question is an arc over the verses it reads. Point at an arc to read what it covers; choose it to open that question. All eight are also listed beneath as numbered buttons, so nothing is hidden.
- **How to read these chapters.** The study's scope, then its three questions to carry, each marked with the tier it governs, then the common ground every view holds (Genesis 1:1).
- **01–08, one question at a time.** The question in the address (`?q=05`), with previous and next at its foot. Each opens with its number, its question, its hook and its main readings, then the three tiers:
  - *What the text shows.* The text's own words. Where the text gives data, it is drawn: in 01 the two sets of three days that answer each other, and the seventh without the refrain; in 04 the ages of Genesis 5 and 11, falling after the flood to Abraham, Moses and Psalm 90's seventy or eighty, with Enoch's line left open; in 06 the flood's calendar as a spiral that passes its own start by ten days, with the ark drawn to scale inside it; in 08 the three verses of Genesis 10 that close "according to their languages".
  - *The readings Christians give.* Each reading as a column of equal width, named in bold, with who holds it. The view that Adam is a figure only (02) is marked as shown so the reader can recognise it, and not the site's view. The "curse of Ham" (07) has its own tier: a reading this study refuses, from the text.
  - *Where the reading stops.* The text's edge, in the study's words. In 08 this tier is "Where Scripture takes it": Babel, Abram's name, Pentecost (dashed and labelled a reading, because Acts does not name Babel) and Revelation 7:9.
  - *Later Scripture on this question.* Every reference in the part outside Genesis 1–11, in Bible order, joined by fine rules.
  - Consider and Reflect side by side.
- **Close.** "Read Genesis 1–11 again": the readings with an open hand and the text with a closed one, the question these chapters answer (will God give up on the world He made?), Galatians 3:8, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Genesis 12:1–3 printed in full beside. A closing drawing: the wide horizon narrowing to one road. Then the sources with how far each was read, and a back link to Explore the Bible.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in one sheet, in the Berean, and puts the reference in the address.

## Colour

History orange, because Genesis opens the site's History section (Genesis to Esther). The site's gold carries the concepts, the title's turn and the closed-hand mark of the text tier. Colour is never the only carrier: the tiers are also told apart by shape (filled mark, open rings, dotted edge) and by their names.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000. God as Creator and man as God's special creation (Articles 2 and 3) are said plainly; so is Adam as a real man whose sin brought sin into the human race. The age of the earth, the length of the days, how Adam fits a long history, the extent of the flood, the sons of God and the order of Genesis 10 and 11 are shown with their holders, side by side and at equal weight, and not chosen. No graphic chooses for the reader either: the long-lives chart draws the numbers as the Berean gives them and no line for the "120 years" of Genesis 6:3, which is read two ways; the flood calendar says that its equal months are the drawing's assumption. The "curse of Ham" is refused in the site's voice, from the text, with the dignity of every person of every race (Articles 3 and 15). Every view shown holds the chapters to be true (Article 1).
