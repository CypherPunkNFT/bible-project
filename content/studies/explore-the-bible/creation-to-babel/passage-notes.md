# Passage notes — Genesis 1–11: the questions readers bring

Study 50 · Explore the Bible · 2026-11-06 · First content draft. Written as step 13 of the
[next-20 sequence](../../../../../Pages/Topics/NEXT_20_DRAFTS.md). The folder keeps the planned name
`creation-to-babel`; the title and scope were reshaped on 2026-11-05 after the
[redundancy review](../../../../../Pages/Topics/REDUNDANCY_REVIEW.md): the study takes the hard questions readers bring
to Genesis 1–11, one at a time, and does not retell the story or teach the image of God and the fall as doctrine.

**Scripture source.** Every Bible quotation in [content.md](content.md) is from the Berean Standard Bible, copied from
the site's own text in [data/text/bsb/](../../../../data/text/bsb/) and checked by script on 2026-11-06: each quoted
piece was matched word for word, ignoring case, punctuation and quote marks, against the whole Berean text and then
its footnotes. Of 146 pieces, 123 match Bible text (one of them, "the Creator", is the confession's phrase and happens also to be Bible wording); 4 match the Berean's own footnotes and are labelled as
notes in the draft ("Literally day one" at Genesis 1:5, "Hebrew Adam" at 5:2, the ark's size in feet at 6:15, and Babel
"sounds like the Hebrew for confused" at 11:9; "Babylonia" at 11:2 and "Babylon" at 11:9 are also note words). The
rest are not Bible text and are not expected to match: Augustine (seven pieces), Justin Martyr (one), Calvin (three),
the Baptist Faith and Message (four phrases), three titles of articles, and the phrase "the curse of Ham". Every Augustine, Justin and Calvin
piece was then matched by script against the held texts named under "Outside sources". The check proves the words are
the Berean's; it does not prove that each reference is the right verse (checked by hand as each was copied) or that the
reading of it is right.

**Counts and sums in the draft, and how they were made** (by hand from the Berean text, 2026-11-06):
- Shem's 600 years is 100 at Arphaxad's birth plus 500 after (Genesis 11:10–11).
- "More than a year": from the 17th day of the 2nd month of Noah's 600th year (7:11) to the 27th day of the 2nd month
  of his 601st (8:13–14).
- Genesis 10 speaks of languages three times (10:5, 20, 31); the word "languages" in those three verses only.
- "The curse names Canaan three times" (9:25, 26, 27).
- The draft makes no count of the nations in Genesis 10: counts differ with the counting rule.

**Scope.** Eight questions, each read from the text and then shown with the views Christians hold and who holds them:
the days and the age of the earth (01); Adam and Eve as real people (02); Cain's wife (03); the long lives (04); the sons
of God and the Nephilim (05); the size of the flood and the ark (06); the curse on Canaan (07); the table of nations and
Babel (08). The way of reading taught is three questions: what the text says and leaves unsaid; where the rest of
Scripture takes it up; which part is the text and which a reading. The close turns from the readers' questions to the
one the chapters answer, whether God will give up on the world, through Genesis 12:1–3 and Galatians 3:8.

**Boundaries kept.**
- **Study 29, Humanity, sin and renewal**, owns the image of God (Genesis 1:26–31; 9:6; James 3:9), the fall
  (Genesis 3) and Adam and Christ in Romans 5 as doctrine. Part 02 uses Romans 5:12, 14 and 19 only to show that Paul
  treats Adam as a man in history, and says so; Genesis 9:6 is quoted once, in part 07, for the dignity of every
  person. Genesis 3 is not walked.
- **The Topics card In the beginning** walks the people and families of the era: no part retells the story in order.
- **Study 53, Angels, demons and spiritual powers** (being drafted in this sequence) owns angels: part 05 reads only
  the three views of Genesis 6:1–4 and points there.
- **Study 30, The hope to come**, owns the coming judgment: part 06 quotes 2 Peter 3:6–7 only as the New Testament's
  use of the flood.

## Context, readings and limits

1. **The days (01).** The two sets of three days are a plain observation of the text (light and lights; sky and sea,
   then birds and fish; land and plants, then animals and man); that they are a *framework* rather than a sequence is
   the framework view's reading, labelled as such. Six views are shown, each with a named holder; none is chosen,
   as the [doctrinal foundation](../../../../../DOCTRINAL-FOUNDATION.md) requires ("The confession affirms God as
   Creator and man as specially created; it does not fix a timeline"). Augustine and Calvin are quoted as early
   witnesses, Augustine on both sides of the modern question (the days hard to conceive; not 6,000 years). The young-earth
   reply on Psalm 90:4 and 2 Peter 3:8 is given in the site's words.
2. **Adam (02).** The site's voice holds Adam to be a real man, specially created, whose sin brought sin into the human
   race, which is how the brief reads Article 3 of the Baptist Faith and Message. The confession's own words speak of
   "Man" and do not use the name Adam; the draft quotes its phrases and says the confession treats the first man as a man
   who lived. Inside that, three ways of fitting Adam to a long history are shown without choosing. The view that Adam
   is a figure only (Enns) is shown so the reader can recognise it and is labelled as outside the site's position, the
   way Study 49 handles the view that Christ's return is past.
3. **Cain's wife (03).** The sister-marriage answer is Augustine's, read directly; Leviticus 18:9 and Genesis 20:12 are
   the textual supports. That Genesis 4 "does not keep time by births" is an observation (Seth is born after Abel's
   death, 4:25; Adam is 130, 5:3). La Peyrère is named for the history of the "people before Adam" idea and is not
   offered as a view Christians hold today.
4. **Long lives (04).** Augustine against short years is read directly. Genesis 6:3 is given both readings, with Calvin
   (read directly) for the time-before-the-flood reading and Lactantius named *as Calvin reports him* for the
   lifespan reading; Lactantius himself was not read. The "numbers with a purpose" view has **no named holder** in the
   draft (see open questions). The Sumerian King List comparison is stated generally ("tens of thousands of years");
   no reign length is quoted. The Luke 3:36 Cainan and the Greek Old Testament's extra hundred years rest on the
   Berean's own footnote at Genesis 11:13; the Matthew 1:8 skip is checked against 1 Chronicles 3:11–12 in the
   Berean.
5. **Sons of God (05).** Three views, each with a holder: angels (1 Enoch as cited in the Berean's note at 2 Peter 2:4;
   Justin Martyr, read directly; Heiser, not read); the line of Seth (Augustine and Calvin, both read directly);
   rulers (the Aramaic paraphrase *as Calvin names and rejects it*, and Kline, not read). The draft says the text does
   not quite make the Nephilim the children of the unions ("when", 6:4). That 1 Peter 3:19–20 bears on Genesis 6 is a
   reading some hold; the draft lists 1 Peter 3 among the main readings but makes no claim from it.
6. **The flood (06).** Two views, each holding that the flood happened, judged mankind and saved Noah's family. The
   global view's argument from 9:11 and the regional view's argument from 41:57 are each given in the site's words. The
   Ark Encounter (Williamstown, Kentucky, 2016) is named as a fact about Answers in Genesis; no claim is made about it.
   "A range, not a named peak": Genesis 8:4 says "the mountains of Ararat".
7. **Canaan (07).** The readings of *why Canaan* and *what Ham did* are given without named holders (see open
   questions). The refusal of the "curse of Ham" is the site's own voice, from four things in the text (the curse
   names Canaan; Canaan's descendants are listed in 10:15–19; nothing is said of skin; Ezekiel 18:20) and from
   Article 3 of the Baptist Faith and Message. The 1845 founding over slavery and the 1995 resolution are stated as
   history, paraphrased, not quoted.
8. **Nations and Babel (08).** Chapter 10 as the wide view and chapter 11 as the close view is the common reading,
   with the Peleg note (10:25) read two ways. The scope of "the whole world" (11:1) is shown both ways **without named
   holders** (see open questions). The ziggurat link is labelled as background brought to the text. Pentecost as
   Babel's answer is labelled a reading, because Acts does not name Babel. Genesis 11:4 and 12:2 ("a name") is a plain
   contrast in the text.

## Doctrine

- **Article 1 (the Scriptures):** every view shown holds Genesis 1–11 to be true; the draft says so ("What no view may
  do is treat Genesis 1 as less than true") and shows no view that treats the chapters as error. Background (the
  Sumerian King List, ziggurats) is labelled as background, never as a correction of the text.
- **Article 2 (God) and Article 3 (Man):** God as Creator and man as God's special creation are the site's voice; so is
  Adam as a real man whose sin brought sin into the human race. The age of the earth, the length of the days, how Adam
  fits a long history, the extent of the flood, and the identity of the sons of God are shown with their holders and
  not chosen.
- **Article 3 and Article 15 (the social order):** the "curse of Ham" is refused in the site's voice, with the dignity
  of every person of every race.
