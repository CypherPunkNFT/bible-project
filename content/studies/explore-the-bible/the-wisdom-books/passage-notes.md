# The wisdom books (65): passage notes

## Job 28 (owned)

**Context.** A poem in the middle of the book, after the friends' three rounds of speeches (Job 3–27) and before Job's final defence (Job 29–31) and Elihu (Job 32–37). The Berean prints it within Job's speech that begins at 27:1; there is no new speaker heading.

**Main readings.** (1) Job's own words, a calmer moment in his argument. (2) An interlude in the narrator's or book's own voice, pausing the debate. Both are held by careful readers; the study names both and does not choose. Theological sense is the same either way: wisdom cannot be mined or bought; God knows its place; for humans it is "the fear of the Lord" (28:28). Note the Berean has "Lord" (master), not "LORD", in 28:28; the study explains the difference.

**Links.** Echoed by Proverbs 2:4–6 (seek it like silver; the LORD gives wisdom), walked in part 03. Opening quotation and closing passage of the study.

## Job 38–42 (owned)

**Context.** God's two speeches from the whirlwind (38:1–40:2; 40:6–41:34), Job's two replies (40:3–5; 42:1–6), and the prose ending (42:7–17), which returns to the style of chapters 1–2.

**Main readings.** God gives no explanation of the heavenly council (1–2); the answer is His presence and His rule over creation. Behemoth and Leviathan: natural animals (hippopotamus, crocodile) described poetically, or images of chaos powers God masters (cf. Psalm 74:14; Isaiah 27:1); confession silent, both shown. Job 42:6: the Hebrew is difficult and renderings differ; the study keeps the Berean and notes this. Job 42:7 ("as My servant Job has"): the study's reading, marked as a reading, is that Job spoke to God honestly while the friends turned God into a formula. The ending is presented as God's act for Job, not a general promise.

**Scripture remembering it.** Romans 11:35 quotes Job 41:11; James 5:11 (Job's endurance, the Lord's compassion); Ezekiel 14:14. Referred to, not quoted (not supplied).

**Links.** What Scripture promises the sick (45, owner of Job 1–2); Fear, anxiety and despair (56, owner of Job 3); Angels, demons and spiritual powers (53, Job 1:6–12). The shape of Scripture (33) samples 42:7–9; Genesis 1–11 (50) quotes 38:7.

**Quoted, not walked:** Job 1:21; 2:10 (from 45); Job 3:1, 3 (from 56).

## Proverbs 1–9 (owned)

**Context.** The prologue to the book: a purpose statement (1:1–6), the motto (1:7), a father's talks to his son, Wisdom's speeches (1:20–33; 8), and two banquets (9). Bracketed by 1:7 and 9:10.

**Main readings.** Proverbs 8:22–31: (1) a poem personifying God's own wisdom; (2) a passage Christians have long heard as pointing to Christ (1 Corinthians 1:24; John 1:3; Colossians 1:16 referred to). The Arian use of 8:22 and Athanasius's reply are named; the site, with article II and the Nicene Creed, holds the Son eternal and does not read each line as a statement about Him. The "forbidden woman" chapters (2, 5, 6, 7) are read as a father's warning to a son, applying to all.

**Scripture remembering it.** Hebrews 12:5–6 quotes 3:11–12; James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5 quote 3:34. Referred to.

**Links.** Sexuality, singleness and divorce (43; keeps 5:15–23, pair; this study quotes only 5:18); Money, possessions and generosity (63) for 3:9–10; Christ in the Old Testament (41).

## Proverbs 10, 16, 22:6, 26:4–5 (owned, selected sayings)

**Context.** The two-line sayings that begin at 10:1 ("The proverbs of Solomon"); 22:6 within the collection 10:1–22:16; 26:4–5 within Hezekiah's collection (25:1).

**Main readings.** The study reads the sayings as true generalisations about God's ordered world, not unconditional promises; Job and Ecclesiastes stand beside them as counterweights. 26:4–5 is used to show that proverbs require judgement (with the Talmud's discussion noted). 22:6: read as a proverb, not a guarantee; the meaning of "the way he should go" has been read more than one way. 16:9, 33: providence explained.

**Scripture remembering it.** 1 Peter 4:8 echoes 10:12.

**Links.** Children and the home (60) for 22:6; Grief and dying (57) for 10:27; Money, possessions and generosity (63).

## Ecclesiastes 1–3 and 12 (owned)

**Context.** Superscription and motto (1:1–2), the Teacher's opening reflection and royal experiment (1:3–2:26), the poem of times (3:1–15), injustice and death (3:16–22); in chapter 12, the call to remember the Creator, the picture of old age, and the frame narrator's epilogue (12:9–14).

**Main readings.** Authorship: traditionally Solomon; many scholars a later writer in Solomon's voice; not chosen. Whole-book reading: (1) the "under the sun" view as deliberately limited, set in light by the epilogue; (2) the Teacher as a faithful realist throughout. Not chosen. 3:21 read beside 12:7 and article X. Hevel explained as breath or vapour. 12:1–7 read, with long Jewish and Christian tradition, as images of ageing.

**Scripture remembering it.** Romans 8:20 (the Greek word for futility used in the Greek Old Testament's Ecclesiastes); Luke 12:16–21 echoes 2:18–21. Referred to.

**Links.** The hope to come (30, owner of Romans 8:18–25); Grief and dying (57); The Ten Commandments (61) could be linked from 12:13.

## Song of Songs 1–8 (owned as a book)

**Context.** A collection of love poems with three voices and recurring refrains (2:7; 3:5; 8:4; and 2:16; 6:3; 7:10), moving through longing, searching, a wedding (3:11), the garden (4:12–5:1), and the seal of love (8:6–7).

**Main readings.** Jewish: read at Passover; the Targum reads God and Israel; Rabbi Akiva's saying in the Mishnah. Christian and Baptist: (1) the plain reading of married love (Daniel Akin named, to be checked); (2) the Christ-and-church reading (Origen, Bernard of Clairvaux; among Baptists John Gill and Charles Spurgeon). Confession silent; both shown, not chosen; both agree with article XVIII. The 7:10 echo of Genesis 3:16 is labelled a reading.

**Links.** Sexuality, singleness and divorce (43; quotes 8:6–7 and the Song for marriage, pair).
