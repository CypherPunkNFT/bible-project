# Children and the home (60): passage notes

## Deuteronomy 6:4–9

**Context.** Moses' address on the plains of Moab before Israel enters the land; follows the Ten Commandments restated in Deuteronomy 5. Addressed to the whole people; verse 7 turns to the home.

**Main ways it is read.**
- *Jewish reading and practice:* the opening of the Shema, recited evening and morning (taking up "when you lie down and when you get up"); verses 8–9 kept with tefillin and the mezuzah (which holds 6:4–9 and 11:13–21). Verse 4 is read as the confession of God's oneness.
- *Christian reading:* Jesus names 6:4–5 as the first and greatest commandment (Mark 12:29–30, owned by Leviticus 47 for 12:28–31). Christians confess verse 4 as the oneness of the triune God (BFM article II); the Trinity articles quote 6:4.
- *Literal or figurative signs:* most Christians read verses 8–9 as calling for God's words to shape action and home; Jewish practice keeps them literally as well. The study describes both without ranking.

**Studies linked.** Trinity articles (6:4); The shape of Scripture (33) samples it; The Ten Commandments (61).

## Deuteronomy 6:20–25

**Context.** Instruction for answering a child's question about the commands; the answer is the exodus story.

**Main ways it is read.**
- *Jewish:* the "wise son" of the Passover Haggadah asks in the words of 6:20.
- *Verse 25, "our righteousness":* read by many evangelical interpreters as the right living of a people already rescued (the rescue in verse 21 comes first), not as earning salvation [check: named commentator]. Paul's use of law and faith (Romans 10:5–10) is read by some as contrasting the law's way with faith; the study refers the reader to *Saved by grace*.
- *Verse 24, "prosperous":* covenant well-being of the people in the land; not prosperity teaching (BFM article XIII).

**Studies linked.** Saved by grace (24); Covenants and promises (26); Christ in the Old Testament (41) owns Exodus 12.

## Psalm 78:1–8

**Context.** Opening of a long historical psalm (heading names Asaph; not quoted). Verse 2 is quoted in Matthew 13:35 of Jesus' parables.

**Main ways it is read.** As a charge to hand on God's works across generations (Jewish and Christian alike); verse 8 as a warning that the older generation was itself unfaithful. Matthew 13:35 is read by Christians as fulfilled in Jesus' teaching.

**Studies linked.** The book of Psalms (48) owns the rest of Psalm 78; 2 Timothy 1:5 quoted.

## Psalms 127–128

**Context.** Songs of ascents (Psalms 120–134); 127 headed "Of Solomon". Traditionally linked with pilgrimage to Jerusalem for the feasts.

**Main ways it is read.**
- *"House" in 127:1:* building, household, dynasty (some link it to Solomon and the temple) [check: named commentators].
- *Children as heritage:* read by Christians and Jews as gift; BFM article XVIII quoted.
- *Blessing in 128:* the usual fruit of fearing the LORD, not a guarantee; the study guards against reading childlessness as disfavour.

**Studies linked.** The book of Psalms (48); Sexuality, singleness and divorce (43); Grief and dying (57).

## Genesis 25:27–28; 37:3–4

**Context.** Isaac's family (25:19–28:9) and the start of the Joseph story (37–50).

**Main ways it is read.** As narrative showing parental favouritism and its fruit; widely read as descriptive, not commending. The pattern across generations (Rebekah favours Jacob; Jacob favours Joseph) is noted by many interpreters [check: named source]. Genesis 50:20 (owned by the God study) frames God's purpose through the family's sin.

**Studies linked.** God: who He is and what He does (21), Genesis 50:15–21; Covenants and promises (26) for the promise to Abraham's line.

## 1 Samuel 2:12–25; 3:13

**Context.** Shiloh before the monarchy; Eli priest and judge (4:18); Samuel's call follows in chapter 3.

**Main ways it is read.**
- *Eli's failure:* failure to use his authority as high priest and judge to remove his sons (the study's reading, from 3:13 "did not restrain them" and 4:18).
- *2:25, "the LORD intended to put them to death":* read as God's judicial hardening after persistent sin (many Reformed and Baptist readers) or as God's judgement already settled, without removing responsibility [check: named commentators]. The study does not settle it.
- *Samuel's sons (8:3):* used to show no simple cause-and-effect between parenting and adult children's choices.

**Studies linked.** Women of the Bible (46) for Hannah [check: whether 46 includes Hannah]; Rulers and their choices (39).
