## Interpretive boundaries

- **New creation:** the draft reads 2 Corinthians 5:17 through verses 14–21. It does not settle every question about the phrase's individual, corporate or wider new-creation implications.
- **Grace and works:** Ephesians 2:8–10 is treated as a complete paragraph. The draft does not settle disputes over the precise grammatical referent of “that” in verse 8 or compare denominational accounts of justification.
- **Old and new:** Ephesians and Colossians are allowed their own wording. Do not turn the clothing language into a rigid sequence of psychological stages or silently resolve debated details of tense and aspect.
- **KJV wording:** “conversation” in Ephesians 4:22 concerns conduct. “Charity” in Colossians 3:14 is discussed as love. The modern wording of Galatians 5:22–23 is explicitly identified as paraphrase; the lexical range of “faith” in the KJV is not being argued in this introductory study.
- **Baptism:** Romans 6:3–4 must remain visible in the explanation. The draft describes its connection to death, resurrection and newness of life without claiming to resolve baptismal disagreements among Christian traditions.
- **Flesh:** do not explain it here as simply the physical body or suggest that embodiment is evil. Romans 6 also calls for the body's members to be presented to God.
- **Forgiveness:** the commands should not be turned into a claim that wrongdoing is harmless, trust is instantaneous or peace depends entirely on the injured person. The practical reflection is editorial application; the text does not supply a complete procedure for every conflict.
- **Continued struggle:** Galatians 5:17 and 1 John 1:8–9 support retaining struggle and confession in the account. Do not create a spiritual scoring system or infer someone's salvation from one illustrative scenario.
