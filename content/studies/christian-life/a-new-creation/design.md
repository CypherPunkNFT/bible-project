## Direction

Teach one habit: before you obey a command in a letter, look back for what the letter says is already true of you in Christ. The picture is a page folded down its middle. What is already so is on the left, what to do now is on the right, and on the fold is the small joining word the letter itself uses: "therefore", "since", "for this very reason". The rewritten text keeps this habit, and it adds growing to it ("Made new. / Now grow into it."), so the fold stays. What the new text does not carry has gone. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The heading has the study trail, the concepts (Already · Therefore · Growing) in gold, and "A new" with "creation" in gold italic. Under it are "what changes when you believe", the lead and 2 Corinthians 5:17. The drawing is in thin lines inside a faint circle and dotted orbit: a rail, an old garment laid down in dotted line, and a new one hung ready with a wash of the letters' colour (Colossians 3:9–10). A slow thread runs between them. A visible button pauses the thread, it stops off-screen, and with reduced motion it is still. No person is drawn. The motto beside it reads "Made new. / Now grow into it." Below it are four computed figures, each saying what it means, then the frame paragraph and the selection note.
- **01 Already so. Now live by it.** This is the signature. There are six lines, one for each part, each crossing the same vertical fold in the verse's exact words, with whose act the first half is written small beneath it. The six places are:
  - 2 Corinthians 5:15
  - Romans 6:10–11
  - Colossians 3:9, which the text calls "the fold at its clearest"
  - Ephesians 4:32
  - 2 Peter 1:3–5
  - Philippians 3:12, where the command comes first and there is no joining word; the line says so

  Choosing a line opens its part in place beneath it, and the line does not move. A part shows its hook, its main readings, the text with the passages printed in full, and its labelled notes. It ends with Consider and Reflect, and a way to the next part. Part 04 also holds Ephesians' four three-part lines (stop, instead, why) as a small table in the verses' own words. A note under the six lines says that which place each part turns on is this study's reading.
- **02 The letter's own word. Six places.** These are the six places the text's own fold note names, in its order: Colossians 3:1, Romans 6:11, Romans 12:1, Ephesians 4:25, Colossians 3:12 and 2 Peter 1:5. They are drawn across the same fold, with the joining word on the crease ("since", "So", "on account of", "Therefore", "as", "For this very reason"). Pointing or keyboard focus shows the verses on a line beneath, and choosing a line opens it on the sheet. The text's own note follows, ending "The pattern is common, not a rule for every sentence."
- **03 Six questions, faced.** A plain ruled list. Each opens the one sheet from the right, with the verse, then this study's reading, then what else has been said, labelled "Other Christians", or by the confession or writer's name.
- **Close: Return to the beginning.** The close's own list is drawn across the fold one last time: five lines, each "so" on the crease, each with the verse it rests on. The list runs: you died with Christ, so count yourself dead to sin; you were raised with Him, so set your mind above; you were forgiven, so forgive; you have been given everything you need, so make every effort; Christ took hold of you, so press on. Then come the way of reading, the invitation to the one who is unsure, the question, and "Read the passage again before you answer" with 2 Corinthians 5:14–17. After them are the way back to Living the Christian Life, a dawn horizon and the sources.
- **Every reference** opens its verses on the sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The letters' colour, because every part reads the New Testament letters. It is used for the wash on the "now" side of the fold, the crease and the moving thread. The site's gold marks the joining words, the concepts and the "to check" tags. Colour never carries meaning alone: "Already so", "Now", the joining word, whose act it is and each reference are all words.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000:
- the new birth as God's work, by grace through faith (article IV);
- growth in grace through the whole of life (article IV);
- the perseverance of true believers, with their real falls into sin (article V);
- baptism as a believer's obedience that pictures the change, not what makes it (article VII).

Where Baptists differ, the page shows both and does not choose: how the law of Moses applies today, how God's choosing fits with human choice, and whether Romans 7 describes a believer. Wesleyan and Arminian teaching on falling away, Lutheran teaching on baptism and Orthodox teaching on theosis are each shown as theirs. The care notes stay in the line of reading where the text puts them: crisis lines beside "Put to death", safety and the domestic-violence lines beside forgiveness, and the note that "forgetting what is behind" does not mean pretending harm never happened. The phone numbers carry the text's "to check before publishing".

## Choices made, and why

- **The fold stays, because the habit stayed.** The rewritten text teaches the same habit as the first draft: its lead and its way of reading both say to look back for what is already true before obeying.
- **One line for each part, chosen from that part's own text.** The fold note says each part turns on one place but names only six places, which fall in four of the parts. So the page picks one place for each part from the part's own main reading, says that the choice is this study's reading, and gives the text's six their own section.
- **The first half is not always God's act.** Some lines are what the readers have done or now are, so each line says whose it is in small words.
- **The close's list becomes the last fold.** Its five pairs are already written as "this, so that". The build splits them and rebuilds the paragraph to prove no word was lost, so the reader reads the text once, laid out.
- **What the text dropped, the page dropped.** The five letters as lines, the look-backs, the Beholds and Colossians' rails had their own wording in the first draft. Keeping them would have meant writing new words for them, and this job designs the page; it does not write the study.
- **No focus ring.** A focused line shows by its colour and underline, not by an outline.
- **Unlike the sibling Christian Life pages.** Faith in daily life, the nearest in idea, sorts each instruction's reason by where it points, in six rooms. Faith under pressure uses dark layers and Bearing witness uses doors. This one uses a single fold that never moves.
