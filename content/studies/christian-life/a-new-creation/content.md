# A new creation

What God has made new. The life we are called to live.

> Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

2 Corinthians 5:17 · KJV

What changes when someone belongs to Christ? The letters answer by speaking both of what God has done and of the life believers are called to live. They describe reconciliation, life with Christ and salvation by grace. They also address the ordinary places where that life takes shape: what we say, how we treat an offender, what we desire, and what we do for someone in need.

These belong together. Ephesians calls believers God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works. Later, the same letter describes those works in the language of truthfulness, generosity, kindness and love. This study follows that connection from identity to practice.

## 01 In Christ

**Read:** 2 Corinthians 5:14–21 and Ephesians 2:1–10.

### A different centre for life

The new-creation statement comes within Paul's explanation of Christ's death and the ministry of reconciliation. Christ died and rose again so that those who live would live for him rather than for themselves. Paul then speaks of a changed way of regarding people before declaring that anyone in Christ is a new creature. The surrounding verses give the statement its direction: life is understood in relation to Christ and what he has done. — 2 Corinthians 5:14–17

The next sentence names the source of this change: God has reconciled us to himself through Jesus Christ. Paul describes a ministry and message of reconciliation, and appeals to his hearers to be reconciled to God. New creation belongs within this account of a relationship restored through Christ. — 2 Corinthians 5:18–21

### Grace and the life that follows

Ephesians begins its account of salvation with people dead in trespasses and sins, following the course of the world and the desires of flesh and mind. The decisive turn comes with God's mercy and love: he makes them alive with Christ. The passage then describes salvation as God's gift, received through faith, leaving no ground for boasting in works. — Ephesians 2:1–9

> For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

Ephesians 2:10 · KJV

The paragraph makes room for good works by placing them within God's creating work. They are the life for which believers are made in Christ. Read verses 8–10 together: grace, faith and a changed way of living all appear in the same argument.

**Consider:** What does each passage say God has done? How does living for Christ in 2 Corinthians 5:15 give direction to the good works of Ephesians 2:10?

**Reflect:** Think of one ordinary responsibility. What might approaching it as someone who belongs to Christ change about your purpose, rather than merely your outward performance?

## 02 The old and the new

**Read:** Ephesians 4:17–24 and Colossians 3:1–17.

### Learning Christ

Ephesians describes a former way of life marked by darkened understanding, alienation from God's life and desires that deceive. Against this it sets what the readers have learned in Christ: putting off the old person, renewal in the mind and putting on the new person, created in righteousness and holiness. In the KJV, “former conversation” means the former manner of life, not only spoken conversation. — Ephesians 4:17–24

> And be renewed in the spirit of your mind;

Ephesians 4:23 · KJV

This renewal reaches beyond a single habit. The passage connects understanding, desire and conduct. What a person learns in Christ is meant to shape how that person lives.

Colossians begins its instructions with the readers' life in Christ: they have died, their life is hidden with Christ in God, and they are to seek the things above. It then names what must be put to death or put away, including sexual immorality, greed, anger, malice and lying. — Colossians 3:1–9

### Putting on a shared life

The positive instructions are just as concrete: compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, forgiveness and love. These qualities describe life with other people. Colossians speaks of one body, mutual teaching and admonition, thanksgiving, and doing everything in the name of the Lord Jesus. — Colossians 3:10–17

The letter can speak of having put on the new person and also command believers to put on these qualities. A changed identity and continuing instruction stand together. Belonging to Christ gives the following commands their setting.

**Consider:** Which parts of these passages address understanding, which address desires, and which address relationships? What is lost if we read only the lists of things to stop doing?

**Reflect:** Name a pattern to leave behind and a corresponding practice to cultivate. Use a connection the passage actually makes, rather than inventing an opposite for every word.

## 03 Words that build up

**Read:** Ephesians 4:25–30 and Colossians 4:5–6.

### Truth because we belong to one another

Ephesians gives a reason for truthful speech: believers are members of one another. A lie harms the relationship in which the speaker and hearer belong. Truthfulness serves that shared life. — Ephesians 4:25

> Let no corrupt communication proceed out of your mouth, but that which is good to the use of edifying, that it may minister grace unto the hearers.

Ephesians 4:29 · KJV

The instruction asks more than whether a sentence avoids a forbidden word. It asks what the words do for the people who receive them. Speech is to build up and benefit its hearers. This gives attention to purpose and effect, alongside truthfulness.

Building someone up can include difficult instruction. Colossians speaks of teaching and admonishing one another, as well as speech marked by grace toward those outside the community. Gracious speech need not mean agreeing with everything; it concerns how we address a real person and what our words seek to accomplish. — Colossians 3:16; 4:5–6

### A change that reaches the hearer

| The instruction | The positive practice | The reason in the passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Put away lying | Speak truth to one's neighbour | We are members of one another — Ephesians 4:25 |
| Stop corrupt speech | Speak what builds up | Benefit those who hear — Ephesians 4:29 |
| Walk wisely toward outsiders | Speak with grace and answer each person thoughtfully | Conduct and conversation belong together — Colossians 4:5–6 |

These are summaries of the passages, not quotations.

**Consider:** What changes when the question becomes “What will these words give the hearer?” How can truth and care guide the same difficult conversation?

**Reflect:** Before a conversation or message, consider its truthfulness, purpose and likely effect. If correction is needed, identify what would help the other person understand and respond.

## 04 Anger and forgiveness

**Read:** Ephesians 4:26–32, Colossians 3:12–15 and James 1:19–20.

### Anger that must be addressed

Ephesians places a warning beside anger: do not sin, do not let wrath continue, and do not give the devil an opportunity. A few verses later it commands the removal of bitterness, wrath, anger, clamour, evil speaking and malice. The passage gives no permission to nurture hostility simply because the grievance feels justified. — Ephesians 4:26–31

James adds the instruction to be quick to hear, slow to speak and slow to anger, explaining that human anger does not produce God's righteousness. Listening and restraint therefore matter before we assume that the force of our reaction makes it right. — James 1:19–20

### Forgiveness with a stated reason

> And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.

Ephesians 4:32 · KJV

The positive answer to bitterness is a way of treating people: kindness, compassion and forgiveness. Both Ephesians and Colossians ground forgiveness in forgiveness already received through Christ. The instruction directs attention to God's action as the pattern for the believer's response. — Ephesians 4:32; Colossians 3:12–13

The warning about carrying anger should prompt attention to it; it should not become a demand that every conflict be fully resolved by a deadline. Romans qualifies its call to peace with “If it be possible” and “as much as lieth in you.” One person cannot determine every response in a relationship. — Romans 12:18

**Consider:** Which responses does the passage tell you to put away, and which does it tell you to practise? How does the stated reason for forgiveness affect the way you understand the command?

**Reflect:** Identify a response that is yours to change: listening before answering, stopping retaliation, speaking honestly or seeking help with a conflict. Forgiveness does not make wrongdoing good; questions of protection, accountability and restored trust require attention in their own right.

## 05 Desires and habits

**Read:** Galatians 5:13–26 and Romans 6:1–14.

### Freedom expressed in service

Galatians introduces this passage by calling believers to use their freedom to serve one another through love. The warning against biting and devouring one another follows immediately. The discussion of flesh and Spirit therefore includes the life of a community, as well as personal choices. — Galatians 5:13–18

The works of the flesh include sexual wrongdoing, idolatry, drunkenness and a series of destructive relations: hatred, rivalry, anger, division and envy. The fruit of the Spirit includes love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control. These last terms are a modern-language summary of the KJV list, not a quotation. — Galatians 5:19–23

The contrast reaches desire and action together. Galatians speaks openly of conflict between flesh and Spirit, then tells those who live in the Spirit to walk accordingly.

> If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit.

Galatians 5:25 · KJV

### Giving oneself to God

Romans 6 rejects the idea that grace supplies a reason to continue in sin. It connects baptism with Christ's death and resurrection, and with walking in newness of life. It then tells believers to reckon themselves dead to sin and alive to God, and to present themselves and their bodily members to God as instruments of righteousness. — Romans 6:1–14

This gives concrete direction to the discussion of habits. The questions include what one serves, what one obeys and how one uses one's body. They also include a positive commitment: giving oneself to God.

These instructions are not a claim that believers have no continuing struggle or need to confess sin. First John warns against claiming to be without sin and speaks of confession, forgiveness and cleansing. — 1 John 1:8–9

**Consider:** Which works of the flesh damage community relationships? How do the positive instructions in Galatians 5 and Romans 6 go beyond simply suppressing a behaviour?

**Reflect:** Consider one repeated choice. What does it train you to serve, and what different practice would express love, faithfulness or self-control? Begin with a specific action rather than a claim to have mastered the whole struggle.

## 06 Walking in love

**Read:** Ephesians 5:1–2, Colossians 3:12–17 and Romans 12:9–21.

### The pattern is Christ

> And walk in love, as Christ also hath loved us, and hath given himself for us an offering and a sacrifice to God for a sweetsmelling savour.

Ephesians 5:2 · KJV

This instruction follows the call to forgive as God has forgiven. Believers are addressed as dearly loved children and called to follow God's example. Christ's self-giving supplies the pattern for the love they are to practise. — Ephesians 4:32–5:2

Colossians places love alongside compassion, patience and forgiveness, describing it as the bond of completeness. Peace, thankfulness, the word of Christ and shared worship belong in the same paragraph. Love is expressed through these relationships and practices. — Colossians 3:12–17

### Love with concrete responsibilities

Romans 12 refuses to leave love as a vague feeling. It joins sincere love with rejecting evil and holding fast to good. It calls for honouring others, meeting needs, offering hospitality, sharing joy and sorrow, and responding to persecution without cursing. — Romans 12:9–16

The instructions extend even to an enemy in need. Believers are told not to avenge themselves, and to overcome evil with good. The passage continues to call evil what it is while directing a different response to it. — Romans 12:17–21

Generosity is another expression of this movement. In Ephesians, the person who stole is told to work honestly so that there is something to share with someone in need. Changed conduct becomes a source of good for another person. — Ephesians 4:28

**Consider:** Which actions make love visible in these passages? How do rejecting evil and doing good to an enemy appear in the same account of love?

**Reflect:** Choose one concrete act of care: meeting a need, offering hospitality, sharing someone's sorrow, giving honour or refusing retaliation. Let the passage supply the reason as well as the action.

## Return to the beginning

Read 2 Corinthians 5:14–21 and Ephesians 2:1–10 again. Then name one connection between God's action and the life described in this study.

The letters keep those connections close: forgiven people are called to forgive; members of one body are called to speak truth; those alive in Christ are called to walk in newness of life. The practical instructions give shape to a life whose foundation is God's work in Christ.
