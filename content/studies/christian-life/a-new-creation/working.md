# A new creation

**Content pass status:** first full draft ready for review, 2026-10-13. Read [the content draft](content.md) and [passage and editorial notes](passage-notes.md). The outline below preserves the starting ideas and alternatives; the content draft is the current treatment.

**Working purpose:** explore what the epistles say about new life in Christ and how that identity is expressed in conduct. Begin with what God has done, then examine the changes the letters call believers to make.

The title is confirmed. The structure, subcard names, draft copy and reader experiences below are proposals for discussion.

## Central question

What changes when someone belongs to Christ, and how do the letters connect that new identity to everyday life?

## Proposed opening

**Working line:** What God has made new. The life we are called to live.

**Opening passage:** 2 Corinthians 5:14–21, with verse 17 as the focal point. Read the new-creation statement within Paul's discussion of Christ's death, living for him and reconciliation.

**Companion passage:** Ephesians 2:1–10. Develop the relationship between grace, God's workmanship and good works before presenting the instructions that follow later in the letter.

The title uses modern wording. If quoting the KJV, retain its wording, “a new creature,” rather than silently changing the translation to match the title.

## An experience worth exploring

The opening should establish identity before inviting the reader into changes in conduct. A possible sequence is **In Christ → The old and the new → Life expressed in practice**.

For the practical passages, compare the instruction in three parts:

| Leave behind | Practice instead | The reason given in the passage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Falsehood | Speak truth to one's neighbour | Believers are members of one another — Ephesians 4:25 |
| Stealing | Work honestly and share with someone in need | Work makes generosity possible — Ephesians 4:28 |
| Corrupt speech | Speak what builds others up | Speech is to benefit those who hear — Ephesians 4:29 |
| Bitterness and malice | Kindness, tenderheartedness and forgiveness | God's forgiveness in Christ — Ephesians 4:31–32 |

These are working paraphrases, not Bible quotations. The pattern should only be used where the passage actually supplies its parts. Do not manufacture a direct opposite for every phrase.

A reader could choose a pair, read its surrounding paragraph, and compare a related passage. The experience should reveal the text's argument, not score the reader's spiritual progress.

## First subcard to develop

### In Christ

**Reader question:** Is this study beginning with a list of improvements, or with what God has done in Christ?

**Read first:** 2 Corinthians 5:14–21.

**Passage observations to develop:**

- How verses 14–15 connect Christ's death with living for him.
- How verse 17 introduces the new-creation statement.
- How verses 18–21 connect the surrounding argument to reconciliation.
- How Ephesians 2:8–10 connects salvation by grace and the good works believers are created for.

**Draft explanatory direction:** connect identity, God's action and the life that follows. Avoid treating Christian formation as self-improvement detached from Christ, or treating grace as making conduct irrelevant.

**Questions for the reader:**

1. Who acts in these passages, and what does each passage say that God has done?
2. What change of purpose appears in 2 Corinthians 5:15?
3. How do Ephesians 2:8–10 distinguish the basis of salvation from the good works that follow?

**Draft outcome:** the full draft develops these passages together under **In Christ**, including reconciliation. All six sections now include explanations and reflection questions.
