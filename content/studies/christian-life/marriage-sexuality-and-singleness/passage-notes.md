## Context, readings and limits

1. **Matthew 19:3–12 is the spine.** The Pharisees' question ("for any reason", 19:3) assumes a dispute about the
   grounds in Deuteronomy 24:1. Jesus answers from Genesis 1:27 and 2:24 before He answers about Moses, and the
   disciples' reaction (19:10) leads into the saying on eunuchs (19:12). The study uses the passage three times
   (opening and part 01; part 04; parts 05–06), which is why it is the opening quotation. The draft says the question
   was disputed in Jesus' day but does not name the rabbinic schools (Hillel and Shammai) because no source for them
   was read for this draft (see open questions).
2. **Genesis 1–2.** "Male and female" (1:27) and "one flesh" (2:24) are read as Jesus reads them. Draft 29, Humanity,
   sin and renewal, owns the image of God (1:26–27) and names Genesis 2:18–25 as not walked there; this study walks it.
   Part 01 limits Genesis 2:18 ("not good ... alone"): it shows people are made for companionship; it does not say an
   unmarried person is incomplete, and the draft says so before part 04.
3. **Proverbs 5.** A father's speech; most of the chapter warns against the adulteress (5:1–14, 20–23). The draft
   quotes the blessing (5:15–19) and the reason (5:21). It does not quote 5:19b ("may her breasts satisfy you always")
   in the reader's text: accurate and not crude, but the line is left to the reader's own reading so the study stays
   suitable for all ages. Revisit in review.
4. **Song of Songs.** Two readings are shown: the allegorical (Christ and His church), quoted from Matthew Henry's
   Concise Commentary; and the first-sense reading as married love, quoted from John Piper. The draft follows the
   first-sense reading, with the textual reason (the poems describe a bride and groom), and labels the allegorical
   reading as a long Christian tradition with a foothold in Ephesians 5:32. This is an interpretation question with a
   best-supported reading (shared rule 9), not a point where Baptists are recorded as divided by the confession.
5. **1 Corinthians 6:12–20.** Draft 29 part 06 already walks this passage for the body and the resurrection. This
   study reads it only for what it says about sex (6:16, the one-flesh bond misused; 6:18; 6:19–20) and links there.
   The draft's protective note (abuse is the abuser's sin, not the victim's) follows the wording draft 29 and draft 05
   already use, so the site speaks with one voice on it.
6. **1 Thessalonians 4:3–8.** The Berean note on 4:4 gives the literal "know how to possess his own vessel"; whether
   "vessel" means one's own body or one's wife is an old question. The draft follows the Berean's main text ("control
   his own body") and does not raise the question. Verse 6 ("violate or exploit his brother") is used for the point that
   sexual sin wrongs another person.
7. **1 Corinthians 6:9–11 and same-sex sexuality.** The draft does not quote the Berean's wording of 6:9; it names the
   list and quotes Article XV of the confession, then sets Article III (every person's dignity) and 6:11 (washed) beside
   it. Plain, not crude; the site's own view follows the confession.
8. **1 Corinthians 7:7 and the "gift".** Two readings shown without choosing, each with its textual reason: a particular
   ability given to some (Calvin on 7:7; Matthew Henry on Matthew 19:11–12), reading verse 9; or the single state itself,
   "a gift for as long as you have it" (Piper, 2008), reading verse 7's "his own gift". The confession is silent.
   "The present crisis" (7:26) is named, and the draft says Paul does not describe it.
9. **Matthew 19:12 and Isaiah 56:3–5.** The three kinds of eunuch are read as Jesus lists them. Isaiah 56 is read in its
   own setting (foreigners and eunuchs welcomed into the LORD's house). The New Testament does not quote Isaiah 56:3–5
   in connection with Matthew 19:12; the draft puts them side by side without claiming that it does. (The Ethiopian
   eunuch of Acts 8 is a natural link but is not drawn here.)
10. **Deuteronomy 24:1–4.** Read as case law: the "if" runs through verses 1–3 and the command is in verse 4. The draft
    says the law assumes divorce and limits its harm; it does not claim more about the certificate's purpose than that
    it gave her proof she was free, and even that is an inference marked by "limits the damage" rather than stated as
    the law's own purpose. Matthew 19:7 ("order") against 19:8 ("permitted") is the textual point.
11. **Malachi 2:16.** The Hebrew is difficult. The Berean's main text is "For I hate divorce"; its own footnote gives
    "Literally 'For He hates sending away,' says the LORD, the God of Israel. 'And he covers his garment with
    violence,'". Calvin rendered the verse "If thou hatest, dismiss", reading it as heightening the men's crime rather
    than permitting divorce (*Commentaries on the Twelve Minor Prophets*, Calvin Translation Society text, CCEL file calcom30, on 2:16; the
    editor's footnote in that edition disagrees with him). The draft says only that Calvin "took the verse quite
    differently" and that every reading targets the man who breaks faith ("break faith" appears in 2:14, 15 and 16).
12. **Jeremiah 3:8.** God speaks of giving faithless Israel a certificate of divorce. Used only to say God knows
    betrayal; the draft draws no rule about divorce grounds from it.
13. **The exception clause and 1 Corinthians 7:15.** The texts are set out first (part 06, "What the texts say"): the
    exception in Matthew 5:32 and 19:9, its absence in Mark 10:11–12 and Luke 16:18, Paul's "remain unmarried or else be
    reconciled" (7:11), the unbeliever who leaves (7:15) and death ending the bond (7:39). The Berean notes that some
    Greek texts (SBL and Byzantine) add to Matthew 19:9 "And he who marries a divorced woman commits adultery"; the
    draft quotes the Berean's main text and does not raise the variant.

## Where Baptists differ: the views shown, and who holds them

The confession says marriage is "for a lifetime" and cites Malachi 2:14–16, Matthew 5:31–32, 19:3–9 and 1 Corinthians
7:1–16 among its proof texts, but sets no rule for when divorce or remarriage is permitted. Part 06 shows four views
and does not choose:

| View | Holder quoted in the draft | Where read | Baptist? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No remarriage while a former spouse lives; Matthew's exception read as betrothal unfaithfulness (Joseph, Matthew 1:19) | John Piper, "Divorce & Remarriage: A Position Paper", 21 July 1986 | Desiring God page, local copy | Yes: then pastor of Bethlehem Baptist Church, Minneapolis (Converge, formerly the Baptist General Conference; not Southern Baptist) |
| Adultery the one exception | John Gill, *A Body of Practical Divinity*, book on the duties of husband and wife | CCEL text, local copy | Yes: eighteenth-century Particular Baptist pastor, London |
| Adultery and desertion | Matthew Henry on 1 Corinthians 7:15; Reagan Marsh on the 1689 Confession (Founders Ministries, 2020) | Local copies | Henry no (English nonconformist, Presbyterian); Marsh yes (writing on the Baptist confession for Founders Ministries) |
| Desertion, adultery or dangerous abuse as grounds for divorce, with members differing on remarriage | Bethlehem Baptist Church, "A Statement on Divorce and Remarriage in the Life of Bethlehem Baptist Church", approved 2 May 1989 | Desiring God page, local copy | Yes |

Supporting facts used in the draft:
- The Westminster Confession (Presbyterian), 24.6: "nothing but adultery, or such wilful desertion as can no way be
  remedied by the Church or civil magistrate, is cause sufficient of dissolving the bond of marriage". The draft
  quotes "such wilful desertion as can no way be remedied".
- The 1689 Second London Baptist Confession, chapter 25 "Of Marriage", has four paragraphs and none on divorce
  (checked in the local copy). Marsh: it "principally follows the Savoy Declaration (1658) in omitting WCF (1646)
  24.5-6", and "Savoy and 2LC leave the divorce question as a matter of conscience".
- Bethlehem's statement shows the difference inside one Baptist church: Piper wrote the 1986 paper for no remarriage,
  and his church's 1989 statement permits remarriage in some cases, with those holding "more limiting standards for
  remarriage" agreeing "not to make them normative for the whole body". This is why the draft uses it.

**Not found and not claimed.** No Southern Baptist Convention resolution on divorce is held locally, so none is cited.
The New Hampshire Confession of 1833 (the basis of the 1925 Baptist Faith and Message) has no marriage article in the
site's copy. Gill's *Exposition of the Bible* (his verse-by-verse comments on Matthew 19 and 1 Corinthians 7) is not
held; his *Body of Practical Divinity* was used instead and says nothing in the passage read about desertion or about
remarriage of the innocent party, so the draft does not attribute either to him. A view that widens the grounds further
(neglect, from Exodus 21:10–11, argued by David Instone-Brewer, a British Baptist minister) is known to exist but no
text of it was read; it is not shown.

## Doctrine

- Article XVIII (the family): marriage "the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime",
  "God's unique gift to reveal the union between Christ and His church" and "the channel of sexual expression according
  to biblical standards". The site's own voice follows it throughout parts 01–03; nothing in the draft affirms what it
  rules out.
- Article XV (the social order): opposing "all forms of sexual immorality, including adultery, homosexuality, and
  pornography". Quoted once, in part 03, beside Article III.
- Article III (man): "every person of every race possesses full dignity and is worthy of respect and Christian love".
  Quoted in part 03 so that the standard and the dignity stand together.
- Where the confession is silent and Baptists differ: (1) the grounds for divorce and whether remarriage is allowed
  (part 06: four views, no choice); (2) what the "gift" of 1 Corinthians 7:7 is (part 04: two readings, no choice).
- The draft does not take up whether a divorced man may serve as pastor or deacon ("the husband of but one wife", 1
  Timothy 3:2, 12), on which Baptist churches also differ. It belongs with the church's offices, not here.

## Open editorial questions

1. **Length.** About 2,730 words of prose without the block quotations (about 3,350 raw with headings and references),
   above the 2,000–2,600 aim. Three questions in one study is the reason. If the design pass needs room, the first cuts
   would be the Hebrews 13:4 line (part 02) and the Jeremiah 3:8 sentence (part 05).
2. **Rabbinic background.** Should part 05 name the two schools of Jesus' day (Hillel's "any reason" and Shammai's
   stricter reading of Deuteronomy 24:1)? It would help readers, but needs a named source read directly (the Mishnah,
   Gittin 9:10, is the usual primary text; not read for this draft).
3. **A Southern Baptist voice on divorce.** The four views are held by Baptists, but the named holders are a
   Converge pastor and church (Piper, Bethlehem), an eighteenth-century English Baptist (Gill) and a Founders Ministries
   writer (Marsh). A Southern Baptist author on each view (for example Köstenberger, above) would strengthen part 06;
   none was held locally.
4. **Proverbs 5:19.** The draft quotes "captivated by her love forever" and leaves out the earlier half of the verse.
   Keep, or quote the whole verse?
5. **Same-sex sexuality.** The draft handles it in one sentence through the confession's Article XV, with Article III
   and 1 Corinthians 6:11 beside it. The owner may want a fuller, separate treatment elsewhere (it is a question many
   readers bring), or may prefer this study to stay brief on it.
6. **Abuse.** Part 06 says no one has to stay in danger, and points to part 03's safety note. Bethlehem's statement
   names "dangerously abusive" behaviour as a ground for divorce; the draft reports that as their view, not the site's.
   Whether the site should say more, and whether to add a pointer to help lines, is for the owner.
