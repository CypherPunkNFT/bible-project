# Sexuality, singleness and divorce

**Study 43 · Christian Life · First content draft · 2026-11-05**

Three questions people bring to the Bible again and again: What is sex for? Is a single life a lesser life? What does Jesus say to someone who is divorced?

The Pharisees brought Jesus one of these questions to trap Him. He did not start with their argument. He started with Genesis.

> Jesus answered, "Have you not read that from the beginning the Creator 'made them male and female,' and said, 'For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh'?

Matthew 19:4–5 · BSB

This study follows Him back to the beginning, then takes three questions in six parts: sexuality (01 to 03), singleness (04), and divorce and remarriage (05 and 06). It is not a general study of marriage; Study 05, Faith in daily life, walks the household. The site writes in agreement with the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, which calls marriage "the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime" (Article XVIII). Where Baptists differ, above all on divorce and remarriage, this study shows each view and who holds it, and does not choose.

Carry three questions through every part:

1. **What was it like from the beginning?**
2. **What does the hard verse say in its own paragraph?** Many of these verses answer a question someone asked. Find the question first.
3. **Where are holiness and grace here?** Scripture never lowers God's standard, and never shuts the door on a sinner who comes back.

Many people read these pages carrying a wound. If you are one of them, read slowly. Nothing here is a verdict on your story.

## 01 Made male and female: the design

**Main readings:** Genesis 1:27–31; Genesis 2:18–25; Matthew 19:4–6.

Before any law or any divorce, there was a garden and a God who called it good.

### Two accounts, one design

Genesis 1 gives the wide view: "in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them" (Genesis 1:27 · BSB). God blesses them together, tells them to "be fruitful and multiply" (Genesis 1:28 · BSB), and looks at it all: "indeed, it was very good" (Genesis 1:31 · BSB).

Genesis 2 comes close. One thing is not yet good: "It is not good for the man to be alone. I will make for him a suitable helper" (Genesis 2:18 · BSB). God makes the woman and brings her to the man, and the first human words in the Bible are a cry of recognition: "This is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh" (Genesis 2:23 · BSB).

> For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and they will become one flesh.

Genesis 2:24 · BSB

### What "one flesh" holds

Three verbs: leave, be united, become. A new household, a lasting bond, one shared life, the body included. The chapter ends in trust: "the man and his wife were both naked, and they were not ashamed" (Genesis 2:25 · BSB).

Jesus reads this as God's own word and draws His conclusion: "So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let man not separate" (Matthew 19:6 · BSB). Marriage is something God does. Paul says the same verse points beyond itself, to "Christ and the church" (Ephesians 5:32 · BSB), and the confession calls marriage "God's unique gift to reveal the union between Christ and His church" (Article XVIII).

### Where the passage stops

Genesis 2:18 does not say an unmarried person is incomplete. Jesus never married (part 04). People are made for companionship; marriage is one form of it, the family of God another (Mark 3:34–35).

**Consider:** Jesus answers a question about divorce by going back before any divorce existed. What does that tell you about how to read the rules that came later?

**Reflect:** Genesis 2 ends with "not ashamed". Where has shame entered your thinking about your body or your relationships?

## 02 Delight within the covenant

**Main readings:** Proverbs 5:15–23; Song of Songs 2:7, 16; 8:6–7; Hebrews 13:4.

A father talks to his son about sex, plainly. A whole book of the Bible is love poetry.

### A father's warning, and a father's blessing

Proverbs 5 warns against the adulteress, but it does not stop at "no". It turns the young man toward his own wife in the language of water, precious in a dry land: "Drink water from your own cistern", and "Let them be yours alone, never to be shared with strangers" (Proverbs 5:15, 17 · BSB).

> May your fountain be blessed, and may you rejoice in the wife of your youth:

Proverbs 5:18 · BSB

He wishes his son to be "captivated by her love forever" (Proverbs 5:19 · BSB), and gives the reason under it all: "a man's ways are before the eyes of the LORD" (Proverbs 5:21 · BSB). Faithfulness is not the end of delight. It is where Proverbs puts delight.

### The Song of Songs

The Song speaks of desire without crudeness, with a refrain that runs through the book: "Do not arouse or awaken love until the time is right" (Song of Songs 2:7 · BSB). Its lovers belong to each other, "My beloved is mine and I am his" (Song of Songs 2:16 · BSB), and near the end it says what such love is like:

> Set me as a seal over your heart, as a seal upon your arm. For love is as strong as death, its jealousy as unrelenting as Sheol. Its sparks are fiery flames, the fiercest blaze of all.

Song of Songs 8:6 · BSB

[care] How should the Song be read? Matthew Henry's Concise Commentary, an abridgement of the Puritan's commentary, calls it "a Divine allegory, which represents the love between Christ and his church". John Piper, for many years pastor of Bethlehem Baptist Church in Minneapolis, reads it as "a lavish, celebration of God's gift of sexual pleasure in marriage". This study follows the second as the poems' first sense, because a bride and groom are what the poems describe. The first is a long Christian tradition, and Paul gives it some ground: marriage itself points to Christ and the church (Ephesians 5:32).

Hebrews puts it in one line: "Marriage should be honored by all and the marriage bed kept undefiled" (Hebrews 13:4 · BSB).

**Consider:** Proverbs 5 gives both a warning and a blessing. How might the blessing protect as well as the warning?

**Reflect:** Do you think of God's design for sex mainly as limits? What changes if you hear Proverbs 5:18 as a blessing?

## 03 Your body belongs to the Lord

**Main readings:** 1 Corinthians 6:9–20; 1 Thessalonians 4:3–8; Matthew 5:27–28.

Corinth had a slogan: "Everything is permissible for me" (1 Corinthians 6:12 · BSB). Paul answers it by asking whose you are.

### One flesh, misused

Paul takes Genesis 2:24 and applies it to a visit to a prostitute: "For it is said, 'The two will become one flesh'" (1 Corinthians 6:16 · BSB). Sex outside marriage is not a small thing done with a body that does not matter; it uses the very bond God made for marriage. So: "Flee from sexual immorality" (1 Corinthians 6:18 · BSB). The reason is belonging: "You are not your own; you were bought at a price. Therefore glorify God with your body" (1 Corinthians 6:19–20 · BSB). (Study 29, Humanity, sin and renewal, walks this passage for the body and its future.)

### Holiness that protects other people

"For it is God's will that you should be holy: You must abstain from sexual immorality" (1 Thessalonians 4:3 · BSB). Then Paul adds what is often missed: "no one should ever violate or exploit his brother in this regard" (1 Thessalonians 4:6 · BSB). Sexual sin is never private; it wrongs someone. Jesus takes it back to the heart: "anyone who looks at a woman to lust after her has already committed adultery with her in his heart" (Matthew 5:28 · BSB).

### What Scripture names, and what it offers

Paul's list in 1 Corinthians 6:9–10 names sexual sins beside theft, greed and drunkenness. The confession follows Scripture in calling Christians to oppose "all forms of sexual immorality, including adultery, homosexuality, and pornography" (Article XV), and says as plainly that every person "possesses full dignity and is worthy of respect and Christian love" (Article III). Both belong together, because Paul's next sentence is about grace:

> And that is what some of you were. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified, in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God.

1 Corinthians 6:11 · BSB

Notice "were". Corinth's church was full of people with sexual pasts, and Paul calls them washed.

[care] These passages are about what a person chooses to do. If someone has used your body against your will, that is their sin against you, not yours, and these verses do not call you defiled. Getting to safety and telling someone you trust goes against nothing here; if you are in danger now, contact your local emergency services. That is this site's application, not Paul's words, and this study is not professional guidance.

**Consider:** In 1 Thessalonians 4:6, who is harmed by sexual sin besides the one who commits it?

**Reflect:** Is there something you have never confessed because you think it is too much for grace? Read 1 Corinthians 6:11 again.

## 04 Singleness: a calling and a gift

**Main readings:** 1 Corinthians 7:7–8, 32–35; Matthew 19:10–12; Isaiah 56:3–5.

The most complete human life ever lived was lived by a man who never married.

### Jesus and Paul

Jesus had a mother, brothers and many friends, and no wife. Paul wrote more about marriage than anyone in the New Testament, and he was single:

> I wish that all men were as I am. But each man has his own gift from God; one has this gift, another has that.

1 Corinthians 7:7 · BSB

His reason comes later in the chapter. The unmarried can give "undivided devotion to the Lord", while a married person's "interests are divided" (1 Corinthians 7:34–35 · BSB). He writes "not to restrict you" (1 Corinthians 7:35 · BSB), and insists that "if you do marry, you have not sinned" (1 Corinthians 7:28 · BSB). He also writes "because of the present crisis" (1 Corinthians 7:26 · BSB), a pressure he does not describe.

[open] What is the "gift"? Calvin and Matthew Henry take it as an ability God gives some people and not others: "Continence is a special gift of God to some, and not to others" (Henry, on Matthew 19). Piper puts it differently: "singleness is a gift for as long as you have it." The paragraph supports both: verse 9 speaks of those who "cannot control themselves", and verse 7 calls each person's state "his own gift from God". This study does not settle it. Both readings agree it is a gift, not a lesser lot.

### Eunuchs for the kingdom

When Jesus forbade easy divorce, His disciples said, "it is better not to marry" (Matthew 19:10 · BSB). He answered:

> For there are eunuchs who were born that way; others were made that way by men; and still others live like eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. The one who can accept this should accept it.

Matthew 19:12 · BSB

Some do not marry by birth, some by what others did to them, some by choice "for the sake of the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 19:12 · BSB). Isaiah had already promised the eunuch, who could have no children: "let the eunuch not say, 'I am but a dry tree'", for God would give him "a name better than that of sons and daughters" (Isaiah 56:3, 5 · BSB).

### Where the passage stops

Paul calls singleness good, not superior; he calls marriage good too (1 Corinthians 7:38). Scripture does not promise that every single person will be content without struggle, or that everyone who longs to marry will. It does promise a family: "whoever does the will of God is My brother and sister and mother" (Mark 3:35 · BSB). And in the resurrection "people will neither marry nor be given in marriage" (Matthew 22:30 · BSB). A church that treats single members as people waiting for real life to begin has not read Isaiah 56 (this site's application).

**Consider:** What freedom does Paul see in the single life, and what does he refuse to say about marriage?

**Reflect:** If you are single, what would it mean to receive this season as a gift to use, not a gap to fill? If you are married, who in your church is single, and do they have a place at your table?

## 05 What Moses allowed, and what God hates

**Main readings:** Deuteronomy 24:1–4; Malachi 2:13–16; Matthew 19:7–8.

The Pharisees had a verse. Jesus asked why it was there.

### A law that limits harm

Deuteronomy 24:1–4 is one long sentence about one case. A man finds "some indecency" in his wife, writes "a certificate of divorce" and sends her away; she marries again; that marriage ends too. Only then comes the command: the first husband "may not remarry her" (Deuteronomy 24:1, 4 · BSB). The law assumes divorce happens; it neither commands nor praises it, and limits the damage. What "some indecency" covered was disputed in Jesus' day, which is why He was asked about divorce "for any reason" (Matthew 19:3 · BSB).

> Jesus replied, "Moses permitted you to divorce your wives because of your hardness of heart; but it was not this way from the beginning.

Matthew 19:8 · BSB

They had asked why Moses would "order" a certificate (Matthew 19:7 · BSB). Jesus' word is "permitted". The law made room for human sin; it never changed what God intended from the beginning.

### The witness at the wedding

Men weep at the altar because God will not accept their offerings. Malachi gives the reason: "the LORD has been a witness between you and the wife of your youth, against whom you have broken faith, though she is your companion and your wife by covenant" (Malachi 2:14 · BSB). God was at the wedding.

> "For I hate divorce," says the LORD, the God of Israel. "He who divorces his wife covers his garment with violence," says the LORD of Hosts. So guard yourselves in your spirit and do not break faith.

Malachi 2:16 · BSB

The Hebrew is difficult. The Berean's own note gives the literal wording, "For He hates sending away", and Calvin took the verse quite differently. On every reading the target is the man who breaks faith with the wife who trusted him; "break faith" is the word the passage repeats (Malachi 2:14, 15, 16 · BSB). God hates divorce for what it does to people He loves, not the people it has happened to. He speaks of giving faithless Israel "a certificate of divorce" (Jeremiah 3:8 · BSB). He knows what it is to be betrayed.

**Consider:** What is the difference between what a law permits and what God intends?

**Reflect:** Malachi says God was a witness at the wedding. If you are married, how would it change an ordinary day to remember that He still is?

## 06 Jesus, Paul and the hard cases

**Main readings:** Matthew 5:31–32; 19:9; Mark 10:11–12; 1 Corinthians 7:10–16.

Every divorce has a story, and the New Testament speaks to more than one.

### What the texts say

> Now I tell you that whoever divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another woman, commits adultery."

Matthew 19:9 · BSB

Matthew gives the saying twice with an exception (5:32; 19:9). Mark and Luke give it without one (Mark 10:11–12; Luke 16:18). Paul passes on the Lord's command that a wife must not separate, "But if she does, she must remain unmarried or else be reconciled to her husband" (1 Corinthians 7:11 · BSB). Then he speaks to a case Jesus had not addressed, a believer married to an unbeliever. Stay if the unbeliever is willing; and:

> But if the unbeliever leaves, let him go. The believing brother or sister is not bound in such cases. God has called you to live in peace.

1 Corinthians 7:15 · BSB

Death ends the bond: "if her husband dies, she is free to marry anyone she wishes, as long as he belongs to the Lord" (1 Corinthians 7:39 · BSB).

### Where Baptists differ

The confession says marriage is for a lifetime and cites Matthew 19 and 1 Corinthians 7, but sets no rule on when divorce or remarriage is allowed. That silence is old. The Presbyterian Westminster Confession allowed divorce for adultery or "such wilful desertion as can no way be remedied"; the Baptists' 1689 Second London Confession, built largely on it, left those paragraphs out. Reagan Marsh, writing for Founders Ministries, says it leaves "the divorce question as a matter of conscience". Four views are held today:

- **No remarriage while a former spouse lives.** John Piper's 1986 paper argues for "a New Testament prohibition of all remarriage except in the case where a spouse has died". He reads Matthew's exception as unfaithfulness discovered during betrothal, as when Joseph planned to "divorce her quietly" (Matthew 1:19 · BSB).
- **Adultery as the one ground.** John Gill, the eighteenth-century Baptist pastor, called marriage "indissoluble but by death, excepting in one case, unfaithfulness in the one to the other, by adultery or fornication".
- **Adultery and desertion.** Matthew Henry, on 1 Corinthians 7:15: "the deserted person must be free to marry again". Among Baptists, Marsh reads the unbeliever's departure as giving "full freedom to remarry a believer".
- **Desertion, adultery or dangerous abuse.** Bethlehem Baptist Church's 1989 statement says "Divorce may be permitted when a spouse deserts the relationship, commits adultery, or is dangerously abusive". Its members did not agree about remarriage, and the statement says so: those with "more limiting standards for remarriage" agreed "not to make them normative for the whole body".

This study does not choose. All four agree on more than divides them: God's design is lifelong; reconciliation is sought first; whoever broke faith is called to repent; and divorce is not the unforgivable sin. Whatever view a church holds, no one has to stay in danger: getting to safety is not disobedience (part 03).

### The church and the divorced

W. A. Criswell, pastor of First Baptist Church in Dallas, told his people that the church's attitude to those "traumatically single by death, or by divorce, or by desertion" should be "one of infinite sympathy, and understanding, and love, and encouragement". The God of Malachi 2 is also the God of 1 John 1:9.

**Consider:** Paul marks one command "not I, but the Lord" (1 Corinthians 7:10 · BSB) and the next word "I, not the Lord" (1 Corinthians 7:12 · BSB). Why does that matter for how you read the chapter?

**Reflect:** If divorce is part of your story, do you most need to hear a call to repent, a word of comfort, or both?

## Back to the beginning

Jesus answered the hardest question of His day by asking, "Have you not read?" The three questions still work on any hard verse: what was it like from the beginning; what does this verse say in its own paragraph; and where are holiness and grace. You can be single, married, divorced or remarried, and still be His.

**Your question:** Which of these three, your body, your singleness or your marriage, do you most need to bring to the God who made you, and what would it mean to bring it to Him honestly this week?

Read the passage again before you answer.
