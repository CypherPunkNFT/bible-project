## Direction

Teach one habit: before you read a hard verse, find the question it was answering, then go back to the beginning. Jesus did exactly this in Matthew 19:3–12. Asked about divorce, He went first to Genesis ("Have you not read that from the beginning…", 19:4), and when pressed about Moses He went back again ("it was not this way from the beginning", 19:8). That one conversation holds all three of the study's subjects: the Pharisees' question about divorce, Jesus' answer from Genesis, and the disciples' "it is better not to marry", which leads to His saying on those who do not marry. So the page draws the conversation first and lets every part grow out of it. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

The shape is Scripture's own (stage 2 of the study prompt, kinds 1 and 2): one conversation that holds the whole subject, and one verse, Genesis 2:24, that the New Testament quotes four times.

## Colour

History orange. The study's habit is to go back to Genesis 1–2, the start of the Bible's history; Jesus points there twice, and the drawings are of the garden. The site's gold marks the care notes.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: marriage as "the uniting of one man and one woman in covenant commitment for a lifetime" (Article XVIII), the call to oppose "all forms of sexual immorality" (Article XV), and every person's "full dignity" (Article III), set side by side as the study sets them. Where Baptists differ, the page shows both or all and does not choose: the four views on divorce and remarriage (part 06) and the two readings of the "gift" in 1 Corinthians 7:7 (part 04). The page draws no person and no figure of God. The care notes (abuse is the abuser's sin, not the victim's; no one has to stay in danger; nothing here is a verdict on the reader's story) are placed where the study places them, in gold, never hidden.
