## Direction

Teach one habit: when you hear a claim, put three questions to it, in order. What does it say about Jesus? Is it so, in the passage read whole? And, turned on yourself: what is driving me as I judge it? The three come from the study's own frame (the letters test the message, test it against Scripture, and test the one doing the testing), and each is anchored in a verse: 1 John 4:2 with Galatians 1:8, Acts 17:11, and James 3:14–17. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

The shape is the frame's own, not a form laid on it. The page draws it once as a line that a claim travels along, through two stations that point outward and a third where the line turns back on the reader, and then every part carries a small mark showing which of the three questions it trains.

## The page

- **Opening.** The study trail; *Test · Search · Weigh* in gold capitals; the title *Learning* in ink with *to discern* in gold italic; the lead; Acts 17:11 in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, a drawing of an open book on a reading stand with a small lamp, and three faint lines of sound arriving from the left and settling on the page, moving slowly and without end. The motto under it: *Hear the claim. / Open the book.* No person is drawn. A bar of four computed figures, each with what it means: the passages walked, the verses printed in full, the seven qualities of wisdom from above, and the five questions to listen for. A row of links to every part.
- **00 One habit, three questions.** The frame paragraph, then the signature graphic: a claim's line through three stations. Point at a station to read its verse on the line below; choose a part number to go there. Part 05 is noted under it as the first and third questions asked together, from the study's own words ("Fruit and doctrine are tested together"). Then the selection note, saying plainly that this is a selection.
- **01–06 The parts.** Each part opens with its number, its title, the hook, its main readings and a small drawing, with a three-dot mark showing which question it trains. Inside: the text, the passages printed in full, notes marked "With care", "How this study reads it" and "Shown, not chosen", and Consider and Reflect side by side. Each part has one figure taken from its own passage:
  - 01 John's test as two clauses (1 John 4:2–3): what is confessed about Jesus, and where each answer comes from, with verse 4 under it as the ground the testers stand on.
  - 02 Who is under the warning (Galatians 1:8–9): "we", "an angel from heaven" and "anyone", all above one fixed line, the gospel already received.
  - 03 Widen the view (Acts 17): the verse alone, then its paragraph (17:10–12), then the account it belongs to (17:1–15), each from the Berean, so the reader practises reading a verse in its place.
  - 04 Two wisdoms (James 3:14–17): what comes from below, in James's words, beside the seven qualities of wisdom from above in his order, "first of all pure" set apart.
  - 05 Five marks in three verses (2 Peter 2:1–3): the passage printed, and pointing at one of Peter's marks lights its words in the passage.
  - 06 What to listen for: the five questions as a ruled list, each linked back to the part it comes from. The two groups are given in their own words, each outside quotation labelled with where it comes from, and every point the text still marks "check" is shown as an open mark, not hidden.
- **Questions readers ask.** Four questions that open in place, separated by rules, not boxes.
- **Close.** The habit restated, the close and the invitation, the question for the reader, "Read the passage again before you answer", and 1 John 4:1–6 printed in full. Then the sources, what was checked by script and what was not, and the way back to Living the Christian Life.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet from the right, in the Berean, and the sheet's verse is in the address.

## Colour

Prophets blue. The study is about telling true prophets from false: 1 John 4:1, 2 Peter 2:1 and Matthew 7:15 all name false prophets, and Peter roots the warning in Israel's own prophets. The site's gold marks the concepts and the care notes. Faith in daily life, the other study in this category built so far with letters at its centre, uses Epistles gold; this page does not.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000: one God, eternally Father, Son and Holy Spirit (article II); Scripture as the final measure (article I); salvation by grace through faith (article IV). On 2 Peter 2:1 ("the Master who bought them") the Calvinist and non-Calvinist Baptist readings are both shown and the page does not choose (article V). The Latter-day Saints and Jehovah's Witnesses are quoted in their own words, with every unconfirmed wording visibly marked to be checked before publishing; Seventh-day Adventists are named as confessing the Trinity; prosperity teaching is kept apart from classical Pentecostal churches. The judgement is about teaching, never about a person's heart, and the page treats both groups as neighbours (articles III and XVII). The crisis lines in part 06 stay marked "check before publishing" until confirmed. No figure or face of God, of Christ or of the Father is drawn.
