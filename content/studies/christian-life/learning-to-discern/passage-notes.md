## Passages this study owns

### 1 John 4:1–6

**Context.** Follows 1 John 2:18–27, where John says a group "went out from us" (2:19) and warns of antichrists. Chapter 4 opens a new test after 3:24's mention of the Spirit. The paragraph is followed by 4:7–21 on love (owned by *God: who He is and what He does*).

**Main readings.**
- *The test of 4:2.* Widely read as aimed at teachers who denied that the divine Christ truly became human and died. Patristic background often cited: Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.26.1, on Cerinthus, who taught that "the Christ" came on Jesus at his baptism and left before the crucifixion [check: citation and summary of Irenaeus]. The later label "docetism" is applied to such views. Raymond E. Brown, *The Epistles of John* (Anchor Bible, 1982), argues the opponents were secessionists from John's own community [check: summary of Brown's thesis]. The study presents all this as a reconstruction; the letter names no opponent.
- *"Us" in 4:6.* (a) The apostolic eyewitnesses of 1:1–3, whose witness is now Scripture (the reading followed); (b) all faithful believers. No commentator is quoted for either in the content.
- *"Antichrist."* Left to *The hope to come* (30).

**Linked studies.** *God: who He is and what He does* (1 John 4:7–21); *The hope to come* (antichrist); *Who do you say that I am? Who Jesus really is* (incarnation, John 1).

### Galatians 1:6–12

**Context.** Paul omits his usual thanksgiving. The content of the "different gospel" is inferred from later chapters (5:2–4; 6:12–13): Gentile believers urged to be circumcised and keep the law of Moses.

**Main readings.**
- *The troublers.* Often called "Judaizers"; the label is later and can be used carelessly. The study describes circumcision in Jewish terms first and says Paul's dispute is about adding it as a condition for Gentiles, not about Jews keeping the covenant sign.
- *Works of the law.* The Reformation reading (Luther, Calvin): human attempts at merit. The "New Perspective on Paul" (E. P. Sanders; James D. G. Dunn, who named it in a 1982 lecture [check: date and title]): markers of Jewish identity such as circumcision, food laws and Sabbath. Not taken up in the content; either way the site affirms grace through faith in Christ alone (article IV).
- *1:8 and later revelation.* The study draws the principle that every later message is measured by the gospel received, and applies it, labelled as the site's reading, to claims of new revelation.

**Linked studies.** *The good news: how a person is made right with God*; *Saved by grace: how God forgives and changes us*.

### Acts 17:10–12

**Context.** Paul and Silas leave Thessalonica after opposition (17:5–9) and go to Berea. Luke records that Paul reasoned from the Scriptures that the Messiah had to suffer and rise (17:2–3). Followed by trouble from Thessalonica (17:13) and Athens (17:16–34, the speech in 17:22–31 owned by the God study).

**Main readings.** "Noble-minded" (Greek *eugenesteroi*) is read as open, generous hearing rather than social rank. The verse is widely used as the pattern for testing all teaching by Scripture. The study is careful that the commended listeners are synagogue Jews, and does not cast the Thessalonian opposition as typical of Jewish people.

**Linked studies.** *God: who He is and what He does* (17:22–31); *Reading the Bible well: where to start and how to keep going*; *What the Bible says about itself*.

### James 3:13–18

**Context.** Follows James's warning about the tongue (3:1–12) and his call for few to become teachers (3:1); leads into quarrels and their source (4:1–3). Echoes James 1:5 on asking God for wisdom.

**Main readings.** "Unspiritual" (*psychikē*, natural, merely human) and "demonic" (*daimoniōdēs*). Readers agree the passage tests the character of the one claiming wisdom; the study applies it to the one doing the discerning. "Accommodating" read as willing to yield and be reasoned with.

**Linked studies.** *The church and its calling* (Ephesians 4:11–16, quoted 4:14–15).

### 2 Peter 2:1–3

**Context.** Follows 2 Peter 1:16–21, on the apostles as eyewitnesses and prophecy coming from God; begins a chapter-long portrait of false teachers, parallel in places to Jude.

**Main readings.**
- *"The Master who bought them."* Calvinist reading: *despotēs* as sovereign owner; "bought" as ownership by creation or as purchase professed but not real. John Owen, *The Death of Death in the Death of Christ* (1647), treats the verse [check: Owen's treatment and where]. Non-Calvinist reading: Christ's death was for all, including those who finally deny him; David L. Allen, *The Extent of the Atonement* (2016), a Southern Baptist, argues for unlimited atonement [check: whether and where Allen discusses 2 Peter 2:1]. Both readings are held by Baptists within article V; the content does not choose and names no writer.
- *Greed and exploitation.* Linked carefully to prosperity teaching under article XIII, with an explicit line that classical Pentecostal churches are not the Word of Faith movement.

**Linked studies.** *Chosen and kept: can I be sure?*; *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount* (Matthew 7:15–20, quoted 7:15–16).
