# Learning to discern

Try the spirits. Hold fast that which is good. Seek wisdom from above.

> Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.

1 Thessalonians 5:21 · KJV

The letters ask believers to listen carefully and make judgments. A spiritual claim is to be tested. Teaching must be examined. Wisdom is shown in conduct, and understanding grows through practice. These tasks require attention both to what we receive and to how we respond.

Discernment serves the good: holding to what is true, refusing what is evil, and helping people grow in Christ. The passages below also examine the person making the judgment. Envy, pride, haste and contempt can distort an attempt to correct someone else.

## 01 Try the spirits

**Read:** 1 John 4:1–6; 1 Thessalonians 5:19–24.

### What is being claimed about Christ?

First John warns that many false prophets have gone into the world. The instruction to try the spirits concerns claims that present themselves as coming from God. The readers are not told to accept a message simply because it has spiritual language or an impressive messenger. — 1 John 4:1

The passage gives a particular test: the confession that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. It distinguishes the Spirit of God from the spirit of antichrist and connects truth with hearing the witness represented by the letter's “we.” The identity of Jesus and the received witness about him are central. — 1 John 4:2–6

This test has content. Asking what a teaching actually says about Jesus is different from deciding whether a speaker seems persuasive. The confession should be heard as an affirmation of who Jesus is, rather than treated as a password that makes every other claim trustworthy.

### Testing and receiving

First Thessalonians places three instructions together: do not quench the Spirit, do not despise prophesyings, and test what is presented, retaining the good. Receptiveness and examination belong in the same passage. Neither automatic acceptance nor contempt for every claim fulfils the whole instruction. — 1 Thessalonians 5:19–22

The paragraph ends with prayer for God's sanctifying work and confidence in the faithfulness of the one who calls. Testing is carried out within dependence on God, not as proof that the examiner can never be mistaken. — 1 Thessalonians 5:23–24

Christians differ about how prophecy operates today. Those differences require their own discussion. Here the immediate concern is the passage's combination of openness, testing and holding to the good; a claim of spiritual authority does not remove the responsibility to examine it.

**Consider:** What specific confession does First John name? Which instructions surround the call to prove all things in First Thessalonians?

**Reflect:** Take a spiritual claim you have encountered. State clearly what it says about Christ and what reason is offered for accepting it. Distinguish the claim itself from the speaker's confidence or reputation.

## 02 Examine teaching

**Read:** Galatians 1:6–12; Acts 17:10–12.

### The message and the messenger

Paul's opening rebuke in Galatians concerns a departure from the grace of Christ toward a distorted gospel. He places even himself, or an angel from heaven, under the warning against proclaiming a contrary message. The messenger's standing does not make an altered gospel acceptable. — Galatians 1:6–9

The passage also distinguishes seeking human approval from being Christ's servant. Paul describes his gospel as received through revelation of Jesus Christ. This is his account of the message entrusted to him; a later teacher cannot establish equivalent authority merely by claiming a revelation. Their teaching still needs examination. — Galatians 1:10–12

The severity of this warning concerns the gospel. It should not be borrowed to pronounce the same judgment over every disagreement about practice or every unfamiliar expression. First identify what is actually being taught and how it relates to the passage's concern.

### Receiving and searching

Acts describes the Bereans receiving the word readily and searching the Scriptures daily to see whether the things being taught were so. Their willingness to listen and their examination of the message appear together. Many then believed. — Acts 17:10–12

> These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.

Acts 17:11 · KJV

A present application is to read a cited passage beyond the sentence used to support a claim. Ask who is speaking, what question is being addressed and whether the proposed conclusion follows. Those are reading practices drawn from the example of examination; they are not an exhaustive method stated in this verse.

**Consider:** Why does Paul include himself in the warning? What two responses are commended in the account of Berea?

**Reflect:** Choose one teaching and trace its main claim to the passage offered in support. Read the surrounding paragraph before deciding whether the interpretation is sound.

## 03 Wisdom from above

**Read:** James 3:13–18; compare James 1:5–8.

### Wisdom made visible

James asks who is wise and understanding, then directs attention to conduct and works done in the meekness of wisdom. The claim to wisdom must be considered alongside the life in which it appears. Bitter envy and rivalry contradict the wisdom being claimed and produce disorder. — James 3:13–16

> But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.

James 3:17 · KJV

The description gives several things to look for together. Purity is joined to peace, gentleness, mercy and sincerity. Being peaceable does not mean making truth unimportant; being concerned for truth does not make envy or harshness wise. James closes with righteousness sown in peace by those who make peace. — James 3:17–18

### Willing to listen

The KJV's “easy to be intreated” describes a willingness to be approached and reasoned with. It offers a searching question for someone making a judgment: can I receive a question or correction, or must every challenge be treated as hostility?

Earlier, James invites the person lacking wisdom to ask God, who gives generously and without reproach. The following warning calls for faith rather than divided allegiance. Asking for wisdom acknowledges need; it does not certify every thought that follows as a message from God. — James 1:5–8

These passages direct examination inward as well as outward. We can ask whether a conclusion is supported and whether envy, rivalry or the wish to win is shaping our response. Good conduct does not replace careful attention to truth, but neither may truth be invoked to excuse corrupt motives.

**Consider:** What distinguishes the two kinds of wisdom in James? Which qualities concern how a person receives or treats others?

**Reflect:** Before continuing a disagreement, examine your purpose. Identify where patient listening, mercy or willingness to be corrected may change how you proceed.

## 04 Mature judgment

**Read:** Hebrews 5:11–6:3; Ephesians 4:11–16; Philippians 1:9–11.

### Understanding exercised through use

Hebrews rebukes its readers for becoming dull of hearing. By this time they ought to be able to teach, yet they need the beginnings taught again. Its contrast between milk and solid food leads to a description of discernment developed through use. — Hebrews 5:11–14

> But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil.

Hebrews 5:14 · KJV

The emphasis is on trained understanding, not possession of an impressive vocabulary. The following verses call the readers to go on toward maturity rather than repeatedly laying the foundation. The foundational teaching is something to build upon, not something declared false or unimportant. — Hebrews 6:1–3

### Growing with the body

Ephesians describes ministries given for the equipping and building up of Christ's body. One purpose is that believers cease being carried about by every wind of teaching. Speaking truth in love and the contribution of each part belong to that growth. Stability is presented as part of a community growing into Christ. — Ephesians 4:11–16

Philippians likewise joins love with knowledge and judgment, so that believers may approve what is excellent and bear the fruits of righteousness. Discernment is directed toward a faithful life and God's praise, not merely winning an argument. — Philippians 1:9–11

Practice may include reading attentively, applying what is understood, receiving correction and reconsidering an earlier conclusion. These are ways to pursue trained judgment; they do not produce a person who no longer needs others or cannot be wrong.

**Consider:** What does Hebrews connect with the ability to discern? How does Ephesians place stability in teaching within the life of the whole body?

**Reflect:** Identify a teaching you understand well enough to explain and one you need to revisit. Seek help with the latter without treating the need to learn as a failure to belong.

## 05 Good counsel

**Read:** Proverbs 15:22; 18:13–17. Compare James 3:13–18 and 1 Thessalonians 5:12–15.

### Hear before answering

> He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.

Proverbs 18:13 · KJV

Proverbs also observes that the first person to present a case seems right until another comes and examines it. Together these sayings warn against haste. A confident account may leave out something important, and an immediate answer may precede understanding. — Proverbs 18:13, 17

This does not require treating every account as dishonest. It asks us to hear and examine rather than letting the first presentation settle everything. In a present disagreement, distinguish what you know directly, what someone reports and what you have inferred.

### Counsel that can help

Proverbs commends a multitude of counsellors. Read alongside the warning about answering too quickly, this encourages seeking help that broadens understanding. It is not a promise that the largest group is always correct. Advice still needs to be weighed. — Proverbs 15:22; 18:13–17

First Thessalonians asks believers to recognise and esteem those who labour among them, then gives different responses to different needs: warn the unruly, comfort the fainthearted and support the weak, with patience toward all. Helpful counsel pays attention to the person and the situation instead of repeating one response to every difficulty. — 1 Thessalonians 5:12–15

James' account of wisdom also gives grounds for considering counsel: is it truthful, peaceable, merciful and open to examination? A respected person's advice deserves a hearing, but respect does not require treating that person as beyond correction.

**Consider:** What can the first account of a matter leave unresolved? Why does First Thessalonians name different responses to different people?

**Reflect:** Before making a difficult judgment, identify what you still need to know. Seek counsel from someone able to listen carefully and explain their reasons, including reasons you may not prefer.

## 06 Disputed matters

**Read:** Romans 14:1–23.

### Identify the kind of disagreement

Romans describes differing convictions about food and days. It instructs believers to receive one another without contempt or condemnation over these practices. Paul names accountability to the Lord and the intention to honour him as part of the setting. — Romans 14:1–12

This differs from the altered gospel addressed in Galatians 1. Careful judgment begins by identifying what is disputed. A difference in practice should not automatically be called a denial of the gospel; a claim about the gospel should not automatically be dismissed as personal preference.

The comparison is a reading task, not a shortcut that settles every modern disagreement. Establish what each side claims and which passages address it. Some questions will require further study before a conclusion is warranted.

### Conviction joined to care

Paul's instruction continues beyond allowing different practices. Believers must consider whether their conduct becomes a stumbling block to someone else. He directs them toward peace and building one another up, and warns against acting without the conviction that the action is right before God. — Romans 14:13–23

> Let us therefore follow after the things which make for peace, and things wherewith one may edify another.

Romans 14:19 · KJV

Discernment here includes judgment about our own use of freedom. Being persuaded that something is permissible does not finish the question of how to act lovingly. Nor does protecting another person's conscience require pretending every interpretation is equally well supported.

**Consider:** What particular practices does Paul discuss? How does his concern for another person's good shape the use of freedom?

**Reflect:** State a disagreement without exaggerating the other person's position. Ask what is clear from the text, what remains uncertain, and what conduct would serve peace while further understanding is sought.

## Hold fast that which is good

These readings give discernment a purpose: receive truth, refuse evil and grow in a life pleasing to God. They also give it a manner: listen before answering, test claims, welcome sound correction and speak truth in love.

When a question arises, begin with what is actually being claimed and the passage offered for it. Give the text a full hearing. Seek wisdom, weigh counsel and remain willing to learn. Acknowledging what you do not yet know can be part of reaching a sound judgment.
