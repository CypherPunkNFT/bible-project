## Interpretive boundaries

- **Burdens:** the draft does not build a rigid distinction between two kinds of load from the English word “burden.” Galatians 6:2–5 supports discussing mutual help and personal responsibility together without an unsupported word study.
- **Faintheartedness:** the KJV's “feebleminded” in 1 Thessalonians 5:14 is explained in its older sense; it is not used as a modern diagnosis. The neighbouring distinctions are pastoral instructions, not clinical categories.
- **Gathering:** Hebrews 10:25 is read through the paragraph's purpose of mutual encouragement. The study does not derive attendance quotas or settle every circumstance preventing attendance.
- **Work and provision:** 2 Thessalonians 3 addresses unwillingness and disorderly conduct. Do not extend it to incapacity, unemployment despite willingness, or every person needing material help.
- **Discipline:** Galatians, Thessalonians and Corinthians address different situations. Their passages are not treated as a complete universal procedure. No identification of the offender in 2 Corinthians 2 with the person in 1 Corinthians 5 is required by this draft.
- **Restoration and harm:** the reflection about harm, trust and responsibility is editorial application. The draft neither trivialises wrongdoing nor offers a safeguarding or legal procedure.
- **Gifts:** this treatment concerns purpose and mutual care. It does not resolve debates about the continuation of particular gifts, the meaning of Spirit baptism, or qualifications for church offices.
- **Conscience:** the draft keeps Romans 14's food-and-days context visible. It does not declare every doctrine or moral command a disputable matter. No unsupported claim about the precise ethnic identity of every group in the dispute is needed.
- **Unity:** Ephesians' shared confession is retained. The study does not treat unity as either complete agreement about every question or silence about wrongdoing.

## Decisions and remaining refinements

The six original sections remain. Burden-bearing emphasises help and humility; correction has a separate section so that support is not reduced to admonition. The final section distinguishes conscience disputes from conduct requiring correction. Reflection questions are kept beside each section, as in item 01.

Possible later refinements: add a short worked case distinguishing comfort from correction; decide whether a separate discussion of receiving help would strengthen the burden-bearing section. Neither is required to make this first draft readable. No design or implementation work has begun.
