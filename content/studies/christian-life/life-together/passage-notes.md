## Passages this study owns

### John 13:34–35
**Context.** The last supper in John's Gospel, after the foot-washing (13:1–17) and Judas's departure (13:30), at the start of the farewell discourse.
**Main readings.** Nearly all readers take the "newness" to lie in the measure ("As I have loved you") rather than in the command to love, which is in Leviticus 19:18. Readers differ on whether "one another" is first the love among disciples (the reading followed here, with Galatians 6:10 for the wider circle) or love of neighbour in general; most read it first of disciples.
**Links.** *Who do you say that I am?* (11); *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount* (16) for love of enemies.

### 1 Corinthians 13
**Context.** The centre of Paul's argument on spiritual gifts (chapters 12–14) in a divided church.
**Main readings.** Read as a correction of Corinth's pride by most commentators (the reading here). On 13:8–12, "the perfect": most readers on both sides of the continuation question take it as Christ's return; some cessationists read it as the completed New Testament; most cessationists argue from other texts (for example Ephesians 2:20, Hebrews 2:3–4). The confession is silent; the study does not choose.
**Links.** *The Holy Spirit* (23) for the gifts; *The church and its calling* (28) for 1 Corinthians 12:12–31 and 14.

### Galatians 6:1–10
**Context.** After Paul's argument that people are justified by faith in Christ, not law-keeping, and the call to use freedom in loving service (5:13–26, owned by 23 for 5:16–26).
**Main readings.** "Law of Christ" (6:2): most read it as the love command (Galatians 5:14; John 13:34); some as Christ's teaching as a whole. 6:2 and 6:5 distinguished as burden and load (two Greek words, *baros* and *phortion*) [check: confirm the Greek words]. 6:7–9 read as moral and spiritual harvest; prosperity readings rejected (article XIII).
**Links.** *Faith in daily life* (05) for freedom limited by love; *The Holy Spirit* (23) for Galatians 5:16–26; *Money, possessions and generosity* (63).

### 1 John 1:5–2:2
**Context.** John's opening argument against claims to know God that ignore sin.
**Main readings.** 1:7's "fellowship with one another" read of believers together. 1:9 grounded in 2:1–2 (advocate, atoning sacrifice). 2:2 "the whole world": Baptists differ (every person; people of every nation); the confession does not fix the extent of the atonement; not chosen here. Confession practice: Catholic sacrament of Penance and Lutheran private confession and absolution described as theirs; Baptist view stated in the site's voice.
**Links.** *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* (25); *Chosen and kept* (58); *What Scripture promises the sick* (45) for James 5:16; *A new creation* (01) for Ephesians 4:32 and Colossians 3:13.

### Matthew 18:15–20
**Context.** Between the parable of the lost sheep (18:12–14) and the unforgiving servant (18:21–35). Jesus quotes Deuteronomy 19:15, described in Jewish terms as protection for the accused.
**Main readings.** Steps of correction with restoration as aim. "Pagan or tax collector" read as treating the person as outside fellowship, still to be won back. Binding and loosing: Baptist reading as the church's decision agreeing with heaven when it acts by Christ's word; Roman Catholic teaching connects binding and loosing with the authority of the Church's teaching office (not quoted on the page). 18:19–20 read in context of discipline decisions, with the wider prayer-meeting use acknowledged.
**Care.** The page says Matthew 18 does not require a victim of abuse to confront an abuser privately; crimes go to the authorities.
**Links.** *Baptism and the Lord's Supper* (59) for membership; *The church and its calling* (28) for congregational government; *The Christian and the state* (64).

### 2 Corinthians 2:5–11
**Context.** Paul's letter after a painful visit and a "tearful letter"; the church had disciplined an offender.
**Main readings.** "By the majority" (2:6) read as congregational action. Identity of the offender left open: the man of 1 Corinthians 5 (older view, held by many early and older readers) or someone who opposed Paul (many recent commentators, from 2:5 and 7:12).
**Links.** *The church and its calling* (28).

### Hebrews 10:23–25
**Context.** After the long argument about Christ as priest (owned in its parts by 41, 47 and the cross study); a pressured community tempted to drift (10:32–34).
**Main readings.** Three "let us" sentences: hope, mutual consideration, gathering. "The Day" read as Christ's return. Membership drawn from the wider New Testament pattern; article VII quoted on baptism as prerequisite to membership.
**Links.** *Baptism and the Lord's Supper* (59); *The church and its calling* (28); *The hope to come* (30).

### Philippians 2:1–4
**Context.** Paul's appeal to a church with a quarrel (4:2), leading into the hymn of Christ's humility (2:5–8, owned by 11).
**Main readings.** The "if" clauses read as "since". 2:4's "not only… but also" read as allowing legitimate self-interest.
**Links.** *Who do you say that I am?* (11) for 2:5–8; *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* (25) for 2:9–11.
