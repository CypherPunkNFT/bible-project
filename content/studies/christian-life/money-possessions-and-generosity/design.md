## Direction

Teach one habit: before you claim a promise about money, read the address on it. Ask who first heard it, under which of God's promises, what it was for, and what it does not promise. The page draws the habit as an address: a postmark that says where in the Bible the words were spoken, and address lines written on fine rules. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the four concepts in gold (Gift · Tithe · Treasure · Need), the title *Money,* with *possessions and generosity* in gold italic, the lead, 1 Chronicles 29:14, and a drawing in a faint circle and dotted orbit: a bound sheaf, a jar for oil and two small copper coins on the stone step of a gate, with the motto "Everything comes from You. / From Your hand we give." No one is drawn. Four counted figures, each with what it means; the care note with the crisis lines; a row of links to every part.
- **00 Read the address before the promise.** The frame paragraph, then five lines often lifted out of their setting (Deuteronomy 8:18, Malachi 3:10, Proverbs 3:10, Luke 16:9, Mark 12:44), each with its postmark. One switch shows them "As it is often heard", with the study's own words for how each is misused, or "With its address", which adds who first heard it, under which promise, what it was for and what it does not promise. The choice is kept in the page's address. The selection note follows.
- **01–07 The parts.** Each opens with its number in the colour of its part of the Bible, its title, its hook and main readings, and its own address beside them. Then one figure from its own passage, the text, the passages printed in full, notes marked "How this study reads it", "Where readers differ", "Its limits" and "With care", and Consider and Reflect side by side.
  - 01: the order of Deuteronomy 8:12–14, four gifts rising and then "then", the proud heart and forgetting.
  - 02: David's prayer counted: every word that speaks to God (25) and every word for the givers (20), verse by verse.
  - 03: where the tenth went, six years as the rabbis count them (the third and sixth for the poor), and the four guests of 14:29.
  - 04: no figure of its own; the study's own address list (who, the storehouse, the blessing, the result) is set as address lines.
  - 05: the two bills, 100 to 50 and 100 to 80.
  - 06: Luke 16:25 drawn as a crossing, then the gate thread from Deuteronomy 14:29 to Luke 16:20 and 16:29.
  - 07: what each gift left behind, drawn as proportions only, because Mark gives no amounts.
- **Questions readers bring.** Four questions joined by a fine line, one open at a time: Should Christians tithe? (the two Baptist readings side by side, without choosing), Does God promise wealth to those who give?, Is my poverty a sign of weak faith? And what about debt?, Is it wrong to be rich? The first is open from the start.
- **Close.** The habit as a loop of five steps (the address, God, your heart, its limit, again), the way of reading, the close, the invitation, the question for the reader, and 1 Chronicles 29:10–19 printed in full. Then a drawing of a table set outside an open gate, the sources and a back link.
- **On phones** the parts' postmarks are left out (their address lines stay), and the figures stack.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean.

## Colour

The Gospels' violet for the page: the chapter at the study's centre is Luke 16, and no other study in Living the Christian Life uses it. Each postmark and part number takes the colour of its part of the Bible (History orange for Deuteronomy and Chronicles, the Prophets' blue for Malachi, Poetry teal for Proverbs, the Gospels' violet for Luke and Mark), and the section is named in words around the ring, so the colour repeats what the words say. The site's gold marks the concepts, the title's turn, the care notes and the words that speak to God in part 02.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article XIII (stewardship): everything comes from God, and giving is cheerful, regular, systematic, proportionate and liberal. Whether a tenth binds Christians is a question on which Baptists differ, so the page sets the two readings side by side and does not choose; storehouse tithing is shown as one Baptist application. Giving never buys a return from God, and the page never presents a verse as a financial guarantee. Poverty and debt are never treated as a sign of weak faith, and the crisis lines and the Help for life link stand at the top. Salvation is by personal faith in Christ (article IV); the Lazarus story is not read as saving the poor for being poor. Jewish teaching on the tithe and on Malachi is given first and named as theirs. The Pharisees are described as Luke describes those hearers, never as a people. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
