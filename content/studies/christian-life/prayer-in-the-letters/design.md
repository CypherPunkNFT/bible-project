## Direction

Teach one habit: don't stop at the request; ask, so that what? The rewritten text follows the Lord's Prayer line by line and sets the letters' prayers beside it, so the page does the same: the prayer Jesus taught is the way into every part, and each letter prayer is read from its thanks, through its request, to where it arrives. Both shapes say one thing: a prayer is aimed beyond the thing asked for. Only one direction was built, as for every existing draft.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the five concepts in gold (Father · Name · Bread · Forgiveness · So that), "Prayer" and "across Scripture" in gold italic, the lead, Luke 11:1 with its paragraph, the drawing kept from the first design (a leaf of a letter whose lines step in, and a slow dotted thread going on past the page) with the motto "Teach us to pray. / Then ask: so that what?", a pause control, four figures, the frame paragraph and the selection note.
- **01 The prayer Jesus taught. Line by line.** The signature, new in this reshape. The prayer as ruled lines of text: the disciple's request, the address, three lines about God, three about us. Choosing a line shows the part that reads it, beside the prayer on a wide screen and below it on a narrow one. Under each line, the letters this study pairs with it, labelled as the study's pairing. The prayer is the only menu for the parts.
- **The six parts.** Teach us to pray; Hallowed be Your name; Daily bread; Forgive us our debts; Deliver us from the evil one; Our Father. Each has its hook, its main readings, each letter passage whole on a tinted leaf broken into lines that step in at each "so that" (with "For whom" and "What gives confidence"), the study's sub-sections, Consider and Reflect.
- **02 Six prayers in the letters. None stops at the request.** Kept: six columns read from the bottom up, each linking to the part that reads it.
- **03 Six questions, faced.** A plain ruled list. Each opens the one sheet from the right: the passage (two for the question on asking), then the study's reading, care notes and what others have taught. The question on unanswered prayer opens with its care note and crisis lines.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer" with Matthew 6:9–13, the way back and the neighbouring studies, and the sources.

## Choices made, and why

- **The prayer is the menu, not a fourth section.** A plan review showed that a Lord's Prayer section, the columns and a list of parts would be three ways into the same six things. Making the prayer's lines choose the part gives the reader the study's own order and keeps one way in.
- **Ruled lines, not columns, for the prayer.** So it can never be mistaken for the six columns of the letters' prayers, which read upward.
- **Kept from the first design:** the drawing, the stepped lines, the "For whom" and "What gives confidence" notes, the columns, the one sheet, the gold and ink.
- **Removed with the text that needed them:** the three requests about circumstances (the road and the door now live in part 03), the older lines from earlier Scripture, the three prayers to follow unaided, and the altar drawing for Psalm 141:2.
- **One colour.** The letters' gold and ink; each kind of line differs by its word and shape, so colour never carries meaning alone.
- **No face and no praying figure.** Illustrative forms only, captioned as such.

## Doctrine and care

- Prayer goes to the Father and to the Lord Jesus; the page shows the one Mediator question with Roman Catholic teaching labelled as theirs and the Baptist confessions beside it.
- Where Baptists differ (election and "everyone" in 1 Timothy 2:4; whether "pray in the Spirit" includes tongues; how to seek God's will in personal decisions) the page shows the views and does not choose.
- Care notes stay where the text puts them: on forgiveness and abuse, on persecution and safety, and the crisis lines that open the question on unanswered prayer. The page teaches no promise of health or wealth in return for faith.
- Three things in the text need the owner's word, listed in the mock-up's record: the count of "ten" letter passages (the study reads nine), "Savior" for the confession's "Saviour", and "thorn in the flesh" for the Berean's "thorn in my flesh".
