## Direction

Teach one habit: don't stop at the request; ask, so that what? The picture is a prayer read from the bottom up, rising from thanks through a request to where it arrives. Only one direction was built, as with the earlier mock-ups of existing drafts.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the three concepts in gold, "Prayer" and "in the letters" in gold italic, the lead, Colossians 4:2, a thin-line drawing of a leaf of a letter with its lines stepping in and a slow dotted thread going on past the page, the motto, a pause control and the figures.
- **01 Six prayers. None stops at the request.** The signature. Six columns side by side, read upward. Pointing or keyboard focus shows the verse on a line beneath, and choosing opens it on the sheet. Ephesians 3 shows its empty thanks. On narrower screens the columns scroll sideways with the next one showing.
- **02 Keep it whole. See where it goes.** A rail of six readings beside the chosen one. Each passage sits whole on a tinted leaf, broken into lines that step in at each "so that", with "For whom" and "What gives confidence" written beside it. Reading 5 uses the same passages with only the thanks picked out.
- **03 A road, a door, a quiet life.** Three small written climbs, what is asked and where it goes, then what the letters say about asking for circumstances, with a care note.
- **04 Older words, prayed again.** Six older lines beside lines in these passages, each saying how close the two are and on whose word.
- **05 Three more prayers. Follow them yourself.** Three passages unbroken, with this study's lines closed until asked for.
- **06 Six questions, faced.** A plain ruled list. Each opens the one sheet from the right, with the verse, then this study's reading, then what else has been said.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer" with Ephesians 3:14–21, the way back to Living the Christian Life, a small altar-like shape with a thread of incense for Psalm 141:2, and the sources.

## Choices made, and why

- **Lines, not marks.** Faith under pressure already marks passages, with a filter. Stepped lines keep every word in order and need no state beyond which reading is open.
- **Written notes for "for whom" and "what gives confidence".** A mark on one verse would claim too much.
- **One colour.** The letters' gold and ink, with each kind of line differing by its word and shape, so colour never carries meaning alone.
- **Reported prayers and instructions told apart,** on each leaf and in the figures, as the draft asked.
- **No face and no praying figure.** Illustrative forms only, captioned as such.
