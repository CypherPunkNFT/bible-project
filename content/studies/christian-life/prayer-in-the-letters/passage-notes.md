# Passage notes: Prayer across Scripture: learning to pray (04)

Reshape of draft 04 *Prayer in the letters*. The Lord's Prayer is now the spine; the "so that what?" habit and the letter passages are kept; the reader question "How do I know God's will for my life?" is new. All quotations are from the supplied Berean Standard Bible text. Verses not supplied (Philippians 4:6, James 1:13, James 5:14, 3 John 1:2, 1 John 5:14, Hebrews 7:25, 2 Corinthians 12:7–10, Romans 12:19, Luke 6:12, Luke 22:42, Matthew 18:21–35, Exodus 16, Deuteronomy 6:4–9, 1 Thessalonians 4:3 and 5:18, Colossians 4:5–6) are referred to, not quoted. The draft's quotations of 2 Corinthians 12, 1 John 5:14, Hebrews 7:25, Romans 12:19, Psalm 141:2, Revelation 5:8 and 3 John 1:2 were dropped for that reason.

## Matthew 6:5–15 (owned)

**Context.** In the Sermon on the Mount, inside Jesus' teaching on giving, prayer and fasting done "in secret" (6:1–18). The rest of Matthew 5–7 belongs to *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount* (16); this study walks only 6:5–15.

**Main readings.**
- The prayer as words to say and as a pattern for one's own prayers: both held widely; the study follows both.
- The six requests read as three for God's honour and three for human need: a common Christian reading (e.g. the catechisms' petition-by-petition order).
- 6:14–15: forgiving others as the sign and fruit of being forgiven, not its price (the reading followed, consistent with BFM article IV); other readers stress it as a condition. The study reads it with Matthew 18:21–35.
- 6:13 "lead us not into temptation": a request to be kept from overwhelming testing, read beside James 1:13.
- The closing doxology: not printed in the BSB text; treated as a question about the manuscripts.
- Jewish context: Shema, Amidah, Kaddish, "Avinu" described first; 6:5 read as a warning against display, not against Jewish prayer.

**Links.** *Following Jesus and the Sermon on the Mount* (16); *Fear, anxiety and despair* (56) for 6:25–34; *Faith under pressure* (03) for temptation; *Angels, demons and spiritual powers* (53) for "the evil one".

## Luke 11:1–13 (owned)

**Context.** Luke's form of the prayer, prompted by a disciple's request after Jesus has prayed, followed by the friend at midnight and the good father.

**Main readings.**
- Matthew and Luke: Jesus taught the prayer more than once, or one teaching recorded in two forms. This study does not choose and does not raise it on the page beyond noting the shorter form.
- The friend at midnight read as "how much more", God as the contrast to the reluctant neighbour (the reading followed); some read it as a lesson in persistence itself; the study keeps both within the contrast.
- 11:13: the Holy Spirit as the gift named, against a blank-cheque reading.

**Links.** *The Holy Spirit* (23).

## Ephesians 1:15–23 (owned)

**Context.** Paul's report of his prayer after the blessing of 1:3–14 (owned by *Chosen and kept*, 58).

**Main readings.** "A spirit of wisdom" (1:17) as the Holy Spirit's work (Calvin; Barnes; Jamieson, Fausset and Brown, per the draft). No contrary reader was sought. 1:20–23 quoted only for 1:22.

**Links.** *Chosen and kept: can I be sure?* (58); the cross study quotes 1:20–23; 53 quotes 1:20–21.

## Ephesians 3:14–21 (owned)

**Context.** Paul resumes the prayer begun at 3:1, ending in praise.

**Main readings.** "Filled with all the fullness of God" read as God filling His people, not deification. 3:20 read as praise, not a promise of every request (Calvin quoted from the draft).

**Links.** None required beyond the question on asking.

## Philippians 1:3–11 (owned)

**Context.** Thanksgiving and prayer at the opening of a letter from prison. 1:12–30 belongs to *Witness and the nations* (09).

**Main readings.** Love growing "in knowledge and depth of insight" read as discerning love; placed beside the forgiveness line by this study's own pairing.

**Links.** *Witness and the nations: telling others about Jesus* (09); grief study quotes 1:21–23.

## Colossians 1:3–14 (owned)

**Context.** Opening thanksgiving and prayer; the hymn to Christ follows at 1:15.

**Main readings.** "Knowledge of His will" leading to a worthy walk, used as the main text for the God's-will question. The worthy walk read as growth, not merit (BFM article IV).

**Links.** The God's-will question links *A new creation* (01), *Faith under pressure* (03), *The wisdom books* (65).

## Colossians 4:2–4 (owned)

**Context.** Closing instructions; 4:5–6 (conduct toward outsiders) referred to, not quoted.

**Main readings.** A prayer for circumstances (a "door") with a stated purpose.

## 1 Thessalonians 3:9–13 (owned)

**Context.** After Timothy's report from Thessalonica; Paul longs to return.

**Main readings.** Prayer addressed to the Father and the Lord Jesus together (3:11), noted with BFM article II. 3:13 looks to Christ's coming; no end-time order is chosen.

**Links.** *The hope to come* (30) holds 1 Thessalonians 4:13–5:11.

## 2 Thessalonians 1:3–12 (owned)

**Context.** Thanksgiving for a persecuted church, an account of judgement at Christ's revealing, then prayer.

**Main readings.** 1:6 read as God's justice, not licence for revenge; [care] note on safety. 1:6–10 not walked; judgement left to 30.

**Links.** *The hope to come* (30).

## 1 Timothy 2:1–6 (owned)

**Context.** Instructions for the church's prayer; the passage goes on to roles in worship (2:8–15), not walked here.

**Main readings.**
- Prayer for rulers without approval of them (Calvin, from the draft; BFM article XVII).
- "Everyone" in 2:4: Boyce (effectual grace to some, sincere call to all), Calvin (classes of people), Wesley (every person). The confession's limits stated; the study does not choose. The draft's paragraph on the 2012 "Traditional Statement" was dropped for length and because its text was only partly checked; the full discussion belongs to 58.
- "One mediator": Trent quoted as Catholic teaching; Second London Confession quoted as older Baptist teaching.

**Links.** *The Christian and the state* (64); *Chosen and kept: can I be sure?* (58).

## Ephesians 6:18–20 (owned)

**Context.** After the armour of God (6:10–17, not walked here).

**Main readings.** "Pray in the Spirit": prayer led and helped by the Spirit; whether it includes tongues left open, since the confession does not settle whether such gifts continue.

**Links.** *The Holy Spirit* (23); *Witness and the nations* (09); *Angels, demons and spiritual powers* (53) for the armour passage.
