# Prayer in the letters

Giving thanks. Making request. Seeking the good of others.

> Continue in prayer, and watch in the same with thanksgiving;

Colossians 4:2 · KJV

The prayers in the letters tell us what the writers desire for the people they address. They give thanks for faith and love already present, ask for understanding and strength, and look toward lives that honour the Lord. Reading these prayers allows us to hear both affection for a community and concern for its growth.

Begin with each passage as a whole. Ask whom the writer is praying for, what he requests, why he requests it, and what gives him confidence to ask. Then consider how those requests might shape prayer for people you know. The prayers below describe particular communities; their words can guide our prayers without becoming a formula that guarantees a chosen outcome.

## 01 Wisdom and understanding

**Read:** Ephesians 1:15–23.

### Knowing him

Paul begins with what he has heard: the readers' faith in the Lord Jesus and love toward the saints. He gives thanks for them and remembers them in prayer. His request grows out of that gratitude. People who already believe are prayed for as people who still need to grow in knowing God. — Ephesians 1:15–17

> That the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give unto you the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him:

Ephesians 1:17 · KJV

The request concerns knowledge of God. The following verses name what the readers are to understand: the hope of his calling, the riches of his inheritance in the saints, and the greatness of his power toward believers. These give the prayer its substance. It is not simply a request for more information or for advance knowledge of every personal decision. — Ephesians 1:18–19

### The power shown in Christ

Paul describes God's power by speaking of Christ's resurrection and exaltation. Christ is above every power and name, and is given as head over all things to the church, his body. The prayer opens into an account of who Christ is and what God has done in him. — Ephesians 1:20–23

Read the request and this account together. The confidence of the prayer rests in God, whose power has been displayed in Christ. The reader's understanding is directed toward that reality rather than toward confidence in the intensity of the prayer itself.

**Consider:** What does Paul already give thanks for? What three things does he ask the readers to know, and how does the description of Christ support the request?

**Reflect:** Pray for someone who already believes. Alongside a request about their circumstances, ask that they may know God more deeply and understand the hope of his calling.

## 02 Strength within

**Read:** Ephesians 3:14–21.

### Strengthened by his Spirit

Paul bows his knees to the Father and asks that the readers be strengthened by the Spirit in the inner person. The prayer continues with Christ dwelling in their hearts by faith and their being rooted and grounded in love. Strength, faith and love belong together in the request. — Ephesians 3:14–17

This is more than a request to appear strong before others. The prayer concerns the inward life and the readers' relationship to Christ. Its measure is the riches of God's glory, not the resources they can produce by themselves.

Paul then asks that they comprehend with all the saints and know Christ's love, which surpasses knowledge. The prayer holds together real knowledge and a love greater than the reader can exhaust. Its language is shared: the request concerns the community, together with other believers. — Ephesians 3:18–19

### Prayer becoming praise

The prayer ends by praising God as able to do far beyond what is asked or imagined, according to the power at work in believers. Its final concern is glory to God in the church through Christ Jesus. — Ephesians 3:20–21

That ending belongs to the requests that precede it: strength by the Spirit, Christ dwelling through faith, love and fullness. It should be read with those requests before being applied to a person's own ambitions. God's ability is celebrated; the passage does not promise that every ambition will be granted.

**Consider:** What kind of strength is requested? Why does it matter that understanding Christ's love is described as something shared with all the saints?

**Reflect:** Think of a community whose outward difficulties are easy to name. Pray also for its inward life: strength, faith and a deeper knowledge of Christ's love.

## 03 Growing in love

**Read:** Philippians 1:3–11. Then compare 1 Thessalonians 3:9–13.

### Love with knowledge and judgment

In Philippians, thanksgiving comes before the request. Paul remembers the believers' fellowship in the gospel and their sharing with him in his imprisonment and defence of the gospel. He expresses affection for them and confidence in God's continuing work. The prayer is spoken within a relationship, not as an assessment delivered from a distance. — Philippians 1:3–8

> And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment;

Philippians 1:9 · KJV

Increasing love is joined to knowledge and judgment, or discernment. Paul asks that the readers approve what is excellent and be sincere and without offence until the day of Christ. He looks for the fruits of righteousness through Jesus Christ, to God's glory and praise. Love is to grow in how it recognises and pursues what is good. — Philippians 1:9–11

### Toward one another and toward all

First Thessalonians also joins affection, thanksgiving and prayer. Paul longs to see the readers and supply what is lacking in their faith. He asks God to direct his way to them, then asks the Lord to increase their love toward one another and toward all. The prayer looks toward hearts established in holiness at Christ's coming. — 1 Thessalonians 3:9–13

The two prayers share a future horizon while addressing present relationships. Neither treats love as a quality already finished. There is more to learn, more room to grow and a wider circle of people to love.

**Consider:** How does Philippians connect love with discernment? How far does the request for love extend in 1 Thessalonians?

**Reflect:** Pray for love to increase in a particular relationship. Ask also for understanding of what would actually serve that person's good, rather than assuming every agreeable response is loving.

## 04 A worthy walk

**Read:** Colossians 1:3–14. Then compare 2 Thessalonians 1:3–12.

### Knowledge bearing fruit

Colossians begins with thanks for faith in Christ, love for the saints and the hope heard in the gospel. Paul speaks of that gospel bearing fruit among the readers and of Epaphras bringing news of their love. The request that follows continues this concern for a life shaped by the gospel. — Colossians 1:3–9

Paul asks that they be filled with the knowledge of God's will in wisdom and spiritual understanding, so that they walk worthily of the Lord. He describes such a walk through good works, growing knowledge of God, endurance and thanksgiving. Knowledge is directed toward life. — Colossians 1:9–12

The strength requested is strength for patience and longsuffering with joy. It is not measured only by striking achievements. Remaining faithful through difficulty also belongs to this prayer. — Colossians 1:11

### Conduct and grace

The thanksgiving at the end names what the Father has done: enabled the saints to share in the inheritance, delivered them from darkness and brought them into the kingdom of his Son, in whom there is redemption and forgiveness. The call to a worthy walk appears within this account of God's saving action. — Colossians 1:12–14

Second Thessalonians addresses believers enduring persecution. After thanksgiving and an account of coming judgment and rest, Paul prays that God will count them worthy of their calling and fulfil the work of faith with power. The stated purpose is that the name of the Lord Jesus be glorified in them, according to grace. The difficult circumstances remain part of the prayer's setting. — 2 Thessalonians 1:3–12

**Consider:** What does Colossians name as the fruit of understanding God's will? What actions of God surround the request for the readers' conduct?

**Reflect:** Name an ordinary responsibility in which faithfulness is needed. Pray for understanding that becomes action, and for endurance where the good work is slow or unnoticed.

## 05 Thanksgiving

**Read again:** Philippians 1:3–11 and Colossians 1:3–14, now attending to what prompts thanks.

### Naming what has been received

> I thank my God upon every remembrance of you,

Philippians 1:3 · KJV

Paul's thanksgiving in Philippians names fellowship in the gospel and a shared history. In Colossians, it names faith, love, hope and the gospel bearing fruit. Gratitude is particular: the readers can hear what has prompted it. — Philippians 1:3–8; Colossians 1:3–8

This offers a way to pray for others that includes more than their problems. We can notice faithfulness, love shown to another person, generosity, endurance or growth, and give thanks to God for those things. The example invites attention to what is already present without making a claim that a community has no faults.

### Gratitude and further requests

Both passages move from thanks into prayer for growth. Paul can be grateful for the Philippians' fellowship and still ask that their love increase. He can thank God for the Colossians' faith and still pray for fuller understanding and a worthy walk. Thanksgiving and the recognition of need do not cancel one another. — Philippians 1:9–11; Colossians 1:9–14

Read the whole prayers again rather than lifting out their openings alone. The movement from gratitude to request helps us speak truthfully about both the good already received and the good still sought.

**Consider:** What specifically prompts thanksgiving in each passage? How do the later requests build on what the writer has already recognised?

**Reflect:** Name a person or community, identify something concrete for which to thank God, and make one request for their continuing growth.

## 06 Prayer for others

**Read:** 1 Timothy 2:1–6; Ephesians 6:18–20; Colossians 4:2–6.

### Beyond our immediate circle

First Timothy instructs that requests, prayers, intercessions and thanksgiving be made for all people, including kings and those in authority. It names a quiet and peaceable life in godliness and honesty as a purpose, then speaks of God's desire for salvation and knowledge of the truth, the one mediator Jesus Christ, and his giving himself as a ransom. The instruction places public life and concern for others before God. — 1 Timothy 2:1–6

Praying for a ruler is not the same as approving every action of that ruler. The stated concerns are godliness, peace and people's good before God. The breadth of the instruction challenges a prayer life restricted to those we already favour.

### Those who ask for our prayers

Ephesians calls for persevering prayer for all the saints. Paul includes himself, asking for words and boldness to make the gospel known while he is an ambassador in bonds. The one praying for others also asks others to pray for him. — Ephesians 6:18–20

Colossians similarly asks for an open door to speak and for clarity in making Christ known. The following instructions concern wise conduct toward outsiders and gracious speech. Prayer for opportunity sits beside responsibility for how people speak and live. — Colossians 4:2–6

These are instructions and requests for prayer, whereas earlier passages report what Paul prays for his readers. Keeping that distinction helps us hear both sides of the relationship: receiving another person's prayers and joining in prayer for them.

**Consider:** Who is included in these instructions? What does Paul request in Ephesians and Colossians, and what responsibilities accompany the requests?

**Reflect:** Pray beyond your familiar circle. Include someone in public responsibility and someone speaking about Christ; ask for the good named in these passages, including wisdom, boldness and gracious speech.

## Learning to make request

Choose one complete prayer and read it slowly. Notice its thanksgiving, its requests and its reasons. Then pray for an actual person or community in words attentive to both the passage and their circumstances.

The letters give us words for knowledge of God, strength by the Spirit, increasing love, faithful conduct, gratitude and concern for others. They teach us to ask for more than a change of circumstances: also for the people living within them to know God and grow in what is good.
