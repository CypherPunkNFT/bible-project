## Numbers 11:10–17 (owned)

**Context.** Israel in the wilderness after the exodus; the people crave meat beyond the manna (11:4–9). Moses' complaint (11:11–15) ends in "please kill me right now". God's answer (11:16–17): seventy elders at the Tent of Meeting, a share of the Spirit on Moses given to them, "so that you do not have to bear it by yourself". The chapter goes on to the elders prophesying (11:24–25), Eldad and Medad (11:26–29) and the quail with judgement on the craving (11:31–34); those verses are referred to only.

**Main readings.** (1) Pastoral reading followed here: God answers the need beneath the prayer by sharing the burden; God's anger in 11:10 is at the people's complaint. (2) A reading that takes Moses' prayer as itself faulty, a lapse in faith that God bears with. Both agree God answers with provision rather than rebuke at this point. No named commentator has been read for this page [check: add a named commentator on Numbers 11:15, if wanted].

**Links.** Study 21 (*God: who He is and what He does*) for Moses more generally; study 23 (*The Holy Spirit*) for the Spirit on the elders.

## 1 Kings 19:1–18 (owned)

**Context.** Directly after Carmel (1 Kings 18, not walked). Jezebel's oath (19:2); flight to Beersheba and the wilderness (19:3–4); prayer to die; sleep, the angel's touch, bread and water twice (19:5–8); forty days to Horeb; God's question twice and Elijah's identical answer twice (19:9–14); wind, earthquake, fire, "a still, small voice" (19:11–12); commission to anoint Hazael, Jehu and Elisha (19:15–17); the seven thousand (19:18). Elisha's call (19:19–21) is referred to, not quoted.

**Main readings.** (1) Pastoral reading: Elijah in exhaustion and depression; God's care proceeds body first (sleep, food), then presence, then truth, company and task. Spurgeon's lecture "The Minister's Fainting Fits" (*Lectures to My Students*) is a Baptist witness that faithful ministers suffer depression; it is cited for that point only, not quoted. (2) A reading that stresses Elijah's flight as failure and God's question "What are you doing here?" as rebuke, with the commission as recall to duty. (3) A reading that hears the question as gentle invitation to speak. The page holds that the question is asked twice and answered twice without reproach, and that 19:18 corrects "I am the only one left". The angel of the LORD (19:7) is not discussed; study 53 (angels) keeps the angel of the LORD. The phrase in 19:12 is noted as rendered differently in translations, without naming them.

**Links.** Study 53 (*Angels, demons and spiritual powers*) for the angel; study 39 (*Rulers and their choices*) for Ahab and Jezebel; study 38 (*Prophets in their times*).

## Job 3 (owned)

**Context.** After the losses of chapters 1–2 (owned by study 45) and seven days' silence (2:13). Job curses his birthday (3:1–10), asks why he did not die at birth (3:11–19), and why life is given to the miserable (3:20–26). God's speeches (38–42) belong to study 65; 42:7–8 (God's verdict on the friends) is referred to, not quoted; 42:16–17 referred to.

**Main readings.** (1) Job's lament as honest, faithful complaint addressed toward God, vindicated in 42:7 over the friends' explanations. (2) A reading that sees in Job's speeches real faults that God later corrects from the storm (38:2; 40:2, 8) while still preferring Job's honesty to the friends. The page holds both together: God answers without explaining and rebukes the friends.

**Safe messaging.** Job 3:13–19 (the grave pictured as rest) and 3:21–22 (longing for death) are deliberately not quoted. A [care] note names them as the voice of pain, not the book's verdict, cites 1 Corinthians 15:26 (death as an enemy, not quoted) and points to help.

**Links.** Study 45 (*What Scripture promises the sick*) for Job 1–2; study 65 (*The wisdom books*) for Job 28 and 38–42; study 33 samples 42:7–9.

## Jeremiah 20:7–18 (owned)

**Context.** After Pashhur beats Jeremiah and puts him in the stocks (20:1–6, referred to). One of the passages scholars often call Jeremiah's "confessions" (laments found in Jeremiah 11–20) [check: confirm the usual list of passages in the "confessions"]. Accusation (20:7), the word as fire in the bones (20:9), trust (20:11), praise (20:13), then the curse of his birthday (20:14–18).

**Main readings.** (1) The sudden swing from praise (20:13) to curse (20:14) as a true picture of a sufferer's turmoil, kept unedited by Scripture (the page's reading). (2) Readings that see 20:14–18 as a separate lament placed after 20:7–13 by the book's arrangement. Both are compatible with the page's point. On "You have deceived me" (20:7), some render it "enticed" or "persuaded" [check: whether to mention alternative renderings]; the page quotes the BSB only.

**Safe messaging.** 20:17 ("did not kill me in the womb") is not quoted.

**Links.** Study 38 (*Prophets in their times*).

## Psalms 42–43 (owned)

**Context.** A Korahite psalm (superscription referred to, not quoted). The refrain at 42:5, 42:11 and 43:5 binds the two psalms; many readers treat them as one poem [check: whether to state that some Hebrew manuscripts join them]. Exile from worship, taunts of "Where is your God?", self-address, appeal for light and truth.

**Main readings.** Read as Israel's prayer first (Jewish worship, Temple and synagogue), then as a prayer Christians pray. The page's application: speaking hope to one's own soul as a choice. No commentator cited.

**Links.** Study 48 (*The book of Psalms*), which owns the Psalter as a whole.

## Psalm 46 (owned)

**Context.** God as refuge amid cosmic upheaval (46:1–3), the city of God (46:4–7), God ending wars (46:8–11). Refrain 46:7, 11.

**Main readings of 46:10 ("Be still").** (1) A command to the warring nations to cease, from the context of 46:9. (2) A call to God's people to quiet trust. The page presents the context and allows both, without naming commentators [check: add named commentators for each reading, if wanted]. Luther's hymn "A Mighty Fortress Is Our God" ("Ein feste Burg") noted as drawing on this psalm; no date given [check: if a date is added, verify it].

**Links.** Study 48.

## Psalm 56 (owned)

**Context.** Superscription: when the Philistines seized David in Gath (referred to, not quoted). Fear of people (56:1–2, 5–6), trust in the same breath (56:3–4, 10–11), tears kept in God's bottle (56:8), thanks for deliverance (56:12–13).

**Main readings.** Taken plainly as a prayer of trust inside fear; "When I am afraid" admits fear. The imprecatory line (56:7) is not discussed.

**Links.** Study 48.

## Psalm 88 (owned)

**Context.** Given to Heman the Ezrahite (superscription referred to, not quoted) [check: whether to mention the musical heading in the superscription]. The only psalm in the Psalter commonly said to end without any turn to praise. Sheol, the Pit, Abaddon explained; Selah noted as of uncertain meaning.

**Main readings.** (1) A prayer of persevering faith in darkness: the psalmist still names "the God of my salvation" and keeps praying. (2) Readings that see it chiefly as the cry of long sickness (study 45 mentions it so). The page links 45. A [care] note answers the felt wrath of God (88:7, 16) with Romans 8:1 (referred to, not quoted) for those who trust Christ.

**Links.** Study 45 (*What Scripture promises the sick*), which mentions it briefly; study 48.

## Isaiah 41:8–13 and Isaiah 43:1–7 (owned)

**Context.** God's words to Israel/Jacob facing or in the Babylonian exile: chosen and not rejected (41:8–9), "Do not fear, for I am with you" (41:10, 13), redeemed and called by name (43:1), waters and fire (43:2), ransom (43:3–4), gathering from the four directions (43:5–7).

**Main readings.** Jewish reading named first: God's promise to Israel, His people. Christian readings: (1) promises fulfilled in Christ and extended to all believers, Jew and Gentile (covenant theology and progressive covenantal readings among Baptists); (2) promises that remain Israel's, with Christians learning from them what God is like and receiving parallel promises in Christ (dispensational readings among Baptists). The page does not choose and links *Covenants and promises* (26). Limit stated: "when you pass through" promises presence, not exemption.

**Links.** Study 26 (*Covenants and promises*); study 41 (*Christ in the Old Testament*).
