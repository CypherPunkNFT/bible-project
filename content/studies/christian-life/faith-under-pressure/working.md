# Faith under pressure

**Content pass status:** first full draft ready for review, 2026-10-13. Read [the content draft](content.md) and [passage and editorial notes](passage-notes.md). The outline below preserves the initial planning material. All six sections are now developed.

## Central question

What do the letters offer believers facing trials, temptation, loss or opposition?

## Proposed subcards

| Subcard | Starting passages | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trials and endurance | James 1:2–8; Romans 5:1–5 | Endurance, wisdom and hope |
| Temptation | James 1:12–18; 1 Corinthians 10:12–13 | Desire, temptation and God's faithfulness |
| Anxiety and prayer | Philippians 4:4–9; 1 Peter 5:6–10 | Prayer, care and steadfastness |
| Persecution | 1 Peter 3:13–17; 4:12–19 | Suffering for doing good and entrusting oneself to God |
| Grief and hope | 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 | Grief in the light of resurrection hope |
| Contentment | Philippians 4:10–20; Hebrews 13:5–6 | Need, provision, generosity and contentment |

## Experience to explore

Begin with a difficulty, then read a complete passage: the situation, the encouragement or instruction, and its stated ground for hope. Related passages should deepen the reading without flattening distinct kinds of suffering into one answer.

## Open questions

How can the explanations acknowledge grief without implying that faith removes sorrow? Keep pastoral reflection distinct from clinical advice and from promises the passage does not make. Decide how much temptation material belongs here versus A new creation.
