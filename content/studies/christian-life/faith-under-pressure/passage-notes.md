## Interpretive boundaries

- **Trials and enticement:** James uses the KJV's temptation language in more than one setting. The draft explains the distinction through the surrounding verses without claiming that one English definition fits every occurrence.
- **Joy and maturity:** joy in James is connected to endurance's purpose. The draft does not call suffering pleasant or make a person's visible composure the measure of faithfulness.
- **Asking in faith:** the warning about wavering in James 1:6–8 is retained. The draft does not use it to diagnose another person's unanswered prayer or reduce every honest question to the double-mindedness James rebukes.
- **More than one can bear:** 1 Corinthians 10:13 is kept in its temptation-and-idolatry argument. It is not paraphrased as a universal promise of personal emotional capacity; 2 Corinthians 1:8–9 provides a directly relevant comparison.
- **Anxiety:** this is scriptural exposition and pastoral reflection, not diagnosis or treatment advice. Do not infer that persistent distress establishes deficient faith or promise a symptom-free deadline from these verses.
- **Persecution:** disagreement, criticism and consequences of wrongdoing are not automatically suffering for Christ. The letter's own distinction is explicit. The draft does not settle the debated judgment language of 1 Peter 4:17–18 or offer a comprehensive account of providence.
- **Grief:** the reading that hope can coexist with sorrow is presented through the contrast in 1 Thessalonians 4:13. The draft makes no prediction about the length of a particular person's mourning.
- **Future events:** the account in 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17 is retained, including being caught up to meet the Lord. Its relationship to disputed end-times chronologies is reserved for item 30; the study does not quietly endorse a particular chronology.
- **KJV vocabulary:** “careful” in Philippians 4:6 concerns anxiety; “prevent” in 1 Thessalonians 4:15 means precede; “conversation” in Hebrews 13:5 concerns conduct. The explanations clarify the text rather than altering its quotations.
- **Contentment:** Philippians 4:13 is read with verses 11–12, and verse 19 with the account of need and generosity. Neither is treated as a guarantee of chosen achievements or a financial return on giving.

## Decisions and refinements

The six proposed sections remain distinct. A brief introduction and conclusion connect them through 2 Corinthians 1 without imposing one explanation on every hardship. Each section contains passage context, explanation, questions and a clearly labelled reflection.

Possible refinements: whether the opening should feature 1 Peter 5:7 or a verse from 2 Corinthians 1; whether a later revision needs more on receiving help. Neither changes the present first draft's completeness. No layout, artwork, interaction design or site work is included.
