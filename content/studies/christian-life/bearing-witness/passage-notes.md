# Passage notes: Witness and the nations (09)

## Matthew 28:16–20 (owned)
**Context.** Matthew's closing scene. The eleven meet the risen Jesus on a mountain in Galilee he had designated (cf. 26:32; 28:7, 10). Worship and doubt sit side by side (28:17). The commission rests on his universal authority (28:18) and ends with the promise of his presence "to the end of the age", echoing "God with us" (1:23).
**Main readings.**
- The main command is "make disciples", with go, baptizing and teaching as participles describing the work. This is widely noted in commentaries; the study states it as commonly pointed out, without naming one commentator.
- "All nations" (panta ta ethnē): read as all peoples. Mission writers since the Lausanne Congress of 1974 (Ralph Winter's address is usually credited) have read it in terms of ethnolinguistic people groups. Others caution against pressing a modern technical sense. The study reads "all peoples" and does not press the technical definition.
- Whether the commission binds later believers: William Carey, *Enquiry* (1792), argued it does, against those who limited it to the apostles. Article XI of the BFM 2000 affirms it.
**Links.** Baptism and the Lord's Supper (59) quotes 28:19. Following Jesus (16) quotes it. The church and its calling (28) moves its part out under the baptism job. Covenants and promises (26) for Genesis 12:3. The church and its calling (28) for Acts 1:8.

## Romans 10:9–17 (owned)
**Context.** Romans 9–11: Paul's grief for Israel and God's faithfulness. Paul is Jewish and prays for his people (10:1). The paragraph quotes Isaiah 28:16 (v. 11), Joel 2:32 (v. 13), Isaiah 52:7 (v. 15) and Isaiah 53:1 (v. 16).
**Main readings.**
- Verses 9–10: confession and faith as two sides of one response, not a spoken formula. Justification is by faith (BFM article IV).
- Verse 13: Paul applies Joel's "the Lord" (the divine name) to Jesus, a high Christology commonly noted.
- Verses 14–15: the chain of sending, read as grounding missions (BFM article XI).
- Verse 16: Isaiah 53:1. The Jewish reading is named first. In Rashi and in much Jewish tradition the servant is Israel; the Targum connects the servant with the Messiah in 52:13 while reading the suffering differently. The Christian reading identifies the servant with Jesus. Care is taken that unbelief is not laid on the Jewish people as a group (Romans 11:1–2 is referred to).
- "Those who never hear": the confession's exclusivist line (article IV), alongside Lumen Gentium 16 and Pinnock's inclusivism, labelled as others' views.
**Links.** The good news (54). Saved by grace (24). Christ in the Old Testament (41) owns Isaiah 52:13–53:12.

## Romans 15:18–21 (owned)
**Context.** Paul's closing account of his mission, before his plans for Jerusalem, Rome and Spain (15:22–29). Verse 21 quotes Isaiah 52:15 in the Greek form.
**Main readings.**
- Christ works through Paul "by word and deed" and by signs and the Spirit.
- "Jerusalem … to Illyricum": whether Paul preached inside Illyricum or up to its border is debated. The study says only "all the way around to Illyricum", as the text has it.
- Verse 20 is the biblical root of the modern focus on unreached peoples. The study labels that application as the site's.
- Signs and wonders: whether miraculous gifts continue is an open question among Baptists (BFM silent). Classical Pentecostals are named as holding that they continue, with no grouping with the Word of Faith movement.
**Links.** The Holy Spirit (23). The site's people-group explorer. Genesis 1–11 (50) for Revelation 7:9. Christ in the Old Testament (41) for Isaiah 52:15.

## Philippians 1:12–30 (owned)
**Context.** Paul writes as a prisoner. The place is debated: Rome is traditional, and Ephesus or Caesarea are proposed. Shown as open. The Philippians had seen his suffering in their city (Acts 16:22–24).
**Main readings.**
- Verses 15–18: Paul rejoices that Christ is preached without approving envy. This is not tolerance of a different gospel (Galatians 1:8–9, referred to). Calvin reads it this way in his commentary on Philippians; draft 09 quoted him, but the quotation is not carried over here.
- Verses 21–24: handled with safe-messaging care. "Gain" is being with Christ. Paul chooses to remain for others. Crisis lines are given.
- Verses 27–30: courage as shared ("side by side"). Suffering is "granted" on Christ's behalf.
**Links.** Grief and dying (57) quotes 1:21–23. Fear, anxiety and despair (56). Prayer across Scripture (04) owns 1:3–11.

## Colossians 4:5–6 (owned)
**Context.** It follows Paul's request for prayer for a door for the word (4:2–4, owned by 04 and quoted here).
**Main readings.**
- Salt as flavour and preservative, ruling out both insipid and corrupt speech.
- "Redeeming the time": seizing opportunity without forcing it.
- "Answer everyone": answers are fitted to persons.
**Links.** Prayer across Scripture (04) for 4:2–4 and Ephesians 6:18–20. Faith under pressure (03) for 1 Peter 3:15. Faith in daily life (05) for 1 Peter 2:11–17.

## 2 Corinthians 4:1–7 (owned)
**Context.** Paul's defence of his ministry against critics in Corinth.
**Main readings.**
- Verse 2: renouncing deceit, applied to manipulative evangelism.
- Verse 4: "the god of this age" is read by most as Satan. A minority reading in church history (some early fathers, against the Marcionites) took it of God the Father blinding unbelievers. It is not mentioned on the page; the editor may wish to add it.
- Verse 6: echo of Genesis 1:3.
- Verse 7: the power is God's, not the messenger's.
**Links.** Angels, demons and spiritual powers (53).

## 1 Corinthians 3:5–9 (owned)
**Context.** Paul answers factions in Corinth (1:10–4:21).
**Main readings.**
- The first point is against rivalry among teachers. Applying it to personal witness is labelled as application.
- Verse 9: "God's fellow workers" is read either as co-workers with God or as fellow workers belonging to God. Both are shown; no commentator is named.
- The election question (God gives growth, so why speak?): BFM article V, the Abstract of Principles, the New Hampshire Confession and the 2012 Traditional Statement are shown without choosing.
**Links.** Chosen and kept (58). Prayer across Scripture (04).
