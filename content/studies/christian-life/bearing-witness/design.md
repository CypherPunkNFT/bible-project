## Direction

Teach one habit: in any passage about witness, ask whose part is whose. The picture is a door for the word. God opens it, you speak at it, and someone stands beyond it. Only one direction was built, as with the earlier mock-ups, because this was an existing draft.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the three concepts in gold, the title, the lead, 2 Corinthians 4:5, and a drawing in thin line: a door standing ajar in a masonry wall, light through the gap, a chain hanging beside it. Motes of light rise slowly in the gap. A visible button pauses them, they stop off-screen, and with reduced motion they are still. No person is drawn. Below it, four computed figures, each saying what it means, and the statement of what was chosen.
- **01 Five verses, and six doors in a wall.** Colossians 4:2–6 set large as the map of the study, nine phrases underlined. Beneath it, one continuous wall with six arched openings, all the same card: number, word, question. One door always stands open, marked "Viewing now" in words, and its reading lies in a full-width band beneath: title, question, hook and passages on the left, parts, labelled notes, Look closely and Your response on the right. A phrase opens its door and moves there. Six across, then three by two, then two by three, with no sideways scrolling.
- **02 The threshold: whose part is whose?** The signature. A drawn doorway, God on the lintel, the one who speaks on the near side, the one before them beyond, and ten passage names along the sill. Pointing lights a passage; choosing keeps it in the address. Each phrase opens its verse. An empty place says "not named". "Asked" and "hoped" are written beside the phrases they limit. Below 700 wide the drawing gives way to three labelled places under the names. The note beneath names the verses the figure cannot hold.
- **03 Five doors, and what stands beside them.** Five in the New Testament's order, each with the same lines: who opens it, what stands beside it, and a note. The one only asked for is drawn shut.
- **04 Five questions, faced.** Five questions as words joined by fine lines. Each opens the one sheet from the right, with the verse, then this study's reading, then what else has been said.
- **05 Sort it yourself.** Three passages. The study's sort stays hidden until asked for, then appears inside the verse, in words as well as underlines. Where no act of God is named, the page says so and adds none.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation to the one who is asking, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer" with Colossians 4:2–6, the way back to Living the Christian Life, a wall and a path, and the sources.

## Choices made, and why

- **The order is Colossians' order.** The draft's order started with 1 Peter. Following the paragraph means the map is Scripture's own and the reader can see what the paragraph does not hold.
- **No filter pills on the figure.** Three columns of phrases filtered by owner would have looked like a tool. Places around a door teach the same thing with one glance.
- **A door is always open.** Nothing on the page is hidden behind a blank state.
- **Colour never carries meaning alone.** "Viewing now", "asked", "hoped" and the sort's labels are all words. The sort's underlines also differ in style.
- **Unlike the sibling Christian Life pages.** Faith in daily life uses rooms and Faith under pressure uses layers. This one uses a wall, a doorway and light.
