## Direction

Teach one habit, the one the text names: in any passage about witness, ask whose part is whose. What does God do? What is given to the one who speaks? What belongs to the one who hears? The shape comes from two places in the text itself. Romans 10:14–15 is a chain of five links (call, believe, hear, preach, sent), which Paul reads backward, so the page draws it as its main figure. The close asks the three questions, so the page sets all seven passages against them in one table. One direction was built, as for The good news, because the text already exists.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, Sent · Heard · Grown in gold, the title *Witness and the nations* with *telling others about Jesus* in gold italic under it, the lead, and 1 Corinthians 3:6. The drawing, in a faint circle and dotted orbit: a small walled city on a hill, three rings around it (Acts 1:8), a chain of five links reaching out across the rings, and a shoot at the far edge. A soft light passes along the links; a visible button pauses it, it stops off-screen, and under reduced motion it is still. No person is drawn. The motto: "Many feet. One message. / Every nation in view." Four computed figures, each saying what it means, then what was chosen and the two words (gospel, missions).
- **01 How will they hear? The chain of sending.** Five links in Paul's order, each with the verse's own words, which open the verse. Under each link, whose part it is, but only where the study says so, quoted. "Hear" is given to no one, because the study gives it to no one. "Sent" says that the verse names no sender, that the study's close counts sending among what God does, and that its missions question shows churches sending. Romans 10:17 follows. On phones the links stack, one per row, and the drawn chain is left out.
- **02 Seven passages, read in order.** An index of the seven, then all seven parts in full, in the study's order. Each part has its number, title, main reading and also-quoted verses held beside the text as it scrolls. Inside: the text, block quotations, notes labelled "This study reads", "Left open: Baptists differ", "Other Christians" and "Care", then "Its limits", then Consider and Reflect side by side, then a link to the passage's row in section 03. Part 01 draws Acts 1:8 as three rings, with a note that the rings draw the verse's map and do not rank people by distance. Part 02 points back to the chain. Part 07's six pieces of counsel are a ruled list.
- **03 Whose part is whose? Seven passages, three places.** A table: seven rows, one per passage; three columns headed with the close's own questions. Each cell holds phrases from that passage, each opening its verse. An empty place says "Not named in these verses", and the page adds nothing. Under each row, the study's own line for that passage, or, for Colossians 4:5–6, a plain statement that the study gives none and the sort is the page's only. 2 Corinthians 4:4, "the god of this age", stands outside the three places as "another hand", with the study's "An enemy blinds." On phones each passage becomes a block with its three questions as labels.
- **04 Questions readers ask.** Four questions as words joined by fine lines. Each opens the one sheet from the right with the study's reading, then the views that differ, each labelled.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the invitation, the question, "Read the passage again before you answer", and Romans 10:9–17 printed beside them. The way back to Living the Christian Life, a line of ground with a seed, a watered sprout and a shoot, and the sources.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in the sheet, in the Berean. Notes the study still has to check show as small gold diamonds.

## Choices made, and why

- **All seven parts are shown.** The first design kept one reading open at a time behind a wall of doors. The new text "walks seven passages" and the close says "You have walked seven", and part 06 carries care notes that no one should have to find by choosing it.
- **The chain is the main figure, not a road.** A road of stations would claim a journey the text does not make, and The good news already draws a road with footprints. The chain is the text's own structure.
- **The threshold became a table.** The old figure showed one passage at a time on a doorway. Seven at once against the close's own three questions teaches the habit at a glance, and works on a phone as plain labelled blocks.
- **Only Acts 1:8 is drawn as rings.** It is the one verse the text gives "with its map attached". Genesis 12:3 and Revelation 7:9 stay as the text's own quotations.
- **Kept from the first design:** the habit, the sheet and its behaviour, the letters' colour, and the rule that colour never carries meaning alone.
- **Unlike its neighbours.** Faith in daily life uses rooms, Faith under pressure uses layers, The Christian and the state uses a coin inside a circle. This page uses a chain and a table.

## Colour

The letters' gold (the site's epistles colour) for the light along the chain, the rings, the shoot and the marks; ink for the rest. The table's columns differ by rule as well as words: a double rule under God, a single rule under the one who speaks, a dashed rule under the one who hears. The same rules mark the chain's links. Every place is also named in words. Light and dark themes both.

## Doctrine and care

- Doctrine in the site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000; its quotations (articles IV, V, XI, XIV, XVII) are checked by script in their own articles. Where Baptists differ (miraculous gifts; the details of election) the page shows the text's "this page does not choose" as written. Lumen Gentium and Pinnock appear only as the text labels them, as others' views.
- Placing a phrase in a column is this page's judgment, and the page says so. Where the study gives its own "whose part is whose" line, the page quotes it rather than paraphrasing.
- The page adds nothing the text does not say about who sends, who blinds, or what a hearer must do.
- Part 06's care notes, with the crisis lines, are shown in full in the part, never hidden behind a choice. Their numbers keep their "check before publishing" marks.
- No face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father; no person at all.
- One misquotation found (Philippians 1:17: the study's "to add to", the Berean's "can add to") is marked on the page; the study's text was not changed.
