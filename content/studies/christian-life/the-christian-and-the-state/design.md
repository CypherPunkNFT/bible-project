## Direction

Teach one habit: before you read a passage about rulers, sort what it gives the ruler and what it keeps for God alone, then ask whether the two are at peace or in collision. The shape is Jesus' own answer in Mark 12:17, two columns at once, and the study's own frame: the second column is larger, because Caesar is inside it. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** Study trail, the four concepts in gold (rulers, limits, conscience, peace), the title *The Christian* with *and the state* in gold italic, the lead, Mark 12:17, and a drawing of a Roman coin with an emperor's laurelled head, small inside the large faint circle and its dotted orbit. The small coin inside the larger circle is the page's whole idea in one picture. Under the motto "A coin for Caesar. / A life for God." No face or figure of God, of Christ or of the Father is drawn; the head on the coin is the emperor's. Four counts, each with what it means: the six times Samuel says "He will take", the three things Israel's king must not gather, the verses printed in full, and the three questions readers bring.
- **00 Whose is it?** The frame and the selection beside one figure: a large circle for what is God's, and inside it a small coin for what the ruler is owed. Words from the verses float in their places, joined by fine lines: submitting, taxes, honour, prayer for kings and the city's prosperity inside the coin; fear, worship, conscience and the gospel in the ring outside it. Two dashed lines cross the coin's edge: Israel handing the king God's own work, "to fight our battles", and the council reaching into God's ring to command silence. Choosing a word shows its column, its verse and the part that reads it. On a phone the drawing is left out and the same words stand as three short lists.
- **01–06 The six parts.** Each part has the same anatomy: number, title, hook and main readings; one line saying how the two columns stand in that passage (handed over, fenced in by the book, at peace, in collision, a line no ruler crosses, remade); one figure from its own verses; the text with its notes labelled by kind; and Consider and Reflect side by side.
  - 01 draws Samuel's six "He will take" as six lines drawn in to the throne, each with what is taken, and sets "to fight our battles" beside "The LORD will fight for you" (Exodus 14:14).
  - 02 sets the three "must not" lines of the king's scroll against Solomon's record: 12,000 horses, seven hundred wives and three hundred concubines, 666 talents of gold a year, and "his wives turned his heart away".
  - 03 lays out the five things owed (give, submit and pay, honour, pray, seek the city's prosperity) with the ruler each is owed to.
  - 04 sets three collisions face to face, each command against its answer: the council and Peter and John, Nebuchadnezzar's statue and the three young men, Darius's decree and Daniel's open window.
  - 05 draws a line of dates for who wrote what about the ruler and religion, from Helwys (1612) to the confession's article XVII (2000), each tradition marked by shape and name as well as colour.
  - 06 walks Isaiah 2:2–4 in its own order (the nations come, they learn, God judges, the swords are remade), then the two Christian answers on war with what both agree in the middle, choosing neither.
- **Questions readers bring.** Three questions, each with its answer, side by side and all visible; stacked on a phone.
- **Close: Two columns.** The way of reading set out in its own sentences as steps, the close, the invitation, the question for the reader and "Read the passage again", with Acts 4:1–22 printed in full beside them; a drawing of a plowshare in a furrow; the sources; a back link.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet from the right, in the Berean.

## Colour

Gospels & Acts violet, because the page turns on Mark 12:17 and returns to Acts 4, and no other study in Living the Christian Life uses it. The site's gold marks the concepts and the care notes.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000. Article XVII (religious liberty) is the confession speaking plainly, and the page shows its words, found in the copy held on this PC. Where Baptists differ the page shows both and chooses neither: how the law of Moses applies, the millennial readings of Isaiah 2, and just war and pacifism. Article XVI is quoted only as far as it goes. Other traditions (the Westminster Confession, the Second Vatican Council, the Schleitheim and Augsburg confessions) are shown in their own words and labelled as theirs; those not held on this PC are marked as not yet compared. "Whom you crucified" is kept to the council Peter addressed, and no people is made to bear a special guilt for Christ's death. The crisis lines in part 06 stay marked to check before publishing. The page tells no one how to vote, endorses no party and judges no nation's laws. See [passage-notes.md](passage-notes.md) for the rest.
