# Growing in maturity

Rooted in Christ. Built up in love. Pressing toward the mark.

> As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him:

Colossians 2:6 · KJV

The letters address people who belong to Christ and still have much to learn. They call for renewed thinking, steady practice, patience with one another and diligence in doing good. Growth concerns the whole life: what we trust, how we understand, what we practise and how we serve.

The same passages speak of what God has given and what God is doing. Christian growth is not described as leaving Christ behind for something more advanced. It is continuing in him, receiving his gifts and learning to live faithfully. These six readings consider different aspects of that continuing life.

## 01 Renewing the mind

**Read:** Romans 12:1–8.

### A response to mercy

Paul appeals to the mercies of God as he calls believers to present their bodies as a living sacrifice. The instruction concerns an embodied life offered to God, not thought separated from conduct. The next verse joins transformation with the renewing of the mind and the ability to recognise God's good will. — Romans 12:1–2

> And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.

Romans 12:2 · KJV

The contrast asks readers to examine the patterns shaping them. What is accepted simply because it is familiar or rewarded? What needs to change when life is considered in the light of God's mercy? These are applications of the passage's call to transformation; the verse does not supply advance knowledge of every personal decision.

### Thinking soberly about ourselves

Paul immediately warns against thinking too highly of oneself. Renewed thinking includes a sober estimate of one's place among others. He describes many members in one body, belonging to one another, with different gifts given by grace. — Romans 12:3–6

The gifts are then directed toward their use: serving, teaching, encouraging, giving, leading and showing mercy. Growth does not require everyone to become alike in function. It includes learning to use what has been given with diligence and care. — Romans 12:6–8

Read these verses together. A renewed mind is not demonstrated simply by having more to say about other people's errors. Humility, responsibility and service are among the first consequences Paul names.

**Consider:** What grounds Paul's appeal? How do his instructions about self-estimation and gifts develop the call to a renewed mind?

**Reflect:** Identify an assumption about success, status or your own importance that needs examination. Consider how a more sober view could change one act of service this week.

## 02 Rooted in Christ

**Read:** Colossians 2:6–15.

### Continuing in the one received

Colossians calls the readers to walk in Christ as they received him. The following images are of roots and building: being established in him and in the faith they were taught, with thanksgiving. Stability and growth appear together. — Colossians 2:6–7

The warning that follows concerns teaching that would take them captive through empty deceit, according to human tradition and the world's rudiments rather than Christ. Its stated contrast matters. The passage does not require suspicion of every act of thinking or learning; it challenges what would draw the readers away from Christ. — Colossians 2:8

### Remembering what has been given

Paul grounds his appeal in Christ's fullness and authority. He speaks of the readers being complete in him, then describes burial and resurrection with him in baptism, God's raising of Christ, forgiveness, and the removal of what stood against them through the cross. — Colossians 2:9–15

These are substantial reasons for remaining in Christ. The call to grow is not a claim that Christ is inadequate and must be supplemented by a supposedly superior source of spiritual standing. The passage directs the readers back to his person and work.

Thanksgiving belongs to this stability. Remembering what has been received can change how a new promise of spiritual advancement is heard. Before asking whether something seems impressive, ask how it relates to the Christ whom the passage proclaims.

**Consider:** What do the images of being rooted and built up communicate together? What actions of God follow the warning about deceptive teaching?

**Reflect:** Name something in this passage for which to give thanks. Consider whether a teaching or practice you are pursuing helps you remain in Christ or suggests that his work is insufficient.

## 03 Learning through practice

**Read:** Hebrews 5:11–6:3; Philippians 4:8–9.

### Moving beyond repeated beginnings

Hebrews addresses readers who have become dull of hearing. By this time they ought to be able to teach, yet they need the beginnings taught again. The comparison between milk and solid food leads to a description of understanding trained through use to distinguish good and evil. — Hebrews 5:11–14

The next paragraph urges movement toward maturity rather than continually laying the foundation. It names repentance, faith and other foundational teachings. Progress does not mean declaring those teachings unimportant; it means building upon them. — Hebrews 6:1–3

This places a question before the reader: what has been heard often but remains unused? The passage's rebuke concerns a failure to progress, not a licence to belittle a beginner who genuinely needs instruction.

### What has been learned, do

Philippians directs attention to what is true, honourable, just, pure, lovely and commendable. It then turns from attention to action:

> Those things, which ye have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do: and the God of peace shall be with you.

Philippians 4:9 · KJV

Learning involves teaching received and a life observed, followed by practice. Reading, listening and remembering are valuable, but the instruction continues into conduct. A small responsibility carried out faithfully may be an important place to put understanding to use.

A present application is to choose one clear instruction and practise it deliberately: speak truthfully, complete a responsibility, show patience or offer help. These examples are applications, not a programme prescribed by these verses. Repeated practice is not a way to place God in our debt.

**Consider:** What is the relationship between teaching and use in Hebrews? What does Philippians ask the readers to do with what they have received?

**Reflect:** Choose one thing you understand but have been slow to practise. Name a concrete response and revisit it honestly, including where you need help or correction.

## 04 Growing together

**Read:** Ephesians 4:1–16.

### Equipped to serve

Ephesians begins this appeal with humility, gentleness, patience and bearing with one another in love. It calls for preserving the unity of the Spirit and names the shared realities of one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism and one God and Father. — Ephesians 4:1–6

Within that unity, Christ gives gifts. The ministries named in the passage serve the equipping of the saints, the work of ministry and the building up of the body. Their purpose looks toward shared maturity in the knowledge of the Son of God. — Ephesians 4:7–13

Growth is therefore more than an individual's private attainment. Receiving teaching and contributing to others' good belong together. The passage gives room for different contributions while naming Christ as the common direction of growth.

### Truth in love

> But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ:

Ephesians 4:15 · KJV

This follows the warning against being carried about by every wind of teaching. Stability in truth matters, and so does the love in which truth is spoken. Verse 16 describes the body joined together, with each part contributing to its building up in love. — Ephesians 4:14–16

The earlier call to patience remains relevant here. People do not all learn at the same pace or contribute in the same way. A community's pursuit of maturity should include making room for others to learn and serve, not only recognising those already able to speak confidently.

**Consider:** What are Christ's gifts for in this passage? How do patience, truth and the contribution of each part belong to the same account of growth?

**Reflect:** Identify one way you need the help of others and one contribution you can make to their good. Consider an act of encouragement or service that helps someone else participate.

## 05 Pressing on

**Read:** Philippians 3:4–21.

### Knowing Christ

Paul recalls grounds on which he once could have placed confidence, then counts what had been gain as loss for Christ. He seeks to be found in Christ with righteousness from God by faith, to know him and the power of his resurrection, and to share in his sufferings. This gives the pursuit its meaning before Paul speaks of pressing toward the mark. — Philippians 3:4–11

The passage is not a general endorsement of relentless ambition. Its object is Christ, and its horizon includes resurrection. Paul has changed what he counts as gain.

### Continuing without claiming arrival

Paul explicitly says he has not already attained or been made perfect. He presses on because Christ Jesus has taken hold of him. His pursuit begins within that relationship rather than as a boast of independent achievement. — Philippians 3:12

> I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 3:14 · KJV

The call to forget what is behind is part of this forward pursuit. It need not mean erasing memory, denying past harm or refusing responsibility for earlier actions. Paul has just recalled his own past in order to explain his changed confidence. — Philippians 3:4–14

He asks the mature to share this outlook, to live consistently with what they have attained and to attend to faithful examples. The paragraph ends in expectation of the Saviour and the transformation of the body. Perseverance is held together with an admission of incompleteness and hope in Christ's work. — Philippians 3:15–21

**Consider:** What has Paul come to count as gain? How does his admission that he has not arrived shape the instruction to press on?

**Reflect:** Identify where you are tempted either to rest on past achievements or to stop because growth remains incomplete. What would continuing faithfully toward Christ look like in that situation?

## 06 Adding to faith

**Read:** 2 Peter 1:1–11; compare Philippians 2:12–16.

### Given what pertains to life and godliness

Second Peter begins with received faith, grace and peace, then speaks of divine power giving what pertains to life and godliness through knowing the one who calls. It names precious promises, participation in the divine nature and escape from the corruption associated with desire. These gifts precede the call to diligence. — 2 Peter 1:1–4

The reader is then urged to add virtue, knowledge, temperance, patience, godliness, brotherly kindness and charity to faith. The KJV's temperance concerns self-control, and charity here means love. The qualities describe a life being formed, not simply an expanding store of information. — 2 Peter 1:5–7

The list should not be turned into a ladder on which one quality must be completed before the next can begin. The following verse considers the qualities together, present and increasing in the believer.

### Fruitfulness and diligence

> For if these things be in you, and abound, they make you that ye shall neither be barren nor unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ.

2 Peter 1:8 · KJV

The passage also warns about blindness and forgetting cleansing from former sins. It calls for diligence concerning calling and election, and looks toward entrance into Christ's everlasting kingdom. The encouragement and warning both remain part of its appeal. — 2 Peter 1:9–11

Philippians likewise joins active obedience with God's action: believers are to work out their salvation with fear and trembling because God works in them both to will and to do. The following instructions bring this into ordinary conduct, including the refusal of murmuring and disputing. — Philippians 2:12–16

These passages hold received grace and diligent response together. They invite serious attention to growth without supplying a numerical measure of someone's standing before God. Consider the qualities named, seek their increase and remember the gifts with which the passage begins.

**Consider:** What comes before the call to add to faith? How does the passage connect these qualities with knowing Christ and remembering cleansing?

**Reflect:** Choose one named quality that needs attention in your present responsibilities. Ask for God's help and identify one way to practise it, without treating the exercise as a comparison with someone else's maturity.

## Continue in him

The letters give growth a centre and direction: knowing Christ, walking in him and contributing to a body built up in love. They call for diligence while acknowledging God's gifts and work. Keep learning, put what is understood into practice, receive help and offer it to others. The continuing task is faithfulness to Christ in the life before us.
