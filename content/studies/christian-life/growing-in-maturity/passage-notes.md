# Growing in maturity passage and editorial notes

Content pass, item 07. [Reader-facing draft](content.md); [working outline](working.md). First draft ready for review, not approved final copy.

## Purpose and source basis

For general readers exploring continuing growth in Christ. Distinguish growth from status, knowledge accumulation and a private achievement project. Keep divine gifts, personal diligence and communal life visible together.

Read in the repository KJV on 2026-10-13: [Romans](../../../../data/plain/kjv/ROM.json), [Colossians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/COL.json), [Hebrews](../../../../data/plain/kjv/HEB.json), [Philippians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/PHP.json), [Ephesians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/EPH.json), [2 Peter](../../../../data/plain/kjv/2PE.json). Six block quotations reproduce the local KJV. Exposition and reflections are editorial prose. Hebrews is not assigned a named author.

## Interpretive boundaries

- **Romans:** renewing the mind is connected with embodied service and humility. It is not reduced to positive thinking or a promise of private certainty about every decision.
- **Colossians:** the warning identifies teaching that is not according to Christ. The draft does not condemn all philosophy or learning. It retains baptism and Christ's work without resolving differences over sacramental interpretation or the identity of the opponents.
- **Hebrews:** going on from foundations means building upon them, not discarding their truth. The assigned passage ends at 6:3; no conclusion about the later warning is claimed. The rebuke is directed at the readers' lack of progress, not at every new believer's need for basics.
- **Communal growth:** the Ephesians passage is not made to settle contemporary ministry offices. Its immediate purposes of equipping, unity, stability and building up are retained. Different contributions are not ranked by visibility.
- **Philippians 3:** pressing on is framed by knowing Christ and resurrection, not generic ambition. “Forgetting” is not interpreted as suppressing memory or avoiding responsibility. “Perfect” in verse 15 is read as maturity in light of verse 12's explicit denial of completed attainment; the contrast is not silently removed.
- **Second Peter:** divine nature is retained as the passage's wording without implying that believers become independent deities. The qualities are considered together, not as discrete levels completed in sequence. “Temperance” and “charity” are explained as self-control and love.
- **Calling and election:** verses 9–11 retain both warning and encouragement. The draft does not resolve denominational accounts of assurance, perseverance or election, and does not use a performance score to certify salvation.
- **Effort and grace:** Philippians 2:12–13 is read together and followed into conduct. No claim is made that diligence earns salvation or that divine action removes responsibility. A fuller doctrinal comparison belongs in item 24.
- **Applications:** suggested exercises are labelled reflections. They are not a prescribed growth programme, guarantee of rapid change or basis for comparison with another reader.

## Connections and remaining decisions

**01 A new creation** develops the identity and changed conduct that growth continues. **02 Life together** addresses relationships; this study focuses on shared growth and contribution. **03 Faith under pressure** treats endurance in hardship. **04 Prayer in the letters** gives prayers for growth.

**06 Learning to discern** already introduces Hebrews and Ephesians in relation to judgment; here they support the broader movement from hearing to practice and from equipping to service. **08 Freedom that serves** will develop restraint and service. **24 Grace faith and salvation** can give fuller treatment to how traditions relate grace, obedience and assurance.

Possible refinement: a later revision might add how to respond to discouragement about slow growth. The present draft already includes Paul's admission of incompleteness and the need to receive help. Do not add a growth score or claim a uniform timetable.

All six units contain readings, explanation, questions and reflections. No site design or implementation is included.
