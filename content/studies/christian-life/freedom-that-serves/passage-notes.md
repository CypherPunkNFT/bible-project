# Freedom that serves passage and editorial notes

Content pass, item 08. [Reader-facing draft](content.md); [working outline](working.md). First draft ready for review, not approved final copy.

## Scope and sources

For general readers considering liberty, conscience and voluntary restraint. Develop the connected arguments rather than repeating item 05's everyday settings or item 06's method of judgment.

Read in the repository KJV on 2026-10-13: [Galatians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/GAL.json), [1 Corinthians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1CO.json), [Romans](../../../../data/plain/kjv/ROM.json). Source preparation covered Galatians 5:1–6:10, all of 1 Corinthians 8–10 plus 11:1, and Romans 14:1–15:7. Six block quotations reproduce the local KJV. Exposition and reflections are editorial prose.

## Interpretive boundaries

- **Galatians' liberty:** connect circumcision with the argument about justification rather than presenting a universal ban on a physical practice. Do not imply rejection of Jewish people, contempt for Jewish identity or that every obligation is bondage. The draft does not settle the wider relationship between Christians and Mosaic law.
- **Grace and warning:** Galatians 5:4's warning and 5:21's kingdom warning remain visible through assigned readings and exposition. Their relationship to competing accounts of perseverance is not resolved here.
- **The Spirit's fruit:** the paragraph is paraphrased, not printed as a quotation in modernised wording. Do not make freedom moral indifference or imply that struggle disappears instantly.
- **Conscience:** Romans' examples are not a category for every moral question. A conviction matters for how a person acts, without making every conviction equally accurate or incapable of correction.
- **Stumbling:** First Corinthians 8 describes encouragement toward conduct that wounds conscience, not mere annoyance. The description of a weaker brother is tied to this issue; it is not a global ranking of a person's intelligence, worth or faithfulness.
- **Chapters 8–10:** do not isolate chapter 8 to permit idol worship whenever no one objects. Chapter 10 explicitly forbids participation and distinguishes it from market food and an invited meal. The sacrificial argument is retained without settling denominational Eucharistic theology.
- **Disclosure at a meal:** 10:28–29 is explained without identifying the informant more precisely than the passage does. The later rhetorical questions are not used to cancel the instruction to abstain. Their detailed interpretation could receive further notes in a later revision.
- **Voluntary restraint:** Paul's refusal of support is not a universal requirement that teachers work unpaid, nor a warrant for others to demand surrender of legitimate provision. Chapter 9 defends the right before describing the choice.
- **Adaptation:** 9:21's obligation to Christ is retained. Becoming all things to all people is not described as deception or permission to sin.
- **Discipline:** the chapter's athletic and bodily imagery is summarised as purposeful discipline, not a prescription for self-injury. The draft does not settle the full meaning of disqualification in 9:27.
- **Doing good:** Galatians' sowing and reaping concern flesh, Spirit and eternal life; no guaranteed financial return is inferred. Helping with burdens and bearing one's own responsibility are read together without inventing a rigid lexical distinction between the two burdens.
- **KJV vocabulary:** “shambles” in 1 Corinthians 10:25 is paraphrased as market; another's “wealth” in 10:24 concerns their good, not simply money. “Communicate” in Galatians 6:6 concerns sharing. None of these paraphrases silently changes a block quotation.

## Connections and remaining decisions

**02 Life together** develops welcome and burdens; **05 Faith in daily life** gives practical settings; **06 Learning to discern** develops how to judge a dispute; **07 Growing in maturity** develops perseverance. Here the narrower thread is the use of liberty for another's good.

**09 Bearing witness** will develop Paul's concern for gospel reception and gracious conduct. **10 Returning and restoring** will develop Galatians 6:1. **24 Grace faith and salvation** can address justification more fully; this draft preserves its role in Galatians without attempting that entire study.

A later revision could add a carefully bounded contemporary example of voluntary restraint. Avoid declaring a disputed modern practice universally forbidden merely because someone objects. The present six units are complete without such an example.

Each unit includes readings, exposition, questions and a labelled reflection. No site design or implementation is included.
