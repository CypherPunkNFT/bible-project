# Faith in daily life passage and editorial notes

Content pass, item 05. [Reader-facing draft](content.md); [original outline](working.md). All six proposed units are developed; first draft ready for review, not approved final copy.

## Purpose and scope

Central question: how do the letters connect faith with ordinary relationships and responsibilities? Intended for general readers. Each unit distinguishes the textual instruction, its setting and a labelled present application. This is a selective scriptural study, not professional guidance on employment, finance, law or family disputes.

## Source basis

Read in the repository KJV on 2026-10-13: [Ephesians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/EPH.json), [Colossians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/COL.json), [1 Thessalonians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1TH.json), [2 Thessalonians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/2TH.json), [2 Corinthians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/2CO.json), [1 Timothy](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1TI.json), [Romans](../../../../data/plain/kjv/ROM.json), [Hebrews](../../../../data/plain/kjv/HEB.json), [1 Peter](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1PE.json), [1 Corinthians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1CO.json), [Acts](../../../../data/plain/kjv/ACT.json).

Six block quotations are verbatim KJV. Exposition and applications are editorial prose. The draft follows the letters' self-presentation in naming Paul; it does not examine authorship debates.

## Interpretive decisions and limits

- **Households:** the draft retains submission, headship and the commands to husbands rather than quietly removing difficult terms. It identifies two broad applications without assigning either to all Christians or resolving them. A later comparison of named traditions would need their own primary sources; none is claimed here.
- **Harm and authority:** commands to love, cherish and avoid provoking are not converted into entitlement to control or hurt others. The reflections concern the reader's own responsibilities. No requirement to remain in danger is inferred.
- **Slavery:** bondage is explicit in Ephesians 6:8. Modern employment is not substituted for it. Any application to employment is marked as an application across different relationships. The draft does not claim these paragraphs abolish slavery or furnish a complete historical account of it.
- **Work:** Second Thessalonians concerns refusal to work, not all unemployment or inability. Do not equate paid output with personal worth or erase unpaid care. Its closing instruction to admonish as a brother is retained.
- **Giving:** the Macedonian example is read with the Corinthians' means and Paul's rejection of burdening them to ease others. Sowing and reaping are followed through to generosity, need and thanksgiving; no guaranteed cash return is inferred. The study does not prescribe a giving percentage or budget.
- **Hospitality:** contemporary examples are applications, not quotations or universal requirements to offer a home in every circumstance. Hebrews' reference to angels remains in the assigned reading without a speculative identification of every guest.
- **Public authority:** Romans 12 and 13 remain adjacent. Acts is a clearly identified supporting narrative outside the epistles. It prevents an unqualified endorsement of every command, without turning the apostles' case into a comprehensive theory of civil resistance. No legal advice or judgment on present governments is offered.
- **Conscience:** the scope of Romans 14 is not extended to excuse the harms condemned in Romans 13. First Corinthians 8 illustrates being encouraged to act against conscience, not merely disliking someone else's choice. A fuller treatment must read chapters 8–10 together; that belongs in item 08.
- **KJV vocabulary:** “communicate” in 1 Timothy 6:18 concerns sharing; “servants” in the household passages must be read with bondage in view; “conversation” in 1 Peter 2:12 concerns conduct. Explanations do not alter block quotations.

## Connections and remaining editorial choices

**01 A new creation** develops changed conduct; **02 Life together** develops communal relationships; **03 Faith under pressure** already treats contentment at length; **04 Prayer in the letters** includes intercession for rulers. The present study uses everyday settings to connect these themes rather than repeating their full treatment.

**06 Learning to discern** will develop judgment and testing. **08 Freedom that serves** will give conscience and 1 Corinthians 8–10 fuller treatment. **09 Bearing witness** will develop public speech and conduct. **10 Returning and restoring** will address forgiveness and accountability more fully.

Possible later additions include singleness, widowhood and unpaid caregiving. The present household unit is explicitly limited to marriage and parent–child instructions; it does not present marriage as required for Christian maturity. A fuller comparison of household interpretations remains a separate editorial decision.

All six units contain readings, exposition, questions and reflections. No design, routes, artwork or implementation is included.
