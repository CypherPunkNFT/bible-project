# Faith in daily life design

Study 05 · First design draft · Owner review pending · 2026-10-13

## Direction

Begin with an ordinary setting, then connect the passage's instruction, its original context and a present application. This relationship is the study's distinctive feature. The three must stay visibly distinguishable: a modern example is not an additional biblical command, and an ancient relationship is not silently renamed to match a contemporary one.

Preserve the six readings and their order in [the content draft](content.md), with the boundaries in [the passage notes](passage-notes.md). Continue the direct disclosure pattern of the preceding designs, giving each setting its own substantial icon and a reading that opens without subtabs. This is a design specification for review, not a change to the website.

## Opening

Use the site's shared content edges for the header, cards, reading panel and conclusion. Keep the site header stable through page transitions. Present **Living the Christian Life**, the prominent title **Faith in daily life**, and the existing subtitle **At home. In our work. With what we have.** Keep the small study number in the same font family as its adjacent label.

On wide screens, place the existing introductory paragraphs under the title on the left. On the right, give the complete Romans 13:10 quotation a quiet surface with its gold reference and KJV label. Its Scripture action offers Romans 13:8–10, preserving the immediate account of love of neighbour. The full public-authority reading remains available in section 05.

Illustrate the opening with a small still life of an open doorway, a work surface and a shared bowl. Use filled shapes, clear edges and a few internal lines, in warm stone and restrained amber. This suggests home, labour and sharing without making paid work, marriage or a particular household arrangement the sole image of Christian life. No people need to be assigned gendered tasks in the art.

A slight hover glow can lift the shared bowl or doorway edge. The illustration does not animate through a simulated day, and no object secretly opens a reading. Cards provide the explicit controls. On narrow screens, stack the quotation after the introduction and simplify the art. Avoid full-screen height or a large empty band before the choices.

## Six setting cards

Arrange the cards in three columns and two rows on wide screens, two columns at intermediate widths and one on phones. Each begins closed and contains a filled icon, number, existing title, short question and visible down arrow. Card order follows the source content.

| Reading | Card question | Icon direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01 Marriage and family | What responsibility belongs to each person? | Two adult figures and a smaller figure beneath an open roof |
| 02 Work and integrity | How can honest work serve another person's good? | A hand beside a simple working tool |
| 03 Money and generosity | What can we share from what we have? | An open hand passing a small stack of coins |
| 04 Hospitality | Whom can we make room for? | An open doorway with a place set at a table |
| 05 Neighbours and authorities | What do we owe our neighbours and public life? | Two neighbouring doorways beside a shared street |
| 06 Conscience and disputed matters | How might our freedom affect someone else? | Two distinct paths beside one shared table |

These illustrations mark subjects, not a required life course. Do not depict marriage as the first milestone toward maturity or connect the cards with a compulsory route. Use one warm amber interaction accent; Scripture references remain gold. Shape, title and state must communicate meaning without relying on colour.

Titles use the display serif. Questions, context notes and applications use the ordinary body sans serif. Keep actual Scripture quotations visually distinct; do not italicize contextual explanation or cautionary prose. Let long titles wrap naturally and align cards within a row without clipping their text.

## Direct reading behaviour

The six cards control a single panel beneath the complete card group. Selecting a closed card opens its reading; selecting the active card closes it. Selecting another replaces the current reading. The cards stay in their document positions, and selecting one does not navigate or automatically scroll.

Keep a small arrow visible on each closed card. Hover and keyboard focus strengthen it and add a restrained glow. The active card displays an X and a clear edge treatment. Provide a labelled close control at the panel's top right and a circular upward-arrow control at the bottom.

Expand to the actual content height and fade the text in. When switching, fade the outgoing reading, resize, then reveal the replacement. Do not reserve space for the longest reading or introduce an internal vertical scrollbar. Prototype with approximately 280–360 milliseconds for expansion and 120–160 milliseconds for fades, then tune against real content. Rapid input should settle on the latest choice.

Closing near the card group leaves the viewport alone. Closing from the end of a reading makes the originating card visible with the smallest necessary adjustment; a panel close control also returns focus to that card. Reduced-motion mode applies state changes immediately, without animated scrolling. An optional **Next reading** action names its destination. Wheel and swipe gestures do not switch the selected reading.

## Instruction context and application

Each open reading starts with its title and main passage references, followed by a compact three-part orientation strip. The strip labels **The instruction**, **Its setting**, and **A present application**. All parts are visible together. Use neutral surfaces and clear labels rather than an arrow chain implying that historical application is automatic.

These proposed summaries draw on the existing explanations and reflections. They are editorial prose, not KJV quotations or a replacement for the full reading.

| Reading | The instruction | Its setting | A present application |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marriage and family | Love, cherish and attend to one's own responsibilities | Instructions to spouses, children and fathers; Christ and the church shape the marriage paragraph | Examine one act of care or patient instruction entrusted to you |
| Work and integrity | Work honestly and have something to share | Instructions about labour and provision, followed by distinct passages addressing servants and masters | Consider honesty, fairness and the use of authority without equating employment with bondage |
| Money and generosity | Give willingly according to what one has | A collection for the saints, alongside instruction about contentment and wealth | Identify a need and what can responsibly be shared |
| Hospitality | Offer welcome without grudging | Love, strangers, sufferers and service to one another | Offer something modest and helpful willingly |
| Neighbours and authorities | Seek a neighbour's good and meet public obligations | Romans joins nonretaliation, authorities and love of neighbour; Acts supplies a particular conflict of commands | Examine a responsibility and the people affected by one's conduct |
| Conscience and disputed matters | Receive without contempt and attend to another's conscience | Food, days and food offered to idols, rather than every possible disagreement | Distinguish preference, conviction and a possible effect on another person |

The setting column must be as readable as the application. On phones, stack the three parts in that order. None is hidden in a tooltip or behind a **More** link. References and the full explanations follow immediately.

## Explanation and reflection

After the orientation strip, display the two existing subdivisions side by side on wide screens. Place **Consider** and **Reflect** in a second row beneath them. Preserve paragraph order within each subdivision, including quotations, interpretive qualifications and citations. On narrow screens, stack the subdivisions and prompts in source order.

These two rows have natural height. A shorter hospitality reading should not acquire a large blank area to match the work reading. Keep prose measures around 55–70 characters, even when the overall container is wide. Use the full width for columns and comparisons rather than single lines of body text.

The comparison strips are orientation, not extra submenus. Both subdivisions remain visible once the reading opens. No single-item tab, duplicate subsection counter or nested horizontal scroller is needed.

## Give the difficult passages room

**Marriage and family:** Retain the whole reading range beginning at Ephesians 5:21. Keep the source draft's account of differing Christian applications immediately after the discussion of Christ's love and before moving into Colossians. Use a restrained **Interpretation** label, not a vote or a toggle that hides one account. Do not invent denominational attributions or claim this short paragraph settles the debate. The commands to love, cherish and avoid provoking remain fully visible. The child and adult responsibilities appear together in the second subdivision.

**Work and integrity:** Keep **Working so that we can share** distinct from **Reading the servant passages honestly**. The original setting of bondage is explained before any application to employment. Give that explanation normal body size and placement, not a small warning below the final reflection. The commands to masters and the bond/free distinction remain alongside instructions to servants. Keep the distinction between unwillingness and inability to work in the earlier subsection.

**Money and generosity:** Show willingness, means and the recipient's need in the explanation before suggesting a personal response. Retain the collection's setting and the outcomes of generosity and thanksgiving. Do not add a giving calculator, percentage selector, return projection or donation button to this reading. Its application invites consideration; it does not prescribe a personal financial plan.

**Hospitality:** Let this be a comparatively light reading. Keep welcome, attention to overlooked people and ungrudging service visible. Do not fill unused space with entertaining tips, elaborate table-setting art or a checklist that turns suggested applications into requirements.

**Neighbours and authorities:** Keep Romans 12 and 13 connected in the reading. Mark Acts 5:27–29 as a supporting narrative and its particular conflict of commands, not another epistle or a universal formula for resistance. The existing acknowledgement of differing applications remains in the explanation. Do not add a current-government selector or present legal conclusions.

**Conscience and disputed matters:** Keep the food-and-days setting and the Corinthians' idol-food example clearly identified. A simple source label can distinguish Romans from 1 Corinthians without making them separate tabs. Retain the draft's explanation that a wounded conscience is more than a difference in taste. The reflection invites examination, not an automated decision about what is permissible.

## Scripture and onward reading

Selecting a reference offers its surrounding passage with book, chapter, range and KJV visible. Reuse the existing Scripture interaction where it satisfies this behaviour. Cross-chapter ranges must remain intact, especially Ephesians 5:21–6:4, Colossians 3:22–4:1 and Romans 12:14–13:10. Do not silently stop at a chapter boundary.

An explicit **Open in reader** action can navigate away; returning restores the selected setting and scroll position. Ordinary disclosure clicks do not change routes. Keep the site's established page transition and stable header.

Retain **Faithfulness in what is before us** after the reading area, visible even when all cards are closed. Its invitation is to one actual responsibility, not to a form collecting household, employment or financial details. Offer **Prayer in the letters** as the previous study and **Learning to discern** as the next. Link to **Freedom that serves** modestly from the conscience reading rather than duplicating its planned treatment here.

## Accessibility and prototype checks

Use semantic disclosure buttons with expanded state and a named controlled panel. Keep focus on the card when it opens and offer a visible **Read this section** action to move to its heading. Both close controls require accessible names; touch targets should be at least 44 pixels. Clear focus rings and labels must work without hover, colour or animation.

Begin with three cards per row around 1100 pixels of available content width, two below that and one below approximately 640 pixels. Stack the orientation strip and body columns before they become cramped. At 200 percent zoom, all text reflows without clipping, nested scrolling or horizontal page overflow.

A later prototype should show the closed page, Work and integrity open with its historical setting visible, Marriage and family with the interpretation paragraph, a shorter Hospitality reading, a mobile orientation strip, a cross-chapter Scripture range, switch and close behaviour, keyboard access, reduced motion and return from the reader. Inspect at 390, 768, 1366 and 1440 pixels wide plus 200 percent zoom. Check actual font rendering and contrast. These are acceptance criteria, not completed browser tests.

## Decisions and handoff

Retain all six names and the existing Romans 13:10 opening quotation. The design adds short card questions, orientation summaries and interface labels; the content draft remains unchanged. Singleness, widowhood and fuller discussion of unpaid care remain possible editorial expansions, not silently added readings. No new contemporary historical, legal or financial claims are introduced by this design.
