# Grief and dying (57): passage notes

Folder: christian-life/grief-and-dying. All quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible as supplied. Verses outside the supplied text are referred to, never quoted.

## Owned passages

### Genesis 23 (Sarah's death; the cave of Machpelah)
- **Context:** The close of Sarah's life in the Abraham cycle (Genesis 12–25). Abraham is a resident foreigner in Canaan and holds none of the land promised in 12:7. The negotiation follows customs of public sale at the city gate before witnesses.
- **Readings:**
  - Burial as an act of faith in the land promise. This is common in Christian commentary and is the reading this study takes, supported by Hebrews 11:13 (referred to, not quoted).
  - A purely legal or historical reading: the record of a property title, explaining the family tomb used again in 25:9, 49:29–32 and 50:13.
  - Jewish commentary reads the scene as a model of honour for the dead and of courtesy in dealings with neighbours. [check: name a specific Jewish commentator before attributing this]
- **Links:** *Covenants and promises* (26), which owns Genesis 12:1–7.

### Genesis 50:1–14 (Jacob mourned and buried)
- **Context:** Close of the Joseph story. The verses fulfil Jacob's charge (47:29–31; 49:29–32). The God study (21) owns 50:15–21 ("you intended to harm me").
- **Readings:** Egyptian embalming (40 days) and royal-scale mourning (70 days) are described, not commanded. The seven days at Atad are linked in Jewish tradition with shiva. [check: the rabbinic source linking shiva to 50:10, e.g. Jerusalem Talmud, Moed Katan 3:5] Christian readers note that Scripture gives mourning time and ceremony.
- **Links:** *God: who He is and what He does* (21) for 50:15–21.

### 2 Samuel 1:17–27 (the Song of the Bow)
- **Context:** David's lament for Saul and Jonathan after Gilboa (1 Samuel 31). It is quoted from the lost Book of Jashar (1:18; compare Joshua 10:13).
- **Readings:**
  - Verse 26: the usual reading, held by most Jewish and Christian interpreters, takes it as the covenant friendship of 1 Samuel 18:1–4 and 20. Some modern readers take it as romantic. The study follows the covenant-friendship setting and names the other reading without detail.
  - David's honouring of Saul is read as grief that does not wait for a relationship to be good, and also as loyalty to "the LORD's anointed" (1 Samuel 24:6, referred to).
- **Links:** *Rulers and their choices* (39), which owns 2 Samuel 11–12.

### 2 Samuel 18:24–33 (David hears of Absalom's death)
- **Context:** The end of Absalom's rebellion (2 Samuel 15–18). Joab killed Absalom against David's order (18:5, 14). In 19:1–8, Joab rebukes David's public grief (referred to, not quoted).
- **Readings:** The scene is read as complicated grief, for a beloved son who wronged the mourner. Some commentators also read David's grief in the light of his own sins against his family (2 Samuel 12:10–12). The study does not press that link, for pastoral reasons, and names neither view as settled.
- **Links:** *Rulers and their choices* (39).

### Lamentations 1 and 3
- **Context:** Laments over Jerusalem's fall to Babylon (586 BC). Chapters 1–4 are alphabetic acrostics. Chapter 3 is triple, with 66 verses. Chapter 5 has 22 lines but is not an acrostic.
- **Jewish reading (named first):** Hebrew title *Eikhah*. The book is read on Tisha B'Av. Talmudic tradition ascribes it to Jeremiah, though the book names no author. [check: Bava Batra 15a; the current synagogue customs described]
- **Christian readings:**
  - Grief over loss under God's hand, including grief over sin (1:5, 8, 14, 18, 20, 22). This is the plan's line and the study's reading.
  - The turn to hope at 3:21–33, read as hope spoken in the middle of suffering, not after it.
  - The meaning of the acrostic form ("grief from A to Z", or a form that contains chaos) is a suggestion by readers and is labelled so.
  - Some traditions (Catholic, Anglican) read Lamentations in Holy Week (Tenebrae). This is not used in the content.
- **Pastoral limit:** The city's judgment is not read as a pattern for individual deaths (John 9:2–3; Luke 13:1–5, referred to).
- **Links:** *Prophets in their times* (38) for the setting of Jeremiah. *Fear, anxiety and despair* (56) for the Psalms of lament.

### Luke 23:39–43 (the dying thief)
- **Context:** The crucifixion in Luke. Only Luke records this exchange. Matthew 27:44 and Mark 15:32 say both men mocked; harmonisers usually read that one later changed his mind.
- **Readings:**
  - Grace without works or baptism. This is the Baptist reading, and the baptism study (59) and the cross study (25) also cite the verse.
  - The comma after "today": the New World Translation (Jehovah's Witnesses) places it to fit soul sleep. [check: exact NWT wording] Nearly all translations, the Berean included, read "today" with the promise.
  - Sacramental traditions treat the thief as an exceptional case (baptism of desire). This is not used in the content.
- **Links:** *The good news* (54); *The cross, the empty tomb and the throne* (25).
