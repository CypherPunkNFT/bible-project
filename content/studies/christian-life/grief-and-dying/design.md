## Direction

Teach one habit: before you ask what a grief passage explains, ask who is weeping, and to whom. That is the study's own habit, in its lead and its close. The shape is already in the passages: almost no one in them grieves silently, and each grief is spoken to someone. Abraham weeps for Sarah and then speaks to the Hittites. Joseph weeps over his father and then asks Pharaoh's court to let him keep his promise. David has a nation taught his song, and cries out to a son who cannot hear him. Jerusalem calls to everyone passing by, and then to the LORD. The dying man speaks to Jesus. So the page draws those voices and where each one is aimed. One direction was built, because the text already existed.

## The page

- **Opening.** The standard heading: the study trail (Study / Living the Christian Life / Grief and dying), the four concepts in gold capitals (Mourning · Lament · Dying · Hope), the title "Grief" in ink with "and dying" in gold italic, the lead, and Psalm 23:4 in full. Beside it, in a faint circle and dotted orbit, the field at Machpelah: a hillside with the mouth of a cave, the trees within the field's boundaries (Genesis 23:17) and the boundary stones. No one is drawn. The grass moves slowly. Motto: "Tears first. / Then the song." Under it, four counts, each computed by the build, each with what it means. The crisis lines come straight after, before anything else, as the study places them.
- **00 Who is weeping, and to whom?** The habit drawn once. The mourners are on the left. On the right are three places their words go: to the one who died, to the people standing by, and to the Lord. Each fine line is one thing a mourner says, in the verse's own words. Point at a line to read it on the line below. Choose it to open the verse. The one sentence that reads the picture: in Genesis and 2 Samuel the grief is spoken to the dead and to the living, and in Lamentations and Luke it is also spoken to the Lord. On a phone the drawing gives way to the same voices as a list.
- **01–05, one part per passage.** Each part opens with its number, its title, its hook and its one line, then the voices from 00 that belong to it, then the text. Notes keep their kinds apart, each with its own label: With care, How this study reads it, An open question, Other teaching, and the named outside source. Each part has one figure, made from its own passage:
  - **01** The days of mourning that Genesis records, as rows of days: forty for the embalming, seventy for the Egyptians' mourning, seven at Atad. No number is given for Abraham's mourning, and that row says so. Beneath them, the same cave is bought in Genesis 23 and used again in Genesis 50.
  - **02** The Song of the Bow as its nine verses, with the refrain lit where it returns. Then the cry of 2 Samuel 18:33, with each "my son" marked.
  - **03** The alphabet of grief. Lamentations is shown whole, a cell for each of its 154 verses. Chapter 3's verses are drawn in threes. The cells mark where chapter 1 says there is no one to comfort, where hope perishes (3:18), the hope of 3:21–33, and the prayer that is not yet answered (3:42). The figure shows that the hope stands in the middle of the book, not at its end.
  - **04** The two men beside Jesus: what one says, and the three things the other says before he asks. Then a switch shows the comma in Luke 23:43 in two places. The Berean reading comes first, and the count of "Truly I tell you" in Luke shows that "today" follows it only here.
  - **05** Now and then. One line runs from death to being with Christ now, and on to the resurrection. Each stage is marked with the Bible's own words for it. Beneath it are the views that differ, each labelled as whose it is, with the Baptist answer from Scripture.
- **Questions readers bring.** Three questions, written as words joined by a fine line, not boxes. All three are always visible, and one opens at a time. The open question is kept in the address.
- **Close.** The way of reading, the close, the invitation and the question for the reader. Beside them, Lamentations 3:19–33 is printed in full, then "Read the passage again before you answer". After that come the sources and the link back to Living the Christian Life.
- **Every reference** opens its verses in a sheet, in the Berean. Escape closes the sheet.

## Colour

History orange. Four of the six passages, and both passages quoted in the questions, come from Genesis and 2 Samuel, and the voices in 00 are mostly theirs. In 00, each mourner's dot takes the colour of its book's section (History, Prophets for Lamentations, Gospels for Luke), and the section is also named in words. The site's gold marks the care notes. Other studies in Living the Christian Life use Prophets blue, Epistles gold and Poetry teal, so this page does not share a colour with its neighbours.

## Doctrine and care

The site's voice follows the Baptist Faith and Message 2000. The believing dead are with Christ at once and await the resurrection of the body (article X). Salvation comes only by personal faith in Christ (article IV). Those God has accepted in Christ will not fall away (article V). People become transgressors when they are capable of moral action (article III). Life is sacred to its natural end (article XV). The 1677 Second London Baptist Confession is shown as older Baptist witness. The Adventist, Jehovah's Witness and Catholic teaching on the dead is described and labelled as theirs, and the Baptist answer is given from Scripture. Where Baptists differ, on whether all who die in infancy are elect, the page shows both views and does not choose. No death is read as a punishment. The crisis lines stand at the top of the page and again in the question about suicide, and every number still carries the study's "check before publishing" mark.
