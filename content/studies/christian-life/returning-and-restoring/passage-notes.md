# Returning and restoring passage and editorial notes

Content pass, item 10. [Reader-facing draft](content.md); [working outline](working.md). First draft ready for review, not approved final copy.

## Scope and sources

For general readers considering confession, repentance and communal restoration. Distinguish forgiveness, reconciliation, trust, consequences and responsibility without presenting a universal disciplinary process. The reflections are applications, not legal or clinical advice.

Read in the repository KJV on 2026-10-13: [1 John](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1JN.json), [2 Corinthians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/2CO.json), [Galatians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/GAL.json), [Ephesians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/EPH.json), [Colossians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/COL.json), [1 Corinthians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/1CO.json), [2 Thessalonians](../../../../data/plain/kjv/2TH.json). Six block quotations reproduce the local KJV. James 5:16–20 was considered during preparation but is not developed in this draft; it is not counted as an anchor reading.

## Interpretive boundaries

- **Confession:** First John addresses denial and truthful acknowledgement before God. The draft does not resolve differences over sacramental confession or require public disclosure of every private detail. Specific admission is a suggested application, not a formula.
- **Christ's work:** advocate and propitiation remain in the account. The draft does not settle debates over the scope or mechanism of atonement from 1 John 2:2, or quietly infer universal salvation.
- **Godly sorrow:** Paul's description concerns the Corinthians' response, not a psychological test for every individual. Emotional intensity is not used as a diagnostic of repentance. The KJV's “revenge” in 7:11 is not used to endorse personal retaliation.
- **Offender's identity:** Second Corinthians 2 does not name the offender. No identification with the person in 1 Corinthians 5 is asserted. The relation of this episode to the sorrowful letter and chapter 7 is not reconstructed beyond what the assigned passages say.
- **Restoration:** care for a person is distinguished from return to a role. Neither Galatians 6 nor 2 Corinthians 2 supplies a universal timeline for restored leadership, access or trust. Such distinctions are editorial applications, not additional quoted commands.
- **Forgiveness:** Ephesians' changed-conduct instructions remain alongside forgiveness. The draft does not imply that forgiveness erases consequences, suppresses truthful testimony, requires unsafe contact or obliges instant closeness. It does not settle every theological question about forgiveness and repentance.
- **Those harmed:** their good is not erased by concern for the wrongdoer. No demand is made that they provide immediate reassurance or personally manage restoration.
- **Discipline:** Second Thessalonians concerns specified disorder and disobedience in its context, not mere disagreement or inability to work. Its instruction to admonish as a brother is retained. The draft supplies no procedures for ostracism or punitive control.
- **Watchfulness:** 1 Corinthians 10:13 is read in the temptation argument and followed into the command to flee idolatry. It is not universalised into a claim that all suffering remains within personal strength.
