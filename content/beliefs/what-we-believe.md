# What we believe

Bible Guide teaches from the Bible. When a study has to say what the Bible means on a question Christians have
discussed for centuries, it stays inside one confession of faith: the **Baptist Faith and Message 2000**, the
statement of faith of the Southern Baptist Convention. A confession is not a second Bible. It is a summary, written by
a church, of what it understands the Bible to teach. The confession says so itself: confessions are "only guides in
interpretation, having no authority over the conscience."

Here is what each of its eighteen articles says, in our words. The full text is on the convention's site,
[bfm.sbc.net](https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/).

## The eighteen articles

**1. The Scriptures.** The Bible is God's own revelation of Himself, written by people He inspired. It is true, "without
any mixture of error", and it is the final standard for what we believe and how we live. Its subject, from beginning
to end, is Jesus Christ.

**2. God.** There is one God, who has always been Father, Son and Holy Spirit. He is holy, all-powerful and knows all
things. The Father cares for all He has made. The Son, Jesus Christ, became fully human without ceasing to be God; He
died for our sins, rose bodily, and reigns now. The Holy Spirit inspired the Scriptures, gives new life, joins every
believer to Christ at the moment of conversion, and gives gifts for serving the church.

**3. Humanity.** God made man and woman in His image, male and female, the crown of His creation. The first people sinned, and every person
since has a nature bent toward sin. Every person of every race has full dignity and deserves respect and love.

**4. Salvation.** God offers salvation freely to everyone who trusts Jesus Christ, who shed His blood to pay for sin.
It includes being born again, being declared right with God (justification), growing holy (sanctification) and being
made perfect at the end (glorification). There is no salvation apart from personal faith in Christ as Lord.

**5. God's purpose of grace.** God chooses to save sinners, and He does it in a way that is consistent with human
freedom. Those who truly belong to Him will never finally fall away; they may sin and grieve God, but He keeps them.

**6. The church.** A local church is a congregation of baptized believers who govern themselves under Christ, meet to
worship, observe baptism and the Lord's Supper, and take the gospel to the world. Its two offices are pastor (also
called elder or overseer) and deacon. Men and women are both gifted for service in the church; the office of pastor is
limited to men, as Scripture qualifies it. The church is also the whole body of Christ, every believer in every age.

**7. Baptism and the Lord's Supper.** Baptism is the immersion in water of someone who already believes, in the name of
the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. It pictures the believer's faith in Christ's death and rising, and their own
death to sin and new life. It comes before church membership and the Lord's Supper. The Supper is a meal of
remembrance: bread and the cup recall Christ's death and look forward to His return.

**8. The Lord's Day.** The first day of the week is the Lord's Day, set apart for worship in memory of Christ's
resurrection. How each Christian spends it is a matter of conscience under Christ.

**9. The kingdom.** God rules over everything, and in a special way over those who gladly own Him as King. Christians
pray and work for His kingdom, which will come in full when Christ returns.

**10. Last things.** Jesus Christ will return in person and in glory. The dead will be raised, and Christ will judge
everyone. The unrighteous will be sent to hell, a place of everlasting punishment; the righteous will live with the
Lord for ever in heaven, in raised and glorified bodies.

**11. Evangelism and missions.** Every Christian and every church is called to make disciples of all nations, telling
the gospel by word and showing it by life.

**12. Education.** All true learning belongs to God, and Christians should pursue it. In Christian schools, freedom to
teach is held within the authority of Christ and Scripture.

**13. Stewardship.** Everything we have, our time, abilities and money, belongs to God and is held in trust. Christians
should give gladly, regularly and generously for the spread of the gospel.

**14. Cooperation.** Churches may work together in associations and conventions to do more than one church could alone.
Christians may cooperate with other Christians where that does not compromise loyalty to Christ and His Word.

**15. The Christian and the social order.** Christians are to make Christ's will rule in their own lives and in
society: opposing racism, greed and every form of sexual immorality; caring for the orphaned, the needy, the abused,
the aged, the helpless and the sick; and
defending every human life from conception to natural death.

**16. Peace and war.** Christians should seek peace with everyone on the basis of what is right. The true remedy for
the spirit of war is the gospel.

**17. Religious liberty.** God alone is Lord of the conscience. The state may not force anyone's religion, and church
and state should be separate. In the confession's words, "A free church in a free state is the Christian ideal."

**18. The family.** God made the family first. Marriage is the lifelong union of one man and one woman. Husband and
wife are equal in worth before God; the husband is to lead, provide for and protect his family with love, and the wife
is to submit graciously to her husband's servant leadership and help him.
Children are a gift from God; parents are to teach them, and children are to honour and obey their parents.

## Where Baptists themselves differ

The confession leaves some questions open, and so do our studies. Where it is silent, a study shows the views that
Baptists hold, with the people who hold them, and does not choose. Among them: the order of events at the end; the
details of election; the age of the earth; whether the miraculous gifts continue; how the law of Moses applies to
Christians; whether the tithe binds Christians; the just-war view and pacifism; how to read the Song of Songs; how
strictly the Lord's Day is kept; and whether the Lord's table is for members of one church only or for baptized
believers from other churches too.

## Other traditions

Many Christians belong to other traditions, and many readers of this site were raised in one. When a study shows what
Catholics, the Orthodox, Lutherans, Presbyterians, Methodists, Pentecostals or others believe, it quotes their own
documents and labels the view as theirs. Then it says, kindly and plainly, where Baptists read the Bible differently,
and why.
