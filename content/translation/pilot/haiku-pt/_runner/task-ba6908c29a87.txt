- Natural, serious, contemporary Brazilian Portuguese (not European Portuguese), as a careful Brazilian editor would write it. Address the reader as "você". Keep the English meaning exactly; add nothing, leave nothing out.
- Interface labels stay short. A label split across two keys (a heading broken over two lines) must still read naturally when the two parts are joined.
- Bible books, names and places take their traditional Brazilian forms (Gênesis, João, 1 Coríntios, Moisés, Isaías, Jesus, Jerusalém, Judeia). References use Portuguese book names with the same numbers (João 3:16).
- Scripture quoted in the text: translate it faithfully and keep its reference. Where the English is clearly a known verse, follow the wording of the Almeida tradition.
- Doctrinal words: grace = graça; faith = fé; justification = justificação; sanctification = santificação; salvation = salvação; repentance = arrependimento; atonement = expiação; the Lord = o Senhor; the LORD (God's name in the Old Testament) = o SENHOR; the Word (Christ, John 1) = o Verbo; God's word / Scripture = a Palavra de Deus / a Escritura; Holy Spirit = Espírito Santo; church = igreja; elder = presbítero; pastor = pastor; baptism = batismo; the gospel = o evangelho; born again = nascer de novo; covenant = aliança; sin = pecado.

- Hymns: keep each stanza's line breaks and line count, one Portuguese line per English line. Keep the meaning line by line; a natural rhythm is welcome, forced rhyme is not.
- Debate transcripts: keep the speakers' voices and any speaker labels.
- Keep unchanged: people's names other than biblical ones, titles of English books and articles, names of organizations, museum catalogue numbers, Latin or Greek or Hebrew quoted as such, URLs, numbers, and any markup such as **bold**, line breaks or list marks.
