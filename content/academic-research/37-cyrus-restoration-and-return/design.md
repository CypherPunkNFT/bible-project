# Cyrus, restoration and return: design proposal

The research record: [37-cyrus-restoration-and-return.json](../37-cyrus-restoration-and-return.json). There is no page yet. The live landing at /study/cyrus-restoration-and-return (title and artwork only, described in the [discovery-studies design note](../discovery-studies/design.md)) is not touched by this draft. This file proposes what a page could be; nothing here is built.

## What this page is for

A reader knows that Cyrus let the Jews go home. They leave knowing who actually went, in what stages, under whom, and what they built: an altar first, a temple twenty years later, a wall ninety years after that, and a public reading of the Law. They can read a Yehud jar stamp or coin for what it shows about the province and say where it stops, and they can say what the books themselves tell of those who stayed in Babylon and Susa. The question it carries: what did the return restore, and what did it leave unrestored?

## Where the weight sits, and why

The first version of this draft centred on the Cyrus Cylinder. The owner's correction of 2026-11-06 moved the weight: the cylinder, and what it does and does not say about Judah, is already treated in From discovery to history (research record 06) and Israel among the empires (record 07). So the cylinder has one short section here that links both, and the rest of the page belongs to the community that returned: Ezra 1 to 6 and Nehemiah, Haggai and Zechariah, its leaders, the altar and the second temple (520 to 515 BC), the opposition in Ezra 4, the province of Yehud with its stamps and coins, Ezra's law, Nehemiah's wall, and those who stayed. Elephantine, the Jewish garrison in Egypt, is named once with a link to records 07 and 32.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job, and this job is a draft only. So three directions were weighed in writing, and one is recommended; the choice is the owner's.

1. **A road of a hundred years, in stages (recommended).** One horizontal line from 538 BC (Cyrus's first year) to Nehemiah's second visit, with the ten steps of the record's story section as stations: the decree, the vessels, the list, the altar, the foundation with weeping, the standstill, the temple, Ezra, the wall, the reading. The long gaps are drawn as gaps. Each station opens a panel split into what the text says, what follows, and where it stops. It carries the argument directly: the return was slow, partial and staged, and the pauses are as much the story as the buildings.
2. **A building rising, layer by layer.** A cutaway of the temple mount: altar, foundation, walls, then the city wall around it, each layer opened by a step. Vivid, but rejected as the frame: nothing of this temple or altar has been excavated, so a drawing of them would picture what no one has seen, and it hides the people and the opposition. Its good part, building as the thread, survives in the road's station markers.
3. **Two places, side by side: Jerusalem and Babylon.** A split page following the community that went and the community that stayed. True to the books, and the strongest way to show those who stayed, but rejected as the frame because Israel among the empires already draws the Babylonian communities from their own tablets, and this page would repeat it. Its good part survives as section 07, a short column of the verses that keep the east in view.

## Headline voice

Short paired lines, as on the site's other studies, saying the claim and then its limit. Proposed motto for the header: "A door opened. / Not everyone went through." Alternatives: "An altar first. / A wall ninety years later." and "Less than before. / More to come." (from Ezra 3:12 and Haggai 2:9). Each of the four claim tests in the record already has a paired heading in this voice ("One decree. Many journeys.", "A wall was found. Its date is argued.").

## Sections, in order

- **00 Header.** Title, motto, the question. A new drawing of ours, labelled illustrative: a road of small stones running from a clay cylinder at one edge to a city wall at the other. No photograph of the temple, because there is none.
- **01 The cylinder, briefly.** Three short lines (evidence, inference, limit) and two links, to records 06 and 07. Optionally one small picture (see below). No comparison table: that is record 06's.
- **02 One decree, three forms.** The table from the record's decree section: the Hebrew proclamation, the elders' Aramaic report, and the Ecbatana memorandum, compared row by row (which God, who goes, what is built, who pays, what is returned), with the three explanations of how they relate.
- **03 The people who led it.** Seven cards: Sheshbazzar, Zerubbabel, Joshua, Haggai, Zechariah, Ezra and Nehemiah, each with role, what the books credit to them, and what is not known. Below them, the verb the Berean repeats: the LORD "stirred" Cyrus, then the families, then Zerubbabel. (The record flags that the Hebrew must be checked against the site's tagged text before the page says the verb is the same.)
- **04 The road (the main instrument).** The ten stations described above, with the modern date conversions marked as conversions. The two sums worked out by script (Ezra 2's families total 29,818 against 42,360; Ezra 1's vessels 2,499 against 5,400) shown as sums a reader can check.
- **05 Opposition.** Four opponents in the order Ezra and Nehemiah give them, with a small note on the one place the books arrange by subject rather than date (Ezra 4:6 to 23, about the walls under later kings).
- **06 Yehud, the province.** The province's name, one jar stamp, one coin, the governors known only from small objects, and the size estimates, each with its limit. Haggai's signet ring set beside the stamps. The coins are said plainly to be a century after the return.
- **07 Those who stayed.** Six short notes from Ezra, Daniel, Zechariah, Esther and Nehemiah, and a link to Israel among the empires for the Babylonian tablets.
- **08 Ezra's law.** The two questions (who came first, Ezra or Nehemiah; and what Artaxerxes's letter means), each with the views and the site's stance.
- **09 Isaiah names Cyrus.** Four lines from Isaiah 44 and 45, and the two views of when the passage was written, with the site's stance first.
- **10 Four claims, tested.** The record's claims, each with a three-option question.
- **11 What this site holds**, words often confused, the timeline (545 to 330 BC), and the reading trail.

## Keeping it apart from its neighbours

From discovery to history keeps the cylinder as an object and its comparison with Ezra 1. Israel among the empires keeps the Persian empire, the Babylonian communities, Ramat Raḥel's reassessment and Elephantine. Egypt beside Israel keeps Elephantine in its Egyptian setting. The draft studies Prophets in their times and The Twelve keep Haggai and Zechariah as prophets; Rulers and their choices keeps Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes as rulers. The planned study Between the Testaments starts where Nehemiah ends, and Reading Daniel and Revelation keeps Daniel 9's decree. Instruments in use elsewhere: Egypt beside Israel uses rulers and staves, the farming year a wheel, and Home a cutaway, which is one more reason the building cutaway (direction 2) was set aside. A single road is not used by any built study that this draft found.

## Before a page is built

- **Register the sources.** The sources in the record (the Berean text, the two Wikipedia articles, Lipschits and Vanderhooft's stamp corpus, the 2020 updated count, Finkelstein's 2008 article, the report of Mazar's 2007 announcement, Josephus in Whiston's translation, and the commentaries named as located) still need an entry in the site's [SOURCES.md](../../../SOURCES.md). This job did not edit it.
- **Read what was only located.** Bickerman 1946, Williamson's commentary, Finkelstein 2008, the stamp corpus and its 2020 update, and the works of van Hoonacker and Frei are named but not read. Bickerman's volume and pages, and the exact titles of van Hoonacker's and Frei's works, must be confirmed before a page cites them.
- **Recheck second-hand quotations.** Josephus's sentence and the Wikipedia-reported figures came through a tool that returns a report on a web page, not the page itself.
- **Check the Hebrew.** Whether "stirred" renders the same Hebrew verb in Ezra 1:1, Ezra 1:5 and Haggai 1:14.

## What the evidence could not settle

Which Artaxerxes Ezra served, and so whether he came before Nehemiah; how Sheshbazzar's foundation relates to Zerubbabel's; what the difference between 29,818 and 42,360 counts; whether the wall Eilat Mazar found in 2007 is Nehemiah's; and how many people lived in Yehud. The record gives each of these as open, with the views, and the page should do the same.
