# Strong's numbers: design record

The page: [/study/strongs-numbers](../../../src/pages/study/strongs/StrongsPage.tsx). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../26-strongs-numbers.json). Its counts: [the data build](../../../scripts/build-strongs-study.py).

## What this page is for

A reader hears "the Greek word here really means…", or finds a number in an app, and needs to know how far it can be trusted. They leave able to read a dictionary entry part by part, use the tools on this site, and catch three mistakes. The page's question: *what does the verse say, and what am I asking the number to say?*

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one was built, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on review:

1. **The list as a line (built).** The whole numbering drawn as one band split by alphabet letter. It shows at a glance what the page argues, with data nothing else on the site shows.
2. **A printed dictionary page you can click.** Beautiful, and faithful to Strong. Rejected as the main form: it is hard on a phone, and it does not show the whole list. Kept in small form as the entry anatomy in section 04 and as the two scans.
3. **A "myth-buster" page, led by the three mistakes.** Quick to read. Rejected as the main form: it treats the tool as a trap, when the page should first teach it as something useful. The mistakes come third, after the reader knows what the numbers are.

## Form and motion

- The header drawing is new and ours: an open reference book, a number on a thread, a Greek and a Hebrew word. It is labelled as illustrative. Motto: "Numbered by spelling. / Read in the verse."
- Tone colour is the Study hub's field colour for this card. Gold is the site's accent for the turn in a title.
- Motion is limited to a slow orbit, a moving thread and two sparks in the drawing, and all of it stops under reduced motion.
- The band is decorative for assistive technology and pointing only. The letter grid beneath does the same job by touch and keyboard, at a 44-pixel target.
- Hebrew is set right to left in its own font inside each English line.

## What was kept out, and why

- **A verse list for any word across the whole Bible.** Whether the site should show these is an open owner question in the original-languages plan, so the page shows one sample verse per rendering, plus the existing lookup over the 21 letters.
- **Grammar, context, frequency, and translation philosophy.** Each has its own study; the page links rather than repeats.
- **The lexicon's long Meaning column,** which carries its own licence note.
- **Ranking translations.** The Berean's word is stated plainly beside the King James's, and neither is called better.
- **A verdict on John 21.** The tags show two verbs; readers of the Greek differ on what the change means. There is no test on it.

## Data

Generated into [strongs-data.json](../../../src/pages/study/strongs/strongs-data.json), about 17 KB, against a 60 KB budget. Letter ranges take the first row of each number in STEP Bible's lexicons; tags come from the site's King James text. The build stops on a broken assumption rather than writing a wrong number.
