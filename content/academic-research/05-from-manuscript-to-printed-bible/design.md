## The organizing idea

**A proof sheet with living margins.** Start with an actual page, not a history lecture or an illustrative book. The reader selects the work performed by its Greek column, Latin column or printer’s marks. The object enlarges; the adjacent explanation distinguishes observation, editorial purpose and limits. These are three jobs within one production, not three successive stages in the history of the Bible.

The central question is: **what decisions stand between a surviving manuscript and the Bible on a reader’s desk?** The most revealing contrast is between mechanical repetition and continuing editorial change. A press can reproduce a page; it cannot decide what the main text should represent. Translation, source-text editing and reproduction remain separate questions even when one person contributes to several.

The current study already provides substantial content: a press exercise, six page models, three documented edition networks, a primary page, the Comma controversy, five translation projects, Indigenous collaborators, polyglots and modern editorial policies. Preserve this work. Its present opening delays its strongest evidence until chapter four and uses several similarly weighted selector-and-explanation blocks. Bring the actual page into the opening viewport, attach the revision investigation to it, and vary later chapters according to their questions.

Three approaches considered:

| Approach | What it explains | Evidence suitability | Complexity / decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Walk through a reconstructed workshop | Material sequence from type to bound volume | Good for Plantin’s documented craft; weak as a universal history, and Froben’s room is not reconstructed here | High illustrative burden; keep a focused press exercise later |
| An edition family wall | Multiple sources and the difference between inheritance and consultation | Existing networks support selected relationships, not a complete universal stemma | Useful secondary experience; too abstract before readers understand a printed page |
| **Proof sheet with living margins** | Different kinds of work coexist on one surface; print remains revisable | Genuine reusable 1516 page, inspected excerpts, source-linked scholarship | Three selections and a reversible enlargement; strongest evidence fit and earliest reward |

## Page composition

1. **Opening, about 280px below the fixed header on desktop.** Two aligned columns: a large title and a short invitation. Retain “The press repeats the page. People decide what goes on it.” No separate decorative hero. The desk begins immediately; the page and its controls are visible within a laptop viewport.
2. **One page, three different jobs.** A broad paper-and-board composition, roughly 1224px wide. A narrow left column names the tasks; the actual page occupies the center; a readable margin explains the selection. The full page is the initial state. A source trail expands beneath the complete desk, never as an overlay obscuring the object.
3. **The same project, a different decision.** A more intimate two-column comparison follows the sheet. Select 1516, 1519 or 1522. Keep spirit/water/blood stable while the presence of the heavenly clause changes. Then present the disputed promise anecdote through the actual Latin condition quoted by de Jonge. The grammar, his argument and the limits of a motive reconstruction remain close together. Do not hide the whole argument in a source panel.
4. **Make a book.** Shift to a wide horizontal working forme and two retained impressions. Pair the existing invented PRNIT/PRINT exercise with Agata’s actual findings and the Cambridge Gutenberg-to-Eggestein setting-copy relationship. Explain parchment preparation, gatherings, copying, correction and binding before press work. Keep Asian precedence visible. A short production sidebar introduces punch, matrix, casting, compositor, corrector and specialist multilingual type makers.
5. **A page is an editorial architecture.** Present three broad functional spreads: Rabbinic Bible, polyglot and modern critical page. This is a comparison of purposes, not a carousel of similar cards. The existing six edition models remain available through a labeled edition select. Greek/Latin meanings in the 1516 object must not be generalized to the Vulgate or Latin aids in a polyglot. Date printing, authorization and circulation separately.
6. **A series continues; its base can change.** A two-root Hebrew relationship diagram supplies a specific question before the wider edition networks. Printed-source and manuscript-source routes sit above the editions; editorial succession runs beneath them. The selected edition’s two kinds of relationship appear as ordinary text with sources. Preserve the early and modern Greek networks as further cases, with their distinct edge types and access limitations.
7. **A different desk: translation.** Use one substantial project record with three aligned rows—source/revision base, wording decisions, physical publication—rather than implying translations lie on the same source-text family tree. Keep Luther, Tyndale, Coverdale, Rheims and KJV selectable. Give the 1611 preface and the 1663/1685 Massachusett Bible full-width paired readings underneath. Name Job Nesutan and James Printer; explain colonial conversion and later language reclamation without flattening either history.
8. **Read below the rule.** Enlarge the Habakkuk phrase and keep it still while the apparatus changes. Then use the existing editorial-policy comparison to ask what belongs above/below that line. BHQ, HBCE and Prinsloo’s synoptic proposal are different research aims, not a ranking of quality. The Jewish commentary tradition continues through HaKeter; modern Greek work continues through Mill, Nestle, the Alands and ECM/CBGM. End with the dated nature of an edition and links to the source register, deeper reading, gaps and Academic category.

## Signature experience: build specification

**Composition and marks.** At desktop, use a 0.82 : 1.2 : 1 grid for task rail, image and margin. The scan remains in its original aspect ratio, approximately 266×370px in the whole-page state. The surrounding board is larger than the scan so there is room for a locator and credit. Cropping enlarges actual pixels; no invented handwriting or overpainted textual readings. The numbers 01–03 identify tasks, not chronological phases or quantities. The paragraph sign is decorative punctuation suggesting the print room, not a clickable evidence mark.

**Initial state.** Full page, all three task labels, object locator and credit visible. The margin introduces the question. “Two columns are not two independent ancient witnesses” is visible before selecting. No automatic zoom, rotating artwork or onboarding overlay.

**Selected state.** “Establish a text,” “Render its meaning” or “Assemble the book” changes the crop, heading, explanation, reading aid and access limitation. The object identity, controls and source link stay fixed. Greek reveals the short normalized phrase; Latin explains translation dependence; assembly marks distinguish physical instructions from biblical text. On mobile, controls precede the page and explanation follows immediately below it.

**Expanded state.** “Follow the evidence ↓” opens one full-width source trail under the desk. It states what the object supports, where an inference starts and which work was actually accessed. If a different task is selected while open, update this trail in place. Keep it in normal document flow with selectable text and ordinary links. It is not another nested tab system.

**Closed/reset state.** Same trigger, explicit × or Escape closes the trail. The close control returns keyboard focus to the trigger without scrolling unless the origin is above the header. “Whole page / reset ↙” restores the full image and closes the trail. Changing a layer never intentionally scrolls. No huge empty placeholder for the longest explanation; allow modest natural height changes below the stable image and controls.

**Switched historical state.** The year comparison changes the reported clause status while the shorter list remains stationary. It does not change the actual image above into a fabricated 1522 page. The result states its evidence basis each time. It isolates one variant, not every difference between editions.

**Relationship selection.** The Hebrew diagram always shows both source roots and all four printed-edition buttons. Selecting BHK 1–2 highlights Ben Hayyim’s route; selecting BHK 3/BHS/BHQ highlights Leningrad’s route. The succession line remains separate. A definition list beneath supplies source and predecessor relationships independently of the graphic. This is a documented selected family diagram, not an exhaustive manuscript stemma. No line joins the two roots. Date labels are recorded dates/ranges; position and distance do not measure time. BHK 1–2 is an explicitly combined pair of editions, not a single object.

The prototype does not make root labels look like buttons: they orient the selection. In a full implementation they could link directly to the relevant record. Early Greek links based on indexed DTS excerpts must carry that qualification in the selected record; no additional dependencies are invented from proximity. CBGM genealogies concern textual relationships, not the physical descent of books.

## Art direction and copy

Warm uncoated paper `#f5f0e6`, dark ink `#292923`, editorial vermilion `#923d2f`, reference teal `#285f5a`, gray-green board `#e7e3d8`. Thin rules and restrained real-page shadow supply depth. Use the scan as the substantial focal object; its irregular ink and margins are authentic. Modern task rails and sharp typesetting should look deliberately distinct from the scan. Do not add a fake antique texture or pseudo-Latin lines.

Large serif title; quieter body text; compact uppercase locators only for short metadata. Integration uses the site’s Literata/Archivo tokens and current Hebrew/Greek coverage. Prototype uses Georgia/Arial fallbacks so it opens from disk without loading a second font service. It is a composition proof, not a final typography export.

## Responsive, motion and accessibility behavior

Above 1000px, keep the three-part desk. At 651–1000px, move task controls above a two-column image/margin. At 650px and below, stack controls, object, reading margin and source trail. Short task names stay visible; the descriptive subtitles return in the selected explanation. Maintain at least 44px button targets. Ordinary document scrolling only.

The Hebrew family diagram changes representation on a phone: retain the two labeled sources and a two-by-two edition selector, hide the branching lines, and present the exact relationships in the definition list beneath. The textual succession sequence remains. This is not a miniature desktop graph. No swipe or hover is needed.

Native buttons use `aria-pressed`; the source trail uses `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls` and `hidden`. A polite live region reports the selected interpretation; it does not reread the entire page. Native links and selectable text remain available. Keyboard focus uses a contrasting background and inset underline, respecting the owner’s no-outline preference. Selection is identified with words and pressed state, not color alone. Escape closes the source trail. Session storage retains prototype selections across source visits and reloads; this is local interface state, not data collection. Production should preserve corresponding state in the existing route/query conventions.

Only the enlargement has a short 250ms transition. Reduced motion removes it. No perpetual movement. The prototype header is a fixed simplified stand-in, clearly labeled as a prototype. Integration must keep the real site header outside the standard page transition and preserve its existing navigation, theme tokens and back behavior. These React behaviors cannot be certified by an isolated HTML artifact.
