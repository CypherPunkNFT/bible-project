# The patriarchs in their world: design record

The page: /study/the-patriarchs-in-their-world (not yet built). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../39-the-patriarchs-in-their-world.json). Its verses, spans and counts: a data build still to be written.

## What this page is for

A reader hears that archaeology proves Abraham, or that camels and Philistines prove Genesis was written late. They leave able to ask of any parallel how long the custom lasted, to sort the parallels that fit a world from the few that might date a story, to tell the history of scholarly moods from the evidence underneath, and to keep a scholar's date for Abraham apart from what Genesis says. The question it carries: does this parallel fit a world, or date a story, and how long did the custom last?

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one is recommended, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on sight.

1. **Spans against windows (recommended).** One horizontal scale from 2500 to 400 BC. The four placements stand as tall faint bands, side by side, none brighter than another. Across them run the nineteen records, each drawn as the span of its attestations, grouped by kind (names, family law, contracts, into Egypt). The pattern shows at once (rule A5): most spans cross every band. The few short spans, the slave price and Beni Hasan, sit visibly apart, and so do the withdrawn ones (wife-sister, household gods), drawn as a broken line with the word "withdrawn" or "disputed" beside it. The picture is the span test itself.
2. **A journey map, Ur to Haran to Canaan to Egypt.** The obvious shape, and the one readers expect. Rejected as the lead: a map invites the reader to treat a route as evidence, and the evidence here is about customs, not roads. The record gives no locations for most parallels beyond an archive's city. It survives as a small locator inside each record sheet, showing only where the tablet was found.
3. **A ledger of verdicts by decade.** Each parallel as a row, its standing in the 1950s, 1970s and 2000s. Honest about how scholarship moved. Rejected as the lead: it makes the page about scholars rather than evidence, and verdict cells across four decades become the dense comparison block the owner has ruled out. It survives as section 03, four short rows in words.

Against the site's other studies: Egypt beside Israel uses threads between rulers, staves and a word-by-word reader; How the canon took shape, three lists; the church-reading page, a glossed leaf and a line of time; Manuscripts, a dot field. A line of time appears in two of those, so this page's scale must read differently: it holds ranges against bands, never events at points, and it is read vertically (does this span cross that band?) as much as along.

## Form

The header drawing is new: a tent and a clay tablet under a few stars, the tablet's wedges trailing off into blank clay. Labelled "Illustrative forms · not a picture of any patriarch". Tone is the History field's colour. Bands are carried by position and a written label, not tone alone; withdrawn spans are dashed and say so.

## Where the evidence cannot go

- **No point dates.** Every span is a range from the named scholar; a span narrower than a minimum is drawn at the minimum, and the key says so.
- **No placement chosen.** The three bands from scholars who read Genesis as history are drawn equal. The fourth is labelled as the view of scholars who do not.
- **No patriarch on the scale.** The scale holds customs and windows. Nowhere is a figure of Abraham placed on it.
- **Beni Hasan is never captioned as the patriarchs.**
- **Gaps in a span are gaps in the record**, and the key says it.

## Doctrine

Academic study: history reported without ranking. The site's voice holds Genesis as true history (Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article I) and says so at section 04; the confession fixes no date, so none is chosen. Reconstructions are never used to correct the text. Jewish tradition's Avot open the page in its own terms before the New Testament's reading. Thompson's and Van Seters's views are reported as theirs.
