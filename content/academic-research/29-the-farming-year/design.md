# The farming year: design record

The page: [/study/the-farming-year](../../../src/pages/study/farming/FarmingPage.tsx). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../29-the-farming-year.json). Its verses: [the data build](../../../scripts/build-farming-study.py).

## What this page is for

A reader meets a harvest note in Ruth, or a basket of summer fruit in Amos, and the text does not stop to explain it. They leave able to read the Gezer calendar's seasons in order, place a passage on the year, follow the work from plough to vat, and say how sure each of those is. The question it carries is the one the list entry sets: what did the first hearer already know, and how do I know it?

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one was built, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on sight.

1. **A year wheel driven by the stone (built).** The tablet's eight seasons drawn as twelve months, with passages drawn onto the same wheel and the jobs laid on the same months. The material is itself a cycle, and the study's main uncertainty (which modern month?) can be shown as a second, dashed ring.
2. **A walk through one field, season by season.** Easy to follow. Rejected: it needs an invented farm and family, and the evidence cannot bear them.
3. **A word study of the stone's Hebrew.** Rigorous and small. Rejected as the page's frame: it would answer the stone's question and leave out the hearer's. Its best parts survive in section 01.

## Keeping it apart from Home and household

The two are siblings in one field, so they share the required opening, the sheet and the three parts of a record, and they differ in the signature graphic. Home is a cutaway whose strokes carry certainty. This page is a wheel and a twelve-month strip, whose four small marks (a tablet, a book, a cut through soil, a camera) carry the kind of evidence. Its jobs are bars across a year, not rooms in a plan or cards in a grid. The record's "in the ground" mark always comes with a note saying what the object is, because most of it is not Israelite or not Iron Age.

## Form and motion

- The header drawing is new and ours: a tablet, a sheaf, and twelve marks for the months. It is labelled illustrative, not a tracing of the tablet. Motto: "Seven lines on a stone. / A year in every hearer's head."
- Tone colour is the Study hub's field colour for Ancient cultures & daily life. It sits close to the site's gold, so "chosen" is carried by ink, weight and underline rather than by tone.
- Motion is a slow orbit and two sparks in the header. All of it stops under reduced motion.
- The wheel is one described image for assistive technology. The list of eight seasons and the list of eight passages are the interface, at a 44-pixel target. The arcs repeat the list for a pointer and stay out of the tab order. Numbers on the wheel match the list, so no small label has to fit in an arc at phone width.
- Hebrew is marked right to left and isolated from the English around it. On the wheel there is no Hebrew: SVG text and the phone's width are poor company for it, and the lines and the record carry it instead.
- Passages for what a hearer knew are native disclosure elements with a small wheel, so a keyboard works without any script of ours.

## What was kept out, and why

- **What a parable teaches.** The parables study has it. Entries here name the work a hearer pictured and link there, and a test guards their wording against the most obvious slips.
- **Rainfall and terrain.** The planned study of land, climate and water has them. Rain appears once, because Samuel's sign turns on the date.
- **Feasts as celebrations.** The planned study of the year of feasts has them. The Feast of Ingathering appears only as another use of the stone's word, and the seven weeks only as a count from the sickle.
- **Grinding, baking and storage.** Home and household has them. This page stops at the threshing floor and the vat.
- **Invented scenes.** No named farmer, no typical day.
- **A plough or a pruning knife from the ground.** None with an open record turned up, so none is shown, and the page says so.

## What the evidence could not settle

The placing of the year on modern months is this study's inference, stated on the page, with two other placings beside it. Whether the stone's vine word means pruning or picking is left open, so grapes appear in two places on the wheel and on the strip. Borowski's book, the standard work, was not read, and the 2022 paper on Gezer's own threshing floors was refused by its publisher: both are named as such in the reading trail, and that refusal was not worked around.
