# Mesha and the world of Moab: design proposal

The research record: [35-mesha-and-the-world-of-moab.json](../35-mesha-and-the-world-of-moab.json). There is no page yet. The live landing at /study/mesha-and-the-world-of-moab (title and artwork only, described in the [discovery-studies design note](../discovery-studies/design.md)) keeps its title, its address and its record, and is not touched by this draft. This file proposes what a page could be; nothing here is built.

## What this page is for

A reader knows the Mesha Stele as "the Moabite Stone that mentions Israel". They leave able to place Israel's three eastern neighbours, Moab, Ammon and Edom, on the land; to name each one's god and say where that god is known from; to set the kings Scripture names beside the kings the inscriptions and the Assyrian lists name, and see why the two lists so rarely meet; and to follow the three through Scripture from Lot's sons and Esau to Herod's family. The question the study turns on: when Israel's neighbours speak for themselves, what do they add to Scripture's account of them, and what do they leave to Scripture alone? The question it hands the reader to carry to every claim: **whose voice is this, and how far does it reach?**

## Where the weight sits, and why

The title stays as the landing has it, but the scope was widened on 2026-11-05 after the site's redundancy review. The Mesha Stele as an object, its comparison with 2 Kings 3, and the full argument over its damaged line 31 already live in From discovery to history (research record 06), and Mesha's place among the empires in Israel among the empires (record 07). So the stele has one short opening section here that links both, adds only what they do not have (four of its lines read for what they say about Moab itself), and names the two sides of the "house of David" reading in a paragraph. The rest of the page is new ground: the three kingdoms together.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job, and this job is a draft only. So three directions were weighed in writing, and one is recommended; the choice is the owner's.

1. **A ledger of kings, in three columns (recommended).** Ammon, Moab and Edom stand side by side, north to south as on the land, on one vertical line of time from about 1250 BC to the birth of Christ. In each column Scripture's named kings sit on the left of the line and the kings named by inscriptions and Assyrian lists on the right. Two threads cross the line: Mesha, certain, and Baalis, probable. The pattern shows at a glance: Scripture's kings cluster before 800 BC, the outside records' after 734 BC, and they barely meet. That is the study's whole argument in one picture, and it answers the popular idea that such finds exist to "confirm" Scripture. Every other section hangs off it: a column's head opens its land and god, a king opens his record sheet, a find opens its card.
2. **A map from the Jabbok to the Gulf of Aqaba.** A line-art landscape of the three kingdoms, with the gorges as borders, the King's Highway as a thread and the finds as marks. Vivid, but set aside as the frame: several places it would have to pin are argued (Sela, Kir-hareseth, the borders in any one century), a map cannot show the gap in time between the two records of kings, and Israel among the empires already maps Dibon. Its good part survives as a small locator drawing in section 03.
3. **A family tree of brothers.** Terah at the top, Abraham and Lot, then Moab, Ben-ammi, Jacob and Esau, with each nation running down through its stories. True to Scripture's own way of tying the four peoples together, but set aside as the frame because it forces the outside evidence (Assyrian lists, a copper industry, a bottle) into a genealogy it does not belong to, and because the draft study People and genealogies owns family trees. Its good part survives in the opening of section 07, a small tree of the four ancestors.

## Headline voice

Short paired lines, as on the site's other studies. Gold concept line: "Moab · Ammon · Edom". Proposed motto under the drawing: "Kin across the river. / In their own words." Alternatives: "Three kingdoms. / One road." and "Enemies, often. / Kin, always." Section headlines already written in the record: "Two records of kings. They barely meet."; "Enemies, often. Kin, always."; "No palace found. Not proof of no rule."; "Josephus says it. The Gospels do not."; "Seldom confirmation. Mostly a wider world." Proposed for the gods section: "Same practice. Different gods." and for the prophets: "A brother's ruin. A brother's sentence."

## Figures bar

True counts, each with what it means (to be recounted by script when the page is built): 3 kingdoms; 18 named kings in the ledger, of whom 1 certainly and 1 probably appear in both records; 11 finds; 13 steps in Scripture's story of the three; 5 prophets who address them by name.

## Sections, in order

- **00 Header.** Title, motto, the question. A new drawing of ours in the History colour, labelled "Illustrative forms · not facsimiles": a round-topped stone with lines of writing, beside three gorges cut across a plateau.
- **01 The stele, briefly.** Three short lines (evidence, inference, limit) with links to records 06 and 07; four of the stele's lines in Hanson's English, each beside the verse it touches (2 Kings 3:4, Judges 3:12, Numbers 32:34, Jeremiah 48:1, Numbers 21:13); then one paragraph on line 31, the two readings and who holds each, with a link for the full argument.
- **02 Two records of kings (the main instrument).** The three-column ledger described above, with the unnamed kings of Scripture listed beneath it, and its evidence, inference and limit.
- **03 Three kingdoms, one road.** Three cards, north to south (land, chief city, ancestor, god), the locator drawing, and the three roads: the King's Highway, the Arabah road and the way round through the Desert of Edom.
- **04 Three gods.** Chemosh, Milcom and Qos side by side, each with what Scripture says and what the neighbours' own writing says, so the reader sees that Qos is known only from outside Scripture. The Molech question is shown with its views and not settled.
- **05 Their own words, and their own ground.** Eleven cards: the Amman Citadel inscription, the Tell Siran bottle, the seal impression of Baalis's servant, the Ataruz altar, Bozrah, the Faynan copper works, Horvat Qitmit, the Arad letter, the Egyptian report of the Shasu of Edom, the Qos-gabr sealing, and the Deir Alla plaster naming Balaam. Each card: what, where kept, date as a range, evidence, inference, limit.
- **06 Edom: early or late?** The two explanations (a late kingdom under Assyria; an early kingdom without palaces), each with who holds it, for, against and what would show it, then the short note on what Genesis 36 itself claims. The site does not choose.
- **07 Kin and enemies.** Thirteen steps from Genesis 19 to Nehemiah 13, each marked by the nations it concerns, with the small family tree at its head.
- **08 What the prophets charged.** One table, five prophets by three nations, with the hope each holds out, so the reader sees at once that Jeremiah promises Moab and Ammon restoration and gives Edom none.
- **09 From Edom to Idumea.** Six short steps to Herod's family and Mark 3:8, with Josephus's words in Whiston's translation, and the plain note that the New Testament never calls Herod an Idumean.
- **10 Four claims, tested.** The record's claims, each with a three-option question.
- **11 What this site holds**, words often confused, the timeline (1250 BC to the birth of Christ, every dated object drawn as a range), the reading trail, and the closing question: whose voice is this, and how far does it reach? Then the way back to Academic studies.

## Keeping it apart from its neighbours

From discovery to history keeps the stele as an object, its comparison with 2 Kings 3 and the line 31 argument. Israel among the empires keeps Mesha among Omri's dynasty and Assyria, and its map. Cities through their layers keeps the method of dating a site by its layers, which section 06 relies on. The world of Jesus & the apostles and the draft Rulers and their choices keep Herod as a ruler; section 09 here asks only where his people came from. The draft Prophets in their times keeps the prophets in their settings; section 08 reads them only for the three neighbours. The draft Cyrus, restoration and return keeps Tobiah the Ammonite. Instruments in use elsewhere: the Cyrus draft proposes a road of stations, Egypt beside Israel uses rulers and staves, the farming year a wheel. A ledger of three columns against time is not used by any built study this draft found.

## Before a page is built

- **Register the sources.** Every source in the record (the Berean text, Hanson's translation of the stele, the Wikipedia articles, the translations of the Assyrian lists, Beit-Arieh's two articles, Josephus in Whiston's translation, and the reports and articles named as located) still needs an entry in the site's [SOURCES.md](../../../SOURCES.md). This job did not edit it.
- **Read what was only located.** Horn 1969, Thompson and Zayadine 1973 with Cross, Herr 1985, Reed and Winnett 1963, Bean and others 2018, Bienkowski 2002, Levy and others 2008 (open access), Levy, Najjar and Ben-Yosef 2014, Ben-Yosef 2019, Beit-Arieh 1995, Hoftijzer and van der Kooij 1976, Lemaire 1994 and Eissfeldt 1935 are named but not read. Eissfeldt's exact title must be confirmed before a page cites it.
- **Recheck quotations.** Hanson's lines of the stele are a modern adaptation of a 1969 translation; check them against a current edition. Josephus is quoted from Whiston's 1737 spelling ("countrey", "an half Jew").
- **Check three readings the record flags.** That the Hebrew of 2 Samuel 12:30, "their king", can be read as Milcom; that James's wording of Amos 9:12 in Acts 15:17 follows the Greek translation of Amos; and the Mishnah's reading of Deuteronomy 23:3 (Yevamot 8:3), named but not read.
- **Find where four objects are kept.** The Umeiri seal impression, the Ataruz altar, the Qos-gabr sealing and the Deir Alla plaster: their keeping museums were not confirmed.

## What the evidence could not settle

Whether line 31 of the stele names the house of David; whether Edom was a kingdom before the late eighth century BC, and whether the copper-workers of Faynan were Edomites; whether Molech and Milcom are one god; whether the Ba'alyasha of the Umeiri seal is Jeremiah's Baalis; where Sela was; and when Obadiah spoke. The record gives each of these as open, with the views, and the page should do the same.
