# Historical discovery studies — title and artwork landings

14 October 2026. Authorized scope: three landing pages with art and title only. No study body, chapter navigation, interactive investigation or completed research status is introduced.

## Editorial distinction

The existing page is now **From discovery to history**, subtitled Reading archaeology, inscriptions and the biblical record. Its purpose is to teach how historians move from surviving evidence to defensible conclusions. The historical research dossier is preserved under its existing filename; all six examples, source notes and qualifications remain. The former /study/discoveries-biblical-record route continues to work as an alias.

Three individual historical studies now have reviewable landings: Mesha and the world of Moab; Lachish under siege; Cyrus, restoration and return. Contextual links beside Mesha's debate, Lachish's case and Cyrus's comparison—and beside the corresponding selected object—identify them as illustrated landings. The Academic category lists them as smaller Study landing links, not completed-study cards.

## Composition and art direction

All use the existing fixed header, Literata/Archivo typography, page-transition shell, aligned container and a breadcrumb back to the methods study and category. The page body is a title and original SVG artwork with a discreet caption identifying its illustrative character. There is no paragraph, chapter content or speculative historical account.

- **Mesha:** a tall, fractured basalt-form monument fills the artwork vertically. An offset paper-impression form behind it and a detached piece give the silhouette weight and relate to the surviving object's transmission. Sage/patinated green and pale paper establish its identity. Surface guides are abstract; fractures are not a facsimile.
- **Lachish:** a fortified hill rises above an oblique siege approach, with broad relief-panel forms behind. Terracotta and earth tones give the art a landscape composition. Architecture, contours, angles and placements are illustrative, not a reconstruction or measured site drawing.
- **Cyrus:** a horizontal cylinder is the focal point, crossing an offset written-account form and restrained architecture below. Ochre/clay and paper tones distinguish it from the vertical stone and fortified hill. The art does not depict a particular decree or fabricate cuneiform.

Alternatives considered: a photograph-only opening would need additional licensed assets and would limit composition; a generic monument repeated across three pages would obscure their different subjects; subject-specific SVG compositions are chosen because they establish identity while remaining honest about their illustrative status.

## States, responsiveness and accessibility

These are static landings. No inactive controls, empty accordions, chapter tabs or unfinished calls to action appear. Breadcrumb links work by touch and keyboard; the normal shared route transition is retained. Desktop places title and substantial art side by side. Phone stacks title then art, with readable caption and no horizontal overflow. Each SVG has a descriptive accessible name, and the title is real selectable text. There is no motion within the artwork; reduced-motion settings preserve the composition.
