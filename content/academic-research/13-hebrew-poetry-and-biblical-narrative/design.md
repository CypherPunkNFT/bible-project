# Hebrew poetry and biblical narrative: design record

The page: planned at `src/pages/study/hebrew-poetry/` [check: path once built]. Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../13-hebrew-poetry-and-biblical-narrative.json). Its verses and counts: a data build still to be written, which takes every verse from the site's held Berean.

## What this page is for

A reader hears that Hebrew poetry "says everything twice", that the centre of a chiasm is the secret message, that a well-told story must be invented, or that the Bible hides codes in its letters. They leave able to say what a second line adds, test a chiasm before trusting it, name the alphabet poems and what they can and cannot show, follow a key word, and tell a gap from a contradiction. The question it carries: is this pattern in the text for a reader to see, and what does finding it actually allow me to say?

## The material's own shape

Every device on this page makes a shape on the page itself, in the Berean's own lines. A pair of lines sits one above the other. A chiasm folds. An acrostic is a column of twenty-two first letters. A key word lights up in two places far apart. A story is a run of scenes, and a gap is a scene the narrator does not show. So the page is built from those shapes, drawn over real verses, not from a diagram invented for the purpose.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one is recommended, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on sight.

1. **Shapes in the text (recommended).** Each part takes two or three verses and draws its device as a shape in the lines: an indent and an arrow from line one to line two for parallelism; a fold for chiasm, with a test strip beside it that a reader can apply; a column of twenty-two Hebrew letters for the acrostics, with gaps shown as gaps; a lit thread joining two verses for the key word; a strip of story frames for narrative, with an empty frame where the narrator is silent. The pattern is visible before the explanation (rule A5), and every shape reaches the verse.
2. **One passage carrying every device.** Choose a single text and show all the patterns in it. Clean, but rejected: no passage holds them all, and forcing one would teach the very habit of finding patterns that are not there, which the page warns against.
3. **A line of who noticed what, from Lowth to Alter.** It explains the history well. Rejected as the lead, because it puts scholars before the text and implies a clean sequence of discoveries, when Jewish readers saw doubled lines and acrostics long before Lowth. It survives as a short strip in section 07.

## Keeping it apart from its neighbours

Taken on the site: three lists side by side, a dot field, an inspection desk, a year wheel, a house, a glossed page with readers in the margins, rulers and staves. The glossed page is the nearest risk, since both put Berean text at the centre. The difference is kept firm: there, readers surround a passage; here, nobody surrounds it, and the marks are the text's own shape. No margins, no notes tied by letters.

Neighbours in content: How Hebrew works keeps verb forms and words; this page shows Hebrew only as the twenty-two letters and as word order in two chiasms. The kinds of writing in the Bible keeps what each genre asks of a reader. How the Old Testament books were written keeps every question of sources; the flood mirror and repeated scenes are cited and handed over in a sentence.

## Where the evidence cannot go

- **No pattern is drawn that the page does not argue.** Each large chiasm shows the elements its proposer pairs, names the critic, and leaves the tests to the reader.
- **No verdict on authorship or date.** Form shows craft. Every source question is linked out.
- **No metre is drawn.** Line lengths are not scanned, because the scholars disagree and the vowels are later than the poems.
- **The acrostics' purpose is not chosen.**
- **No quotation from Scripture until the build supplies it.** This draft holds references only.

## Doctrine

Academic study: scholars are reported without ranking, and their reconstructions are labelled as theirs. The site's own voice holds Scripture as "truth, without any mixture of error" (Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article I) and says so at the stance and at the art-and-history panel, where Alter's "prose fiction" and Sternberg's historian are both shown and the site's view is stated as its own. The Bible-code claim is reported from the published statistical exchange. Scripture's teaching about itself is linked to the theology study Scripture, never argued here.

## What is checked

Nothing is checked yet; the page is not built. The build is to prove that every reference exists in the Berean, that each verse shows what its line says it shows (by stated phrase, once the wording is taken from the held text), and that the figures bar's counts match the text. None of that proves a reading is right. Every outside source is located, not read, and must be read before publishing; the open list in the research record names each detail to verify.
