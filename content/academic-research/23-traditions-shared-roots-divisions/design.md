# Traditions: shared roots and divisions: design proposal

The research record: [23-traditions-shared-roots-divisions.json](../23-traditions-shared-roots-divisions.json). There is no page yet. This file proposes what one could be, and nothing here is built.

This study also takes in the planned study Creeds & councils (order 22, which will not be written separately) and three parts that existed only as outlines in the site's code: Shared roots, Oriental Orthodox churches, and Separation & encounter ([history-collections.ts](../../../src/pages/places/history-collections.ts)). The fourth outlined part, Worship & spiritual life, stays planned on its own.

## What this page is for

A reader knows there are Catholics, Orthodox and Protestants. They have heard that "the church split in 1054" and that "Baptists don't believe in creeds". They may never have heard of the Copts or the Church of the East. They leave able to name the five points where communion broke, and to say what was at stake at each one, in the words of the people on both sides. They can read a single table that shows what every tradition still holds (the core of the canon, the faith of Nicaea, baptism in the triune name) and where each one parts. They can also follow the Baptist confessions from 1644 to 2000 and say what a Baptist means by a confession. The question it carries: where did they part, and what did each side keep?

## The material's own shape

The material has two shapes, and the page needs both. The first is a road that forks. One church at the start, then a fork at Ephesus, another at Chalcedon, another between Rome and Constantinople, another in the Reformation, and one more where the Baptists leave the Reformed churches. Each fork has two sides, and each side wrote something down. The second is a ledger. Lay the traditions in a row, and some marks run straight across every one of them while others break. The forks explain the breaks in the ledger, and the ledger shows what the forks did not take away.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop cannot happen inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer partway through, and this job is a draft only. So four directions were weighed in writing and one is recommended. The choice is the owner's.

1. **One line that forks, over one ledger (recommended).** A single fine line runs left to right from the apostles, and forks five times, once at each point of division. Each branch ends at the name of a living tradition. Directly under the branch ends sits the comparison table: seven traditions as columns, nine marks as rows. A shared mark (the twenty-seven books, the Nicene faith, the triune name) is drawn as one unbroken thread across all seven columns. A mark that parts (infant baptism, "and the Son", Chalcedon) is drawn as a thread that breaks, with the break placed under the fork that caused it. Point at a fork to read both sides' words; choose it to open its record sheet with the three parts: what the evidence says, what this study infers, and where the evidence stops. This puts the central comparison where it can be seen at once, early on, as the study standard's rule for academic pages asks (the pattern before the detail). The evidence can bear it because it claims only what the documents say. Every cell the study did not check is drawn as an open circle and labelled "not checked".
2. **A family tree of denominations.** This is the familiar chart with hundreds of branches. It is rejected because it pictures institutions descending from each other with a precision the record does not give, and because Baptist beginnings are themselves argued. A tree would quietly pick one view, the very thing this study must not do. Its sound part survives as the five-fork line, which marks only the documented breaks.
3. **A map of the great sees.** Rome, Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, Etchmiadzin, Wittenberg, Geneva, London, drawn with lines of communion between them. It is rejected as the frame because divisions are not places. A line between two dots cannot carry who said what, and the map would leave out the ledger. It survives as a small locator inside each fork's record sheet.
4. **Facing pages.** Each fork drawn as an open book, one side's words on the left page and the other side's on the right. This is strong and exact, but as a whole page it gives five separate spreads and no single view of what is shared. It survives as the inside of each fork's record sheet.

Against the site's other studies, these signature layouts are already taken: a glossed page (30), decreed against recorded (33), lists side by side (04), a line of dated copies (Manuscripts), coins on a line of time (34, draft) and a ledger of three kingdoms (35, draft). No study on the site pairs a forking line with a ledger of what survives every fork. The canon study's side-by-side lists answer "which books"; this ledger answers "which convictions", and links there for the books.

## Drawings

All new, in the colour of the church-history field, in the site's one hand (thin stroke, round caps, faint wash), and labelled "Illustrative forms · not facsimiles".

- **The header drawing:** a single line rising from a small pool of water, which forks into five fine branches. In a faint circle with a dotted orbit, as every study header has.
- **The five fork miniatures** for the record-sheet cards, each drawn from the evidence it stands for. 01: the cross over a lotus cloud carved at the top of the Xi'an stone. 02: two rings that touch without overlapping (the record's "without confusion … without separation"). 03: a folded sentence laid on an altar table. 04: a printed book with a seal. 05: water, and a hand lowering a figure under it.
- **The ledger's threads:** one line per mark. A shared mark is continuous; a parted mark breaks in a small gap with a four-point sparkle at the break. "Not checked" is an open circle, never a gap, so that a gap in the study is never drawn as a break between churches.

Motion: the orbit turns slowly in the header, and a faint glint travels along the forking line from left to right and loops. The threads in the ledger never animate, because a moving break would look like an event. Everything stops when the reader asks for reduced motion.

## Keeping it apart from its neighbours

- **The Trinity and the councils of 325 and 381:** the Theology articles on the Trinity. Linked from section 02 and from the "and the Son" row. Nothing is re-explained. *Finding:* the brief said the Trinity articles also explain Chalcedon's doctrine. They do not, because they stop at 381. So the record states Chalcedon's point in two sentences only, as far as the division needs it, and links the definition's full text in the creeds collection.
- **The creeds' texts:** Catechisms & Creeds. Every quotation links to its document there.
- **The canon:** How the canon took shape (04). One row of the ledger, and a link.
- **The Latin West, 500 to 1350:** The medieval church (33), which hands the East-West split to this study.
- **How each tradition reads the Bible:** How the church has read the Bible (30).
- **Reformations as movements:** the planned Reform, renewal & revival (24). Here, the Reformation appears only where it divided the church and wrote confessions.

## Before a page is built

- **Register the sources.** Every source in the record still needs an entry in the site's [SOURCES.md](../../../SOURCES.md). This job did not edit it.
- **Read what was only reported.** The declarations of 1965 and 1973, the decree of Florence, and the Baptist Faith and Message comparison chart were read through a tool that reports on a page. Each phrase quoted from them must be checked word for word. Do the same for the 1644 Confession's title and articles 39 and 40, against a facsimile or a scholarly edition (Lumpkin and Leonard).
- **Read the Oriental Orthodox churches and the Church of the East in their own words.** The record has no document of either, and the page should not be built until it has at least one each. The fullest English collection that holds both is Pelikan and Hotchkiss, *Creeds and Confessions of Faith in the Christian Tradition* (Yale University Press, 2003).
- **Fill the "not checked" cells** in the ledger, or keep them visibly open.
- **Check the counts of councils** (three, seven, twenty-one) against each church's own list.
- **Check the current text of the Baptist Faith and Message** at bfm.sbc.net before quoting it, as the site's [doctrinal foundation](../../../../DOCTRINAL-FOUNDATION.md) asks. The convention can now amend it.

## What the evidence could not settle

These questions stay open: when the East and West really parted (1054, 1204 or later); whether Chalcedon's "in two natures" and the Oriental churches' "one incarnate nature" confess the same faith, which the churches themselves still discuss; where the Baptists came from; and how far Baptists should use the ancient creeds in worship. The record gives each one as open, with the views and who holds them, and the page should do the same. On baptism, the site states its own Baptist view as its own, from Scripture, and reports every other view in that tradition's own words.
