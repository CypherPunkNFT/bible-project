## What this page is for

A reader is told that "scholars agree" Moses did not write the Pentateuch, that Isaiah is two or three books, or that Daniel was written after the events. Usually nobody shows them the evidence or the answer. They leave able to find what a book says of its own writer, to tell that apart from later tradition and from modern theory, to name the signs of a later hand and what each shows, and to give the documentary hypothesis in its holders' words with the evangelical answer beside it. The question they carry: what does the book say of itself, and what is a reader adding?

## The material's own shape

The subject already sorts itself into three layers that readers usually blur together. There is the book's own witness: "Isaiah son of Amoz", Moses writing at the LORD's command. There is later Scripture and tradition remembering it: Joshua's "book of the Law of Moses", Jesus naming the prophet Daniel, Bava Batra. And there are modern reconstructions. The page is built as those three layers, always in that order and always visibly apart. Every section opens with the book speaking first.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, so three directions were weighed in writing. One is proposed, and the owner may overturn it on sight.

1. **Three bands, one book at a time (proposed).** For each book argued in full, a horizontal strip of its chapters. Above the strip, gold marks show where the book speaks of its own writing (Exodus 17:14, Deuteronomy 31:24, Isaiah 1:1, Daniel's first-person visions). Below it, hollow marks show where a later hand is visible (Deuteronomy 34, Genesis 14:14) or where a theory divides the text (the J and P strands, chapter 40, chapter 7). The central comparison is visible at once (rule A5): the reader sees where the text claims a writer and where the arguments press, on the same ruler. The four Pentateuch criteria and the Isaiah and Daniel evidence open from their marks as record sheets.
2. **A timeline of when each book was written.** This is the obvious shape. It was rejected as the lead because it would draw ranged and disputed dates as points. It would also set traditional and critical dates side by side on one axis as if they were equal options the site weighs. The dates survive as text in the reference table, as ranges.
3. **A family tree of sources (J, E, D, P flowing into the five books).** This is the textbook diagram. It was rejected because drawing the hypothesis as a tree presents a reconstruction as a finding, and the rules forbid that. Its history survives as a plain list in section 02, each step with its year and holder.

Against the site's other studies: the glossed page, three lists side by side, threads and staves, the dot field, the inspection desk, the wheel and the house are all taken. Nobody on the site uses a chapter ruler with two kinds of mark. It must not echo Egypt beside Israel's two rulers. So here it is one ruler per book, and the marks are points above and below the line, not threads between two lines.

## Where the evidence cannot go

- **No point dates.** Every date is a range, and dates tied to the exodus are given both ways.
- **No source strands drawn as documents.** Where a theory divides a chapter, the mark is labelled "proposed division" and opens the holders' words.
- **No verdicts where inerrantists differ.** The Pentateuch editing views and Darius the Mede are shown with holders and are not ranked.
- **No scholar's view is reported as read.** Until each book is checked, the sources list says so, and the open questions carry every [check].

## Doctrine

This is an Academic study, so the site's voice never teaches a doctrine. It does hold Scripture as "truth, without any mixture of error" (Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article I) and God's knowledge of the future (article II). It says so once, at the opening stance, and again at each section's stand. The documentary hypothesis, a second or third Isaiah and a late Daniel are reported in their holders' words as others' views, with the answer, and are never adopted. What Scripture teaches of itself is linked to theology's Scripture (the word of God). The page does not read Isaiah 52:13–53:12, and points to the study that does, where the Jewish reading comes first.

## What will be checked

The build will prove that every reference exists in the Berean, that every quoted phrase is in its verse, that the table has thirty-nine books in both orders, and that every mark on a ruler opens a record that names its verse. None of this proves that a reading is right, or that a scholar holds the view the page gives him. That is checked by reading his book, which the record says has not yet been done.
