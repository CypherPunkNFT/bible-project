# Reform, renewal & revival: design record

The page: /study/reform-renewal-and-revival (planned, not built). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../24-reform-renewal-and-revival.json). Its verses and dates: a data build still to be written, which will fill every verse phrase from the held Berean.

## What this page is for

A reader hears that Baptists go back in an unbroken line to John the Baptist, or that they are Anabaptists under another name, or that Roger Williams founded them, or that Southern Baptists have always been Calvinists, or never were. They leave able to tell the General Baptists from the Particular Baptists by date, place and confession. They can say which links between movements a document attests and which were drawn later. They can follow liberty of conscience from a prison in 1612 to a letter of 1802, and tell a revival from revivalism. The question it carries: which joins does a document actually show, and which were drawn afterwards?

## The material's own shape

The plan's row uses the word itself: the Baptists came "in two streams". The question every popular claim asks is a question about joins: who came from whom. So the page is a watershed drawn from documents. Each movement is a stream that begins where its first dated document begins. Each join between streams is a record in three parts. A join is inked solid only where a document shows it, dashed where it is argued, and left undrawn where it is only claimed. The Landmark line from the apostles appears once, as a dotted line labelled with who drew it and when. The drawing's honesty is its argument.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on sight.

1. **A watershed of streams, inked by evidence (proposed).** It shows at once that the two Baptist streams rise apart, out of different Separatist churches, and it shows the difference between contact and descent by line style and words together. It can bear the evidence, because no line needs to be more precise than its record.
2. **A family tree from 1517 to 1845.** The obvious shape for "where did we come from". Rejected: a tree draws every link as descent, and the evidence for most links between these movements is contact, likeness or argument. It would draw the Landmark claim's own picture.
3. **A single line of time.** Clear for sequence. Rejected as the lead: it lays Zurich, Amsterdam and London on one track and invites the reader to read order as cause. It survives as a section, with ranges drawn as ranges and the empty centuries before 1500 stated as the selection's edge.

Against the site's other studies: three lists side by side, a dot field, an inspection desk, a year wheel, a house, a glossed page, rulers and staves are all taken. Nobody on the site draws joins as evidence records. The liberty section is a relay of seven documents passed hand to hand, and it must not echo the canon study's lists. Each document stands alone as a card, joined by a fine rule, never in a comparison grid.

## Behaviour

The view lives in the address: `stream` (which stream is lit), `join` (which join's record is open), `doc` (a liberty document), `open` (the record sheet). An unknown value falls back to the default. Choosing a join lights its two streams and opens its three parts in the sheet from the right. Line style is carried by solid, dashed and absent strokes and by a word on every join ("attested", "argued", "claimed"), never by colour alone. Motion is limited to a slow drift of the water lines and two sparkles in the header, and all of it stops for reduced motion. The streams are a list first, at a 44-pixel target. The drawing repeats them for a pointer, out of the tab order.

## Where the evidence cannot go

- **No descent where only contact is shown.** Smyth beside the Waterlanders is drawn as contact; Stassen's thesis on the 1644 Confession is drawn dashed, with its answer named once it is read.
- **No chain before 1500.** The empty centuries are labelled as the selection's edge, so the absence does not read as a verdict on the church.
- **No groups from Carroll's chart are drawn.** They are named in words only, so the page does not draw the claim it is testing.
- **Every source is located, not read, in this draft.** The page cannot be built until each attributed claim is checked in its book, and every [check] in the record is cleared.

## Doctrine

Academic study, so history is reported without ranking traditions. The site's voice holds Scripture as the sole authority (the preamble of the Baptist Faith and Message 2000), believer's baptism by immersion (article VII) and liberty of conscience (article XVII), and says so at the stance. It shows both Baptist readings of election (article V) and does not choose. Luther's writings against the Jews and the Convention's founding over slavery are each stated plainly, and neither is softened or made the whole story. The 1995 resolution is reported. Classical Pentecostal history is left to its own study and grouped with no other movement.

## What will be checked
