# Home and household in Old Testament Israel: design record

The page: [/study/home-and-household](../../../src/pages/study/home/HomePage.tsx). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../27-home-and-household.json). Its verses: [the data build](../../../scripts/build-home-study.py).

## What this page is for

A reader has seen the drawing of an Israelite house, with animals below and a family above, and needs to know how much of it was dug up. They leave able to walk through the rooms, follow bread and cloth through the house, and sort any claim about home life into what was found, what is reconstructed, and what a passage says. The page's question: *what was found on the floor, and what am I picturing?*

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one was built, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on review:

1. **The cut with certainty in its line (built).** One usual plan, cut open, with the stroke itself saying how a thing is known: solid for found, dashed for reconstructed, dotted for written. No other study on the site uses this, and it teaches the page's question before a word is read.
2. **A day in the house, dawn to dark.** Warm and easy to follow. Rejected: it needs an invented family and a timetable the evidence cannot bear, which is the exact mistake this page is trying to catch.
3. **A gazetteer of excavated houses, site by site.** The most rigorous. Rejected for now: the measured room-by-room data sits in reports and a thesis that could not be read from open sources. Worth returning to if those are obtained.

## Form and motion

- The header drawing is new and ours, built from the same projection as the section's cutaway. It is labelled as illustrative, no single excavated house. Motto: "Solid where it was found. / Dashed where we picture it."
- Tone colour is the Study hub's field colour for Ancient cultures & daily life. It sits close to the site's gold, so "chosen" is carried by ink, weight and underline rather than by tone.
- Motion is limited to a slow orbit, moving smoke and two sparks in the header, and all of it stops under reduced motion.
- The cutaway is one label for assistive technology. The six room buttons beside it are the interface, at a 44-pixel target. Below 640 pixels the task table gives way to eight task buttons, and the chosen task's list of places is there at every width.
- Each kind of evidence has its own stroke and its own word, so nothing rests on colour alone.

## What was kept out, and why

- **First-century homes, field work, water supply, feasts, courts, health, childbirth, household gods.** Each has its own study, built or planned. The "Not here" list on the page names them.
- **How a family should be ordered.** Scripture's teaching, not a question floors can answer. Carol Meyers's conclusions about women's standing in the household are reported as hers; the page's own voice takes no position from the archaeology, and nothing in it is at odds with the Baptist Faith and Message 2000.
- **Any invented scene.** No named family, no timetable, no "typical evening".
- **Any house given as exact.** The two published sizes are drawn to one scale and left unreconciled.
