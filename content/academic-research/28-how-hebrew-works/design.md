# How Hebrew works: design record

The page: [/study/how-hebrew-works](../../../src/pages/study/hebrew/HebrewPage.tsx). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../28-how-hebrew-works.json). Its words and counts: [the data build](../../../scripts/build-hebrew-study.py).

## What this page is for

A reader meets Hebrew in commentaries and sermons and has no way to judge what is being said about it. They leave able to sound out a pointed line, see how a word is built, and ask of any form: *what does the form show, and what am I reading into it?* They do not leave able to translate, and the page says so.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one was built, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on review:

1. **A line you can peel, then a bench to sound it out (built).** A real verse hangs from a ruled line, and its accents and points come away and go back. Then the reader says it, letter by letter. No other study on the site does this, and it teaches the page's question before a word is read.
2. **A full grammar, chart by chart.** The most complete. Rejected: it is what puts readers off, and it drifts into teaching translation. One real grid of one verb was kept, because it shows what the charts are for.
3. **A word-by-word walk through Psalm 23.** Warm and familiar. Rejected: the divine name stands in its opening line, which this page leaves to its own study, and a walk through a beloved psalm slides straight into translating it.

## Form and motion

- The page looks like a ruled sheet: hairlines that letters hang from, large Hebrew as the art, sections numbered by a letter and its digit, and plain underlined choices. It has no cards and no pills, so it does not read as a sibling of Strong's numbers, which uses an alphabet band and cards.
- The header drawing is new and ours: בָּרָא hanging from a rule with a reed pen and a right-to-left arrow. It is labelled as illustrative and not a facsimile. Motto: "Read from the right. / Sound it before you look it up."
- Tone colour is the Study hub's field colour for Languages & translation, the same as Strong's numbers. "Chosen" is carried by ink, weight and rule, so nothing rests on colour alone.
- Motion is limited to a slow orbit, drifting points and two sparks in the header, and all of it stops under reduced motion.
- Hebrew is set in the site's Hebrew face, with no letter-spacing and no synthesised weight, so the marks stay where the font puts them. It is marked as Hebrew and right to left, and isolated from the English around it.
- The alphabet follows the reading direction: the left arrow goes to the next letter. Previous and Next carry the bench from the keyboard, and pressing a word is a pointer shortcut.
- The chart of forms by book is a real ordered list with its values written out, so it is its own table view. It uses one hue, because only size is being read.

## What was kept out, and why

- **Looking words up by number.** Strong's numbers does that, and its three mistakes concern the number. The claims here concern the root and the form.
- **Who marked the points, and when.** One handover line to the planned Masoretes study. Dating them would open an old argument this page does not need to carry.
- **The divine name.** One note. The page gives no pronunciation as fact, and no line it practises contains the name.
- **Greek and Aramaic.** Each has its own planned study. Aramaic words are set aside and counted, so the reader can see the rule.
- **Every verse with a word.** Whether the site should offer such lists is a question still with the owner in the original-languages plan. The page shows a count and one verse.
- **Tense or aspect.** Four accounts are named with exactly how much of each was read, and the page does not choose.
- **The Psalms Scroll from Qumran as a picture.** It was the first choice for a page with no points, but the Israel Antiquities Authority holds rights in its images. The Nash Papyrus, from a 1903 plate, shows the same thing with a clear licence.
- **The Great Isaiah Scroll as a picture.** Not needed once the Nash Papyrus was chosen.

## How the sounds were checked

The sounds are one classroom scheme, stated on the page. Each guided letter has its sound and rule written in the record. The build checks that the steps rebuild the word's sound and that there is one step for each letter of the Leningrad text. It cannot check that the scheme is right, and the page says how far a scheme goes.
