# Between the Testaments: design proposal

The research record: [34-between-the-testaments.json](../34-between-the-testaments.json). There is no page yet, and this file proposes what one could be. Nothing here is built.

## What this page is for

A reader has heard of "the four hundred silent years", of Hanukkah, and of the Pharisees, and they meet Herod on the first page of Matthew without knowing how he came to be there. They leave able to name the six powers that held Judea between Nehemiah and Jesus, and the evidence that shows each one. They can follow the crisis under Antiochus IV step by step, and use 1 and 2 Maccabees as history while saying, from those books themselves, why Protestants do not count them as Scripture. They can set Daniel 8 and 11 beside the record while keeping what the text names apart from what readers identify. The question it carries: who ruled the land when no prophet wrote, and how do we know?

## The material's own shape

These years have no single book to walk through. What they do have is a change of master every few generations, and every master struck coins. A coin is small, real and dated, and it carries the evidence in itself: whose name, which title, which language, which image, or none. Read one after another, six coins tell the story of these four hundred years, and each one shows where its evidence stops. That is the study's spine. Daniel 11 is the Bible's own telling of the same years, and it stands beside the coins as a second line.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, and then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job, and this job is a draft only. So three directions were weighed in writing, and one is recommended. The choice is the owner's.

1. **Six coins on one line of time (recommended).** A single horizontal line from 450 BC to the birth of Jesus, with six coins laid along it in their own periods: a Yehud silver coin, Alexander's tetradrachm, a Ptolemy's eagle, Antiochus IV's "God Manifest", Alexander Jannaeus's anchor and star, and Herod's coin without a portrait. Each coin opens a panel in three parts: what the coin and the sources say, what follows, and where it stops. Daniel 11's verses run as a thin second line above the coins, each one tied to the coin of its period only where readers make the identification, and drawn as a dotted tie, not a solid one. The pattern shows at once (rule A5 of the study standard: the central comparison where it can be seen early): six hands, four hundred years, and one text that tells them without naming a king. The evidence can bear it, since each coin is a real object of a known reign and the line uses ranges, not points.
2. **Daniel 11 as the spine: a two-column ledger, verse on the left and record on the right.** It is strong and direct, but it is rejected as the frame. It would make the whole study a commentary on the most disputed chapter it touches, and it would let the readers' identifications lead, which is the very thing the study asks the reader to hold apart. It also has nothing to say about the synagogue, the parties or Herod's rise. Its good part survives as section 05, where the ledger is drawn with the named lines set apart above it.
3. **A map with moving borders.** Judea's frontier redrawn at each change of master. It is rejected because the sources do not give these borders precisely, and a drawn border would picture a precision the evidence does not have. The Atlas and Israel among the empires (07) already map the empires, and the coin finds would turn a map into a scatter of dots this study has not counted.

Against the site's other studies: a glossed page (30), a wheel (29), a cutaway house (27), rulers and staves (32), a ledger of three kingdoms (35, draft) and a road of stations (37, draft) are all taken. No study on the site uses coins as its instrument. Cyrus, restoration and return (37) shows one Yehud coin inside its province section. Here the Yehud coin is only the first of six, and the period it stands for begins where that study ends.

## Drawings

All new, in the History field's colour, in the site's one hand (thin stroke, round caps, faint wash), and labelled "Illustrative forms · not coin facsimiles".

- **The header drawing:** a single coin seen edge-on and face-on, its face blank where the image would be, a closed scroll behind it. In a faint circle with a dotted orbit, as every study header has.
- **The six coin miniatures** for section 01's cards, drawn from the types named in the record, never traced from a photograph.
- **Section 02:** a small altar drawn twice, once broken and once whole, with the two dates in the Maccabees' own reckoning beneath.
- **Section 05:** two horns and four horns (Daniel 8), drawn only as the text describes them, beside the ledger.

Motion: the orbit turns slowly in the header, and a faint glint travels across each coin in turn. All of it stops when the reader asks for reduced motion. The dotted ties in section 05 never animate, because a moving tie would look like proof.

## Before a page is built

- **Register the sources.** Every source in the record still needs an entry in the site's [SOURCES.md](../../../SOURCES.md). This job did not edit it.
- **Read what was only reported.** The Heliodorus stele's publication, the prayer-house inscriptions in the inscription corpus, the Theodotus inscription's record, the 2025 radiocarbon article (it is open access), and the Polybius and Tacitus passages word for word.
- **Read what was only located.** Jerome's Commentary on Daniel, Stephen R. Miller's commentary (the Southern Baptist publisher's), and at least one of Young or Baldwin for the early date; Collins for the late date; Levine for the synagogue; Steinmann and Filmer for Herod's death.
- **Check the Alexander story.** Whether any Greek historian of Alexander mentions a visit to Jerusalem.
- **Check the Talmud translation's licence** before quoting the oil story on a page.

## What the evidence could not settle

When Daniel was written; which reading of Daniel 11:36–45 is right among those Bible-believing readers hold; why Antiochus IV forbade the Law; which Hasmonean struck the first coins; whether Herod died in 4 or 1 BC; and where and when the synagogue began. The record gives each as open, with the views, and the page should do the same. On Daniel's date it states the site's own view as the site's own, and reports the other as a reconstruction.
