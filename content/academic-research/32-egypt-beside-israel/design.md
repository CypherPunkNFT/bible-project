# Egypt beside Israel: design record

The page: [/study/egypt-beside-israel](../../../src/pages/study/egypt/EgyptPage.tsx). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../32-egypt-beside-israel.json). Its verses, counts and letter lines: [the data build](../../../scripts/build-egypt-study.py).

## What this page is for

A reader hears that Proverbs was copied from Egypt, or that Psalm 104 is Akhenaten's hymn, or that the Habiru are the Hebrews. They leave able to count the matches themselves and say whose count they use, to name where hymn and psalm part, to read a line of a letter, and to tell a likeness from a line of descent. The question it carries: what do these two texts share, and what does the sharing actually show?

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, because the owner cannot answer mid-job. So three directions were weighed in writing, one was built, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on sight.

1. **Three instruments, one for each kind of text (built).** Threads between two true rulers for a wisdom book, two staves for two songs, a map and a word-by-word reader for letters. Each section's picture is the argument for that kind of text.
2. **One long parallel column, all three bodies in a single scroll.** Simple. Rejected: it makes every pair look equally close, and hides that the evidence is of three different kinds.
3. **A courtroom, with each claim on trial.** Lively. Rejected as a frame: it would force verdicts the sources do not give. Its best part survives as section 05's tests.

## Keeping it apart from its neighbours

Same field: Home is a cutaway and Farming a wheel. This page uses rulers, staves and a reader, and no ring at all in its body. A first plan drew the hymn on a sun arc with eight stations; a review pointed out that this forced a day onto Psalm 104, of which only verses 19 to 23 run like one, and echoed the wheel. It became two straight staves.

Other studies: Israel among the empires keeps Egypt's dealings with Judah and Elephantine. From discovery to history keeps Merneptah and Jericho. The planned exodus and settlement study keeps every date for those events, so this page chooses none, and hands over the question of whether any Hebrews were called Habiru. How Hebrew works keeps the language itself: here the Canaanite words are a table of three.

## Form and motion

- The header drawing is new and ours: a sun disc whose rays end in small hands, over a papyrus roll and a clay tablet. It is labelled illustrative. Motto: "Words that run close. / Names that do not." An earlier motto, "Sharing a sentence. / Not sharing a God.", was dropped because it answered the closing question on the opening screen and assumed a relationship section 04 leaves open.
- Tone colour is the Study hub's field colour for this category. It sits close to the site's gold, so "chosen" is carried by ink, weight and underline rather than by tone.
- Motion is a slow orbit and two sparks in the header, stopped under reduced motion.
- A thread a judge does not count is cut to two stubs, not faded, and every row says in words who counts it. So shape and words carry the meaning, not colour.
- Each drawing is one described image. The lists are the interface, at a 44-pixel target (the two-column phone list of cities included), and the drawings repeat them for a pointer, out of the tab order.
- Numbers appear on a thread only when it is chosen, so nothing has to fit at phone width. Tests hold the thread ends and the staff marks apart.
- The two site fonts lack subscript numerals and half-brackets. So sign numbers and determinatives are real sub and sup elements, and damage is a dotted underline, explained beside the reader. Signs are hidden from screen readers in favour of the plain rendering, and marked not to be machine-translated.
- Hebrew is marked right to left and isolated, in the table only, never inside a drawing.

## What was kept out, and why

- **A British Museum photograph of the papyrus.** Commons tags it public domain, but gives the ancient scribe as the artist and the museum's own image as its source, and that does not establish the photo's rights. The Met's open flake with the book's beginning stands in, and shows the book being copied centuries on.
- **Standard modern translations.** Moran and Lichtheim are under copyright, and named as located, not read. Griffith 1926 and Breasted 1912 are older than today's scholarship and the page says so: the English of "How manifold are thy works" is itself a test question.
- **A count for the exodus.** Claim 3 is answered from what each text says of its God, not from a chronology.
- **Dion's name.** Buys cites him at 2009, and a reviewer doubted that date. He is not needed, so he is not named.

## What the evidence could not settle

The eleven usually cited are named by Ruffle as a number, and this page's list is his passage-by-passage discussion: another list of eleven is conceivable. Seven is this study's count from his list, and the page says so. All three judges reach the page through one sceptical article, so the page also names the higher counts of Erman and Gressmann, without drawing them. Griffith's text was read from a scan with errors, corrected by hand: a one-off check in the job's workspace compared each quoted line against that scan, and the build itself does not hold a copy to compare against. The picture for the hymn is a drawing, not a photograph.
