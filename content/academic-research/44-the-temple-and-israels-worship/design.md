# The temple and Israel's worship: design record

The page: /study/the-temple-and-israels-worship (to build). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../44-the-temple-and-israels-worship.json). Its verses and measurements: a data build still to write, which adds each verse's Berean phrase from the site's held text.

## What this page is for

A reader meets the temple in pieces: a tent in Exodus, cedar and gold in Kings, Zechariah's lot in Luke, a feast in John, the stones in Mark 13, a guide at the Western Wall. They leave able to put the pieces in one place: four houses to one scale, a priest's week, a day of offerings, a year of feasts, and the end in AD 70. Above all they leave able to say, of each thing they know, where the knowing comes from: Scripture, the ground, Josephus, or the Mishnah written down about AD 200. The question it carries: which witness told me this, and how near was it to the temple?

## The material's own shape

The temple is built as nested holiness: courts around a house, a house around one small room. All four buildings keep that room, and the room's size barely changes while everything around it grows. So the study's central comparison is already in the record: four plans at one scale, the inner room fixed at the middle. The day and the year are the temple's own second shape, set in Numbers 28–29.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, so three directions were weighed in writing and one is recommended. The owner may overturn it on sight.

1. **Four plans at one scale, laid over one another (recommended).** The tabernacle, Solomon's house, the second temple and Herod's platform drawn from the record's measurements, centred on the inner room. The tabernacle's room is drawn from the frame count and marked inferred; the second temple is a dashed outline because no plan survives; Herod's platform carries the finds as marks, each opening its record sheet. The pattern shows at once (rule A5): one room, ever wider courts. The evidence can bear it, because every line is either a measurement Scripture or the Mishnah gives or is drawn dashed.
2. **A walk inward, gate to inner room, as the main view.** It teaches the order of holiness well. Rejected as the lead: Home and household is already a cutaway, a walk through Herod's courts would need the Mishnah's plan treated as fact, and it shows one building, not four. Its best part survives as the Day of Atonement strip in section 05, where Leviticus 16 itself walks inward and out.
3. **A ring of the year with the feasts on it.** Rejected: the farming year owns the wheel, and a ring would fold the daily offering and the four buildings into a calendar that cannot hold them. The year becomes a straight strip instead.

## Form and motion

- The header drawing is new: four nested outlines around one small square, a lampstand faint at the centre, labelled illustrative. Motion is the orbit and two sparkles, stopped for reduced motion.
- Every witness has a fixed mark used everywhere on the page: a filled tag for Scripture, an outlined tag for Josephus, a dotted tag for the Mishnah and later rabbinic texts, a square for a find in the ground. Shape and word carry the kind; colour never carries it alone.
- An inferred line is dashed and says "inferred" in words beside it. A missing measurement leaves a gap, never a guess.
- The view lives in the address: `house`, `find`, `feast`, `open`. The lists are the controls at a 44-pixel target; the drawings repeat them for a pointer, out of the tab order.
- Hebrew feast names are given in transliteration in the body, with Hebrew script only in the record sheet, marked right to left.

## Where the evidence cannot go

- **No placed sanctuary.** The house is drawn inside Herod's courts without a fixed spot on today's platform; the proposals are listed, not chosen.
- **No exodus date.** The tabernacle has no bar on the line of time.
- **No Mishnah as fact.** Every detail drawn from it carries its tag and the line "written down about AD 200".
- **No verdict on Titus.** Two accounts, side by side.
- **No meaning of sacrifice.** Each offering links to theology's Leviticus and does not teach it.
