# Israel from the exodus to Solomon: design record

The page: `/study/israel-from-the-exodus-to-solomon` (to build, under `src/pages/study/exodus-to-solomon/`). Its evidence and limits: [the research record](../38-israel-from-the-exodus-to-solomon.json). Its verses and spans: a data build to write, `scripts/build-exodus-to-solomon-study.py`.

## What this page is for

A reader hears that archaeology has disproved the exodus, or that Jericho's walls prove it. They hear that David never existed, or that Solomon's gates settle the matter. They leave able to tell three kinds of date apart: a number the text gives, a range the ground gives, and a scholar's way of joining the two. They can name the three verses every exodus date rests on, and read the two readings of Exodus 12:40. They can say what was found at Jericho, Ai, Hazor, Shiloh, Khirbet Qeiyafa and Jerusalem, and what each find cannot show. They can describe the invention view fairly and give the answer. The question it carries: whose date is this — the text's, the ground's, or someone's joining of the two?

## The material's own shape

This period's argument has a fixed form. Scripture gives spans: 480 years in 1 Kings 6:1, 430 in Exodus 12:40, 300 in Judges 11:26. Each span hangs from a point, and each can be read two ways. The ground gives ranges, site by site. Two outside records act as nails: Merneptah, about 1208 BC, and Shoshenq, about 925 BC. Every exodus date is made by hanging the text's cords from one nail and seeing where they fall among the ground's bars. So the page draws that.

## Directions, and one honest gap

The study standard asks for three or four directions built as mock-ups, then a stop for the owner to choose. That stop is not possible inside a remote job, so three directions were weighed in writing. One is recommended, and the choice is the owner's to overturn on sight.

1. **Cords and nails (recommended).** A single track from 1900 to 900 BC. Above the line are Scripture's spans, drawn as cords of their stated length, hung from Solomon's fourth year. Each cord has two strands where it has two readings: the 480 as a count or as a measure, the 430 in Egypt alone or with Canaan. Below the line, the ground's ranges are drawn as bars. The two outside records are drawn as nails through both. The reader chooses a reading, and its cords fall into place, showing which bars they meet. Both exodus dates are visible at once (rule A5). The page shows how each is built and chooses neither.
2. **A route map from Goshen to Jerusalem.** It is the obvious shape for an exodus. It was rejected as the lead because the evidence cannot bear it: the crossing, Sinai, Kadesh, Ai and Pithom are all disputed placements, and a map would choose. Ai keeps a small two-pin map inside station 3, with both pins and both names.
3. **A grid of dig-site cards.** It was rejected because it gives every site equal weight and hides the chronology question the period turns on. It survives as the six station sheets.

The page is also kept apart from its neighbours. Cities through their layers (08) owns the cut-through mound, so this page draws no strata column. Egypt beside Israel uses threads between rulers. The cords here hang from one point and run one way, with no second ruler. Israel among the empires (07) is the nearest built neighbour on the shelf, and must be checked on the preview before porting so that the two tracks do not look alike [check: 07's main graphic].

## Behaviour

The view lives in the address: `span480` (count or measure), `span430` (Egypt or Canaan), `station`, and `open` (the record sheet). An unknown value falls back to the default. One record sheet opens from the right. Focus returns to whatever opened it. The cords move only when a reading is chosen, in one slow ease; under reduced motion they jump. The drawing's orbit and two sparkles loop, and stop for reduced motion. The span choices are pressed buttons with words, and the cords carry a dashed or solid line as well as tone, so colour is never the only carrier.

## Where the evidence cannot go

- **No point dates.** Every bar is a range no narrower than its source gives. The two exodus dates are drawn as small ranges and labelled as each view's.
- **No attacker on a burned layer.** The station sheets say what burned and when, never who burned it, unless the text is quoted for its own claim.
- **No identification is drawn as fact.** Ai, Shaaraim, Gibeah, the Millo and David's palace are each labelled as a proposal with its holder.
- **No Egyptian date is more exact than the usual chronology allows,** and the key says Egyptologists differ.
- **The illustrative radiocarbon range in the gates test** is labelled illustrative, not a published result.

## Doctrine

This is an Academic study, so it reports and does not teach doctrine. The site's own voice appears once, at the stance. It holds Scripture as "truth, without any mixture of error" (Baptist Faith and Message 2000, article I) and the events as having happened. The exodus date, the stay in Egypt, Ai and the tenth century are disputes among people who hold that. They are shown with their holders and not chosen. The Jewish reading of Exodus 12:40, from Seder Olam and Rashi, is given first in its own terms. The invention view is described as others' view, in its holders' terms, with the evangelical answer. What Scripture says of itself is linked to the theology study Scripture (the word of God).

## What is to be checked

The build must prove that every reference exists in the Berean, and must add each verse's wording from the site's text. No verse was quoted in this record, because none was supplied. Each cord's length must match its verse, and every bar's range must match the record. That proves the references and arithmetic, not any reading or any scholar's view. Every scholar's view is marked located, not read. Before building, each must be checked to the page, and the record's editor checks list must be cleared.
