**Licence, in full honesty.** The card releases *this dataset's own curation* as CC0. The underlying
passages are genuinely public domain — the World English Bible, the Nicene and Apostles' Creeds, the
Didache, the Ante-Nicene Fathers in the Roberts-Donaldson translation, and Project Gutenberg or
pre-1923 logic and philosophy texts. The questions, reasoning traces and answers were generated by
third-party model APIs (Gemini/Gemma, Mistral, NVIDIA-hosted models), and the card itself warns that
anyone who cares about those providers' output terms should check them before redistributing at
scale. So: no human wrote any question here, and the CC0 covers the curation rather than the model
providers' terms.

**Format.** JSON Lines, one example per line. Each carries `conversations` (a system / human / gpt
turn structure, with the reasoning trace pulled out into the gpt turn's own `think` field), `context`
(the source passage the example is grounded in), `pericope_ref`, `source`, `source_tier`, `variation`
and `lint_flags`. Composition: 2,952 single-passage World English Bible, 5,000 paired-passage, 1,938
logic and philosophy, 999 patristic, 216 creeds and Didache.

**Five sampled questions** (lines 0, 2000, 5000, 8200, 10500 of `master.jsonl`):

1. *(Leviticus 25:47-55)* "These offerings come after the tabernacle is already finished and set up.
   Why celebrate again with these twelve days of dedication?"
2. *(2 Kings 21:16-26)* "If the old counsel had been taken seriously, would the kingdom have held?"
3. *(2 Samuel 17:1-13 + Nehemiah 1:1-11)* "If my life seems chaotic, does that mean God isn't guiding
   me at all?"
4. *(Clement of Rome, First Epistle to the Corinthians, Ch. XLVII)* "If the author admits that the
   early Corinthians were already splitting into factions over their favorite leaders, doesn't that
   suggest that internal conflict and personality cults are an inherent flaw in the structure of the
   church…?"
5. *(Euclid, Elements, Book I, Def. xx)* "If we define a triangle as three straight lines joined
   end-to-end, why does the definition exclude the possibility of a 'degenerate' triangle…?"

**Do these look like real comprehension or story test questions? No.** Not one of the five has a
checkable answer. These are open-ended pastoral and discussion prompts — written to train a
counselling model, with answers averaging around 2,000–3,000 characters of essay plus a separate
reasoning trace. There is no right answer to mark, so nothing here can be scored. A fifth of the set
(the 1,938 logic and philosophy examples on Mill, Boethius, Lewis Carroll and Euclid) is not about
the Bible at all.

**Two quality notes, found by inspection:**

- **The first record is misgrounded.** Line 0 asks about the twelve days of dedication offerings —
  that is Numbers 7 — but its `pericope_ref` and its `context` are both Leviticus 25:47-55, on
  redeeming an Israelite who has sold himself. Question and passage do not match. Three further
  spot-checks (lines 100, 1500, 3000) were correctly grounded, so this is not obviously widespread,
  but it was the very first row looked at and it was wrong. Any use needs its own grounding check.
- **98.6% of records carry at least one of the dataset's own advisory quality flags** (10,945 of
  11,105), overwhelmingly `think_slop_structural` at 98.0%. The card is upfront that this lint pass
  is advisory in this release and was not used to filter or rewrite anything.

**Licence status: unclear, and doubly so.** With no licence, default copyright applies and all rights
stay with the author — downloading for private study is one thing, redistributing is another. On top
of that, the question bank itself
([data/bible_qa/1001.csv](Jarvis/Projects/BibleProject/Website/content/questions/sources/raw/helen-jiahe-zhao-bibleqa/data/bible_qa/1001.csv))
is a numbered "1001 Bible questions" list whose numbering runs past 955 for the 886 questions kept,
which means it was transcribed from some third-party quiz compilation whose own rights are nowhere
recorded. Two unclear layers, not one.

**Accordingly: use for private research only. Do not publish, redistribute, or put any of this text
on the website, and do not treat the questions as reusable content without clearing the underlying
compilation first.** Its value right now is as a worked example of what good quiz questions look
like, and as a benchmark to measure our own generated questions against.

**Format.** Tab-separated files that carry a `.csv` extension — the header line has no commas, so a
plain comma-based reader hands back a single column; they must be split on tabs. The derived sets
have columns `ID, Question, Verse_Code, KJV_Verse, ASV_Verse, YLT_Verse, WEB_Verse, Label`, repeating
each question against three candidate verses with a 0/1 relevance label: `bible_qa_no_context.csv`
holds 2,658 rows for 886 questions (886 labelled correct), `bible_qa_train.csv` 1,860 rows for 620
questions. The bank file is `N. Question<TAB>N. Answer (Ref)`. The repo also ships seven complete
Bible translations as CSV, JSON variants of the question list, a word2vec embedding file, and the
2017 model code (`baseline_squad.py` is an empty file; `process_wiki_qa.py` is a 12-byte stub).

**Five sampled questions** (rows 1, 120, 330, 560, 845 of `1001.csv`):

1. "What was the name of Jesus' mother?" → Mary (Matt 1:18)
2. "When the law was given to the children of Israel what were they told not to worship?" → Other
   gods (Exo 34:14)
3. "Which two sisters married Jacob?" → Rachel and Leah (Gen 29:28)
4. "What did Ahab want to buy from Naboth?" → His vineyard (1 Ki 21:2)
5. "According to Proverbs how do those 'seeking death' acquire their wealth?" → By lying (Prov 21:6)

**Do these look like real comprehension or story test questions? Yes — these are the real thing.**
Short, unambiguous, one checkable factual answer each, every answer carrying its verse citation, and
the spread runs across the whole Bible from Genesis through the Law, the histories, the wisdom books
and the Gospels. This is exactly the shape of a Sunday-school or Bible-quiz question. It is the one
source of the three that actually holds quiz questions — and it is also the one that may not be
published. That tension is the main finding of this download.

## What this corpus does and does not give us

The starter corpus is down and provenanced, but it does not yet contain a publishable set of Bible
quiz questions, and it is worth being plain about why. The only real quiz questions in it are
BibleQA's 886, and those are the ones that may not be published. The 11,105 examples in the Open
Biblical Dataset are freely licensed but are pastoral discussion prompts with essay answers and no
markable answer — the wrong shape for a quiz. BibleData is the one that is both publishable and
genuinely useful, and it holds no questions at all, only the facts to build them from.

So the realistic path to a publishable quiz is: **generate questions from BibleData's facts** (and
from the project's own existing public-domain sources), using **BibleQA as the private yardstick** for
what a good question looks like. That is a suggestion, not work done — nothing has been generated,
processed or wired in.

## How the general-knowledge sources were filtered

Three sources are general trivia sets that merely contain some Bible questions:
OpenTriviaQA, el-cms Open-trivia-database and the Open Trivia Database API. Filtering
them ran in recorded stages rather than one regex, because deciding what counts as a
Bible question is a judgement and ought to be auditable:

1. Keyword matching in three tiers - unambiguous markers, Bible-specific names and terms,
   and ambiguous words with common non-Bible senses - all matched on whole words, so
   "Christ" cannot match "Christmas".
2. Veto rules that disqualify an ambiguous match sitting in a plainly non-biblical
   context. These kill the three big false-positive families: US history (Abraham
   Lincoln), film and pop culture (Raiders of the Lost Ark, Black Sabbath), and religion
   that is not the Bible - other faiths, Catholic devotion to later saints, and
   post-biblical Jewish practice. Questions drawn from the Torah are biblical and are
   deliberately not vetoed.
3. Every surviving match was then read. 138 were ruled out individually and recorded in
   [tools/bible_filter_exclusions.json](Jarvis/Projects/BibleProject/Website/content/questions/sources/tools/bible_filter_exclusions.json)
   with a reason each.

The large uncategorized file required a Bible-specific marker rather than a loose one: a
loose net over its 31,000 records returned 1,800 hits that were overwhelmingly ordinary
first names (John 397, James 214, Michael 113), too many to read and too few of them
real.

## What was looked for, and what was left out

Left out deliberately:

- **General-knowledge question books.** "The question book" was a whole genre of
  nineteenth-century schoolroom cramming — arithmetic, geography, teacher certification.
  Most of what the phrase returns is not about the Bible at all.

**How copyright was checked.** Two independent checks, not one. **Where the licence does not settle it.** There is no licence here to interpret: these works are out of copyright and the Internet Archive adds no terms of its own to a public-domain scan. **Quality note.** This is machine-read text off nineteenth-century paper, and that is the thing to understand before using any of it. Three shapes of question book appear — question-and-answer pairs, numbered questions, and bare interrogatives — and each book was tried against all three with the best result kept; the per-book table records which shape won and what share of candidates survived the quality gate. That gate is deliberately strict: a candidate must open with an interrogative or imperative, end in a question mark, run 20 to 400 characters, carry no character that does not belong in English prose, and contain at most two stray single letters. Across the batch it threw out about a third of what the patterns matched. The commonest fault the scanner makes is reading a question mark as a 1, 7, l, I, P or ^, which welds two questions into one unusable record; those are put back where the surrounding shape is unmistakable, and the count of recoveries is recorded per book. **How copyright was checked.** Project Gutenberg's whole premise is that it distributes only works in the US public domain, and each ebook's RDF record was saved alongside its text. Publication dates run 1692 to 1914, all comfortably before 1929. One candidate failed this test and was dropped: ebook 1670, *Luther's Little Instruction Book*, is a 1994 translation of a 1529 catechism — the underlying work is ancient but that English rendering of it is modern, and it is not what the brief asked for.

**Where the licence does not settle it.** Project Gutenberg asks that its trademark not be used once its boilerplate is stripped, which is exactly what the extraction does — the text is cut at the START and END markers before anything is parsed, so no Gutenberg licence text reaches the extraction. So credit the book, not Project Gutenberg. This is the same conclusion job 412 reached for the Baltimore Catechism, and it is applied here for consistency. Readers outside the United States should check their own country's term of copyright, as the Gutenberg header warns.

**Format.** Per book: the plain-text transcription (`pg<n>.txt`) and the Gutenberg RDF metadata record (`pg<n>-metadata.rdf`), matching the naming job 412 used for the Baltimore Catechism. Each book has its own small parser, because a transcription has a reliable printed shape that a scan does not.

**Quality note.** Far cleaner than the scans, and the reason this source is worth having despite being a fifteenth the size: these are human transcriptions, so the text needs no proofreading pass, and 4,994 of the 6,125 questions arrive with the answer the book printed. Two books stand out. **Hurlbut's Bible Lessons for Boys and Girls** (1903) is the closest thing in the whole corpus to what the brief described — story-comprehension questions on the Bible narrative, grouped by lesson, each with its answer. **Bible Readings for the Home Circle** (1914) answers every one of its 2,520 questions with a quoted scripture and its reference, which is where 1,901 of this batch's references come from; its doctrinal slant is Seventh-day Adventist and shows in which topics it chooses to cover. Two transcription quirks were dealt with and are worth knowing about. Hurlbut's printed book marks every proper name for pronunciation and the transcription carries those marks as bracketed codes wrapped around the letters themselves, so deleting the brackets deletes the vowels with them; the letter inside each code is kept and the stress marks and syllable hyphens dropped, which turns `J[=e]'sus` back into `Jesus`. About 75 records of 6,125 still carry a stray transcription marker. Three of the eight books print their review questions without answers beside them, the answers being in the exposition above; those 1,127 records have a question and no answer, which is how the book published them.
