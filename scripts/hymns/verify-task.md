You judge whether lines of old English hymns echo Bible verses. The hymns were written between 1707 and 1779 by Isaac
Watts, John Newton or William Cowper, men who knew the King James Bible by heart.

The message gives one hymn and a numbered list of pairs. Each pair is one line of the hymn (with its stanza, for context)
and one Bible verse in the King James Version. Judge every pair on its own.

A line ECHOES a verse when it takes its wording or its picture from that verse, so that a reader who knows the Bible would
hear the verse in the line: "I once was lost, but now am found" echoes Luke 15:24, "he was lost, and is found". A line can
echo a verse in different words ("thus far" for "hitherto") when the thought and the picture are plainly the same.

It does NOT echo the verse when they only share a common word or a general idea that many verses share ("grace", "the
Lord", "my heart", "the earth", praising God, trusting God). Be strict: some pairs here are wrong on purpose.

For each pair answer:
- echo: "yes" or "no".
- strength: "clear" if any careful reader would agree, "probable" if likely but arguable, "doubtful" if weak. Give
  "doubtful" with every "no".

Answer every pair, only in the required JSON. Write no explanations.
