# Cities through their layers: research and design record

Local expansion, 14 October 2026. No production deployment is authorized by this request.

## Intellectual structure

The opening asks how a deposit forms before asking which event it represents. Readers manipulate a hypothetical sequence of floor, burn deposit, pit cut, fill and rebuilding. The key inference is that vertical depth alone cannot date an object. This prepares them to inspect the four retained cities rather than immediately identify a conquest.

Each city now has a substantial investigation beside its existing evidence and biblical passages. Jericho separates numerical measurement from calendar calibration and historical agency. Lachish follows the relationship between a floor, a wall and dated organic material. Jerusalem introduces the problem of dense urban rebuilding and the limits of access to a recent chronological model. Corinth challenges the equation between destruction and abandonment. These four investigations answer different questions and do not award artificial confidence scores.

The source trail links foundational recording practice to reanalysis, renewed fieldwork and laboratory methods. Kenyon, Tufnell and Ussishkin are important reading destinations but are explicitly marked as bibliographic leads, not books read in full for this expansion. The structured claim ledger is `content/academic-research/08-cities-through-layers.json`; page source records and access statements are in `src/pages/study/history/cities-research.ts`.

## Evidence corrections and distinctions

- Bruins and van der Plicht's abstract uses “18 samples”; their methods identify 16 original samples and 18 determinations because one charcoal sample was split into three fractions. The source annotation now makes that distinction. Six cereal values and laboratory errors were transcribed from Table 1. The plotted axis is radiocarbon years BP, not BCE; no conversion by subtraction is performed.
- The two calendar intervals quoted are the paper's specific 1993-curve OxCal, one-sigma option. They are not newly calculated, not the entire paper's calibration discussion, and not modern IntCal20 results. No calendar probability curve is fabricated.
- The 2019 Lachish report's Floor C308 relationship is distinguished from the authors' later-tenth-century construction and state-formation inference. Its Figure 4 treatment of an older pit makes sample association visible. This is not presented as a complete adjudication of Iron Age chronology.
- The 2024 Jerusalem study's accessible abstract reports 103 determinations and five areas. Indexed summaries elsewhere sometimes use four; the page follows the article abstract. Supplementary models were not accessible and no fine-grained chronology was extracted from them.
- Romano's Hesperia title, author, volume, year and pages were verified through the institutional catalogue. Only the indexed discussion on p. 62 was accessible. The lesson is limited to evidence of activity, without a population estimate or a reproduced catalogue.

## Traditions and interpretation

The bibliography includes British excavation records, Italian–Palestinian fieldwork and conservation, Israeli-led radiocarbon projects with international collaboration, and Greek archaeology documented by ASCSA. These are research contexts, not blocs assumed to hold a uniform position. The living city, preservation, access, excavation location and selection of monumental remains all affect the sample available to historians.

Biblical texts are compared at their relevant scale: Joshua 6 and a proposed destruction association; 2 Kings 18 and Lachish's Assyrian setting; Jeremiah 34 and the later crisis; Mark 13 and Jerusalem's destruction; Acts 18 and a Roman civic setting. These literary relationships are retained from the first study. Laboratory chronology cannot identify an attacker or resolve a theological reading by itself. Josephus-specific material remains excluded from this general study.
