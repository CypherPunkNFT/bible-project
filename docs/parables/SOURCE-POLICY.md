# Parables source and interpretation policy

Established 11 October 2026 for the Parables of Christ revision. This completes implementation-plan step 1. Steps 2 through 10 require their own user authorization.

The studies explain Scripture for Christian readers. The biblical passage governs the exposition; commentary supplies accountable assistance. An author's academic standing, denomination, popularity, or agreement with the editor does not make an interpretation correct.

## Source roles

| Role | Appropriate use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scripture | Establish wording, narrative sequence, audience where stated, and explicit explanations | Distinguish the translation from interpretation; do not silently merge Gospel settings |
| Teaching commentary | Develop an explanation with named, explicitly Christian sources and textual reasons | Eligibility is not blanket endorsement of an author or every conclusion |
| Historical commentary | Show established Christian readings and their reasoning | Age and public-domain status do not establish historical or linguistic accuracy |
| Comparative scholarship | Identify a disputed reading, literary form, or linguistic question | Attribute the view; do not introduce it as the project's settled teaching |
| Inventory aid | Locate passages and compare counting conventions | A chart is neither a complete canon of parables nor an authority for equating stories |

The initial teaching-source pool includes historic Protestant exposition and contemporary evangelical exposition. The register states the relevant Reformed, Anglican, Lutheran, or other perspective. This is a working source selection, not adoption of a new denominational confession for the project. Where a doctrinal position has not been established by the owner, do not invent one or describe one tradition's conclusion as the unanimous Christian position.

## Editorial rules

1. Read the whole passage and its immediate setting before consulting commentary. Use the site's registered KJV text for displayed KJV wording and links. Record edition differences when they materially affect the explanation. Do not assume that a modern translation or an older commentary resolves a disputed Greek term by itself.
2. Keep three kinds of statement distinguishable: **observation** (what the passage says), **interpretation** (what the detail means and why), and **application** (how a reader might respond). An application must not become an extra command attributed to Jesus.
3. Identify the source at the claim it supports. The internal record must contain the author or responsible institution, work, edition/date where available, exact passage or section, URL/local location, role, and date checked. A general bibliography does not substantiate a particular historical claim.
4. Verify church affiliation from a public biography or institutional record. Separate personal affiliation from a publisher's statement of faith. Mark unknown affiliations as unknown. Describe interpretive tendencies as editorial assessments when they are not self-descriptions. Do not infer a person's salvation or sincerity from a label.
5. For a consequential disputed interpretation, consult at least two substantive treatments with independently stated reasoning, rather than two websites repeating one source. Explain the best-supported reading and a material alternative fairly. Do not manufacture disagreement over an explicit textual statement, or give every speculative view equal weight.
6. Keep the argument local before drawing wider biblical connections. A plausible allusion is not an explicit quotation. Theological synthesis must account for both the passage and the relevant wider texts; neither may be dismissed to force an easy resolution.
7. Explain difficult features rather than replacing them with a generic warning. Historical customs, monetary equivalents, word meanings, and reconstructed motives require evidence. If uncertain, qualify or omit them. Old commentaries must be checked for outdated scholarship and unsupported cultural claims.
8. Avoid unrestricted allegory, but do not prohibit symbolic correspondences that Jesus himself supplies. Identify the main comparison without assuming that every parable must have exactly one point or exactly three narrative moments.
9. Treat each Gospel's framing as meaningful. A shared image does not establish one historical occasion. Distinguish parallels, repeated imagery, and different stories. Never use a chart's row alignment as the sole reason to merge passages.
10. Write original explanations and cite the intellectual contribution. Public access is not republication permission. Preserve edition and rights metadata for held texts; link to modern commentary and summarize within permitted limits rather than copying it into the study.
11. Describe verification precisely. A verse-existence check establishes reference integrity, not exegetical correctness, scholarly consensus, inventory completeness, or editorial approval.

## Decisions from the existing bibliography review

- **Greg Carey:** retain the audit citation in the research trail, identified as a UCC author. His Matthew 25 article is not the teaching basis for this revision. It may document an attributed alternative only when that comparison is useful. The prior phrase "progressive Protestant" is an editorial assessment supported by his public positions, not a judgment that he is not Christian.
- **Arland Hultgren:** retain the specific article for the limited counting/definition question. Do not extend that citation into approval of all his theological conclusions.
- **R. C. Trench:** retain as a historical commentator and inventory comparator. Coverage of the thirty chapters in the held edition does not establish a complete inventory of Jesus' figurative teaching.
- **Crossway's chart:** retain only as a secondary comparison aid. Its placement of Mark 4:26-29 in the weeds row must not be adopted. The Gospel texts remain decisive.
- **Other audit sources:** preserve their actual role and limitations in the register. A source consulted while finding a problem is not automatically a recommended teaching source.

## Claim review record for later steps

For each substantive revision record: study and passage; proposed wording; observation/interpretation/application; supporting verses; named commentary and section; significant alternative and reason for the adopted reading; confidence or unresolved limit; reviewer/date; and whether it has reached the user-approved implementation step.

An entry is ready when its references are accurate, its explanation accounts for the text, its consequential interpretations have traceable support, and its applications do not overstate that support. The owner retains control over consequential project-wide doctrinal decisions.
