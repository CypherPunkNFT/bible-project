# Parables inventory reconciliation

11 October 2026. Implementation-plan step 2, authorized by the owner. Local revision only. The inventory is now 57 studies, up from 50, in the existing seven categories. The selector remains All studies plus seven categories, four columns by two rows on desktop. Steps 3–10 are not implemented by this review.

## What the number means

This is a curated Synoptic teaching collection, not a universal count of the parables of Jesus. No single catalogue settles the boundaries. The inclusion policy retains the existing narrative parables and compact comparisons, fills seven clear omissions within that broad scope, and labels related figurative teaching explicitly. It does not turn every metaphor or proverbial sentence into another study. This boundary is an editorial selection, not a claim that the omitted sayings cannot be called parabolic.

Every entry has one required form, independent of its browsing category:

- **Narrative parable:** a particular narrated situation with characters and unfolding action. Length alone does not decide this: the two debtors remains narrative.
- **Brief comparison:** a compact scene, general observation or hypothetical example. Includes mustard, leaven, treasure, pearl, net and growing seed, as well as images such as the builders and the watchful servants. Some have narrative features; this working label describes their presentation here, not scholarly unanimity.
- **Related figurative teaching:** the returning spirit and the sheep-and-goats judgment scene. These belong in the collection but should not silently swell a count of ordinary narrative parables. The label does not imply that the realities described are merely fictional.

## Evidence and reconciliation

Primary evidence: the site's registered KJV corpus, `data/plain/kjv/MAT.json`, `MRK.json`, `LUK.json`. The newly included ranges and their immediate settings were read directly. The automated checker verifies verse existence only; it cannot certify interpretation or completeness.

Inventory comparisons, checked 11 October 2026:

1. R. C. Trench, *Notes on the Parables of Our Lord*, held 1847 edition, contents: [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/notesonparableso00tren). All thirty chapter subjects remain represented. Useful historic baseline, not an exhaustive inventory of short comparisons.
2. Crossway, [The Parables of Jesus](https://www.esv.org/resources/esv-global-study-bible/chart-40-03/), ESV Global Study Bible chart. All story subjects have a home in the existing collection. Do not adopt its placement of Mark 4:26–29 beside the weeds, or collapse the talents/minas and distinct banquets because they share a row.
3. Saint Mary's Press, [The Parables of Jesus](https://www.smp.org/dynamicmedia/files/b447f1dd59ed132f17cbd7a90cbae786/TX004664_2-F-The_Parables_of_Jesus.pdf), 2015 handout. Its broader list includes salt, the fasting exchange and sheep/goats; its lamp range also includes Mark's measure saying. The divided kingdom, eye and good gifts were identified by reading the Gospel comparisons themselves, not supplied by this chart. Its inclusion of the historical trial scene in Mark 15:6–15 does not make that event a parable; inconsistent headings and repeated subjects are not adopted.
4. Arland J. Hultgren, [Preaching the Parables, Part One](https://www.workingpreacher.org/theology-and-interpretation/preaching-the-parables-part-one), 27 April 2009. Used only for the distinction between stories and comparisons and the absence of an agreed total. The project's three labels are editorial; they are not attributed wholesale to Hultgren.

The new notes primarily describe what is visible in these passages. No historical reconstruction or lexical claim is needed to establish their inclusion. Sheep/goats deliberately leaves the disputed identity of the brethren and the works/salvation relationship for the authorized deeper treatment in step 4. This is not a final certification of its exposition. Douglas Sean O'Donnell's [Matthew commentary, “The Parable of the Sheep and the Goats”](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/matthew/) was checked for its recognition of the mixed form; the adopted label describes form only. The commentary's substantive doctrinal conclusions have not been silently installed.

## Grouping and boundary decisions

| Candidate or grouping | Decision and reason |
| --- | --- |
| Talents / minas | Keep separate: different story details and settings. |
| Royal wedding / great supper / lowest place | Keep three: invitation, judgment and taking seats are distinct units. |
| Patch / wineskins / bridegroom | Keep three connected comparisons, each with its own image and conclusion. |
| Tower / king considering war | Keep separate comparisons in one discipleship discourse. |
| Lamp / eye | Keep separate: making light visible and receiving light are different images. Luke 11:33 remains a parallel-coverage task for step 5, not an extra study. |
| Sower / growing seed / weeds | Keep three; harvesting language does not make them the same story. |
| Lost sheep in Matthew and Luke | Keep a comparison study with distinct settings; does not assert one occasion. |
| Returning spirit (Matthew 12:43–45; Luke 11:24–26) | Retain, explicitly labelled related figurative teaching. |
| Two masters (Matthew 6:24; Luke 16:13) | Candidate for a later study, not counted here; a compact figurative maxim, not a missing variant of shrewd manager. |
| Narrow gate/door (Matthew 7:13–14; Luke 13:24–30) | Candidate for later treatment, not counted here. Distinct from the current doorkeeper study; a broader collection could reasonably include it. |
| Dogs and pearls (Matthew 7:6) | Not counted: concentrated metaphorical admonition; does not belong to the merchant's pearl story. |
| Physician (Matthew 9:12–13; Mark 2:17; Luke 5:31–32) | Not counted: figurative saying in a call/meal scene, not a separate narrative parable. |
| Harvest labourers (Matthew 9:37–38; Luke 10:2) | Not counted: mission metaphor, distinct from the vineyard wage story. |
| Hen and chicks (Matthew 23:37; Luke 13:34) | Not counted: comparison within lament; reasonable future figurative-teaching candidate. |
| Weather signs (Matthew 16:2–3; Luke 12:54–56) | Not counted: proverbial observation in a rebuke; not the budding fig comparison. |
| Birds/lilies (Matthew 6:25–34; Luke 12:22–31), lightning/carcass (Matthew 24:27–28; Luke 17:24, 37) | Not counted as independent studies: examples and images within larger discourses. |
| City on a hill (Matthew 5:14) | Neighbouring light imagery, not counted again as a standalone guide. |
| John 10 and 15 | Outside this Synoptic collection; Scripture links remain visible in the scope note. |
| Mark 15:6–15 | Historical Passion narrative, not an invented comparison; exclude the handout's misclassification. |

The page is candid that these uncounted images can support further studies. Fifty-seven is the reviewed selection, not a claim to have exhausted every defensible broad definition. No eighth category is needed: the additions fit the existing thematic homes. Multi-theme relationships remain possible without duplicate counting.

## Remaining approved-plan boundaries

All existing entries are classified, but their full audiences, settings, interpretations and applications still require step 3. Difficult theological treatments belong to step 4. Missing parallels and account-specific presentation belong to step 5. Navigation, search, typography and overall editorial flow remain for their own steps. No publication or design acceptance is implied by passing automated checks.
