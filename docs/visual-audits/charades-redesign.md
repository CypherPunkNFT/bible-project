# Redesign: Family charades (/games/charades)

**Written:** 11 November 2026, from the audit [charades.md](charades.md) and the shared brief [REDESIGN-BRIEF.md](REDESIGN-BRIEF.md). Before-screenshots are in [charades/](charades/), after-screenshots in [charades/after/](charades/after/), named by the audit's stage numbers at both sizes (a name ending in `-light` is the light theme). My step's own brief and the shared brief did not disagree. My brief adds one thing: the phone-on-the-forehead controls, tilt with its button fallback, must stay intact, and the game's sound file must stay as it is. They do: the tilt reading, the one-second sensor check, the button and keyboard paths and the sound tones are untouched, and the headless browser used for the screenshots has no tilt sensor, so every round below was played with the buttons, as the audit's were.

## 1. What changed

Family charades now looks like a game of cards held up to a room. The start page opens on the game itself: beside the title sits a tilted scene card in the game's deep red, with the edges of two more cards behind it, and its title changes slowly from Noah building the ark to David and Goliath to Jonah and the great fish to Daniel in the lions' den. Start sits right under it, on the first screen of a phone, and the deck choices follow; controls, sound, pictures and teams are folded behind one "More options" row that says the current choices in words. In the round the scene is a real card: a rounded, tinted card with a red edge, the deck's name in small capitals at the top, a row of green ticks growing along the top as scenes are got, and "9 cards left" at the bottom, with the edges of the cards still to come showing behind it so the room can feel the deck shrinking. Across the top a thin red bar drains with the clock, the seconds beside it twice as large as before, turning the pass orange for the last ten seconds and pulsing for the last five; the score sits large and green at the other end and pops each time it grows, with a "+1" floating off it. Got it throws the card down and off with a green tick stamped on it, Pass throws it up with an orange arrow, while the whole screen floods green or orange as before and the next card slides in from the far side; the two buttons turn into white outlines on the flood instead of going muddy. The countdown digits land large and settle; "Time's up!" lands with the round's scenes under it. The recap counts the score up from zero beside a bar measured against this device's best, with a "New best" ribbon and one gold burst when it is one, and Play again sits beside the score rather than under the list; the marks turn over when tapped. Teams get a colour each: the hand-over is a "You're up" card with the team's name very large in its colour and every team as a chip with a bar against the leader, and the final standings rise as a podium, third, second, first, with a gold burst for the winner and the table underneath for ties. Every button, chip, mark and link shows focus with the same lift and edge as hover, and nothing draws a focus outline. The rules, the scenes, the scoring, the saved settings and best scores, the saved team game, the tilt controls and their button fallback, the sound and the address are untouched.

## 2. The audit's list, item by item

1. **Make the timer something the room can see.** Built as asked, with the kit's timer bar. A thin bar across the top of the card area drains from full to empty in the game's red, turns the pass orange under ten seconds and pulses with the digits for the last five. The seconds are drawn large beside it (about twice their old size on a phone, larger still on a desktop), and the bar lives inside the part of the screen that turns with the phone, so it stays upright for the room when the phone's rotation is locked. The game keeps its own clock exactly as before; the kit only draws it.
2. **Draw the card.** Built as asked. The scene sits on a rounded card tinted with the game's red at a soft mix, with a two-pixel red edge, the deck's name in small capitals at the top and the cards left at the bottom. The text sizing is exactly as it was (as large as fits in three lines, never below 28 pixels). On an answer the card slides off in the direction of the tilt, down for got it with a green tick stamped on its corner, up for pass with an orange arrow, while the flood colour shows behind it, and the next card slides in from the far side. The audit's "card 4 of 12" became "9 cards left": the deck brings passed cards back and skips a second scene from the same story, so a fixed total would have been wrong; the count follows the dealer's own rule and is tested. Under reduced motion the leaving card is simply gone and the next is simply there.
3. **Let the score react.** Built as asked. The score pops up a step and settles each time it grows, a "+1" floats off it, and a row of green ticks along the top of the card counts the scenes got this round (the last twelve, then a sum). On the recap the "3 correct" counts up from zero over just over half a second, with a bar under it measured against this device's best (the best marked on the bar), a "New best" ribbon and one gold-and-cream burst when the round is a new best.
4. **Fix the flood's buttons.** Built as asked. During the Correct and Pass flood the two buttons become white outlines with white words on the colour, and so do the Pause and Hide pills, the seconds and the score.
5. **Give the countdown and "Time's up!" a beat.** Built as asked. Each digit lands at 1.7 times its size and settles over its second, in the game's red; "Time's up!" (or "You got through the whole deck!") lands the same way with the round's scenes ("4 scenes") rising under it.
6. **Show the game on the start screen and shorten the form.** Built as asked. The hub drawing is replaced by the tilted sample card with two card edges behind it, which cycles slowly through four well-known scenes (and stops while a round is up or when motion is reduced). Start and the best score moved up under the two mode cards, so they sit on the first phone screen; the deck chips follow, and controls, sound, pictures and teams fold behind the kit's "More options" row, whose summary names the current choices ("Tilt and buttons · Sound on · Pictures off · Teams off"). The fold opens by itself when a saved team game brings teams back on, so the names are never hidden. Everything chosen is remembered as before.
7. **Make the team screens feel like turns.** Built as asked, with the kit's team pieces. Each team name on the setup carries its colour dot (the site's book colours in turn). The hand-over is the kit's "You're up" card with the team's name very large in its colour, the Ready button inside it, and every team as a chip with its score and a bar against the leader, the team on turn lifted. The final standings rise as a podium, third, second, first, the winner's step in gold with one short burst of confetti, the totals counting up; the table underneath keeps every team and every tie exactly as the audit saw them.

**My own additions.** The deck behind the card: the edges of the cards still to come show behind the live card and thin out as the deck runs down (two edges, then one, then none). The sample card on the start screen cycling through real scenes with a slow sway, the one living detail on the page. The "+1" that floats off the score. A small breathing line drawing of a face with the phone on its forehead (or beside it, for Act it) on the get-ready screen, in the game's red.

## 3. Faults fixed

From "Anything broken":

- **The ready-screen notice blamed the player for a refusal they never made.** The cause was a little different from the audit's guess: on this browser the motion-permission call does not fail, it answers with the word "prompt" (it never asked anyone anything), and the game read every answer other than "granted" as "denied". Now only a real "denied" is a refusal. Any other answer, or a call that throws, is treated as unproven: the game waits its usual second for a reading, and if none comes it says "Tilt isn't available here, so use the buttons", without switching the device to Buttons only next time, because nothing was learned about the sensor. A real "denied" still gets the old wording, and a device that answered "granted" but sends nothing still gets "This device isn't sending tilt here". Tested for both the "prompt" answer and a thrown error.
- **A failed scenes load shows the site's generic screen.** Not changed. The scenes ship inside the game's code chunk, so the site's chunk-failure screen reaches the reader first; the game's own Try again path is kept for the case where the data file is served separately. Changing the way the data is bundled is outside a look-and-feel redesign.
- **Keyboard focus was invisible.** The game's own outline rule (which the site already overrode) is gone, and every control shows focus the way it shows hover: chips, mode cards, marks, pills and the Pause and Hide tools lift two pixels and take a red edge; Start and the primary buttons lift with a shadow; links underline; the team-name inputs take their team's colour on the edge.

From "Phone problems":

- **Start was far down the page.** Start sits under the hero and the two mode cards, on the first screen at 390 wide; the sample card is small beside the title at phone width and the mode cards sit side by side.
- **The timer and score were small for a forehead game.** The seconds are about twice their size on the phone and the score is large and green beside them, both in the row that turns with the card.
- **Chips were 36 pixels tall; the pause pill 40 by 31.** Every chip, the Pause and Hide tools, the recap marks, the team-name inputs, the fold rows and every pill are at least 44 pixels tall; the answer bars are at least 48 pixels with tilt and 64 without, as before.
- **The notice on the ready screen came first and pushed Start round down.** The instruction comes first, then Start round, and the notice is a small muted line under them.
- The audit's "done well" list is kept: the full-width answer bars, the large card text, the full-screen flood, the recap rows that fit, and the team screens without sideways scrolling.

From "Accessibility": the chip legends are a little larger; the flood still carries a symbol and a word; the timer bar's colour change is paired with the pulsing digits and the tick; the round layer, the spoken timer and score, the polite feedback and the focused headings are as they were.

## 5. Checks

- **Build** (`npm run build`): passed; the preview served the new build for every screenshot.
- **Play-through on the preview with Playwright** at 1440 and 390, dark and light, from a fresh browser: the start screen, Act it, teams on, How to play, get ready, the countdown, the first card, Got it, Pass, the keyboard arrows, Pause and Resume, the last ten and five seconds, Time's up, the recap, a mark fixed, Act it with the picture, Hide card, a two-team game through the hand-over, a reload at the hand-over, the final standings, and Tab focus on the start screen. The browser console showed no errors in any run.
- **Kit change:** the score count-up now starts from the first frame's own clock when that clock runs behind the page clock (the headless test browser's does), so a recap counted in a test no longer sticks at zero; every existing kit test passes unchanged.
