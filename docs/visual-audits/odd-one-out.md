# Visual audit: Odd one out (/games/odd-one-out)

Audited 11 November 2026 on the local preview, which was a build newer than the last change to the game's source. I played a whole round of ten from a cleared browser at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): the start screen and its chips, a right answer, a wrong answer, an answer by number key and Next by Enter, a second tap after the answer locked, the middle of the round, the last puzzle and See results, the summary with a row opened, Play again, a reload mid-round, leaving for All games and coming back, the start screen with a best score, a Mixed round, a Places Hard round and the light theme. Follow-up checks covered a 320-wide phone, a phone turned sideways, a failed load, the moment just after answering, and where the screen is left after each Next. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [odd-one-out/](odd-one-out/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size; numbers 1 to 33 are the same stages at both sizes (the desktop has one extra hover shot at 6, so its numbers run one ahead from there), and 41 and up are the follow-up checks.

My step's own brief said only "Audit odd one out", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

The puzzle itself is sound and the explanation after each answer is the best thing in the game. But the whole thing is a short form sitting on an ordinary site page, and the page gets in the way of playing it.

The start screen is a title, one line of lede, two rows of pill chips (People, Places, Books, Mixed; Easy, Medium, Hard) and a gold Play pill. That is all, so on the desktop the game fills the top third of the screen and the site footer fills the rest ([desktop-01](odd-one-out/desktop-01-start.png), [phone-01](odd-one-out/phone-01-start.png)). Choosing Books greys out Medium and Hard with no visible reason; the reason is a hover tooltip that a phone cannot see ([desktop-03](odd-one-out/desktop-03-start-books-levels-disabled.png)).

The puzzle is a small caps line ("1 OF 10 · PEOPLE · SCORE 0"), the prompt "Which one doesn't belong?" in the site's serif, and four rectangles in a two-by-two grid, each with a tiny grey number and a name in serif. The rectangles are 83 pixels tall with a one-pixel border, and the grid is 178 pixels tall on a 900-pixel screen, so the footer shows under it at both sizes ([desktop-05](odd-one-out/desktop-05-puzzle-1.png), [phone-05](odd-one-out/phone-05-puzzle-1.png)). Hovering turns a border gold; pressing shrinks the card by two per cent; there is no other motion anywhere in the game, and nothing on the screen says "card" rather than "option".

Tapping an answer draws the result in place: the odd one gets a two-pixel lavender border and "✓ Odd one out", a wrong pick gets a dashed red border and "✗ Your answer", and the other cards fade. Because the mark is added inside the card, the card grows from 83 to 98 pixels and its row jumps ([desktop-07](odd-one-out/desktop-07-puzzle-1-right.png), [phone-41](odd-one-out/phone-41-wrong-instant-before-verse.png)). Under a rule comes the reveal: a tick or cross with "Right!" or "Not quite: the odd one out was Paul", the one-sentence reason, a line for each of the four with a short brief and a verse link, the key verse in a quote box, an "Also true" line, then Next. This is a lovely block of content, 600 to 840 pixels of it, and on both sizes the Next button is below the bottom of the screen: 700 pixels down on the desktop and about 850 on the phone, a screen and a half ([desktop-08](odd-one-out/desktop-08-puzzle-1-right-fullpage.png), [phone-07](odd-one-out/phone-07-puzzle-1-right-fullpage.png), [phone-08](odd-one-out/phone-08-puzzle-1-right-reveal-bottom.png)).

Pressing Next is where the game breaks down. The reveal vanishes, the page shrinks, and the browser keeps its scroll position, so the new puzzle appears above the top of the screen. On the desktop the prompt and the top row of cards are hidden under the header and only the bottom two cards show ([desktop-10](odd-one-out/desktop-10-puzzle-2-after-next.png)). On the phone the whole puzzle is 500 to 700 pixels above the screen and what the player sees is the site footer: "Spend time in the Word", "Follow your questions" ([phone-09](odd-one-out/phone-09-puzzle-2-after-next.png), [phone-47](odd-one-out/phone-47-puzzle-5-as-seen-after-next.png)). Every one of the ten puzzles on a phone goes: scroll down a screen and a half to Next, tap it, scroll back up a screen to find the next puzzle. Play again does the same, landing on the bottom of the footer ([phone-23](odd-one-out/phone-23-play-again-puzzle-1.png)).

The wrong-answer heading has a layout fault of its own. It is laid out as a row of three parts (cross, sentence, name), so on the phone the sentence wraps to two lines and the name is pushed to the right edge on its own, reading like a table ([phone-10](odd-one-out/phone-10-puzzle-2-wrong.png), [phone-42](odd-one-out/phone-42-wrong-after-verse.png)); on the desktop it shows as a double space before the name ([desktop-11](odd-one-out/desktop-11-puzzle-2-wrong.png)).

The summary is a heading "You scored 7 of 10", one muted line ("Best streak: 3 · Your best for People, Easy: 7 of 10") and ten bordered rows, each a tick or cross, the four names joined by dots and the reason sentence; a row opens to show the full reveal again ([desktop-20](odd-one-out/desktop-20-summary.png), [desktop-22](odd-one-out/desktop-22-summary-row-2-expanded.png), [phone-19](odd-one-out/phone-19-summary.png)). Play again and Change game sit under the ten rows, below the fold on the desktop and a screen and a half down on the phone ([phone-22](odd-one-out/phone-22-summary-buttons.png)). It is a tidy answer sheet; nothing counts up, nothing is celebrated, and the best score is a clause in a sentence.

Underneath it is solid. Number keys 1 to 4 answer and Enter moves on; a second tap after the answer does nothing ([desktop-14](odd-one-out/desktop-14-puzzle-3-right-via-key.png)); a reload mid-round comes back on the same puzzle with the answer still shown ([desktop-25](odd-one-out/desktop-25-reload-mid-round.png)); leaving for All games and coming back resumes ([desktop-26](odd-one-out/desktop-26-back-from-all-games.png)); the start screen remembers the best ([desktop-27](odd-one-out/desktop-27-start-with-best.png)); Mixed rounds show the kind in a pill ([desktop-29](odd-one-out/desktop-29-mixed-puzzle-1.png)); long names like Beth-jeshimoth wrap rather than cut ([phone-29](odd-one-out/phone-29-places-hard-puzzle-1.png)); at 320 wide the cards go to one column ([phone-43](odd-one-out/phone-43-narrow-320-puzzle.png)); a failed load gives a clear message and Try again ([phone-46](odd-one-out/phone-46-cant-load.png)); the light theme is clean ([desktop-33](odd-one-out/desktop-33-light-puzzle.png)). The console was clean throughout.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The four choices are a radio group.** Four thin-bordered rectangles with a number in the corner, the same shape as the site's chips grown large. Nothing is dealt, nothing is turned over, nothing is picked up ([desktop-05](odd-one-out/desktop-05-puzzle-1.png)).
- **Answering edits the form.** The mark is a line of small text added inside the rectangle and a border change. Right is lavender, wrong is dashed red, the rest fade; no stamp, no shake, no lift ([desktop-07](odd-one-out/desktop-07-puzzle-1-right.png), [desktop-11](odd-one-out/desktop-11-puzzle-2-wrong.png)).
- **Progress is a line of small caps.** "3 OF 10" and "SCORE 2" are labels, not a track; nothing fills in as you go ([desktop-15](odd-one-out/desktop-15-puzzle-6-mid-play.png)).
- **The reveal is an article.** Rule, heading, paragraph, list, quote, Next. Good reading, but every puzzle ends with a scroll through it to reach Next ([phone-07](odd-one-out/phone-07-puzzle-1-right-fullpage.png)).
- **The round ends with a list.** Ten rows and two buttons. No strip of results, no count-up, no moment for a best ([desktop-21](odd-one-out/desktop-21-summary-fullpage.png)).
- **The game lives on a web page.** The footer is on screen during play at both sizes, and after each Next it is the only thing on a phone screen ([phone-05](odd-one-out/phone-05-puzzle-1.png), [phone-09](odd-one-out/phone-09-puzzle-2-after-next.png)).
- **The start is settings.** Two chip rows and a button; no glimpse of a puzzle ([phone-01](odd-one-out/phone-01-start.png)).

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **NYT Connections** (the New York Times, 2023): the sixteen words are tiles on a board; a tap lifts a tile and fills it in, a wrong group shakes the row and costs a dot from a row of four, a right group slides to the top as a coloured bar with the category named on it, and the result is a share-able grid of coloured squares. Shake for wrong, slide-and-name for right, and a row of dots for progress are the three beats this game lacks.
- **Only Connect** (BBC, 2008 to now): the connections round shows four clues one at a time and the payoff is the host reading out the link. Here the reason sentence is that payoff, and it should land like one, not sit under a rule as the first paragraph of an article.
- **Odd One Out** (BBC, Paul Daniels, 1982 to 1985): the four are on the board together, the contestant names the odd one, and the reveal lights it up before the explanation. Lighting one of four is the whole picture.
- **Sesame Street, "One of These Things (Is Not Like the Others)"** (1969 onwards): four things in a two-by-two square, exactly this layout, and the answer is shown by the odd one being circled and the three others bouncing together. Children know this shape; the game can lean on it.
- **Duolingo**: select-the-card questions show large tappable cards, the chosen card lifts and goes green or red with a sound, and the verdict and the Continue button sit in a bar pinned to the bottom of the screen, so the next step never needs scrolling. That pinned bar is the answer to this game's Next problem.
- **Kahoot** and **Trivia Crack**: four big coloured answer cards fill the screen, and a right answer is a full-card flash with a sound before the next question slides in. The next question arriving on a fresh screen is what Next here fails to do.
- **Wordle**: the end screen is a grid of coloured squares that fills in and a share button; a row of ten squares would give this summary its picture.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Keep the puzzle on screen and put Next where the thumb is.** After Next, scroll the page so the prompt is at the top (one call when the puzzle index changes), and on the phone pin a bar to the bottom with the verdict ("✓ Right!" or "✗ Not quite: Paul") and the Next button, so Next is always one tap and the full reveal is there to read if wanted. On the desktop the reveal fits with Next in view if the grid and reveal sit in one panel. This fixes the two worst faults and is the biggest single gain ([phone-09](odd-one-out/phone-09-puzzle-2-after-next.png), [phone-07](odd-one-out/phone-07-puzzle-1-right-fullpage.png)).
2. **Make the four choices cards that react.** Give each a fixed height with the mark's space reserved so nothing jumps; on a right answer lift the chosen card, stamp "ODD ONE OUT" across it and sink the other three together; on a wrong answer shake the chosen card, stamp it with a cross, then lift the true odd one. All in the site's surface colour and radius with its lavender and red; three CSS animations under half a second, none under reduced motion ([desktop-07](odd-one-out/desktop-07-puzzle-1-right.png), [phone-41](odd-one-out/phone-41-wrong-instant-before-verse.png)).
3. **Make the reason land as a reveal, not a paragraph.** Put the headline sentence in large serif directly under the grid, with the odd name in lavender, the moment the cards settle; fold the four lines, the key verse and "Also true" under a "Why" row that opens on tap. The content is already written; this is ordering and one collapsible ([desktop-08](odd-one-out/desktop-08-puzzle-1-right-fullpage.png)).
4. **Give the round a track.** Replace "3 OF 10 · SCORE 2" with ten small squares across the top that fill lavender or red as each puzzle is answered, the kind pill beside them, and the score counting up with a small pop. It is the Wordle grid turned into a progress bar, and it carries straight into the summary ([desktop-15](odd-one-out/desktop-15-puzzle-6-mid-play.png)).
5. **Build the puzzle a stage.** Put progress, prompt, cards and reveal inside one bordered panel in the site's surface colour, centred, with the page padded so the footer sits below the fold while a round is on. On the desktop grow the cards to about 120 pixels tall with the name at 24 pixels, so four names fill the screen the way four tiles fill a Connections board ([desktop-05](odd-one-out/desktop-05-puzzle-1.png)).
6. **Give the summary its picture.** Open with the ten squares filling in left to right, the score counting up under them, "New best" as a ribbon when it is one, and Play again and Change game right under that; the ten rows stay below for anyone who wants to re-read ([desktop-20](odd-one-out/desktop-20-summary.png), [phone-22](odd-one-out/phone-22-summary-buttons.png)).
7. **Show a puzzle on the start screen.** Deal a sample set of four face-up cards beside the title (the game already has a fixed opener), with the chips and Play under it, and a visible line when a level is greyed ("Books: Easy only for now"). Same chips, same button, one picture ([desktop-01](odd-one-out/desktop-01-start.png), [desktop-03](odd-one-out/desktop-03-start-books-levels-disabled.png)).

## Phone problems

- **Every Next leaves the player looking at the footer.** The page stays scrolled by 730 to 900 pixels while the new puzzle is 500 to 700 pixels above the top of the screen; the screen shows "Follow your questions" and "Download the code" ([phone-09](odd-one-out/phone-09-puzzle-2-after-next.png), [phone-47](odd-one-out/phone-47-puzzle-5-as-seen-after-next.png)). Play again lands the same way ([phone-23](odd-one-out/phone-23-play-again-puzzle-1.png)).
- **Next is a screen and a half down.** After an answer the Next button sits at 1,130 to 1,300 pixels on an 844-pixel screen ([phone-07](odd-one-out/phone-07-puzzle-1-right-fullpage.png)).
- **The summary's buttons are a screen and a half down**, at 1,383 pixels, under all ten rows ([phone-20](odd-one-out/phone-20-summary-fullpage.png)).
- **The footer takes the bottom half of the screen during play.** The grid ends at 393 pixels and the footer starts at about 500 ([phone-05](odd-one-out/phone-05-puzzle-1.png)).
- **The wrong-answer heading splits.** The name is pushed to the right edge on its own line ([phone-10](odd-one-out/phone-10-puzzle-2-wrong.png)).
- **Sideways, the puzzle starts half hidden**: Play is below the fold on a 390-tall screen, and the puzzle then renders with the prompt under the header ([phone-45](odd-one-out/phone-45-landscape-puzzle.png)).
- Done well: cards are 83 pixels tall and 173 wide, well over a thumb; names are 19 pixels in serif; at 320 wide the grid drops to one column with no sideways scroll ([phone-43](odd-one-out/phone-43-narrow-320-puzzle.png)); long names hyphenate rather than cut ([phone-29](odd-one-out/phone-29-places-hard-puzzle-1.png)); the light theme reads cleanly ([phone-32](odd-one-out/phone-32-light-puzzle.png)).

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus anywhere.** Tab onto a card or the Play button and nothing changes: the site's base stylesheet switches outlines off everywhere, marked as the owner's call of 6 October 2026, so the game's focus never draws ([desktop-13](odd-one-out/desktop-13-puzzle-3-choice-2-focused-via-tab.png), [desktop-04](odd-one-out/desktop-04-start-play-focused.png)).
- **"Right" is lavender.** The odd one and the "✓ Odd one out" mark use the site's Gospels colour, which nothing else on the site reads as "correct"; wrong is red. The tick and the words carry the meaning, so it is readable, but the pair does not say right and wrong at a glance ([desktop-11](odd-one-out/desktop-11-puzzle-2-wrong.png)).
- **The wrong-answer sentence is broken into parts** for layout, so it reads oddly visually, though a screen reader still gets the whole line through the live region.
- **Why a level is greyed is a tooltip only**, so a touch screen or a screen reader never gets the reason ([desktop-03](odd-one-out/desktop-03-start-books-levels-disabled.png)).
- **There is no way to leave a round.** Once Play is pressed the only exits are finishing all ten or leaving the page, and coming back resumes; Change game appears only on the summary.
- Done well: every mark has a symbol, a word and a colour; the result is announced to a screen reader and takes focus; keys 1 to 4 and Enter work and typing in the site's correction form does not trigger them; cards lock after the first answer; the summary icons are labelled Right and Wrong; reduced motion removes the two transitions.

## Anything broken

Nothing crashed, and the console showed nothing. Five things to fix:

- **The page is left scrolled after Next and after Play again**, so the new puzzle is off the top of the screen at both sizes, on a phone entirely ([desktop-10](odd-one-out/desktop-10-puzzle-2-after-next.png), [phone-09](odd-one-out/phone-09-puzzle-2-after-next.png)). The first card is focused without scrolling, which is deliberate, but nothing then brings the puzzle back into view.
- **The wrong-answer heading lays its name out as a separate column** ([phone-10](odd-one-out/phone-10-puzzle-2-wrong.png)): the heading is a flex row and the bold name is its own item, so it is pushed aside with a gap, a double space on the desktop and a right-aligned word on the phone.
- **The grid jumps on answer.** The marked card grows from 83 to 98 pixels and drags its row with it ([phone-41](odd-one-out/phone-41-wrong-instant-before-verse.png)).
- **The "Not enough puzzles" note never shows.** Choosing Books resets the level to Easy before the note can appear, so the greyed levels explain themselves only in a hover tooltip ([desktop-03](odd-one-out/desktop-03-start-books-levels-disabled.png)).
- **The same trio can come round twice in one round, and so can the same answer.** On the phone run Barabbas, Aquila and Apollos appeared with Rahab and then again with the Queen of Sheba, for the same reason; on the desktop run Esther was the odd one out twice ([phone-19](odd-one-out/phone-19-summary.png), [desktop-20](odd-one-out/desktop-20-summary.png)). One phone set also put Anna beside Annas ([phone-22](odd-one-out/phone-22-summary-buttons.png)). These come from the puzzle bank and the round builder, not the screen, but this is where a family meets them.

One content note rather than a fault: several Easy sets rest on "the only woman" or "the only man" (Esther among prophets, Ezekiel among women). The design allows it, but its own research note says surface differences like these never make good headline reasons, and they feel thin next to "was not one of the Twelve".
