# Redesign: Hot seat (/games/hot-seat)

**Written:** 11 November 2026, from the audit [hot-seat.md](hot-seat.md) and the shared brief [REDESIGN-BRIEF.md](REDESIGN-BRIEF.md). Before-screenshots are in [hot-seat/](hot-seat/), after-screenshots in [hot-seat/after/](hot-seat/after/), named by the audit's desktop stage numbers at both sizes (a name ending in `-light` is the light theme). My step's own brief and the shared brief did not disagree. My brief adds one thing: this game is a phone in one hand, one reader, one player and a clock, so the count-down, the marks and the hand-over are the moments to make land, and the question pools and the way questions are dealt must stay as they are. They do: the pools, the deal, the rules, the scoring, the saved game, the records on this phone and the address are untouched, and a game saved earlier tonight carries on.

## 1. What changed

Hot seat now has a pace. Before each turn the chair-and-stopwatch drawing from the start page stands faded behind the player's name like a spotlight, the round line, the name, the reader and the Start button rising in one beat after another. The 3, 2, 1 lands large and settles with a ring breathing out behind each digit. In the turn the question and its answer sit on one card in the middle of the screen, where there used to be a dark gap; tapping Right throws the card off to the right with a green tick stamped on it, Wrong throws it left with a red cross, Pass drops it away with an amber arrow, and the next card, already drawn, settles up at once while "+1" floats out of the score. Three right answers in a row light a small flame beside the score. The clock is a dial in the middle of the top bar, big enough for the room, that turns red and pulses with its digits for the last five seconds; at the buzzer a red "Time!" stamp lands over the dial, so the question never moves while the reader is mid-sentence. The recap opens with the turn as a strip of tiles, green, red and amber, the score counting up and a "New best on this phone" ribbon when it is one; changing a mark flips its tile, and Save turn sits in a band pinned to the bottom of a phone screen. The standings settle in row by row with the totals counting up and a green wash on anyone who climbed, and the next player is a "You're up" card that is the button to the hand-over. The final standings open on a podium that rises third, second, first, with one burst of gold and cream for the winner, the shout-outs as two cards, then the table and the night's questions. The start page is a line-up of player cards, dealt in as they are added, each with a coloured edge for its age group, with the settings folded under one row that says the current choices in words, so Start game sits on the first screen at both sizes. End game asks in the site's own dialog. Every button lifts on hover and on keyboard focus, and nothing draws a focus outline.

## 2. The audit's list, item by item

1. **Make each question a card that is dealt and thrown.** Built as asked. The question, the two choices and the answer band are one card with a surface, a thin edge and rounded corners, centred in the middle of the screen, so the empty gap is gone. On a mark the old card is kept in the layer for a third of a second and slides off right (green tick), left (red cross) or down (amber arrow) with the mark stamped on it; the next card settles up from below at the same instant, since it was drawn by the same tap. The score no longer turns red on Wrong: the card carries the verdict, and "+1" floats out of the score on Right. Under reduced motion the thrown card is already gone (the animation ends at nothing) and the next card is simply there.
2. **Make the clock the drama.** Built as asked. The ring and its digits grew from 70 pixels to about 100 on a phone and 115 on a desktop and moved to the middle of the top bar, between the score and Pause. For the last five seconds the ring and the digits turn red and the digits beat once a second with the tick. At the buzzer a "Time! Finish this question." stamp drops over the dial, tilted, red-edged, and the question stays exactly where it was. The red stub the audit saw at zero is gone: the ring fades out when it is empty.
3. **Stage the hand-over and the count-down.** Built as asked. The game's own drawing (the empty chair under the lamp beside the sixty-second stopwatch) is behind the name, large, faded to a fifth and masked to a soft pool of light; it scales in, and the round line, the name, the reader, the note, Start and the three small buttons rise in at 110 millisecond beats. Each count-down digit lands at one and a half times its size, settles and fades as the next arrives, with a thin ring breathing out behind it; the first card then settles in from below the moment the count ends. Every button works from the first frame; the entrance only decorates it.
4. **Turn the recap into a strip.** Built as asked, with the kit's progress trail. Above the list the turn is a row of tiles, one per question, green for right, red for wrong, amber for a pass and empty for one left unmarked, the newest popping. The score counts up from zero (the real number is there for a screen reader from the first frame) with "points" beside it in the serif, and the "New best on this phone" ribbon pops in with its three tags under it. Tapping a mark flips the badge over and recolours its tile. Save turn is in the kit's feedback band, which is pinned to the bottom of the screen on a phone with the points beside it, so it is never a screen below the list; on a desktop it sits under the list as before.
5. **Give the final standings a podium.** Built as asked, with the kit's podium. Three steps rise into place, third first and the winner last, with the names on them in the page's serif and the totals counting up; a shared place puts two names on one step; one burst of gold and cream confetti flies from the winner's step, once. The three buttons come straight after the podium so they are on the first screen, then the table with a column per round, the two shout-outs as cards with the game's gold edge, and the "All questions tonight" list, which is as it was.
6. **Let the standings move between turns.** Built differently. The rows settle in one after another in their new order, from the top, with the totals counting up, and a row that climbed takes a green wash that fades; the small triangles are kept beside the place for anyone who missed the motion. The audit's idea of rows sliding past each other would need each row to know where it was a moment ago, and with one row changing per turn the settle-in reads the same and stays honest under reduced motion. The next player is the kit's "You're up" card with the name very large and "Next player" inside it; the whole card is the button, and it lifts on hover and focus.
7. **Make the start screen feel like a line-up.** Built as asked. Each player is a small card with a numbered pip, the name field, the group picker and the three small buttons, dealt in with a short stagger when added, with a coloured left edge for its age group (poetry violet for Little ones, gospels for Kids, the game's history brown for Teens and adults, prophets for Bible experts). The site's inputs and chips are kept. The five chip rows and the group legend are folded under one row that reads "Settings" followed by the current choices ("60 seconds · Both · 2 rounds · Sound on"), each in its own element so the translation check can register them. Start game and the time estimate sit above the fold, on the first screen at both sizes.

**My own additions.** The flame beside the score, lit from three right answers in a row and pulsing each time the run grows (the kit's streak flame). The "+1" that floats out of the score on a right answer. The book written out in full in every reference, which the audit listed under "Anything broken" and which the question bank cannot fix on its own: the reference's abbreviation is replaced with the full name from the bank's book list when the link names the book. The End game confirmation in the site's own dialog instead of the browser's grey box. Every button, chip and card showing focus as a lift and a gold edge.

## 3. Faults fixed

From "Anything broken":

- **The buzzer shifted the question.** "Time!" is a stamp over the dial in the top bar; the card does not move.
- **Book names were abbreviated unevenly.** "1 Sam 16:11" is now "1 Samuel 16:11", "Pro 19:12" is "Proverbs 19:12", wherever a reference is shown (the turn, the recap, the night's questions), using the bank's own book list.
- **Players who had not played yet were shown as having dropped.** A player still waiting for a first turn shows no movement; only a player who has sat can climb or drop. Tested.
- **The clock ring left a red dot at zero.** The ring fades out when it is empty.
- **The two-choices question mark floated.** The question mark is attached to the second choice, so it never hangs on a line of its own.

From "Phone problems":

- **Start game was almost two screens down.** The settings are folded, the hero is compact at phone width, and Start game follows the players and the estimate.
- **Save turn was below the fold on the recap.** It is pinned to the bottom of the phone screen in the band.
- **The menu hid End game and How to play.** Resume, How to play and End game are the first row of the sheet, under the title.
- **The final page was long and the third button was cut.** The buttons come straight after the podium and are compact at phone width.
- **Sideways, the turn did not fit.** The layer's padding, the dial, the card and the three buttons are smaller in landscape and the buttons are a column with the words beside the symbols, so Undo is no longer clipped.
- **The keyboard line showed on the phone.** It is hidden on touch screens, on screens without hover and at phone width.

From "Accessibility":

- **No visible keyboard focus.** The game's own focus-outline rule is gone (the site has no outline) and every control shows focus with the same lift and edge as hover: the mark buttons rise and shadow, pills rise and take a gold edge, the next-player card rises.
- **A wrong answer turned the tick red.** It no longer does; the thrown card carries the cross.
- **End game used the browser's confirm box.** It is the site's dialog, with "Keep playing" and "End game".
- **"Carry on tonight's game" was both the heading and the button.** The heading now reads "Tonight's game is saved".

## 6. Left for later

- The standings rows settle into their new order rather than sliding past each other (audit item 6); a true slide would need each row to remember its last position, and the settle-in reads the same for one changed row.
- The question bank's own references are still abbreviated unevenly in its data; the game writes them out from the book list, but any other page that shows the bank's references as they are will still show "1 Sam".
- The end-of-turn and the podium have no sound of their own; the existing ticks, chime, pass note and buzzer are unchanged.
