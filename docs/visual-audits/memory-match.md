# Visual audit: Memory card match (/games/memory-match)

Audited 11 November 2026 on the local preview. I played from a cleared browser at desktop size (1440 wide) and phone size (390 wide): the start screen and its chips, two players' name boxes, "See the pairs first", an Easy board dealt, one card up, a wrong pair and its wait, a right pair, the middle of a board, keyboard focus, a reload mid-board, the last pair, the solo end screen, Play again, Quit, the start screen with a best, Medium and Hard boards, the "When I tap" setting, a two-player game to its end, the light theme and a failed load. On the phone I also checked a 320-wide screen and a phone turned sideways. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [memory-match/](memory-match/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size; the stages are the same at both sizes, except that the desktop has one extra hover shot at 8, so its numbers run one ahead from there.

My step's own brief said only "Audit memory match", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

This is the most game-like game I have seen in this sequence, and the owner should know that first. The board is real cards: striped backs in the site's two dark surfaces, a gold border that is solid for a Who card and dashed for a Story card, a small WHO or STORY pill in the middle, and a true three-dimensional turn of a third of a second when you tap one ([desktop-06](memory-match/desktop-06-board-easy-dealt.png), [desktop-09](memory-match/desktop-09-first-card-mid-flip.png), [phone-06](memory-match/phone-06-board-easy-dealt.png)). On a phone the Easy board fills the screen edge to edge in three columns of four, which looks right ([phone-06](memory-match/phone-06-board-easy-dealt.png)). The face of a card is the name or the story in the site's serif, with a tiny WHO or STORY label at the top. A match tints both cards in one of the site's seven book colours, draws a two-pixel border in that colour, writes "✓ Matched" along the bottom and gives the cards a four per cent pulse ([desktop-15](memory-match/desktop-15-right-pair-settled.png), [phone-14](memory-match/phone-14-right-pair-settled.png)). A board half done is a pleasant patchwork of coloured, named cards and striped backs ([desktop-16](memory-match/desktop-16-mid-play-3-of-6.png)). It remembers a board across a reload, it has arrow-key movement across the grid, it reads each flip aloud to a screen reader, it offers a timer you can hide, and it lets two to four people take turns on one phone. The underneath is strong.

Where it stops short is in the beats around the cards. A wrong pair is a non-event: the two cards sit face up for three seconds and then turn back, with nothing on screen saying "not a pair" and no change in the cards themselves ([desktop-11](memory-match/desktop-11-wrong-pair-both-up.png), [desktop-12](memory-match/desktop-12-wrong-pair-still-up-1-6s.png), [desktop-13](memory-match/desktop-13-wrong-pair-turned-back.png)). You cannot hurry them unless you chose "When I tap" on the start screen, and then a "Turn them back" pill appears under the grid, which on a phone is the bottom of the screen ([phone-32](memory-match/phone-32-when-i-tap-turn-them-back-button.png)). A right pair stays on the board as a full card, so the board never thins out as you play; the only sign of progress is the small-caps bar, "4 MOVES · 0:07 · PAIRS FOUND 3 OF 6", and a text list called "Pairs found" that grows under the grid in bordered rows ([desktop-16](memory-match/desktop-16-mid-play-3-of-6.png), [phone-15](memory-match/phone-15-mid-play-3-of-6.png)).

The screens either side of the board are forms. The start screen is four rows of chips (Deck, How hard, Players, Turn non-pairs back) and a gold "Deal the cards" pill, with the site footer under it on the desktop and a full screen of chips on the phone; nothing on it shows a card ([desktop-01](memory-match/desktop-01-start.png), [phone-01](memory-match/phone-01-start.png)). "See the pairs first" is a list of six rows and a button ([desktop-05](memory-match/desktop-05-study-pairs-first.png)). The end screen is a heading, "All 6 pairs in 7 moves · 0:10", three stars, and then "The stories": one bordered card per pair with its key verse quoted, nearly 1,100 pixels of them on the desktop and 1,450 on the phone, with Play again and Change game at the very bottom, under all of them ([desktop-21](memory-match/desktop-21-summary-solo.png), [desktop-22](memory-match/desktop-22-summary-solo-fullpage.png), [phone-21](memory-match/phone-21-summary-solo-fullpage.png), [phone-22](memory-match/phone-22-summary-buttons.png)). The stories are good reading, but nothing counts up, the stars do not arrive one at a time, and the three star icons are stacked in a column beside the words "3 stars" rather than in a row, which looks like a mistake and is one ([desktop-21](memory-match/desktop-21-summary-solo.png), [phone-20](memory-match/phone-20-summary-solo.png)).

Two-player play works but has no handover. The bar says "THEO'S TURN" with two score pills, the current player's pill tinted gold; when a turn passes, the words swap and nothing else moves ([desktop-35](memory-match/desktop-35-two-players-turn-passed.png), [phone-34](memory-match/phone-34-two-players-turn-passed.png)). The end is "Theo wins with 6 pairs" and a numbered list, then the same stories ([desktop-37](memory-match/desktop-37-summary-two-players.png)).

Harder boards hold up on the desktop: Medium is six by three, Hard six by four with plain backs and no Who or Story hint, all on one screen ([desktop-28](memory-match/desktop-28-board-medium-dealt.png), [desktop-30](memory-match/desktop-30-board-hard-dealt.png)). On the phone Hard is four columns of six at 87 by 104 pixels, which just fits the screen, but the words drop to 12 pixels and long ones break mid-word: "the Ten Commandme / nts" ([phone-31](memory-match/phone-31-board-hard-two-up.png)). The light theme is clean ([desktop-39](memory-match/desktop-39-light-theme-board.png)), a reload mid-board comes back with the same cards and moves ([desktop-19](memory-match/desktop-19-reload-mid-board-resumed.png)), a failed load gives a clear message and Try again ([desktop-40](memory-match/desktop-40-cant-load-or-loading.png)), and the console was clean apart from the site's usual font preload warnings.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **A miss is silent.** Two wrong cards just wait three seconds and turn back. No shake, no tint, no word on screen. The player learns nothing happened by nothing happening ([desktop-11](memory-match/desktop-11-wrong-pair-both-up.png)).
- **A match is a border and nine-pixel text.** "✓ Matched" is 9 pixels tall and the pulse is four per cent. The cards stay exactly where they are, so a finished board looks like a full board with colours on ([desktop-15](memory-match/desktop-15-right-pair-settled.png), [desktop-20](memory-match/desktop-20-last-pair-instant.png)).
- **Progress is a line of small caps.** "4 MOVES · 0:07 · PAIRS FOUND 3 OF 6" is a status line. Nothing fills in, and the star thresholds the game scores you on are never shown while you play ([desktop-16](memory-match/desktop-16-mid-play-3-of-6.png)).
- **Pairs found is a list.** Bordered text rows under the grid, the same shape as the study list, where a real game would stack the won pairs beside the board ([phone-15](memory-match/phone-15-mid-play-3-of-6.png)).
- **The start is settings.** Four chip rows and a button; on the phone the chips fill the whole screen and no card is in sight ([phone-01](memory-match/phone-01-start.png)).
- **The end is an article.** Heading, stars, then a long scroll of quote boxes with the buttons at the bottom; no count-up, no reveal, the best score a muted clause on the start screen afterwards ([desktop-22](memory-match/desktop-22-summary-solo-fullpage.png), [desktop-27](memory-match/desktop-27-start-with-best.png)).
- **Turns change by text swap.** In two-player play the only sign that it is now Theo's turn is the bar's words changing ([phone-34](memory-match/phone-34-two-players-turn-passed.png)).

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **Ravensburger memory** (the card game, 1959, the one every family has): a matched pair is picked up off the table and stacked in front of the player who found it. The board visibly empties as you go and the score is the height of your pile, not a number. This game's "Pairs found" list is that pile written out as text.
- **Classic Concentration** (NBC, 1987 to 1991, and the original Concentration from 1958): each matched pair removes two panels and uncovers part of a rebus picture behind the board. A match does two things at once: it clears space and it reveals something. Here a match could reveal the verse or the reference under the pair.
- **Duolingo, Match Madness and the "match the pairs" lesson**: two tapped tiles that do not go together flash red and shake for a quarter of a second, then reset at once; a pair goes green and both tiles drop out of the board; a streak counter ticks up. The instant red shake is the miss beat this game lacks, and the tiles dropping out is the thinning board.
- **Clubhouse Games: 51 Worldwide Games** (Nintendo Switch, 2020, the "Matching" game): cards flip with a snap, a pair lifts and glows before it is taken, and the turn passes with the other player's marker sliding in. The slide-in is the handover beat.
- **Microsoft Solitaire**: winning sets the cards bouncing off the table. An end screen that moves, before any text, is what makes a finish feel like one.
- **Wordle and the NYT games**: the end screen leads with a short statistic and a streak, the buttons sit right there, and the detail is below. The stars and moves here want that top billing with the stories under them.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Fix the two things that are broken.** After Play again and after Quit, scroll to the top so the new board or the start screen is on screen (one call when the board changes); today the desktop lands on "Pairs found" and the footer with the board above the screen, and the phone lands on the very bottom of the footer ([desktop-24](memory-match/desktop-24-play-again-as-seen.png), [phone-23](memory-match/phone-23-play-again-as-seen.png), [phone-25](memory-match/phone-25-start-after-quit-as-seen.png)). And lay the three stars in a row: they stack in a column today ([desktop-21](memory-match/desktop-21-summary-solo.png)). Two small changes, and the game stops looking faulty.
2. **Give a miss a beat.** When the second card is wrong, after the flip finishes tilt both cards a few degrees side to side for a quarter of a second with a thin red border, and let a tap anywhere on the board turn them back at once, with the timed turn-back as the fallback. Then the "When I tap" setting and its button become unnecessary and the three-second wait stops feeling like a freeze ([desktop-11](memory-match/desktop-11-wrong-pair-both-up.png), [phone-32](memory-match/phone-32-when-i-tap-turn-them-back-button.png)).
3. **Let a match leave the board.** After the pulse, lift the two cards, slide them together into a small stack to the side (desktop) or a shelf above the grid (phone), and leave a flat empty slot behind. The stack is the Ravensburger pile and replaces the "Pairs found" text list; tapping it shows the names and verses. Make the "✓" a proper stamp at 16 pixels while the cards are still on the board. The board thins as you play and the shelf shows how far you are ([desktop-16](memory-match/desktop-16-mid-play-3-of-6.png)).
4. **Give the bar a track.** Replace "PAIRS FOUND 3 OF 6" with six, nine or twelve small pips above the grid that fill in each pair's colour as it is found, the move count ticking up beside them, and a faint mark showing where three stars and two stars end, so the player is chasing something ([desktop-16](memory-match/desktop-16-mid-play-3-of-6.png)).
5. **Make the end a moment.** Keep the heading, but have the stars drop in one at a time with the move count and time counting up under them, "New best" as a gold ribbon when it is one, and Play again and Change game right under the stars. Fold "The stories" under that as a section the player scrolls to or opens; it is the reward for finishing, not the thing in the way of playing again ([desktop-22](memory-match/desktop-22-summary-solo-fullpage.png), [phone-22](memory-match/phone-22-summary-buttons.png)).
6. **Give two-player play a handover.** When the turn passes, slide a short banner across the top of the grid, "Theo's turn", for a second, and let the score pill grow when a pair is taken; at the end, put a crown on the winner's line and let their pile sit beside it ([phone-34](memory-match/phone-34-two-players-turn-passed.png), [desktop-37](memory-match/desktop-37-summary-two-players.png)).
7. **Show cards on the start screen.** Put a small fan of three face-down cards beside the title with one turning over on a loop, and tighten the four chip rows to three by moving "Turn non-pairs back" into a gear or dropping it once improvement 2 is in ([desktop-01](memory-match/desktop-01-start.png), [phone-01](memory-match/phone-01-start.png)).

## Phone problems

- **Play again and Quit leave the player at the bottom of the footer.** The page stays scrolled by about 1,400 pixels while the new board is 1,200 pixels above the top of the screen ([phone-23](memory-match/phone-23-play-again-as-seen.png), [phone-25](memory-match/phone-25-start-after-quit-as-seen.png)).
- **The end screen's buttons are two screens down**, at about 1,780 pixels on an 844-pixel screen, under all six story cards ([phone-21](memory-match/phone-21-summary-solo-fullpage.png)).
- **Hard boards break words mid-word.** At 87 pixels wide the card text is 12 pixels and "the Ten Commandments" splits as "Commandme / nts" ([phone-31](memory-match/phone-31-board-hard-two-up.png)). At 320 wide the cards are 69 by 70 pixels and three-line stories fill them to the edge ([phone-40](memory-match/phone-40-narrow-320-hard-two-up.png)).
- **A phone turned sideways needs scrolling to play.** The columns are fixed when the board is dealt, so a Hard board dealt upright stays four across and runs to 635 pixels on a 390-pixel-tall screen ([phone-41](memory-match/phone-41-landscape-hard-board.png)).
- **"Turn them back" sits at the bottom of the screen** when the "When I tap" setting is on, under the whole grid ([phone-32](memory-match/phone-32-when-i-tap-turn-them-back-button.png)).

## Accessibility

- **Keyboard focus is invisible.** The game asks for a two-pixel gold outline on the focused card and has arrow-key movement, but the site's rule of no focus outline anywhere overrides it, so a keyboard player moving across the grid cannot see which card they are on ([desktop-18](memory-match/desktop-18-keyboard-focus-on-card.png)).
- **The labels are very small.** WHO and STORY on a card face are 8 pixels; "✓ Matched" is 9 pixels; the bar is 12-pixel small caps. The match is also carried by the tick and the words, not colour alone, which is right.
- **What is good:** every flip, pair and miss is announced to a screen reader; face-down cards cannot be read by one; the timer can be hidden; reduced motion swaps the turn for a cross-fade; Windows high contrast is handled; a failed load is a real alert with a Try again.

## Anything broken

- Play again and Quit do not bring the new screen into view at either size ([desktop-24](memory-match/desktop-24-play-again-as-seen.png), [desktop-26](memory-match/desktop-26-start-after-quit-as-seen.png), [phone-23](memory-match/phone-23-play-again-as-seen.png)).
- The three stars on the end screen stack vertically instead of sitting in a row ([desktop-21](memory-match/desktop-21-summary-solo.png), [phone-20](memory-match/phone-20-summary-solo.png)).
- Hard boards on a phone break long words without a hyphen ([phone-31](memory-match/phone-31-board-hard-two-up.png)).
- Nothing else failed. Tapping a matched card does nothing, a reload resumes the board, the best score is saved and shown, every deck offered all three levels, and the console was clean.
