# Visual audit: Hot seat (/games/hot-seat)

Audited 11 November 2026 on the local preview, which was a build newer than the last change to the game's source. I played a whole game through from a cleared browser at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): the start screen and How to play; three players, one in each of the Teens-and-adults, Kids and Little-ones groups; the hand-over, the 3-2-1 count-down and a full turn for each, with right, wrong and passed answers, an undo, a keyboard mark, the last five seconds and the buzzer; the recap with a mark changed; the standings between turns; a pause, the menu, a reload mid-turn and "Carry on tonight's game"; the final standings; the light theme; a phone turned sideways; and a failed load. To keep the run short I set 30 seconds a turn and one round; the defaults are 60 seconds and two rounds, and nothing else differs. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [hot-seat/](hot-seat/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size; numbers 1 to 39 are the same stages at both sizes (the desktop has one extra hover shot, so its numbers run one ahead from 11), and 41 to 48 are follow-up checks.

My step's own brief said only "Audit hot seat", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

This is one of the better-built games on the site, and the turn itself is already more than a form. The trouble is that everything around the turn is a settings page or a table, and the turn, good as it is, has no motion in it.

The start screen is a form from top to bottom: serif title, one line of lede, a How-to-play link, a small drawing of a chair and a stopwatch, then two name boxes with age-group drop-downs and three round icon buttons each, Add player, a four-line legend of the age groups, five rows of pill chips (seconds, testament, two choices, sound, rounds), "About 4 minutes" and a gold Start game pill ([desktop-01](hot-seat/desktop-01-start.png), [desktop-06](hot-seat/desktop-06-setup-filled-fullpage.png)). It is clear and it works, but it reads like booking a room. On the desktop Start game sits just under the first screen; on the phone it is almost two screens down ([phone-02](hot-seat/phone-02-start-fullpage.png)).

The hand-over is the first thing that feels like a game night. The whole screen goes dark and quiet, a gold kicker reads "ROUND 1 OF 1 · TURN 1 OF 3", the player's name stands in large serif type under "IN THE HOT SEAT", then "READING" and who reads, "Sit facing the reader. No peeking!", a gold Start 30 seconds pill and three smaller pills ([desktop-07](hot-seat/desktop-07-handover-1-adults.png), [phone-07](hot-seat/phone-07-handover-1-adults.png)). It is a good screen, but it is a poster, not a stage: nothing arrives, nothing moves, and the chair from the start page's drawing is nowhere to be seen. The count-down is a single serif digit filling the screen, 3 then 2 then 1, each simply replacing the last with no scale or fade ([desktop-09](hot-seat/desktop-09-countdown-3.png)).

The turn is the heart of the game and its layout is right: a ring clock with big digits top left, "✓ 0" in green in the middle, a pause button top right, the player's name, the question in large serif type, the answer in a soft green-tinted band with its reference, and three big coloured buttons across the bottom, Wrong in red, Pass in amber and Right in green and widest, under the right thumb, with a small Undo above them ([desktop-10](hot-seat/desktop-10-turn-1-first-question.png), [phone-10](hot-seat/phone-10-turn-1-first-question.png)). The buttons are 74 pixels tall, every mark has a symbol and a word as well as a colour, the ring drains once a second and turns red with the digits for the last five seconds ([desktop-18](hot-seat/desktop-18-turn-1-last-five-seconds.png)), and the arrow keys work. The reader can judge an answer at a glance, which is the whole point.

What the turn lacks is any feeling of pace. Tapping Right swaps the question text in place with no transition at all; the only feedback is the score digit growing by a quarter for a quarter of a second ([desktop-12](hot-seat/desktop-12-turn-1-after-right-flash.png)), and on Wrong the "✓ 1" turns red for the same quarter second, a red tick that reads oddly ([desktop-14](hot-seat/desktop-14-turn-1-after-wrong-flash.png)). Hovering a button changes nothing. The question and answer take the top third and the buttons the bottom sixth, and between them is a dark gap of more than 400 pixels on the desktop and about 350 on the phone, so the screen looks half empty. At the buzzer a red-edged band, "Time! Finish this question.", appears above the question and pushes it 56 pixels down the screen at the one moment the reader is mid-sentence ([desktop-19](hot-seat/desktop-19-turn-1-time-up-band.png), [phone-18](hot-seat/phone-18-turn-1-time-up-band.png)). Little ones get the two choices on a line under the question, the right one underlined in green, which works well for a reader ([desktop-32](hot-seat/desktop-32-turn-3-two-choices.png), [phone-31](hot-seat/phone-31-turn-3-two-choices.png)).

The recap is a list: the name, "4 points" in serif, a green "New best on this phone" with three tags, and one row per question with a pill button showing its mark, the question, the answer in bold and the reference as a gold link ([desktop-20](hot-seat/desktop-20-recap-1.png), [phone-19](hot-seat/phone-19-recap-1.png)). Tapping a mark cycles it, with no motion ([desktop-22](hot-seat/desktop-22-recap-1-after-tapping-a-mark.png)). Save turn is a full-width gold pill under the list, on screen at desktop size and a full screen down on the phone.

The standings between turns are a plain table with "1st, 2nd ▼, 2nd ▼" and a Next player pill with the next name beside it ([desktop-23](hot-seat/desktop-23-standings-after-turn-1.png), [phone-22](hot-seat/phone-22-standings-after-turn-1.png)). The final standings go back into the site's page: "Final standings", three names in a text list with "1st 2nd 3rd" in gold, the same table with a column per round, two shout-outs ("Best single turn", "Longest run of right answers"), three pills and then every question of the night ([desktop-37](hot-seat/desktop-37-final-standings.png), [desktop-38](hot-seat/desktop-38-final-standings-fullpage.png), [phone-36](hot-seat/phone-36-final-standings.png)). The names are big and handsome, but a winner is announced the way a league table is printed: nothing rises, nothing counts, nothing celebrates.

Underneath, it is solid. The game runs in a fixed layer above the page, so it never inherits a scrolled position, and the final standings land at the top of the page ([phone-48](hot-seat/phone-48-handover-from-scrolled-start.png)). A reload mid-turn offers "Carry on tonight's game" and comes back paused with the question hidden ([desktop-33](hot-seat/desktop-33-reload-mid-turn-resume-offer.png), [desktop-34](hot-seat/desktop-34-after-carry-on-paused.png)). The menu is a proper sheet with players, groups and settings ([desktop-27](hot-seat/desktop-27-menu-sheet.png), [phone-26](hot-seat/phone-26-menu-sheet.png)). The light theme is clean ([desktop-43](hot-seat/desktop-43-light-turn.png), [phone-46](hot-seat/phone-46-light-recap.png)). A phone turned sideways gets the question on the left and the three buttons stacked on the right ([phone-47](hot-seat/phone-47-landscape-turn.png)). A failed load gives a clear message and a proper Try again button ([phone-41](hot-seat/phone-41-cant-load-in-view.png)).

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The start screen is a settings form.** Name boxes, drop-downs, five rows of chips and a legend; the only picture is a small drawing at the side ([desktop-01](hot-seat/desktop-01-start.png)).
- **Nothing moves in the turn.** A mark swaps the question text in place; the only feedback is a quarter-second nudge of the score digit ([desktop-12](hot-seat/desktop-12-turn-1-after-right-flash.png)). The stylesheet has two transitions and no keyframes.
- **The middle of the turn screen is empty.** Question and answer at the top, buttons at the bottom, a dark gap of 350 to 440 pixels between ([phone-10](hot-seat/phone-10-turn-1-first-question.png)).
- **The buzzer is a notice bar.** "Time! Finish this question." is a line of text in a red-edged band that shoves the question down ([desktop-19](hot-seat/desktop-19-turn-1-time-up-band.png)).
- **The count-down is three static digits** with no breath between them ([desktop-09](hot-seat/desktop-09-countdown-3.png)).
- **The recap, the standings and the final are lists and tables.** Pills, rows and columns; the podium is three lines of text ([desktop-20](hot-seat/desktop-20-recap-1.png), [desktop-37](hot-seat/desktop-37-final-standings.png)).
- **The hand-over is a poster.** Good type, no entrance, no chair, no spotlight ([desktop-07](hot-seat/desktop-07-handover-1-adults.png)).

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **The Chase, the Cash Builder** (the round this game copies): one player, sixty seconds, open questions. The clock is a big drum at the front of the set, the money ticks up on screen with a sound on every right answer, and the end of the minute is a klaxon, not a notice.
- **Heads Up!** (the Ellen DeGeneres phone game, Warner Bros.): the nearest thing on a phone. Each word is a full-screen card; a correct answer flips the card away with a green stamp and a chime, a pass drops it with a red stamp, the next card is already behind it, and the minute ends with the cards fanned out as the recap, right ones green and passed ones red, before the score counts up.
- **Catch Phrase** (Hasbro) and **Taboo**: the timer is the tension. Catch Phrase's ticking speeds up as time runs out; Taboo's buzzer is a thing in someone's hand. Both end with a sound you cannot miss.
- **Mastermind** (BBC): the black chair under a single spotlight, the name read out, and "I've started, so I'll finish", which is exactly this game's "finish this question" rule. The ceremony of sitting down is the game.
- **5 Second Rule** (PlayMonster): the twisting timer is on the table where everyone sees it, and the player to the left reads, as here.
- **Kahoot**: the countdown ring is large and central, every answer gets a full-screen colour and a sound, and the final screen is a three-step podium that rises into place with names and confetti before the table is shown.
- **Jackbox** (Quiplash, Trivia Murder Party): scores are counted up live between rounds, with players' names sliding past each other, and the winner is revealed last.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Make each question a card that is dealt and thrown.** Put the question and its answer band on one card surface in the middle of the screen, filling the empty gap. On Right the card slides off to the right with a green tick stamped on it; on Wrong it slides off left with a red cross; on Pass it drops away with an amber arrow; the next card is already waiting beneath and settles up. The score pops as it counts. All the state exists (the game already sets a quarter-second "flash" mark on every tap); this is keeping the old card on screen for a fifth of a second and three CSS animations, with none under reduced motion ([desktop-10](hot-seat/desktop-10-turn-1-first-question.png), [desktop-12](hot-seat/desktop-12-turn-1-after-right-flash.png)).
2. **Make the clock the drama.** Grow the ring to a central dial that the room can read, let it pulse once a second for the last five, and make the buzzer a full-width "TIME!" stamp that drops over the top bar rather than a band above the question, so the question does not move while the reader is still saying it. The ticking sound exists; the visual beat does not ([desktop-18](hot-seat/desktop-18-turn-1-last-five-seconds.png), [desktop-19](hot-seat/desktop-19-turn-1-time-up-band.png)).
3. **Stage the hand-over and the count-down.** Bring the chair and stopwatch from the start page's drawing onto the hand-over as a spotlight behind the name; let the name rise in and the Start pill follow a beat later; make each count-down digit scale up and fade with its tick, and let the first card deal in from the "1". Same type, same colours, one picture that is already drawn ([desktop-07](hot-seat/desktop-07-handover-1-adults.png), [desktop-09](hot-seat/desktop-09-countdown-3.png)).
4. **Turn the recap into a strip.** Above the list, show the turn as a row of small tiles, one per question, filling in green, red and amber left to right as the screen opens, then count the score up and drop the "New best" ribbon in. The list stays for fixing marks; a changed mark flips its tile. On the phone, pin Save turn to the bottom of the screen so it is never a screen below the list ([desktop-20](hot-seat/desktop-20-recap-1.png), [phone-20](hot-seat/phone-20-recap-1-fullpage.png)).
5. **Give the final standings a podium.** Three steps that rise into place, third first and the winner last, with the names on them in the serif the page already uses, and a small burst for first place; then the table, the shout-outs as two cards, and the buttons. The "All questions tonight" list is good as it is ([desktop-37](hot-seat/desktop-37-final-standings.png)).
6. **Let the standings move between turns.** Instead of a red triangle beside "2nd", slide the rows into their new order with the totals counting up, and show the next player as a card ("Tobias, you're up") that becomes the hand-over ([desktop-23](hot-seat/desktop-23-standings-after-turn-1.png)).
7. **Make the start screen feel like a line-up.** Each player as a small card with their name and a group pip, dealt in as they are added, and the settings folded under a single "Settings" row with the current choices in words ("60 seconds · both testaments · 2 rounds"), so Start game sits on the first screen at both sizes. Keep the site's inputs and chips; this is grouping, not restyling ([phone-02](hot-seat/phone-02-start-fullpage.png)).

## Phone problems

- **Start game is almost two screens down.** The start page is 3,050 pixels tall and the Start game pill sits at 1,486 on an 844 screen, under two name rows, the legend and five chip rows ([phone-02](hot-seat/phone-02-start-fullpage.png)).
- **Save turn is below the fold on the recap.** After a six-question turn it sits at 1,013 pixels on the 844 screen, so the reader scrolls past every answer to save the points ([phone-20](hot-seat/phone-20-recap-1-fullpage.png)).
- **The menu hides End game and How to play.** The sheet is 776 pixels tall with 934 of content, so the two buttons at the bottom are off screen until it is scrolled; on the desktop the same happens at 900 tall ([phone-26](hot-seat/phone-26-menu-sheet.png), [desktop-27](hot-seat/desktop-27-menu-sheet.png)).
- **The final page is long**, 4,407 pixels, with the third button cut by the first screen's edge and the night's questions a screen below ([phone-37](hot-seat/phone-37-final-standings-fullpage.png)).
- **Sideways, the turn does not quite fit.** At 844 by 390 the layer is 32 pixels taller than the screen, so the Undo link is clipped under the Pass button ([phone-47](hot-seat/phone-47-landscape-turn.png)).
- **The keyboard line shows on the phone.** "On a computer: → Right, ← Wrong…" appeared under the hand-over in my phone run; the stylesheet means to hide it on touch screens, so it is worth a check on a real phone ([phone-07](hot-seat/phone-07-handover-1-adults.png)).
- Done well: the three mark buttons are 74 pixels tall, above the design's own 64 minimum, and Right is the widest under the right thumb; text is 16 pixels or more; nothing scrolls sideways; the game runs in a fixed layer, so starting from a scrolled page lands correctly and the final standings land at the top ([phone-48](hot-seat/phone-48-handover-from-scrolled-start.png)); a reload mid-turn comes back paused with the question hidden ([phone-33](hot-seat/phone-33-after-carry-on-paused.png)).

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus anywhere.** Tab onto Start, Skip this round or the Right button and nothing changes: the site's base stylesheet switches outlines off everywhere, marked as the owner's call of 6 October 2026, so the game's own focus ring never draws ([desktop-08](hot-seat/desktop-08-handover-1-focus-via-tab.png), [desktop-17](hot-seat/desktop-17-turn-1-right-button-focused.png)).
- **A wrong answer turns the tick red.** The score is "✓ 1" and on Wrong the whole thing, tick included, flashes red, so the symbol says right while the colour says wrong ([desktop-14](hot-seat/desktop-14-turn-1-after-wrong-flash.png)).
- **End game uses the browser's own confirm box**, which is not styled like the site and cannot be read in the site's language setting.
- **"Carry on tonight's game" is both the heading and the button**, so a screen reader hears it twice ([desktop-33](hot-seat/desktop-33-reload-mid-turn-resume-offer.png)).
- Done well: every mark carries a symbol, a word and a colour; the clock is a labelled timer and the question is announced to a screen reader as it changes; the game layer is a dialog that takes focus and gives it back; Escape pauses; the reader links open in a new tab and say so; the chips use pressed state; names are limited and cut without breaking emoji; the light theme reads cleanly ([desktop-43](hot-seat/desktop-43-light-turn.png)).

## Anything broken

Nothing crashed. The console showed only the headless browser refusing the screen wake lock and the site's two font preload notices. Five things to fix:

- **The buzzer shifts the question.** The "Time! Finish this question." band is inserted above the question, which drops 56 pixels at both sizes while the reader is mid-question ([desktop-19](hot-seat/desktop-19-turn-1-time-up-band.png), [desktop-45](hot-seat/desktop-45-light-turn-time-up-band.png)).
- **Book names are abbreviated unevenly.** The same recap shows "Ephesians 5:6", "Numbers 19:21" and "Haggai 2:8" beside "Jam 5:15", "Pro 19:12", "Gen 4:1-2" and "1 Sam 16:11", so some references look like typos. The text comes from the question bank, not the game, but this is where a family sees it ([desktop-20](hot-seat/desktop-20-recap-1.png), [phone-19](hot-seat/phone-19-recap-1.png)).
- **Players who have not played yet are shown as having dropped.** After the first turn the two players still waiting show "2nd ▼" with a red triangle, because they were joint first at 0 before anyone played ([desktop-23](hot-seat/desktop-23-standings-after-turn-1.png)).
- **The clock ring leaves a red dot at zero.** When time is up the drained ring still shows a small red stub at the top, the rounded end of the stroke ([desktop-19](hot-seat/desktop-19-turn-1-time-up-band.png)).
- **The two-choices question mark floats.** The "A or B ?" line puts a gap before the question mark, and when the second choice wraps the mark hangs alone ([desktop-32](hot-seat/desktop-32-turn-3-two-choices.png)).

One screenshot note: the failed-load screen was produced by blocking the question bank, and the game shows its own message in place of Start game ([desktop-41](hot-seat/desktop-41-cant-load-in-view.png), [phone-41](hot-seat/phone-41-cant-load-in-view.png)).
