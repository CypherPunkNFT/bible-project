# Odd one out redesign (from its visual audit)

**Done:** 11 November 2026, sequence "Bible Guide: redesign every game from its audit", step 7. The audit it answers is [odd-one-out.md](odd-one-out.md); its screenshots are in [odd-one-out/](odd-one-out/) and the new ones in [odd-one-out/after/](odd-one-out/after/), named the same way so each stage pairs up. The shared instructions were [REDESIGN-BRIEF.md](REDESIGN-BRIEF.md); my step's own brief said the same things and added nothing that disagreed. No correction arrived during the job.

## 1. What changed

The game now plays like the picture on its hub tile: four things on a hillside, and one of them circled. The four names are cards that deal in one after another, lift when the pointer or the keyboard reaches them, and press down when tapped. A right answer tints the chosen card green, stamps "Odd one out" in its corner and draws a hand-drawn green ring round it; a wrong pick turns red with a dashed edge, shakes once and is stamped "Your pick", and a beat later the true odd one lifts with its ring, while the other cards sink and fade a little. Above the cards a row of ten small squares fills green or red as the round goes, with the score popping up each time it grows and a small flame that lights from two in a row. The verdict and the Next button rise together in a bar under the cards, with the reason as one large sentence (the odd name in the game's blue) and the four lines, the key verse and "Also true" folded under a "Why" row, so Next is on the first screen in every round, on a phone and a desktop alike, and each Next brings the new puzzle to the top of the screen. The round ends on a result card: the score counts up over ten squares, a title for the score, a ribbon and one gold burst for a new best, the best streak as a flame, and Play again, Change game and Share right under it; the ten rows stay below as plain lines for anyone re-reading. The start screen shows a sample deal of four cards with the ring drifting slowly from one to the next, never settling, and says in words why a level is greyed. Rules, data, scoring, saved progress (the same storage keys), keyboard play and the address are unchanged.

## 2. The audit's list, item by item

1. **Keep the puzzle on screen and put Next where the thumb is.** Built as asked. The verdict and Next sit in the kit's feedback band, which stays at the bottom of a phone screen, and the page scrolls back to the top whenever a new puzzle, the summary or the start screen opens (after Next, See results, Play again and Change game alike). Measured on a 390 by 844 phone: after a right answer Next sits from 547 to 591 pixels, after a wrong one from 560 to 604, and after Next the page's scroll is 0 in every case; on the 1440 desktop Next is at about 535 to 580 on a 900-tall screen ([phone-06](odd-one-out/after/phone-06-puzzle-1-right.png), [phone-09](odd-one-out/after/phone-09-puzzle-2-after-next.png), [desktop-07](odd-one-out/after/desktop-07-puzzle-1-right.png)). The reading under "Why" scrolls inside the band and is capped at under half the screen, so the band can never swallow the cards.
2. **Make the four choices cards that react.** Built as asked, with the kit's answer card: 120 pixels tall at both sizes with the key number in one corner and the stamp's place reserved in the other, so nothing jumps when a mark arrives. Right: green tint, "Odd one out" stamp, the ring draws itself round the card in under a second. Wrong: red dashed edge, a short shake, "Your pick" stamp, then the true odd one's stamp and ring arrive a third of a second later; the other cards sink. All CSS, all under half a second, and every delay is zeroed under reduced motion ([desktop-11](odd-one-out/after/desktop-11-puzzle-2-wrong.png), [phone-10](odd-one-out/after/phone-10-puzzle-2-wrong.png)). Right is green and wrong is red, as the audit's accessibility note asked; the game's blue carries the rest.
3. **Make the reason land as a reveal, not a paragraph.** Built, with one change of place. The reason is one large serif sentence with the odd name in the game's blue, and the four lines, the key verse and "Also true" fold under a "Why" row. It sits inside the band under the verdict rather than loose under the grid, because a pinned band would otherwise cover it on a phone ([phone-08](odd-one-out/after/phone-08-puzzle-1-right-why-open.png)).
4. **Give the round a track.** Built as asked: ten squares across the top that fill green or red, the newest popping, with "3 of 10" beside them, the kind as a blue pill in Mixed rounds (or "People · Easy" as a small label in a single-kind round), the score as a serif number that pops when it grows, and a flame for the streak ([desktop-15](odd-one-out/after/desktop-15-puzzle-6-mid-play.png)).
5. **Build the puzzle a stage.** Built differently. The audit asked for a bordered panel round progress, prompt, cards and reveal; the owner's rules say no boxes round boxes, and the cards are already the game's one card shape. So the play area is layout only: it is given the height of the screen so the footer waits below the fold during a round, and the cards grew to 120 pixels tall with the name at 24 pixels on the desktop ([desktop-05](odd-one-out/after/desktop-05-puzzle-1.png)).
6. **Give the summary its picture.** Built as asked with the kit's result panel: the ten squares, the score counting up from zero, a title for the score ("Keep looking", "A good eye", "Hard to fool", "Nothing gets past you"), "New best" as a ribbon with one gold-and-cream burst when it is one, the best streak as a flame number, your best for this round's kind and level, and Play again, Change game and Share right under them. The ten rows are plain lines with a rule between them, each opening to its four lines ([desktop-20](odd-one-out/after/desktop-20-summary.png), [phone-19](odd-one-out/after/phone-19-summary.png)). A ten out of ten makes the squares hop in turn. A finished round reopened after a reload shows its score without the ribbon or the burst again.
7. **Show a puzzle on the start screen.** Built as asked, with one guard. The opener's four names (Peter, John, Paul, James) are dealt face up over the chips, but the ring drifts from card to card on a slow loop and never settles, because that exact set opens a new player's first round and a settled ring would answer it. When a level is greyed a line says so in words ("Easy only for now: the other levels need more puzzles"), and the greyed chips point to it for a screen reader ([desktop-01](odd-one-out/after/desktop-01-start.png), [phone-03](odd-one-out/after/phone-03-start-books-levels-disabled.png)).

**My own additions.**
- **The ring.** The hub's drawing of this game is three sheep and a goat with the goat circled; the game now circles its odd one the same way, a hand-drawn ellipse that draws itself round the card, and on the start screen the same ring keeps looking.
- **Share.** The round had no share; it now has the kit's share button with a four-line text: the score, the kind and level, the ten squares, and the address. It never names a puzzle, so it spoils nothing.
- **A way out of a round.** The audit noted there was none. A quiet "Leave round" link in the track row opens a site-styled confirm; leaving returns to the start screen with the title focused.
- **No repeats inside a round.** The audit found the same three names, and the same answer, coming round twice in one round. The round builder now spreads them: within the fresh sets, and then within the oldest seen ones, a set that would repeat an answer or three of the same four is left for later, and only if the pool allows nothing else does it come back. Freshness still comes first, so a set seen lately never displaces a new one. A test sweeps every kind and level over sixty seeds and finds no clash.

## 3. Faults fixed

From "Anything broken": the page left scrolled after Next and Play again (scroll measured at 0 after each); the wrong-answer heading splitting its name into a column (the verdict is now "Not quite" on one line and the name with "was the odd one out" on the next, in their own elements); the grid jumping on answer (the stamp lives in reserved corner space; card height measured at 120 before and after answering); the "Not enough puzzles" note never showing (a visible line now says why a level is greyed, and screen readers get it as the chip's description); the same trio or answer twice in one round (the spread rule above). Anna beside Annas is in one Easy set's data and is left as it is, noted under "left for later".

From "Phone problems": every Next now lands at the top of the new puzzle; Next is on screen after every answer; the summary's buttons are right under the score; the footer sits below the fold during a round; the wrong heading no longer splits; sideways, the band sits in the flow under the cards and the cards go four across (it was covering all four when pinned). At 320 wide the cards go to one column, 70 pixels tall with the stamp at the right edge, and nothing scrolls sideways (document width measured equal to the screen at 320, 390, 844 and 1440).

From "Accessibility": focus shows as the card's own lift and blue edge and the buttons' lift (no outline); right is green, wrong is red, each with a symbol and a word; the verdict is one live region in the band (the old duplicate announcement is gone); a level's reason is on screen and in the chip's description; a round can be left. Keys 1 to 4 still answer and Enter or Space moves on, but not when a button, link or fold has focus, and nothing answers behind the open confirm dialog.
