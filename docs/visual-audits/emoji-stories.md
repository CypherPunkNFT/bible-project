# Visual audit: Emoji stories (/games/emoji-stories)

Audited 10 November 2026 on the local preview, which was a fresh build of the current source. I played the game through from a cleared device at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): the start screen and its settings; a ten-puzzle Practice round with Choices, including a right answer, a wrong answer, all three hints, an answer from the keyboard, the light theme, a reload mid-round and the score screen; a Practice round with Typing; today's Daily puzzle with Typing (a wrong guess, a "right person, different story" guess, then the answer and Share) and again with Choices (two wrong picks); a full Show-a-room round; and a failed load. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [emoji-stories/](emoji-stories/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size and the number gives the stage; numbers 1 to 50 are the same stages at both sizes, 51 to 64 are follow-up checks.

My step's own brief said only "Audit emoji stories", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

The start screen is the strongest in the game. Under the serif title and one line of lede sits a live example, five emoji at 35 pixels and "= Noah's ark and the flood", and under that three columns with a thin top rule each: Today's puzzle, Practice (with Difficulty and Stories-from chips) and Show a room, each with a gold pill button ([desktop-01](emoji-stories/desktop-01-start.png), [phone-01](emoji-stories/phone-01-start.png)). The example row is the only hint of what the game looks like; the three "cards" have no surface, only a line above, so they read as three columns of text. On the phone they stack into a 730-pixel column and Start practice sits at 786 on an 844-pixel screen, under the browser bar on a real phone; Start a room round is a full screen further down ([phone-02](emoji-stories/phone-02-start-fullpage.png)).

A Practice puzzle is a small grey "1 OF 10 · SCORE 0" line, a centred row of emoji at 64 pixels on the desktop and 48 on the phone, then four grey boxes in two columns, each with a tiny number in the corner and the story title, then a grey "Hint (−1 point)" pill ([desktop-05](emoji-stories/desktop-05-practice-round-1.png), [phone-51](emoji-stories/phone-51-practice-as-landed-after-start.png)). The emoji row is genuinely nice: big, colourful, centred, and it is the one thing on the page that looks like a game. The boxes under it are form controls. Hover draws a thin gold border and nothing else ([desktop-07](emoji-stories/desktop-07-practice-round-1-hover.png)). Nothing enters, nothing moves, nothing is pressed.

The reveal is where the game feel goes missing. The moment you tap a box, all four boxes vanish and an article appears under a thin rule: a bold "Right!" in purple (16 pixels), the story title in serif, the four emoji again with their passage words, the key verse as a quotation, two underlined links, "3 points" and a gold Next puzzle button ([desktop-08](emoji-stories/desktop-08-practice-round-1-reveal-right.png), [phone-08](emoji-stories/phone-08-practice-round-1-reveal-right.png)). Wrong looks the same with "Not this time" in red and a muted "You picked The tower of Babel" line ([desktop-14](emoji-stories/desktop-14-practice-round-2-reveal-wrong.png), [phone-14](emoji-stories/phone-14-practice-round-2-reveal-wrong.png)). The box you picked is never marked; the right box is never shown among the wrong ones; there is no flash, no colour, no sound, no pause. Right and wrong are the same page with a different first word. Credit where due: on the desktop Next is always within the first screen (609 pixels down for a right answer, 734 for a wrong one), and the next puzzle lands at the top.

Hints are a numbered list that grows under the emoji: "1. Where: Judges · Old Testament", "2. The pictures: 🌴 Palm ⛺ tent 🥛 milk 🔨 hammer", "3. Who: A judge of Israel, and a woman in her tent" ([desktop-13](emoji-stories/desktop-13-practice-round-2-hint-3.png), [phone-13](emoji-stories/phone-13-practice-round-2-hint-3.png)). The second hint is the whole answer key, which is generous, but the list itself looks like notes, and the cost (a point each) is only in the button label.

The score screen is a heading, "You scored 15 out of 30", a muted best line, then ten rows of emoji, title and "✓ Right" in purple or "✗ Missed" in red, each row opening the full reveal inline, then Play again ([desktop-21](emoji-stories/desktop-21-practice-end.png), [desktop-23](emoji-stories/desktop-23-practice-end-row-opened.png), [phone-21](emoji-stories/phone-21-practice-end.png)). It is a clear, readable ledger, and that is the problem: nothing counts up, nothing is celebrated, and Play again is below the fold on the phone (903 pixels down on an 844 screen).

Typing mode replaces the boxes with a labelled search box and a Guess button; suggestions drop down in a dark panel as you type, and the game judges the story you pick, never your spelling ([desktop-26](emoji-stories/desktop-26-practice-typing-suggestions.png), [phone-26](emoji-stories/phone-26-practice-typing-suggestions.png)). It works well and reads like a search form.

The Daily is the Practice page with "Today's puzzle #2" as the heading, the Answer-with switch, the search box, a "GUESS 1 OF 3" line over three numbered dashes, and a "Hint (shows on your share)" pill ([desktop-29](emoji-stories/desktop-29-daily-start.png), [phone-29](emoji-stories/phone-29-daily-start.png)). A wrong guess writes "Not this one." in muted grey under the box, opens the first hint, and fills slot 1 with the title and a red "Not this one" ([desktop-31](emoji-stories/desktop-31-daily-guess-1-wrong.png)). A guess with the right person gets an amber "Right person, different story" ([desktop-33](emoji-stories/desktop-33-daily-guess-2-close.png)). Solving it shows the same article, the three slots with their verdict words, a row of five small stats, and a Share button ([desktop-34](emoji-stories/desktop-34-daily-solved-reveal.png), [phone-34](emoji-stories/phone-34-daily-solved-reveal.png)). Share, when the clipboard is blocked, drops a text box with 🟥🟨🟩 squares and no "copy this" line ([desktop-36](emoji-stories/desktop-36-daily-share.png), [phone-36](emoji-stories/phone-36-daily-share.png)). Those three coloured squares are the one piece of game graphic the Daily produces, and they exist only in the share text. In Choices mode a wrong pick does mark the box in place, faded with a red "Not this one" ([desktop-59](emoji-stories/desktop-59-daily-choices-after-wrong-pick.png), [phone-59](emoji-stories/phone-59-daily-choices-after-wrong-pick.png)); it is the only in-place marking anywhere in the game.

Show a room is the most game-like mode. One emoji at 144 pixels on a dark page, each new clue fading and scaling in over a third of a second, a row of pill buttons at the bottom (Next clue, Hint, Show choices, Reveal, Full screen) and a keys line ([desktop-39](emoji-stories/desktop-39-room-story-1-clue-1.png), [desktop-42](emoji-stories/desktop-42-room-story-1-clue-3.png)). Hints appear at 32 pixels, choices as a plain numbered list in two columns ([desktop-44](emoji-stories/desktop-44-room-story-1-choices.png)), the reveal is the same article, and the round ends with "That was the last story of the round." and two buttons, no score of any kind ([desktop-49](emoji-stories/desktop-49-room-end.png)).

Underneath it is well made: 106 stories across both testaments, each emoji named for screen readers, missing emoji drawn as a dashed box with the word, keys 1 to 4 and Enter, a reload mid-round that comes back to the same puzzle in the same theme ([desktop-17](emoji-stories/desktop-17-practice-round-4-light-after-reload.png)), a proper combobox for typing, the Daily shared across tabs, and a clean console apart from the site's two font preload notices.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The answers are four form boxes.** Two columns, a small number, a title, a one-pixel border; hover is a border colour change ([desktop-07](emoji-stories/desktop-07-practice-round-1-hover.png)). Nothing is a card you would want to tap.
- **The reveal is an article, not a moment.** The boxes disappear, a rule and a heading appear, and right or wrong is one word in purple or red at 16 pixels ([desktop-08](emoji-stories/desktop-08-practice-round-1-reveal-right.png), [desktop-14](emoji-stories/desktop-14-practice-round-2-reveal-wrong.png)). Your pick is never shown.
- **Nothing moves outside the room.** The whole Practice and Daily stylesheet has no animation; the only motion in the game is the room's clue fade.
- **Progress and score are a caption.** "3 OF 10 · SCORE 3" is 12-pixel grey uppercase above the emoji ([desktop-14](emoji-stories/desktop-14-practice-round-2-reveal-wrong.png)). Points are a line of text under the verse.
- **Hints are a numbered list** and their cost is only in a button label ([desktop-13](emoji-stories/desktop-13-practice-round-2-hint-3.png)).
- **The Daily's three guesses are three dashes** in a numbered list, and the coloured squares that would show them appear only in the share text ([desktop-29](emoji-stories/desktop-29-daily-start.png), [desktop-36](emoji-stories/desktop-36-daily-share.png)).
- **The end is a ledger.** Ten table rows and a heading, no count-up, no strip of results, no "new best" ([desktop-21](emoji-stories/desktop-21-practice-end.png)).
- **The start's three modes are three columns of text** with a rule above each, not cards ([desktop-01](emoji-stories/desktop-01-start.png)).

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **Guess the Emoji** (Random Logic Games) and **Emoji Pop**: the phone games this one is closest to. The emoji sit on a card in the middle of the screen, large and on a soft tile; the answer is built from a bank of letter tiles that fly into slots, and a right answer bursts (confetti, chime, coins counting up) before the next card slides in. Hints are coins you spend, with the balance always on screen.
- **4 Pics 1 Word** (Lotum): the same loop with pictures. Its lesson is the hint economy: a coin balance in the corner, hints that visibly remove wrong letters or reveal one, and a level counter that ticks up with a sound.
- **Kahoot**: four answer cards in four colours and shapes; on answering, the right card stays lit and the others drop, before any explanation. Right and wrong look different from across the room.
- **Duolingo**: a right answer slides a green sheet up with a tick and the Continue button on it; a wrong one slides a red sheet with the correction. The player never scrolls to continue.
- **Wordle**: for the Daily. Guess rows are visible tiles from the start; each guess flips its tiles to colour; the stats sheet counts up; Share copies the grid with "Copied to clipboard" shown.
- **Jackbox** (Fibbage, Quiplash) for the room: the big screen is a stage, clues arrive with motion, the four choices are large lettered cards, and a running team score sits in a corner.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Turn the answer into a moment, in place.** Keep the four boxes on screen when one is tapped: the tapped one presses down and takes a gold edge, then a quarter-second later the right one tints green with a tick and the picked wrong one tints red with a cross and the other two fade. A short band slides in above the boxes, "Right! +3" or "Not this time, it was Deborah and Jael", with Next puzzle on the band. The title, words, verse and links fold in below the band as they do now. Everything needed exists (the verdict, the pick, the choices, the colours); this is keeping the boxes mounted and adding two keyframes ([desktop-08](emoji-stories/desktop-08-practice-round-1-reveal-right.png), [desktop-14](emoji-stories/desktop-14-practice-round-2-reveal-wrong.png)).
2. **Make the four answers cards.** Give each a surface and a rounded corner that stands off the page, a circled serif number at the left, a small lift and shadow on hover, a press on tap, and let them deal in with a short stagger when the puzzle begins. Same palette, same two-column grid ([desktop-05](emoji-stories/desktop-05-practice-round-1.png), [phone-51](emoji-stories/phone-51-practice-as-landed-after-start.png)).
3. **Deal the emoji in, on tiles.** Put each emoji on a soft tile (the site's surface colour, rounded) and let them arrive one at a time left to right with the room's existing fade-and-scale, so a new puzzle feels dealt rather than loaded. At the reveal, the passage word appears under each tile in place instead of as a separate list ([desktop-05](emoji-stories/desktop-05-practice-round-1.png)).
4. **Draw the score as ten squares.** Replace "3 OF 10 · SCORE 3" with a strip of ten small squares that fill green, amber (right with a hint) or red as you go and pop when they fill, with the running points beside them. The end screen counts the score up, shows the same strip and a "New best" when it is one, and offers a Share line of those squares like the Daily's ([desktop-21](emoji-stories/desktop-21-practice-end.png), [desktop-36](emoji-stories/desktop-36-daily-share.png)).
5. **Make hints feel like spending.** Show three lamp or coin pips beside the emoji that dim one by one, and let each hint slide in as a chip under the tiles ("Judges · Old Testament") rather than a numbered line. The "3 points" line becomes three coins that drop to two when a hint is opened ([desktop-13](emoji-stories/desktop-13-practice-round-2-hint-3.png)).
6. **Give the Daily Wordle-style guess tiles.** The three slots become three empty tile rows under the emoji from the start; each guess flips its row to red, amber or green with the verdict word; the solved screen flips the stats in and the Share button says "Copied", or shows "Copy this" over the text box when it cannot copy ([desktop-29](emoji-stories/desktop-29-daily-start.png), [desktop-36](emoji-stories/desktop-36-daily-share.png)).
7. **Stage the room and keep a score.** A full-bleed dark stage, the clues flipping in on tiles, the four choices as large lettered cards, Reveal flipping all four, and a simple team tally the leader taps ("Lions got it") with a winner named at the end instead of "That was the last story of the round." ([desktop-44](emoji-stories/desktop-44-room-story-1-choices.png), [desktop-49](emoji-stories/desktop-49-room-end.png)).

A small note for the owner: "Right!" and "✓ Right" are purple, the colour the site uses for the Gospels, while the share squares and most games use green for right. The words carry the meaning, so it is not a fault, but item 1 is the moment to pick one success colour.

## Phone problems

- **Starting from a scrolled page leaves the puzzle off-screen.** The game never resets the scroll when it changes screen. Start practice sits 786 pixels down and Start a room round about 1,000 down, so a player who has chosen a difficulty and tapped Start lands with the emoji 179 pixels above the top and the first row of answers cut in half ([phone-64](emoji-stories/phone-64-practice-as-landed-from-scrolled-start.png)). A room round lands with the emoji 386 pixels above the top and the Reveal button at the top of the screen ([phone-63](emoji-stories/phone-63-room-as-landed-after-start.png)). Taken from the top of the page, the same taps land correctly ([phone-51](emoji-stories/phone-51-practice-as-landed-after-start.png)).
- **The start page is long** at 2,685 pixels, with Start practice at the edge of the first screen and the room button a screen below ([phone-02](emoji-stories/phone-02-start-fullpage.png)).
- **The room does not fit a phone.** Each clue is 144 pixels tall and they stack vertically, so three clues are three screens, the controls wrap to two rows under them, and the keys line talks about a keyboard ([phone-44](emoji-stories/phone-44-room-story-1-choices.png)). The mode is for a big screen, but a leader will often drive it from a phone cast to a TV.
- **Play again is below the fold** on the score screen, at 903 pixels on an 844 screen after ten rows ([phone-22](emoji-stories/phone-22-practice-end-fullpage.png)).
- **Five-emoji stories wrap to a second line** at 390 wide, four tiles then one centred below, which breaks the left-to-right order the story depends on ([phone-53](emoji-stories/phone-53-practice-choice-focused.png)).
- **The "Stop this round" button crowds the back link** at both sizes (see Anything broken).
- Done well: answer boxes are 174 by 92 pixels and every button is at least 41 pixels tall; text is 16 pixels; nothing scrolls sideways; Next is within one screen of the emoji for right answers (655 pixels down) and just inside it for wrong ones (803); the next puzzle lands at the top; the typing box and its suggestions work at 390 wide ([phone-26](emoji-stories/phone-26-practice-typing-suggestions.png)).

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus on answers or Next.** Focusing a choice or the Next button changes nothing: outline none, no shadow ([desktop-53](emoji-stories/desktop-53-practice-choice-focused.png), [desktop-55](emoji-stories/desktop-55-practice-next-focused.png)). The site's base stylesheet switches outlines off everywhere, marked as the owner's call of 6 October 2026, so the game's own focus rule never applies. The site's own header buttons draw a focus ring with a shadow instead, so there is a site idiom the game could borrow.
- **Share gives no instruction when it cannot copy.** The text box appears, the status region stays empty, and nothing says "copy this" ([desktop-36](emoji-stories/desktop-36-daily-share.png)).
- **"Try again" on the failed-load screen is unstyled text**, the same colour as the sentence around it, with nothing to say it is a button ([desktop-50](emoji-stories/desktop-50-cant-load.png)).
- Done well: every emoji carries its name for a screen reader and the row is labelled "Clues: …"; missing emoji fall back to a dashed box with the word; right, close and wrong are always words as well as colours; the verdict heading takes focus after an answer; the Daily feedback is a live region; the typing box is a proper combobox with arrow keys and Escape; chips use aria-pressed; keys 1 to 4, Enter and the room's keys are explained on screen; the light theme reads cleanly ([desktop-19](emoji-stories/desktop-19-practice-round-4-light-reveal-right.png)).

## Anything broken

Nothing crashed, and the console was clean apart from the site's two font preload notices. Four things to fix:

- **"Stop this round" overdraws the back link.** The pill button starts exactly where the "All games" text ends, with no gap, so the button's border cuts into the last letters at both sizes ([desktop-52](emoji-stories/desktop-52-practice-top-back-link-and-stop-button.png), [phone-52](emoji-stories/phone-52-practice-top-back-link-and-stop-button.png)). The same happens with "Back to the start" in the Daily ([phone-59](emoji-stories/phone-59-daily-choices-after-wrong-pick.png)).
- **Screens keep the previous scroll position** (described under Phone problems). On the desktop this rarely shows because the start page fits; on a phone it hides the puzzle.
- **The Share status is empty** when the clipboard is blocked (described under Accessibility).
- **The room has no score**, even though the mode exists for groups and the code's own notes say the room keeps one. The round ends with "That was the last story of the round." ([desktop-49](emoji-stories/desktop-49-room-end.png)).

Two screenshot notes: the full-page captures show the site's sticky header part way down the page, which is the capture tool, not the site ([phone-44](emoji-stories/phone-44-room-story-1-choices.png)); and the failed-load screen was produced by blocking the stories data chunk, which this game handles with its own message rather than the site's generic "page has been updated" screen, so its Try again path is reachable ([desktop-50](emoji-stories/desktop-50-cant-load.png)).
