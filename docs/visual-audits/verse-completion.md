# Visual audit: Verse completion (/games/verse-completion)

Audited 10 November 2026 on the local preview. I played every way the game can be played, from a fresh start, at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): a full ten-card Pick round on Easy, with right answers, wrong answers and "I don't know"; a Medium card; a full Hard card built word by word; Type with whole words, partly right, word perfect and completely wrong, with the "Show next word" hint; Type with first letters; the round summary; My verses; leaving a round; reloading mid-round; the keyboard; the light theme; and a small phone (360 by 640). No code was changed. The screenshots are in [verse-completion/](verse-completion/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size, and the number gives the stage.

My step's own brief said only "audit the verse completion game", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief. I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

The start screen is a settings form ([desktop-02](verse-completion/desktop-02-start.png), [phone-02](verse-completion/phone-02-start.png)). Under the title and a line of text come four groups of pills: Answer (Pick or Type), Typing (whole words or first letters), Difficulty (Easy, Medium, Hard, each with a line of small print) and Verses from (Whole Bible, Old Testament, New Testament). Then a gold "Play" button and a "How to play" link. Above the pills sits a gold text line, "My verses: 0 · due: 0 · memorised: 0", which is also a link to your saved verses. After a round a flame and "Day streak: 1" appear beside it ([desktop-10](verse-completion/desktop-10-start-after-round-streak.png)). On the first visit a plain "How to play" box opens over the page with nine bullet points ([desktop-01](verse-completion/desktop-01-first-visit-help.png)).

Play is one card at a time inside a bordered box ([desktop-03](verse-completion/desktop-03-pick-easy-card.png), [phone-03](verse-completion/phone-03-pick-easy-card.png)). Above it a thin grey line reads "Round · 1 of 10", "Stars so far: 0" and "Leave round". On the card: the reference in small gold capitals, the start of the verse in the site's serif, and a short grey dash for every hidden word. What comes next depends on the mode:

- **Pick, Easy and Medium:** three endings, each a full-width bordered box in serif, and an "I don't know" link. You tap one ([desktop-12](verse-completion/desktop-12-pick-medium-card.png)).
- **Pick, Hard:** you rebuild the ending one word at a time. Three word buttons, numbered 1, 2 and 3, sit under the dashes, and the words you've chosen build up above them with a small cursor at the end ([desktop-13](verse-completion/desktop-13-pick-hard-word-by-word.png), [desktop-14b](verse-completion/desktop-14b-pick-hard-mid-play.png)). A wrong word shakes for a third of a second and is written in red with a line through it ([desktop-14](verse-completion/desktop-14-pick-hard-wrong-word-shake.png)). You get one try per word, and a Hard card can have thirty or more hidden words.
- **Type, whole words:** a plain text box with "Check", "Show next word" and "I don't know" under it ([desktop-17](verse-completion/desktop-17-type-words-card.png), [phone-17](verse-completion/phone-17-type-words-card.png)). Enter also checks.
- **Type, first letters:** one small text box. Each letter you type reveals the next word above it, in red with a line through it when the letter was wrong ([desktop-23](verse-completion/desktop-23-type-first-letters-mid.png), [phone-23](verse-completion/phone-23-type-first-letters-mid.png)).

When you answer, the card is replaced, with no motion, by the whole passage. Every hidden word gets a small mark after it: a green tick, a red cross, a red circle for a word you never gave, and, when you typed, the word you typed in grey with a line through it. Under the verse are three star outlines, filled gold for the stars you earned, sometimes a line like "2 of 5 words right · missed: 3", then "Next", "Add to My verses" and "Read in context" ([desktop-04](verse-completion/desktop-04-pick-easy-right-result.png), [desktop-05](verse-completion/desktop-05-pick-easy-wrong-result.png), [desktop-19](verse-completion/desktop-19-type-words-partly-right-result.png)). A perfect typed answer gets a small green "Word perfect" pill ([desktop-21](verse-completion/desktop-21-type-words-word-perfect-result.png)). The Next button takes focus, so Enter moves on.

After ten cards comes "Round finished": a heading, "Stars: 9 out of 30" in bold, "Verses dealt: 10", and a table of ten rows, each a reference, three small stars and an "Add" pill. Under it are "Add all missed to My verses", "Play again" and "Change settings" ([desktop-08](verse-completion/desktop-08-round-summary.png), [phone-08 full page](verse-completion/phone-08-round-summary-fullpage.png)). Tapping a reference opens the verse under its row ([desktop-09](verse-completion/desktop-09-summary-row-expanded.png)).

My verses is another table: a reference, a five-segment meter, "Box 1 of 5 · Learning", a due date, and two drop-down menus for filtering and sorting ([desktop-11](verse-completion/desktop-11-my-verses.png), [phone-11](verse-completion/phone-11-my-verses.png)). The learning system underneath is well made: five boxes, verses come back after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, a miss sends a verse back to box one, and typing a verse perfectly in a Hard round is what marks it memorised.

Everything works. Progress saves, a reload resumes the round at the right card ([desktop-25](verse-completion/desktop-25-after-reload-mid-round.png)), and the marking of typed answers is forgiving in the right ways: capitals, punctuation and small typos don't count against you. The problem is feel. Apart from the one shake on a wrong word in Hard mode, nothing in the game moves, flips, fills, slides or celebrates.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The start screen is a settings page.** Four rows of pills with small print, a Play button and a stats line. Nothing shows you a verse, a card or what play will look like. The "My verses: 0 · due: 0 · memorised: 0" line reads like a database row, and it's the first thing a new player sees.
- **Pick is a multiple-choice question.** Three bordered boxes stacked under a question, then a link saying "I don't know". That is a quiz form. The chosen box turns green or red, with no motion, and the question is swapped for the answer page.
- **Type is a web form.** A grey label, "Type the rest of the verse", a text area, and a Check button. It is the same shape as a comment box.
- **The dashes don't become the words.** The hidden words are shown as little grey dashes, which is a nice promise: fill these in. But on the answer the dashes simply vanish and the full verse is printed in their place. There is no moment where the words land in the gaps.
- **Right and wrong look almost the same.** Both show the same card, the same buttons, the same layout. The difference is a green tick versus a red cross, and gold stars versus grey outlines ([desktop-04](verse-completion/desktop-04-pick-easy-right-result.png) against [desktop-05](verse-completion/desktop-05-pick-easy-wrong-result.png)). The stars don't fill in, nothing pops on three stars, and no message cheers a perfect card except the small "Word perfect" pill in Type mode.
- **The round ends on a table.** "Round finished" is the same heading whether you scored 30 out of 30 or 0. The score is a bold line of text, and the rest is a list with Add buttons ([phone-08](verse-completion/phone-08-round-summary.png)).
- **Progress is a sentence.** "Round · 6 of 10" and "Stars so far: 3" are grey text in the header. There's no bar filling up, no row of ten cards with stars under them, nothing that shows how far you are at a glance.
- **Hard mode is a slog that looks like a redaction.** A thirty-word verse becomes three rows of grey dashes, then thirty one-shot guesses in a row from three words each ([phone-13](verse-completion/phone-13-pick-hard-word-by-word.png)). Get one wrong and it's gone. By the end, the result can be a wall of struck-out words with red crosses, almost unreadable ([desktop-15](verse-completion/desktop-15-pick-hard-result.png), [phone-15](verse-completion/phone-15-pick-hard-result.png)).
- **Leaving a round opens a browser alert.** "Leave this round?" is the browser's own grey confirm box, not something in the site's style.

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **Duolingo.** The nearest thing to "finish the sentence" that millions play. The words to use are a bank of tiles at the bottom; you tap one and it flies up into the sentence. A right answer slides up a green panel with a chime and the word "Excellent"; a wrong one slides up a red panel with the correct sentence. A thin bar across the top fills as the lesson goes on, and a streak flame lights up when you keep it. Every one of those is feel, not content, and every one is missing here.
- **The Bible Memory app and the Verses app.** The two best-known verse memorising apps. Both take one verse through stages: read it, then tap its words into order from a scrambled bank, then fill in blanks that grow in number, then type the first letters, then type it all. The verse stays on screen and the gaps fill in as you go. Progress on each verse is shown as a ring or badge that grows toward "memorised", not as "Box 1 of 5". This game has all of those stages under the hood, but shows them as four settings on a form instead of a journey for a verse.
- **Quizlet.** Its flashcards flip with a real card turn, and its Learn mode fills a bar as you go and throws confetti when a set is done. Its "Write" mode marks a typed answer by showing your answer and the right one side by side in green and red, which is clearer than struck-out words inside the verse.
- **Wordle and this site's own Timeline game.** Both are built around one reveal you watch: tiles turning, a row hopping on a win. Here the answer appears all at once.

What they share: the answer lands on the screen in a way you can watch, the gaps you were shown are the gaps that get filled, and the score at the end is a moment, not a table.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Fix the small broken pieces first (high impact, tiny effort).** They are listed under "Anything broken" below: the ticks and crosses that look as if they belong to the next word, the hint word shown as a wrong struck-out copy of itself, the struck-out word and the real word running together, and the browser alert for leaving a round. Each is a small change, and together they make the result card readable.

2. **Make the answer land in the gaps (biggest gain in feel, small to medium effort).** When you answer, keep the card on screen and fill the dashes one word at a time, left to right, about a tenth of a second apart. A right word drops into its dash and turns green. A wrong word drops in red and gives a short shake, with what you chose shown small above it. Then the three stars fill in one after another with a small pop, and three stars get a brief gold shimmer. Under "reduce motion" the words simply appear, as they do now. This one change turns every card into a reveal you watch.

3. **Turn the choices into tiles (high, small to medium).** On Easy and Medium, make the three endings proper cards: rounded, slightly raised, a lift on hover and a press-down on tap, like the Timeline cards should be. The chosen card turns green or red; the right one, if you missed it, glows green a beat later. On Hard, borrow Duolingo's bank: show the three words as tiles in a strip, and when you tap one it flies up into the next dash. Also shorten Hard: cap a card at about twelve hidden words by hiding only the last part of a long verse, so one card is a minute, not a chore. The keyboard shortcuts 1, 2 and 3 should stay.

4. **Give the round a real ending (high, small to medium).** Replace the "Round finished" table with a result panel: a big star count that counts up from zero, a title that changes with the score ("Word perfect round!", "Good round", "Keep going"), the streak flame lighting up, and "Play again" right there. Below it, the ten verses as a row of small cards with their stars, so you can tap one to read it. "Add all missed to My verses" becomes a single card with the missed count on it. On a phone the result panel should fit on one screen without scrolling.

5. **Make the start screen a lobby (medium, small to medium).** Show the three levels as three small cards, each with a tiny example on it: a famous verse with its last few words dashed for Easy, half dashed for Medium, nearly all dashed for Hard. Make Pick and Type a single two-way switch, and keep "Verses from" as chips below. Show the streak as a flame badge and My verses as a small ring (verses saved, how many memorised), not a text line. A round in progress shows as a card you tap to resume.

6. **Show progress through the round (medium, small).** Replace "Round · 6 of 10" and "Stars so far: 3" with a bar of ten short segments across the top of the card, each filling green, amber or grey as the card is answered, with the star total beside it. It is the first thing Duolingo shows, and it makes ten cards feel like a run.

7. **Make typing happen on the verse, not in a box (lower, medium).** In First letters, give each hidden word a blank tile as wide as the word, and let the typed letter fill the tile with the word. In Whole words, keep the text box but put it inside the card directly under the dashes, in the serif, with the dashes dimming as your typed words cover them. This keeps the eye on the verse while you type, which is the whole point of memorising.

## Phone problems

- **On a small phone (360 by 640) the floating feedback pencil button covers the first answer.** It sits on the bottom right corner of the first ending option ([phone-28b](verse-completion/phone-28b-small-phone-360x640-card.png)). On a 390-wide phone it sits below the card and covers nothing ([phone-03](verse-completion/phone-03-pick-easy-card.png)).
- **In Type mode the Check button will sit under the on-screen keyboard.** The text box is in the middle of the screen, and Check is just below it ([phone-17](verse-completion/phone-17-type-words-card.png)). When a phone keyboard opens, which usually takes the bottom third, the Check button lands at its top edge. Enter checks too, so it still works, but the button should sit above the box or the box should be nearer the top.
- **Hard mode's dashes make a block.** A thirty-word verse becomes three full rows of dashes and a stray cursor on a line of its own ([phone-13](verse-completion/phone-13-pick-hard-word-by-word.png)). On the result, the thirty or so marked words fill most of a screen with struck-out words and red crosses ([phone-15](verse-completion/phone-15-pick-hard-result.png)).
- **The round summary is three screens long** on a phone, and "Play again" is at the bottom ([phone-08 full page](verse-completion/phone-08-round-summary-fullpage.png)).
- **What works well:** the verse text is a good size on a phone, every button is tall enough to tap, nothing scrolls sideways at any size, the first-letters mode stops the phone keyboard from suggesting the next word, and a round that is interrupted resumes where it was.

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus.** Tabbing onto an ending or a word button shows nothing ([desktop-26](verse-completion/desktop-26-keyboard-focus-on-second-ending.png)): both the outline and the shadow are off. The cause is the site-wide rule that hides focus outlines, the same one the earlier audits found. The game itself is keyboard-friendly: 1, 2 and 3 pick words on Hard, Enter checks and moves on, Escape closes the help.
- **The marks are hard to read by eye.** Every word state has a symbol and a hidden label for screen readers, which is good. But the symbol sits after its word with a gap, so visually it reads as if it belongs to the next word: "and ✓all ✓these ✓things" ([desktop-04](verse-completion/desktop-04-pick-easy-right-result.png)). On a right answer with fifteen hidden words that is fifteen ticks floating through the verse ([phone-04](verse-completion/phone-04-pick-easy-right-result.png)).
- **A grey dash is all a sighted player gets for the hidden words**, and the dashes are low contrast on both themes. Screen readers get "11 words to complete", which is better than what the eye gets.
- **Good:** the word states never rely on colour alone, results are announced to screen readers, the help box is a proper dialog that takes focus and closes on Escape, the light theme reads well ([desktop-27](verse-completion/desktop-27-light-theme-result.png), [phone-27](verse-completion/phone-27-light-theme-result.png)), and the one animation is switched off under "reduce motion".

## Anything broken

- **A hinted word is shown as a wrong copy of itself.** In Type mode, after "Show next word" filled in "save", the result shows "~~save~~ save ✗" and the note "you typed save" ([desktop-19](verse-completion/desktop-19-type-words-partly-right-result.png)). The rule is that a hinted word counts as wrong, which is fair, but the display says you typed the right word and got it wrong. It should say "shown" with its own mark.
- **The ticks and crosses float between words**, as described under Accessibility. Confirmed in every result screenshot.
- **The struck-out word and the real word run together.** In Hard mode and in Type mode the word you gave and the right word are shown with no space between them, so a screen reader hears "amongand", "righteousnessunderstanding" ([desktop-15](verse-completion/desktop-15-pick-hard-result.png)). By eye there is a small gap, but the two words are hard to tell apart in a long verse.
- **"Leave round" uses the browser's own alert box.** It is outside the site's design, and on a phone it looks like an error.
- **The feedback pencil covers the first answer on a small phone**, as above.

No errors or warnings appeared in the browser console at either size, and nothing scrolled sideways.
