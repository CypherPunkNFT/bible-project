# Visual audit: Map quest (/games/map-quest)

Audited 10 November 2026 on the local preview. I played the game through from a fresh start at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): the game home; the whole Daily Five with a pin on the true site, a pin 900 km out, a near miss, a pin dropped with the keyboard after zooming in, and the last place; a reload in the middle and the Resume button; the summary, Share, and the home afterwards; a Beginner practice round in the Life of Jesus era with Play again; a Disputed sites round; the light theme; and a map that cannot load. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [map-quest/](map-quest/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size and the number gives the stage.

My step's own brief said only "audit map quest", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

The home is three text cards between hairlines ([desktop-01](map-quest/desktop-01-home.png), [phone-01](map-quest/phone-01-home.png)). "Daily Five" with a gold "Play today's puzzle"; "Practice" with three radio buttons for Beginner, Standard and Expert, a native drop-down for the era and "Start practice"; "Disputed sites" with an outlined button. Under them four serif numbers: Played, Streak, Best streak, Best score. There is no map anywhere on the home, no picture, and nothing that moves. After a finished day the first card shows "Puzzle #4 done: 2,697 of 5,000 points", the five coloured squares, Share and "Next puzzle in 1 h 10 min" ([desktop-27](map-quest/desktop-27-home-daily-done.png)).

The play screen is one big map with a name bar above it: "Place 1 of 5" on the left, the place name in large serif in the middle, "Total 0" on the right ([desktop-06](map-quest/desktop-06-daily-place1-map-drawn.png), [phone-06](map-quest/phone-06-daily-place1-map-drawn.png)). The map is the site's own earth-toned basemap with every name and border taken away, a faint grey crosshair in the centre, plus and minus buttons top right and the OpenStreetMap credit bottom right. Under it sits one grey sentence of instructions and a Confirm button that stays dim until a pin exists. In the dark theme the land and the sea are almost the same tone: a dull olive-brown against a dull slate, with no coastline stroke, so Cyprus, the Nile and the Dead Sea are hard to find ([desktop-29](map-quest/desktop-29-practice-jesus-place1.png)). The light theme is a proper map: sand land, blue sea, green hills ([desktop-40](map-quest/desktop-40-light-practice-pin.png)).

A tap or click puts down a gold teardrop pin ([desktop-08](map-quest/desktop-08-daily-place1-pin-dropped.png), [phone-08](map-quest/phone-08-daily-place1-pin-dropped.png)). It can be dragged, and a tap somewhere else moves it. Nothing marks the moment: no bounce, no shadow, no ring, and Confirm simply turns gold. Zooming in with the plus button and pressing Enter drops the pin at the crosshair ([desktop-21](map-quest/desktop-21-daily-place4-enter-pins-centre.png)). The game's stylesheet has no transition or animation at all; the only motion on the page is the map's own nine-tenths-of-a-second zoom when the answer is shown.

Confirm is the best moment and the page hides it. The map zooms to frame your pin and the true site, draws a thin dashed line between them and a small gold dot on the answer ([desktop-12](map-quest/desktop-12-daily-place1-reveal-right-map-top.png), [desktop-14](map-quest/desktop-14-daily-place2-reveal-wrong-map.png), [phone-14](map-quest/phone-14-daily-place2-reveal-wrong-map.png)). But focus jumps to "Next place" and the browser scrolls the page down to it, so what the player actually sees is the bottom slice of the map and a card ([desktop-10](map-quest/desktop-10-daily-place1-reveal-right.png), [phone-10](map-quest/phone-10-daily-place1-reveal-right.png)). On the phone more than half the map is above the top of the screen. The card itself is good: a coloured square or a bullseye emoji, the name, "4 km (3 mi) away, 977 points", the verse in serif with its reference as a gold link, "Mentioned in 2 verses", "Open in atlas" and the Next button ([desktop-15](map-quest/desktop-15-daily-place2-reveal-wrong-card.png)). Disputed sites adds "Scholars propose 2 sites. You were scored against the nearest." and draws the other sites as rings ([desktop-36](map-quest/desktop-36-disputed-reveal-map.png), [phone-36](map-quest/phone-36-disputed-reveal-map.png)).

The end is a heading and a table. "2,697 of 5,000 points", the five squares, five rows of name, distance and points, then Share, "Streak: 1 day", the countdown and "Back to the game home" ([desktop-24](map-quest/desktop-24-daily-summary.png), [phone-24](map-quest/phone-24-daily-summary.png)). Practice ends with "Play again" and "Change settings" ([desktop-33](map-quest/desktop-33-practice-summary.png)). No map of the five pins, no best round, no flourish for a bullseye.

Underneath, the game is sound. A reload in the middle of the Daily Five reopens at the right place ([desktop-17](map-quest/desktop-17-daily-resumed-after-reload.png)) and the home offers "Resume (2 of 5)" ([desktop-18](map-quest/desktop-18-home-mid-daily-resume.png)). The distance and points are read out to a screen reader. A map that cannot load gives a plain panel with Retry and All games ([desktop-42](map-quest/desktop-42-map-failed.png)). The light theme is clean ([desktop-41](map-quest/desktop-41-light-reveal.png)). The land drew within a quarter of a second of the map appearing on this local preview, though there is no loading sign if it ever takes longer. It is a working GeoGuessr-style engine with the celebration left out.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The home is a settings page.** Radio buttons 13 pixels across, a native drop-down and three submit buttons ([desktop-02](map-quest/desktop-02-home-fullpage.png)). Nothing on it looks like a map or a game.
- **The map is a blank form field.** A grey crosshair, two zoom buttons and a sentence of help text under it ([desktop-06](map-quest/desktop-06-daily-place1-map-drawn.png)). In the dark theme it is a dim rectangle until you look hard.
- **Dropping the pin gets no reaction**, and Confirm is a form's submit button.
- **The score is a sentence.** "4 km (3 mi) away, 977 points" in grey under the name ([desktop-15](map-quest/desktop-15-daily-place2-reveal-wrong-card.png)). There is no bar, no count-up, no colour on the map itself beyond a dot.
- **Progress is "Place 2 of 5" and "Total 1,161" in small grey text** at the two ends of the name bar.
- **The summary is a table with a Share button.** The coloured squares are the only hint of a game.

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **GeoGuessr** (web and app), the model for this exact mechanic: a small map that grows when you hover or tap it, a pin, a big green Guess button, then a results map that fills the screen with your pin, the true spot and the line between them while a score bar fills and the points count up. A round strip at the top shows five slots filling in; the final screen is one map with all five pins and a total bar. Three or four seconds of celebration per round is where the game lives.
- **Worldle** (the country-shape daily): six guesses, each answered with the distance, an arrow pointing toward the answer and a percentage of closeness, finishing in a grid of coloured squares to share. The arrow and percentage are the cheapest way to turn a miss into a clue.
- **Globle**: every guessed country is painted on a globe from cold to hot by distance, so wrong guesses build a picture rather than a list.
- **Seterra** and the GeoGuessr map quizzes ("Click on Jericho"): a blank map, a target name, a timer, a tick or cross drawn where you clicked, and a percentage at the end.
- **TimeGuessr**: a photo, a year slider and a map pin together, with two animated score bars per round and a daily share grid, which is the closest in spirit to a Daily Five.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Keep the answer on screen and let it play out.** Stop moving focus to "Next place" on Confirm, keep the map where it is, and instead draw the dashed line out from the pin over about half a second, pulse the gold dot on the true site, and count the points up from 0 in the card as a thin coloured bar fills under the place name. On the phone, put the card over the bottom third of the map as a sheet with Next within thumb reach. Today the reveal animation runs above the top of the screen ([phone-10](map-quest/phone-10-daily-place1-reveal-right.png), [desktop-10](map-quest/desktop-10-daily-place1-reveal-right.png)). This is one scroll behaviour, one line animation and one number tween, and it changes the whole feel.
2. **Make the dark map readable.** Lift the land two or three shades, deepen the sea, and add a soft coastline stroke, so the shape of the Mediterranean reads at a glance the way it does in the light theme ([desktop-40](map-quest/desktop-40-light-practice-pin.png) against [desktop-06](map-quest/desktop-06-daily-place1-map-drawn.png)). A handful of colour values in the quest style.
3. **Turn the name bar into a round strip and a score bar.** Five slots across the top that fill with the round's colour as you play, the place name under them, and the running total as a bar that grows, in place of the two small grey labels. The slots become the five share squares at the end, which GeoGuessr and TimeGuessr both do.
4. **Give the pin a drop.** Scale it in from above with a short bounce and a soft shadow, flash a ring on the map where the tap landed, and lift it slightly while it is dragged. Change the help sentence to a one-line caption that disappears after the first pin. A few lines of CSS on the marker element.
5. **End on a map, not a table.** The summary shows one map with all five pins and true sites and their lines, the five slots, and the list beneath it. A clean 1,000 gets a short burst of gold confetti and the bullseye scales in. "Best round" and "Best ever" sit beside the total.
6. **Put a map on the home.** The Daily Five card gets a small blank map with "Puzzle #4" on it and the five slots; Beginner, Standard and Expert become pill chips with one line each; the era becomes a row of six pills instead of a drop-down; the streak gets a flame. The three-column layout and the hairlines stay.
7. **Warn when the answer is off the map, and name the lakes.** In the Life of Jesus era three of the eighteen Beginner places (Babylon, Nineveh and Sidon) lie outside the opening view, and in Exodus and conquest six places do, so the pin has nowhere right to go until the player thinks to zoom out. Either open those rounds on the whole region or show a small "zoom out, this one is further afield" chip. And at the Life of Jesus zoom, Beginner shows no "Sea of Galilee" or "Dead Sea" even though the level promises sea and lake names; only "MEDITERRANEAN SEA" appears, and only in the whole-Bible view.

## Phone problems

- **Confirm is below the fold when the map opens.** At 390 by 844 the button's top edge sits at 823 pixels, so the player sees the map, three lines of help text and the top sliver of a gold button ([phone-06](map-quest/phone-06-daily-place1-map-drawn.png)).
- **The reveal scrolls the map away.** After Confirm the map's top is 235 pixels above the screen out of a 473-pixel map, so the line and the true site are usually out of view ([phone-10](map-quest/phone-10-daily-place1-reveal-right.png)); the player must scroll up to see what happened ([phone-12](map-quest/phone-12-daily-place1-reveal-right-map-top.png)).
- **The zoom buttons are 29 pixels square**, below the 44-pixel touch size, and they sit over the map's top-right corner where the pin sometimes needs to go.
- **The help text is three lines of 12.8-pixel grey** under the map ([phone-06](map-quest/phone-06-daily-place1-map-drawn.png)).
- **The summary lands scrolled** about 108 pixels down, so the "Map quest" title is half under the sticky header ([phone-24](map-quest/phone-24-daily-summary.png)).
- **The home is 2,868 pixels tall**, with the practice radios 13 pixels across ([phone-02](map-quest/phone-02-home-fullpage.png)).
- Done well: the map fills the width and takes 56 percent of the screen height, the name bar stacks neatly, Confirm goes full width, a second tap moves the pin without fuss ([phone-09](map-quest/phone-09-daily-place1-pin-tapped-again.png)), the reveal card fits without horizontal scrolling, and the era views are re-framed for a portrait box.

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus on any button.** Tabbing to "Play today's puzzle", Confirm or Next place shows nothing: the computed outline is none and there is no shadow ([desktop-03](map-quest/desktop-03-home-primary-focused.png)). The map itself does get a gold focus ring.
- **Dark-theme land and sea have almost no contrast**, about 1.05 to 1, which is a problem for low vision and for anyone on a dim phone.
- **The crosshair is drawn at 55 percent opacity**, so the keyboard target is faint.
- Done well: the map is an application region named "Map. Drop a pin where Caesarea Philippi stood"; Enter pins the centre and never confirms; a polite live region reads "Your pin was 4 km from Caesarea Philippi. 977 points."; "Next place" takes focus so a screen reader hears the result; the band emoji is hidden from screen readers and the text carries the meaning; the radios and drop-down are native and labelled; the reveal zoom honours reduced motion.

## Anything broken

Nothing broke. The console was clean at both sizes apart from two site-wide font preload notices; the map-blocked test logged the errors it should. Four things are worth knowing:

- **Some places start off the map.** The era opening views are fixed boxes, and the Life of Jesus and Exodus pools include places outside them (above, improvement 7). The round still scores correctly, but a Beginner given Nineveh in the Jesus era has no fair first guess.
- **Beginner's promised water names are thin.** Only "MEDITERRANEAN SEA" appeared in my runs, and nothing in the Life of Jesus view.
- **The help sentence and a disabled Confirm stay on screen under the "map couldn't load" panel** ([desktop-42](map-quest/desktop-42-map-failed.png)). Harmless, but they should go with the map.
- **Share fell back to the copy box in the audit browser** because a headless browser blocks the clipboard; the fallback worked as designed ([desktop-26](map-quest/desktop-26-daily-summary-after-share.png)), and the real Share or Copy path should be checked once on a phone.
