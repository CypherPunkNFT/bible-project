# Visual audit: Family charades (/games/charades)

Audited 10 November 2026 on the local preview. I played the game through from a fresh start at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): the start screen in both modes, with Teams on and with "How to play" opened; a full 30-second Guess it round with Got it, Pass, the keyboard shortcuts, Pause and Resume, the last ten seconds and "Time's up!"; the recap with a mark fixed and unfixed; Play again and a round ended early from the pause sheet; an Act it round for Little ones with the picture and "Hide card"; a two-team game through the hand-over, a reload during the hand-over, and the final standings; the light theme; keyboard focus; and a failed load. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [charades/](charades/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size and the number gives the stage.

My step's own brief said only "audit family charades", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written. One thing to know about the screenshots: the headless browser has no tilt sensor, so every round here was played with the buttons, which is also what a laptop or a tablet without motion access gets. The tilt path itself (tip down for got it, tip up for pass) could not be exercised. Charades has no wrong answer, only Got it and Pass, so "right and wrong" here means those two.

## What it looks and feels like now

The start screen is the site's article layout: a serif title, one line of lede, and the hub's line drawing of a person with raised arms and seated watchers ([desktop-01](charades/desktop-01-setup.png)). Under it are two bordered boxes, Guess it and Act it, then six rows of pill chips: who's playing, deck, difficulty, round length, controls, and a row of sound, pictures and teams ([desktop-02](charades/desktop-02-setup-fullpage.png)). Teams on adds two more chip rows and two text inputs inside a left-bordered block ([desktop-05](charades/desktop-05-setup-teams-on.png)). "How to play" is a fold-out with a small tilt drawing and a numbered list ([desktop-06](charades/desktop-06-setup-how-to-play.png)). The gold Start button sits under all of that, below the fold on a 900-pixel desktop and about one and three-quarter screens down on the phone ([phone-02](charades/phone-02-setup-fullpage.png)). Nothing moves, and nothing on the page shows what a round will look like.

Start brings up a black full-screen layer: a serif instruction ("Hold the phone on your forehead, or give it to a helper...") and a Start round pill ([desktop-08](charades/desktop-08-ready-guess.png), [phone-08](charades/phone-08-ready-guess.png)). Then 3, 2, 1, Go! in very large serif, each swapped in with no movement ([desktop-09](charades/desktop-09-countdown-3.png), [desktop-11](charades/desktop-11-countdown-go.png)).

The round itself is the game's strongest screen and it is almost bare. The scene title fills the middle in serif, fitted to three lines at up to 159 pixels on the desktop and 58 on the phone ([desktop-12](charades/desktop-12-live-card-1.png), [phone-12](charades/phone-12-live-card-1.png)). The timer is a plain number in the top-left corner, the score a smaller grey number top-right, and a pause pill under it. Along the bottom are two full-width bars, Pass in orange and Got it in green. For Little ones in Act it mode an emoji sits above the title ([desktop-29](charades/desktop-29-live-act-picture.png), [phone-29](charades/phone-29-live-act-picture.png)), and a "Hide card" pill blanks the card to a small grey "Card hidden" ([desktop-30](charades/desktop-30-live-card-hidden.png)). There is no card, no edge, no colour behind the words, no progress through the deck, and no sense of time passing other than the number ticking.

An answer floods the whole screen for seven tenths of a second: green with "✓ Correct!" or orange with "↷ Pass" in big sans ([desktop-13](charades/desktop-13-feedback-correct.png), [desktop-15](charades/desktop-15-feedback-pass.png), [phone-13](charades/phone-13-feedback-correct.png), [phone-15](charades/phone-15-feedback-pass.png)). It is a good, bold moment, and it is the only one in the game. The score goes 0 to 1 without a pop. The two buttons stay on screen at half opacity during the flood and go a muddy olive against the green and the orange. Under ten seconds the timer turns orange ([desktop-18](charades/desktop-18-timer-low.png)); the last five seconds tick if sound is on. At zero the screen says "Time's up!" in serif for just over a second ([desktop-20](charades/desktop-20-times-up.png)) with no score and no flourish. Pause is a black screen with Resume, End round now and a sound toggle ([desktop-17](charades/desktop-17-paused.png)).

The recap drops back into the site layout: "3 correct", "1 passed" and a green "New best!" in plain text, "Tap a mark to fix it", then a list with a circled tick, arrow or dash, the scene title, its reference and "Read the story" ([desktop-21](charades/desktop-21-recap.png), [phone-21](charades/phone-21-recap.png)). Tapping a mark swaps it instantly ([desktop-23](charades/desktop-23-recap-mark-fixed.png)). Play again and Change deck are the two pills. The team screens are the same material: "TURN 1 OF 1", the team name, "Pass the phone to your next guesser", a two-row table and a Ready pill ([desktop-35](charades/desktop-35-handover.png), [phone-35](charades/phone-35-handover.png)); "Final standings" with "The winner Lions" over the same table ([desktop-37](charades/desktop-37-final-standings.png)). A reload during the hand-over comes back to the same hand-over ([desktop-36](charades/desktop-36-handover-after-reload.png)).

Underneath it is well built: the clock keeps time from an end stamp, the buttons and the arrow keys both answer, a hidden tab pauses the round, the light theme reads cleanly ([desktop-40](charades/desktop-40-light-live-card.png), [desktop-41](charades/desktop-41-light-feedback-pass.png)), and the console was clean apart from the site's two font preload notices and the expected wake-lock refusal in a headless browser. The scenes list is the game's quiet strength: "Lot's wife looks back", "Ten lepers, one comes back", "Abraham counts the stars" are good charades, and every one links to its passage afterwards.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The start screen is a settings form.** Nine groups of radio chips and a submit button at the bottom. The only picture is the hub drawing, which does not show a card or a phone on a forehead ([desktop-02](charades/desktop-02-setup-fullpage.png)).
- **The card is not a card.** It is centred text on the page background. Heads Up players recognise the game by its coloured card; here nothing marks the scene as something you hold up ([desktop-12](charades/desktop-12-live-card-1.png)).
- **Time is a number in a corner.** No bar, no ring, no colour draining away. On the phone the number is 26 pixels tall, which the actors across the room cannot read ([phone-12](charades/phone-12-live-card-1.png)).
- **The score is a small grey digit** that changes with no movement, so a run of three in a row feels the same as the first.
- **"Time's up!" is a heading**, and the recap is a list with a heading. There is no result card, no comparison with your best, and the "New best!" is two words of green text ([desktop-22](charades/desktop-22-recap-fullpage.png)).
- **The team screens are a table.** Two names and two numbers. Nothing shows who leads, who is up, or that something was won ([desktop-37](charades/desktop-37-final-standings.png)).

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **Heads Up!** (Warner Bros, the app this design follows): the deck has a painted cover; the countdown flips the card up; the word sits on a solid coloured card edge to edge, with the timer as a big number across the top; tilt down flashes green with a chime, tilt up flashes orange with a buzz; the round ends with a list of every word marked got or missed, and the player can tap a mark to change it. The structure here is the same. The difference is colour, a card, and a loud timer.
- **Charades! (Fat Chicken Studios)** and **Reverse Charades**: the same forehead loop with a thick coloured frame on the card and the score drawn as large as the timer, so both can be read across a room.
- **Catch Phrase** (Hasbro): the timer is a tick that speeds up as time runs out, so the room tenses together without anyone looking at the device.
- **Time's Up!** and **Monikers** (card games): each card is a picture with a name, and the deck of cards still to go is visible on the table, so everyone can feel the round shrinking.
- **Jackbox** and **Kahoot** team screens: the hand-over names the team in its colour with a big "You're up", and the end is a podium that builds from third place up, with a short confetti burst for the winner.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Make the timer something the room can see.** A thin bar across the top of the round layer that drains from full to empty, in the page's gold, turning the pass orange under ten seconds, and the number itself at least twice its current size on the phone. The bar is one element with a width transition driven by the clock the game already keeps ([phone-12](charades/phone-12-live-card-1.png)).
2. **Draw the card.** Put the scene on a rounded card with a solid tint (the page's gold at a soft mix, or a tint per deck: the "In the beginning" deck one colour, "Jesus' life" another) and a two-pixel border, with the deck name in small capitals at the top and "card 4 of 12" at the bottom. Keep the text sizing exactly as it is. On an answer, let the card slide off in the direction of the tilt (down for got it, up for pass) and the next one slide in from the far side, so the flood becomes a card going past rather than a colour change. One wrapper element and two keyframes.
3. **Let the score react.** When Got it lands, the score number pops up a step and settles, and a thin green strip at the top of the card counts the scenes got this round as a row of ticks. On the recap, count the "3 correct" up from zero over half a second and show a bar against your best, with a short gold burst on a new best ([desktop-21](charades/desktop-21-recap.png)).
4. **Fix the flood's buttons.** During the Correct and Pass flash, hide the two buttons entirely or turn them into white outlines, instead of leaving them at half opacity where they go olive ([desktop-13](charades/desktop-13-feedback-correct.png), [phone-15](charades/phone-15-feedback-pass.png)). A single CSS rule on the feedback state.
5. **Give the countdown and "Time's up!" a beat.** Scale each count in from large to normal over its second, and have "Time's up!" land with the round's score under it ("Time's up! 4 scenes") before the recap takes over. The screens exist already; this is two keyframes and one line of text ([desktop-20](charades/desktop-20-times-up.png)).
6. **Show the game on the start screen and shorten the form.** Replace the hub drawing with a tilted sample card ("Noah builds the ark" on the tinted card from item 2) and move the long tail of settings (controls, sound, pictures, teams) behind one "More options" fold, so Start sits within the first screen on the phone ([phone-02](charades/phone-02-setup-fullpage.png)). Remember what was chosen as the game already does.
7. **Make the team screens feel like turns.** Give each team a colour chip on the setup, show the hand-over as "Doves, you're up" in that colour with a large score pair, and build the final standings as a podium with the winner's name largest and a short burst, keeping the same table underneath for ties ([desktop-35](charades/desktop-35-handover.png), [desktop-37](charades/desktop-37-final-standings.png)).

## Phone problems

- **Start is far down the page.** The hero drawing, two stacked mode boxes and nine chip rows push the Start button to about 1410 pixels on an 844-pixel screen, and the Best score sits beside it where it is easy to miss ([phone-02](charades/phone-02-setup-fullpage.png)).
- **The timer and score are small for a forehead game.** On the phone the timer is 26 pixels and the score 18 pixels, in the top corners. The guesser holds the phone facing away, so these are read by the actors from a few metres ([phone-12](charades/phone-12-live-card-1.png)).
- **Chips are 36 pixels tall**, under the 44-pixel touch size, on every row of the setup ([phone-01](charades/phone-01-setup.png)). The pause pill in the round is 40 by 31.
- **The notice on the ready screen** fills the width with four lines of grey on a tinted band before the instruction, and it pushes the Start round button below centre ([phone-08](charades/phone-08-ready-guess.png)). It is also the first thing a new player reads.
- Done well: the Pass and Got it bars are 64 pixels tall and full width, the card text is large, the feedback flood fills the screen, the recap rows fit with the "Read the story" link wrapping under the title, and the hand-over and standings fit without horizontal scrolling ([phone-21](charades/phone-21-recap.png), [phone-35](charades/phone-35-handover.png)).

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus.** Tabbing to a chip or to the Got it button shows no ring; the stylesheet's focus-visible rule for this game does not take effect, and this matches the site-wide choice noted in earlier audits ([desktop-45](charades/desktop-45-setup-keyboard-focus-chip.png), [desktop-46](charades/desktop-46-live-keyboard-focus.png)). In the round the arrow keys and Space cover answering, so the cost is mainly on the setup form.
- **The chip legends are 10-pixel uppercase grey** text, and the "Best in 60 seconds" line is 14-pixel grey.
- **The feedback flood is colour plus a symbol plus a word** in every case, so it does not rely on colour alone. The timer going orange is colour-only, but the ticking covers it when sound is on.
- Done well: the round layer is a labelled dialog that takes focus and locks the page behind it; the timer has a spoken "30 seconds left" and the score a spoken "Score 3"; the feedback is a polite live region; the recap heading and the hand-over heading take focus; each recap mark is a button named "Correct: Lot's wife looks back. Tap to change"; and reduced motion switches off the transitions that exist.

## Anything broken

Nothing broke in play. Three things are worth knowing:

- **The ready-screen notice blames the player for a refusal they never made.** In this browser, which has the motion permission function but no sensor, the notice read "Tilt is off because motion access was not allowed. You can play with the buttons, or reload the page and allow it" ([desktop-08](charades/desktop-08-ready-guess.png)). The code treats a permission call that fails as a refusal. A laptop or desktop user who was never asked anything is told to reload and allow it, which will not help. The sensorless wording ("This device isn't sending tilt here, so use the buttons") is the right one for that case.
- **A failed scenes load shows the site's generic screen, not the game's.** When I blocked the game's data chunk the page showed "This page has been updated. Reload to get the latest version" ([desktop-44](charades/desktop-44-cant-load.png)) rather than the game's own "The scenes could not be loaded" with Try again. On the built preview the scenes ship inside a chunk, so the site's chunk-failure handler gets there first. Acceptable, but the Try again path appears unreachable in the build.
- **Keyboard focus is invisible**, as described above.

Two screenshot notes: the full-page captures show the site's sticky header part way down the page ([desktop-06](charades/desktop-06-setup-how-to-play.png)); that is the capture tool, not the site. And the second load-failure screenshots ([desktop-47](charades/desktop-47-cant-load-json-only.png), [phone-47](charades/phone-47-cant-load-json-only.png)) show a normal setup screen because blocking only the JSON file does nothing on the built site.
