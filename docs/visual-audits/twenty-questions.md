# Visual audit: Bible twenty questions (/games/twenty-questions)

Audited 10 November 2026 on the local preview. I played both modes through from a fresh start at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide). In "I think, you guess" I answered honestly as Moses, using the game's own fact list, and the site got him in 17 on desktop and 20 on the phone. I also played a game it could not win, took the five extra questions, gave up, told it the answer was Deborah and disputed one of its notes, and tried a name it does not know. In "You ask the questions" I played Easy with hints on and off, opened a "Why?" verse, filtered the list, guessed wrong, guessed right, lost a game by running out of turns, reloaded mid-game to check Continue, and looked at both screens in the light theme. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [twenty-questions/](twenty-questions/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size and the number gives the stage.

My step's own brief said only "audit twenty questions, both modes", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written. Two notes on method: the desktop guess screens were re-shot once because my own keyboard-focus test had scrolled the page, which is not something the game does; and the game's one anonymous end-of-game report went to the local preview server only.

## What it looks and feels like now

The start screen is the best screen in the game ([desktop-01](twenty-questions/desktop-01-start.png), [phone-01](twenty-questions/phone-01-start.png)). The arched drawing of a thinking head with a question cloud, a gold Yes and an outlined No, and a row of dots sets up the whole idea at a glance. Under it, a serif title, a one-line invitation, two modes separated by hairlines, an Easy or Hard choice for the second mode, a "Played, guessed, won" tally once you have played ([desktop-40](twenty-questions/desktop-40-light-start.png)), and a "How does it guess?" fold-out that explains the game honestly ([desktop-03](twenty-questions/desktop-03-start-how-open.png)).

"I think, you guess" is one question at a time ([desktop-05](twenty-questions/desktop-05-guess-first-question.png)). Twenty small hollow dots and "TURN 1 OF 20" at the top, the question in large serif, three wide pill buttons, Yes, No and a greyer Don't know, and quiet Undo and Quit links. Answer, and the next question replaces the last with no motion at all; one dot fills in. That is the entire loop for up to twenty-five turns ([desktop-08](twenty-questions/desktop-08-guess-mid-play.png), [desktop-15](twenty-questions/desktop-15-guess-kept-going-25.png)). After six Don't knows a grey line says "That's all right: try a few yes or no answers if you can" ([desktop-13](twenty-questions/desktop-13-guess-dont-know-streak-hint.png)); from turn fifteen it says "Thinking hard…" ([desktop-09](twenty-questions/desktop-09-guess-thinking-hard.png)). The guess arrives as "Are you thinking of… Moses?" with his one-line brief and two buttons ([desktop-10](twenty-questions/desktop-10-guess-the-guess.png)); a wrong guess adds a gold diamond to the dot row. Winning is a serif "Got it in 17!", the person's name, brief and story, a Read about link, a collapsed "Your answers" list, Play again and Swap ([desktop-11](twenty-questions/desktop-11-guess-won.png), [desktop-12](twenty-questions/desktop-12-guess-won-answers-open.png)). Losing is "I'm out of questions" with Keep going or give up ([desktop-14](twenty-questions/desktop-14-guess-out-of-questions.png)), then "You win! Who were you thinking of?" over a name box ([desktop-17](twenty-questions/desktop-17-guess-lost-who-was-it.png)). Name the person and you get "It was Deborah" and a stack of every place your answers differed from the game's notes, each with "Oh, I see" and "I still think your note is wrong" ([desktop-20](twenty-questions/desktop-20-guess-lost-it-was-lesson-fullpage.png)). A name it does not know gets a plain "Nobody in the game by that name" ([desktop-22](twenty-questions/desktop-22-guess-lost-not-in-list.png)).

"You ask the questions" is two columns on desktop ([desktop-24](twenty-questions/desktop-24-reverse-empty.png)): "Who am I thinking of?", the dots, a gold Make a guess button with a Show hints tick box, then a log on the left and a long grouped list of ninety-two questions on the right with a search box. Click a question and it greys out in the list with its answer under it, and the log gains a bold line, "Is your person an Israelite? — Yes." ([desktop-25](twenty-questions/desktop-25-reverse-two-answers.png)). Some answers carry a "Why?" that opens a verse card with the reference ([desktop-26](twenty-questions/desktop-26-reverse-why-open.png)), which is a genuinely good touch. Hints on adds "People still possible: 218" and a thin gold bar under every open question showing how evenly it splits them ([desktop-27](twenty-questions/desktop-27-reverse-hints-on.png)). Opening the people list drops 218 rows into the page above the columns ([desktop-28](twenty-questions/desktop-28-reverse-people-possible-open.png), [desktop-29](twenty-questions/desktop-29-reverse-hints-fullpage.png)). Make a guess opens a name box ([desktop-31](twenty-questions/desktop-31-reverse-guess-box.png)); a wrong name becomes a muted "No, it isn't Jacob." in the log ([desktop-32](twenty-questions/desktop-32-reverse-after-wrong-guess.png)). The end is "Yes! It was Rehoboam. You got it in 4." or "Out of turns. It was Zipporah.", the person, and "The site would have needed about 15 turns" ([desktop-33](twenty-questions/desktop-33-reverse-won.png), [desktop-36](twenty-questions/desktop-36-reverse-lost.png)).

Underneath, it is solid. Undo and the quit confirmation work ([desktop-06](twenty-questions/desktop-06-guess-after-undo.png), [desktop-07](twenty-questions/desktop-07-guess-quit-confirm.png)), the keys Y, N, D and U do what the code promises, a reload offers Continue your game ([desktop-38](twenty-questions/desktop-38-start-continue-after-reload.png)), the light theme is clean ([desktop-41](twenty-questions/desktop-41-light-guess-question.png), [desktop-42](twenty-questions/desktop-42-light-reverse.png)), and there were no console errors in any run. The engine is clever and the writing is warm. But nothing on screen ever moves, nobody is doing the guessing, and the climax, the guess, looks exactly like question twelve.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The guesser has no presence.** The start screen draws a thinking head, then the game never shows it again. Twenty questions is a game against a mind; here it is a survey. Akinator is nothing but its genie.
- **Every turn looks the same.** Question in, question out, with no motion, no pause and no reaction to Yes or No. A player cannot feel the net closing. The only trace of progress is a row of eleven-pixel dots ([desktop-08](twenty-questions/desktop-08-guess-mid-play.png)).
- **The guess is not an event.** "Are you thinking of… Moses?" uses the same layout, type and buttons as a question ([desktop-10](twenty-questions/desktop-10-guess-the-guess.png), [phone-10](twenty-questions/phone-10-guess-the-guess.png)). This should be the drumroll moment of the whole game.
- **Winning and losing are headings.** "Got it in 17!" and "You win!" are a serif line over a bio and two buttons ([desktop-11](twenty-questions/desktop-11-guess-won.png), [desktop-17](twenty-questions/desktop-17-guess-lost-who-was-it.png)). Nothing celebrates either side. The person it guessed gets no picture, no card, no flourish.
- **"You ask the questions" is a reference list with a transcript.** The right column is a ninety-two-row settings page; the log is plain text lines ([desktop-24](twenty-questions/desktop-24-reverse-empty.png)). The suspects, the thing the whole mode is about, are hidden behind a hint toggle and shown as a bare list of names.
- **The lesson screen is a form.** Eight boxed disagreements, sixteen pill buttons ([desktop-20](twenty-questions/desktop-20-guess-lost-it-was-lesson-fullpage.png), [phone-19](twenty-questions/phone-19-guess-lost-it-was-lesson.png)). The content is good; the shape is a feedback survey.

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **Akinator**, the web and phone game this mode is modelled on: a genie sits beside every question and changes pose with your answers, leaning in, rubbing his chin, looking puzzled on "Don't know". A progress bar fills. When he is ready, the screen changes colour, he says "I think of…", the character's picture and name slide in on a card, and a win gets a flourish and a "Played again?" The questions themselves are the same plain Yes, No, Probably, Probably not, Don't know.
- **20Q**, the handheld ball and 20q.net: a tiny screen and blinking lights, but it narrates ("I am thinking…", "Was it a close call?") and counts questions out loud, so the pressure is felt. It also says "You win!" with a flourish when it loses, which this game does in words only.
- **Guess Who?**, the board game with face cards: the model for "You ask the questions". You see all the suspects at once, and each answer makes you flip rows of faces face-down. The remaining faces are the score. That is exactly the "People still possible" count, made visible and satisfying.
- **Heads Up!** and **Cluedo** for feel: a big single card with the question or name centred and bold, swipes for Yes and No, and a detective notebook that ticks suspects off as the evidence comes in.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **Bring the thinking head onto the question screen and let it react.** Put a small version of the start-screen drawing beside or above the question on every turn. On Yes the cloud shows a tick for a moment, on No a cross, on Don't know the question mark wobbles, and from turn fifteen the cloud grows and "Thinking hard…" sits inside it. The question text fades out and the next fades in over about a quarter of a second. One illustration file and a handful of CSS keyframes; the layout does not change.
2. **Make the guess a reveal.** When the engine decides to guess, hold a beat: the dots pulse gold, the cloud reads "I think I know…", then a card scales in with the person's name in large serif and their brief, and the buttons change to "Yes, that's them!" and "No". Won: the dots fill gold one after another, the card gets a short confetti burst in the site's gold and cream, and "Got it in 17!" counts up from 1. Lost: "You win!" deserves the same burst, because the player beat the site. On the phone the card fills the empty middle of the screen ([phone-10](twenty-questions/phone-10-guess-the-guess.png)).
3. **Turn "People still possible" into a suspect board.** In "You ask the questions", show the candidates as a grid of small name cards above the log, with the count as a heading, and on each answer the eliminated cards flip face-down and shrink away. On Easy that is 146 cards, so show the grid as rows of tiny cards with names on hover, or the first forty with "and 104 more". Hints are then the visible game, not a tick box. This replaces the 218-row inline list ([desktop-28](twenty-questions/desktop-28-reverse-people-possible-open.png)).
4. **Make the log a conversation.** Each asked question becomes a bubble on one side, the answer a bubble on the other, newest sliding in at the bottom with the "Why?" verse folding out of the answer bubble. A wrong guess is a bubble with a crossed-out name. Same data, same words, a shape that reads as play.
5. **Give the three answer buttons character.** Yes gets a gold fill on press and a tick, No an outline that flashes, Don't know stays soft but gets a small shrug glyph, and each shows its key letter (Y, N, D) as a small grey tag on desktop so the shortcuts are discoverable. A quick scale-down on press is enough to make a tap feel like a tap.
6. **Fill the phone's empty middle in guess mode.** The question hugs the top and the buttons sit at the bottom for thumbs, which is right, but two thirds of the screen is black ([phone-05](twenty-questions/phone-05-guess-first-question.png)). Centre the question vertically in the space with the reacting head from improvement 1 above it.
7. **Shape the lesson screen as cards you swipe or tap away.** One disagreement at a time, "You said Yes, our notes say No, here is the verse", with "Oh, I see" and "I still think you're wrong" as the two choices, and a count "3 of 8". The sixteen-pill stack becomes a short, almost playful review, and the verse evidence the site already has can be shown rather than linked.

## Phone problems

- **The question list is a scroll box inside a scrolling page.** On the phone the ninety-two questions are capped at eighty percent of the screen height and scroll on their own ([phone-24](twenty-questions/phone-24-reverse-empty.png)). The finger scrolls the list until it hits the end, then the page jumps instead. With hints on, the "Why?" verse card above the box pushes it further down ([phone-27](twenty-questions/phone-27-reverse-hints-on.png)).
- **The answer lands above the list.** When the list is scrolled, tapping a question greys it out and prints its answer underneath, which is enough, but the bold log line appears above, under the sticky header ([phone-44](twenty-questions/phone-44-reverse-after-tap-answer-off-screen.png)). A chat-style log that sits below the list, or a bottom sheet for the list, would fix both this and the point above.
- **The people list is a full-screen wall.** Opening "People still possible" shows 144 rows of names and briefs before the log or the question list ([phone-28](twenty-questions/phone-28-reverse-people-possible-open.png)).
- **Tapped buttons keep their hover ring.** After a tap, Don't know keeps the gold hover border until something else is tapped ([phone-05](twenty-questions/phone-05-guess-first-question.png), [phone-13](twenty-questions/phone-13-guess-dont-know-streak-hint.png)). A hover-capable media query would stop it.
- **The lesson screen is long.** Each disagreement is two full-width pills, so eight disagreements are about three screens of buttons ([phone-19](twenty-questions/phone-19-guess-lost-it-was-lesson.png)).
- Otherwise the phone layout is good: every button is at least 48 pixels tall, the answer buttons are at thumb height, the start page fits with one scroll ([phone-02](twenty-questions/phone-02-start-fullpage.png)), and Continue your game appears after a reload ([phone-38](twenty-questions/phone-38-start-continue-after-reload.png)).

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus on any game button.** Tabbing to Yes, No, Make a guess or a question shows nothing: the computed outline is none and there is no shadow ([desktop-04](twenty-questions/desktop-04-start-primary-focused.png) is the primary button with focus on it). The site deliberately has no focus outline, but a game driven by keys needs some sign of where you are, even a gold underline like the games hub uses.
- **The keyboard shortcuts are invisible.** Y, N, D, U and Enter all work, but nothing on screen says so.
- **The hint bars explain themselves only by tooltip.** The thin gold split bar has a mouse-hover title and no text, so touch and screen-reader users get nothing from it.
- Done well: the dots are hidden from screen readers and replaced by "Turn 7 of 20"; the question area is a polite live region; the reverse log announces each answer; result headings take focus; guess dots are diamonds as well as gold, so colour is never the only signal; muted text on the dark background is about 6.8 to 1 contrast; buttons are 48 pixels or taller throughout; there is no motion to need a reduced-motion rule.

## Anything broken

Nothing broke. No console errors or warnings at either size in any run. Two things are worth knowing:

- On an Easy game whose secret was James son of Zebedee, the end screen said "This one is hard: the site couldn't find them in 20 turns either" ([phone-33](twenty-questions/phone-33-reverse-won.png)). That is the site reporting that its own guess mode cannot find one of the twelve apostles, which is a notes problem rather than a visual one, but a player reads it as the game admitting it is weak.
- When the site loses and the player types a name it does not have, the two messages "Nobody in the game by that name" and "No one else by that name either" stack with a lone "Someone not on this list" link ([desktop-22](twenty-questions/desktop-22-guess-lost-not-in-list.png)). It works, but reads like an error state rather than the end of a game the player just won.
