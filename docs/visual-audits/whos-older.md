# Visual audit: Who's older? (/games/whos-older)

Audited 10 November 2026 on the local preview. I played the game through from a fresh start at desktop size (1440 wide) and again at phone size (390 wide): the start screen with Relaxed opened and "How we date people" unfolded; a Streak run of six right answers and then a miss, answered by keyboard and by tap, through the five-in-a-row milestone to the run-over screen, the missed pair unfolded and Share; a reload in the middle of a run and the Continue button; a Relaxed Easy run with a wrong answer, a right answer and all three hearts lost; the light theme; and a game that cannot load. No code was changed. The screenshots are in [whos-older/](whos-older/). Names starting `desktop-` or `phone-` give the screen size and the number gives the stage.

My step's own brief said only "audit who's older", so there was nothing to reconcile with the shared brief, and I followed the shared brief as written.

## What it looks and feels like now

The start screen is a title, one line of rule and two bordered boxes ([desktop-01](whos-older/desktop-01-home.png), [phone-01](whos-older/phone-01-home.png)). The left box holds a gold "Streak" pill, a sentence and "Best streak: 0"; the right box holds an outlined "Relaxed" pill that unfolds four small pills, Easy to Mixed ([desktop-04](whos-older/desktop-04-home-relaxed-open.png)). Under them is a fold-out about dating. There is no picture, no pair of cards to look at, and nothing moves. The games hub draws two portraits with an hourglass between them on this game's tile; the game itself has no picture at all.

A round is a tiny uppercase status line ("STREAK   STREAK 0", or "RELAXED   SCORE 0" and three red hearts), the serif question "Who lived first?", a grey hint, and two cards side by side with a small key number, the name in serif and a one-line tag ([desktop-06](whos-older/desktop-06-streak-round1.png), [phone-22](whos-older/phone-22-relaxed-round1.png)). On the desktop the two cards are wide and squat, about 354 by 128 pixels, and the page is mostly empty with the footer starting a third of the way down. On the phone they stack, each the full width and about 106 pixels tall ([phone-06](whos-older/phone-06-streak-round1.png)). Hover lifts the border to gold; a press shrinks the card by two percent. That is the whole of the game's motion before an answer.

The answer is the game's best part and it is handled quietly. The right card takes a solid green border and the words "Came first"; a wrong pick takes a dashed red border and "Wrong"; the other card dims ([desktop-09](whos-older/desktop-09-streak-reveal-right.png), [desktop-14](whos-older/desktop-14-streak-reveal-wrong.png)). Under a hairline comes "Right!" with a tick, "Daniel came first", a timeline strip with two soft blurred bars and a year axis, one sentence of reason ("Even on the dating systems that bring them closest, Daniel was born at least 558 years before Peter"), a dated note on each person with a link to their page, "Something look wrong? Tell us", and a Next button ([desktop-10](whos-older/desktop-10-streak-reveal-right-fullpage.png)). Five in a row adds a gold-tinted pill, "5 in a row — a good start!", which pops in over half a second ([desktop-13](whos-older/desktop-13-streak-milestone-5.png)). The marks appear instantly, with no stamp, no shake, no count-up and no pause; the strip does not draw itself in. The game's "one stays" rule, where the newer person moves to the left card and a new challenger arrives, happens in the data but not on screen: Adam was on the right in one round and on the left in the next with nothing to show he had moved ([desktop-12](whos-older/desktop-12-streak-round4-midplay.png)).

On the phone the reveal slides up as a sheet over the lower three quarters of the screen in a quarter of a second ([phone-08](whos-older/phone-08-streak-reveal-right.png)). It is a good sheet, with a soft shadow and a rounded top, but it covers both cards completely, so the green and red card marks are never seen on a phone, and the Next button sits below the bottom of the screen inside the sheet, so every round needs a scroll before it can go on ([phone-10](whos-older/phone-10-streak-reveal-right-sheet-scrolled.png)).

The run-over screen is "Streak of 6" in large serif, a gold "New best!" pill that pops in, "Your best streak: 6", three pills (Play again, Share, Back to start) and "The one you missed" as a bordered row that unfolds into the same reveal block ([desktop-16](whos-older/desktop-16-streak-runover.png), [desktop-18](whos-older/desktop-18-streak-runover-missed-open.png), [phone-15](whos-older/phone-15-streak-runover.png)). Relaxed ends with "1 right" and the three missed pairs ([desktop-29](whos-older/desktop-29-relaxed-runover.png)). No confetti, no bar against your best, no picture.

Underneath, it is sound. A reload mid-run brings back the start screen with "Continue (streak 1)" ([desktop-21](whos-older/desktop-21-home-after-reload-midrun.png), [desktop-22](whos-older/desktop-22-streak-resumed.png)); keys 1 and 2, the arrows, Enter and Space all work; Share falls back to a copy box when the clipboard is blocked ([desktop-20](whos-older/desktop-20-streak-runover-shared.png)); the light theme is clean ([desktop-33](whos-older/desktop-33-light-reveal.png), [phone-32](whos-older/phone-32-light-reveal.png)); and a game that cannot load shows a plain "The game didn't load" with Try again ([desktop-35](whos-older/desktop-35-cant-load.png)). The explanations are the strongest thing on the page: honest, specific and short. The game is a Higher Lower engine with the show taken out.

## Where it reads as a form or quiz instead of a game

- **The start screen is two settings boxes with submit buttons.** Nothing on it shows what the game is ([desktop-02](whos-older/desktop-02-home-fullpage.png)).
- **The cards are text boxes.** Name, tag, a key number, one hairline border. No portrait, no colour, no era, no feeling of holding two cards ([desktop-06](whos-older/desktop-06-streak-round1.png)).
- **The score is 11-pixel grey capitals** in the top-left corner. A streak of five looks the same as a streak of zero.
- **Answering is a form validation.** A border changes colour and a label appears, all at once, then a block of reading with a Next button at the end ([desktop-14](whos-older/desktop-14-streak-reveal-wrong.png)).
- **The run-over screen is a heading and three buttons.** The "New best!" pill is the only thing that behaves like a game ([desktop-16](whos-older/desktop-16-streak-runover.png)).

## How the best-known games of this kind do it

- **The Higher Lower Game** (the web game this design names as its model): two tall cards fill the screen edge to edge, each with a full-bleed photo behind the name. You tap; the right card's number counts up over a second, a big green tick or red cross stamps in, the score at the top ticks up, and then the whole right card slides left to become the next question while a new card slides in. That slide is the signature of the genre and it is exactly the "one stays" rule this game already has in its data.
- **Wikitrivia** (Tom J. Watson's Wikidata timeline game): you drag a card onto a growing timeline; a right placement flips it green and shows its year, a wrong one flips red and still shows the truth. Three hearts drawn large at the top, and the lost one goes grey with a wobble.
- **Timeline** (the Asmodee card game): the pleasure is the card itself, with a picture on the front and the year on the back, and the reveal is the flip.
- **Duolingo and Wordle streaks**: the streak is a large number with a flame that pulses when it grows, and a milestone is a full-screen moment rather than a tinted line of text.

## Improvements, ranked by impact for the effort

1. **On the phone, keep the verdict visible and put Next in reach.** Open the sheet below the cards, or shrink the two cards into a single strip at the top of the sheet showing the tick and the cross, and pin Next to the sheet's bottom edge so it is always on screen. Today the cards' marks are hidden under the sheet and Next is below the fold in every round ([phone-08](whos-older/phone-08-streak-reveal-right.png), [phone-10](whos-older/phone-10-streak-reveal-right-sheet-scrolled.png)). A layout change to one sheet and a sticky footer.
2. **Make the answer a moment, and show the chain.** On a tap: the chosen card lifts, the tick or cross stamps in with a short scale, a wrong card shakes once, the timeline bars draw in from the left over half a second, and the streak number pulses up. On Next: the winner slides into the left slot and a new challenger slides in from the right, the way Higher Lower does, so the player sees the "one stays" rule instead of a fresh random pair ([desktop-12](whos-older/desktop-12-streak-round4-midplay.png)). A few keyframes on the card classes that already exist, plus one transition on Next.
3. **Give the cards presence.** Taller cards, the name much larger, and a soft era tint or a small medallion (the person's initial, or the hub tile's drawing style) at the top of each card, with Old Testament and New Testament people in different tints. The desktop cards are 128 pixels tall in a 720-pixel column and the page is two thirds footer ([desktop-07](whos-older/desktop-07-streak-round1-fullpage.png)). Keep the 1 and 2 key hints but make them a corner badge.
4. **Turn the status line into a scoreboard.** A large streak number with a flame beside the question, hearts at 22 pixels that break and grey out when lost, and the milestone as a short burst over the cards rather than a tinted line of text ([desktop-13](whos-older/desktop-13-streak-milestone-5.png)). Also stop the top line reading "STREAK 0" while the sheet reads "Run over — streak of 6" ([desktop-14](whos-older/desktop-14-streak-reveal-wrong.png), [phone-13](whos-older/phone-13-streak-reveal-wrong.png)).
5. **End on a result card.** The final number large with a bar against your best, a short gold confetti burst on a new best, the missed pairs each with their small timeline strip already open, and Share as a card preview of the share text. Today it is a heading and three pills ([desktop-16](whos-older/desktop-16-streak-runover.png)).
6. **Make the timeline strip the proof it is meant to be.** Thicker, unblurred bars with the years written under each bar's ends, a minimum visible width when a window is only a few years (Herod's is a dot, [desktop-13](whos-older/desktop-13-streak-milestone-5.png)), and the gap between the bars labelled "at least 558 years", so the sentence underneath becomes a caption rather than the only evidence. Prefer the strip stacked with its labels on one line on the phone, where they now wrap ([phone-13](whos-older/phone-13-streak-reveal-wrong.png)).
7. **Show the game on the start screen.** Replace the two bordered boxes with one sample face-off (two small cards, "Moses or Paul?") above a single row of Streak and Relaxed buttons, difficulty pills at 44 pixels, and the best streak as a number with a flame. Keep the dating fold-out as it is.

## Phone problems

- **The reveal sheet hides both cards.** The sheet starts 186 pixels from the top and the cards sit between 242 and 518, so the green and red marks are never seen on a phone ([phone-08](whos-older/phone-08-streak-reveal-right.png)).
- **Next is below the bottom of the screen in every round.** On an 844-pixel screen the button's top edge sits at 868, and at 934 when a milestone is shown. Every round needs a scroll inside the sheet first ([phone-10](whos-older/phone-10-streak-reveal-right-sheet-scrolled.png), [phone-12](whos-older/phone-12-streak-milestone-5.png)).
- **The status above the sheet contradicts the sheet** after a miss ("STREAK 0" over "Run over — streak of 6", [phone-13](whos-older/phone-13-streak-reveal-wrong.png)).
- **The difficulty pills are 36 pixels tall**, under the 44-pixel touch size ([phone-04](whos-older/phone-04-home-relaxed-open.png)).
- **The hearts are 15 pixels** and easy to miss at the top left ([phone-22](whos-older/phone-22-relaxed-round1.png)).
- **The timeline labels wrap** onto two lines on the phone, and the second lane's label collides with its bar ([phone-23](whos-older/phone-23-relaxed-reveal-wrong.png)).
- **A sticky hover.** After tapping Streak, the first card of the new round shows the gold hover border because it sits under where the finger was ([phone-06](whos-older/phone-06-streak-round1.png)). It reads as a pre-selection.
- Done well: full-width cards that are easy to hit, the sheet's slide and shadow, Share's copy-box fallback, Continue after a reload, and the run-over screen fits without horizontal scrolling ([phone-15](whos-older/phone-15-streak-runover.png)).

## Accessibility

- **No visible keyboard focus on the cards or buttons.** This is the site-wide choice recorded in the stylesheet on 6 October 2026, not a game bug, but in this game it matters more: the focused card is the one Enter will pick and nothing shows which it is ([desktop-03](whos-older/desktop-03-home-streak-focused.png)). The 1 and 2 keys soften this.
- **The status line is 11.5-pixel uppercase grey text.** Readable in contrast terms, but small for a score.
- **After an answer on the desktop the page jumps 160 pixels** so the reveal heading can take focus, which hides the question under the sticky header ([desktop-09](whos-older/desktop-09-streak-reveal-right.png) against [desktop-12](whos-older/desktop-12-streak-round4-midplay.png)). A gentle scroll or no scroll would be kinder.
- Done well: the cards are real buttons named "Daniel, prophet and royal adviser" with key shortcuts declared; right and wrong always carry an icon and a word as well as a colour; a polite live region reads "Right! Even on the dating systems…"; focus moves to the result heading; the hearts have a spoken count; the timeline has a spoken summary; the milestone is a status; reduced motion switches off the slide and the pops.

## Anything broken

Nothing broke. The console was clean at both sizes apart from the site's two font preload notices and the expected clipboard warning when Share fell back to the copy box. Four things are worth knowing:

- **Adam and Eve are giveaways.** Whenever either is dealt the answer is always them, and the "one stays" rule keeps them on for a second round. In my desktop Streak run Adam was in rounds three and four and Eve in rounds six and seven, so four of seven rounds were decided by "is it Adam or Eve". A rule that keeps them to one round and never as the stayer would help.
- **The score line contradicts the reveal** after a miss in Streak ("Streak 0" against "streak of 6"), described above.
- **No reveal quoted a verse** in about twenty rounds at both sizes. The verse box exists in the code, so it is rare rather than missing; worth a check that the pairs with a deciding verse are being dealt.
- **"Something look wrong? Tell us" is the brightest thing above Next** on the phone sheet, so the report link draws the eye before the button that continues the game ([phone-10](whos-older/phone-10-streak-reveal-right-sheet-scrolled.png)).
