# The medieval church: the page

Study 33 of the Academic shelf, widened per [ACADEMIC_PAGE_RUN.md](../../../../../Pages/Topics/StudyPlan/ACADEMIC_PAGE_RUN.md)
section 4 (sequence 28, step 9). Address: `/study/the-medieval-church` (preview: http://127.0.0.1:8931/study/the-medieval-church).
Research record: [33-the-medieval-church.json](../../../../content/academic-research/33-the-medieval-church.json). Design note:
[design.md](../../../../content/academic-research/33-the-medieval-church/design.md). Build script:
[build-medieval-study.py](../../../../scripts/build-medieval-study.py).

**Direction in one sentence:** the medieval church from 451 to 1350, read as a set of powers and witnesses. The page now shows the
parting of East and West as two strands that drift apart over centuries rather than one break in 1054, and sets the crusades and the heresy
trials out by who wrote each one down, with the site's own word kept apart from the evidence.

## What the widening added

- **The record starts at 451.** The council of Chalcedon is the first step of the new East and West section.
- **East and West** (section 04, `split` in the record): seven steps from Chalcedon (451) through the Acacian break (484–519), Photius
  and Bulgaria (863–880), the two sentences of 1054, the sack of Constantinople (1204), the council of Lyon (1274) and the council of
  Florence (1439). Drawn from the record of study 23 (Traditions: shared roots and divisions) and re-checked where that record had not
  been. A note at the end of the drawing says the sentences of 1054 were removed in 1965.
- **Crusade and inquisition** (section 05, `crusade` in the record): nine events, five crusade and four heresy trials (Clermont 1095,
  the Rhineland 1096, Jerusalem 1099, Hattin 1187, Béziers 1209, the bishops' and friars' commissions 1184–1233, Avignonet and
  Montségur 1242–44, the bull that allowed torture in 1252, and Bernard Gui 1307–25). For each it gives which of five sides left a written
  witness (Latin, Greek, Jewish, Muslim and the accused), how historians disagree, and a "Where this site stands" panel. Persecution is
  reported on every side that did it.
- **Schools and parish condensed** so the teaching text stays near 8,000 words. The pairs, stations and rounds were lightly trimmed, and
  the boundaries, outcomes, premise and reach rewritten for the new span.
- **Words:** before, 7,279 by the shelf counter (6,980 reading words). After, 8,692 (8,241 reading words); the build script counts about
  7,531 words of reading text on the page itself.
- **One old fault fixed:** the Concordat of Worms quotation used the King James word "thy", which the Bible check rejected. It is now
  restated in plain words and not set as Scripture.
- Backup of the record before widening:
  [33-the-medieval-church.before-widening-2026-11-07.json](../../../../../Pages/Topics/StudyPlan/batch/backup-checks/33-the-medieval-church.before-widening-2026-11-07.json).

## Check notes

- A passage that is not yet settled is highlighted, and clicking it opens an editor's note (Still open · what was asked · the words
  checked · what is still needed). Escape closes it. Opening a note closes a record sheet, and the other way round.
- Markers are allowed only in reading text. Buttons, the drawing's labels and the table use `plainText()`, so a marker can never show
  as `{{check…}}` there. The tests and the browser both found none on the page.
- Highlights show unless the release build sets `VITE_CHECK_NOTES=off`.

## What the build script refuses (proved)

`python scripts/build-medieval-study.py` writes the data, and `--check` reports whether it is current. Exit codes: 0 built or up to date,
1 stale, 2 refused (nothing written). A harness kept in the job folder (`refusals_cd313e0c.py`) changed a copy of the record in memory
13 ways. Nothing was written. **All 13 were refused**, each with a message naming the place:

1. John 18:36 with one word changed ("world" to "earth");
2. a verse that does not exist (John 18:99);
3. an inline "· BSB" quotation that was not word for word (Matthew 26:52, "knife" for "sword");
4. a route that leaves the site (`//evil.example/x`);
5. a source link that is not https (`javascript:`);
6. a marker with no note (c99);
7. a note whose marker was removed (c6);
8. a malformed marker with no closing;
9. a marker in a field the page shows inside a button;
10. a crusade event running past 1350, into study 24's years;
11. a witness said to write before the event;
12. a note missing what it was;
13. a step of an unknown kind.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

- **Proved:**
  - every verse shown is the site's Berean, word for word, and every reference exists;
  - every marker has its note and every note its marker;
  - no marker syntax reaches the page;
  - every link stays in the site or is https;
  - the data is current with the record.
- **Not proved:** that any date, figure or reading is right. The 16 notes above are where this page knows it is unsure. The Latin
  witnesses come from English selections of 1912 and 1921, and no Arabic or Hebrew text was read.

## Could be confused with

- **Traditions: shared roots and divisions (23):** it also tells the parting of East and West. This page draws on its record, but stops
  at Florence and reads the steps as evidence. Study 23 covers the traditions that came out of the parting.
- **Reform, renewal and revival (24):** it begins where this page stops (1350 to 1517 and on). The build refuses any crusade event
  after 1350 for that reason.
- **The early church (21):** its neighbour before 451, with the councils up to Chalcedon.
