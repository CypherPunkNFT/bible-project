# New Testament history: from Herod to the fall of Jerusalem (study 43)

Address: `/study/new-testament-history`. Built 2026-11-07 by the Academic page run (sequence 28, step 4), from
[the research record](../../../../content/academic-research/43-new-testament-history.json) and
[its design note](../../../../content/academic-research/43-new-testament-history/design.md).

**Direction (the design note's recommended one, built as written):** a lattice of reigns. Four lanes (Rome, Herod's
family, the governors of Judea, the New Testament), every reign a bar, and each New Testament anchor a line crossing
them, so the reader sees at once which rulers fix a year and whether each ruler's own date is a fixed point or a
reckoning. The owner had not chosen between the note's three directions; this is its recommended one, and the choice
is still the owner's.

## How the data is built

`python scripts/build-nt-history-study.py` (no arguments; `--check` confirms the file on disk is current and writes
nothing). It writes only inside this folder, atomically, with LF line endings and sorted keys, so the output is the
same on every run. It:
- takes every listed verse from `data/text/bsb`, refusing a verse that does not exist, a label that does not name its
  own book, chapter and verse, or a phrase that is not word for word the Berean;
- finds every Bible reference written in the record's prose, including ranges and chains ("John 2:13; 6:4; 11:55",
  "Acts 24:24, 27"), refuses one that does not exist, and supplies its Berean words so it opens in the sheet
  (77 verses under 66 references);
- checks the three quotations marked "(… · BSB)" word for word against the Berean;
- checks the timeline (four lanes, no bar backwards, none off the chart unless its note says so);
- defines the lattice's eight anchors (the birth, Luke 3:1–2, the forty-six years, the cross, Agrippa's death, Gallio,
  Festus, the fall of Jerusalem): each year range must be stated in the record's own words at a named place, every
  ruler the verse names must be in office at every year of it by the record's own bars, and the three disputed dates
  (birth, Luke 3:1, cross) must be drawn twice. The early birth is measured against Herod's death in 4 BC, the late
  one against 1 BC;
- checks Paul's five fixed points and the six reckoned stages against the record's words;
- refuses any outside link, and any page link that is not an address src/App.tsx routes.

## What the checks prove, and what they do not

They prove that every reference exists, every Berean quotation is word for word, every bar and anchor year is the
record's own, the disputed dates are drawn twice, and the page links only inside the site. They do **not** prove any
reading or reckoning right, and they do not check the quotations from Josephus, Tacitus, Pliny, Suetonius, Nostra
Aetate or the Mishnah (the record's own editorial list says these must be checked against held public-domain texts
before release).

## Choices this page made that the owner may want to change

- **Solid or hatched is this page's reading, not the record's.** The record does not mark each bar as fixed or
  reckoned. The build marks a bar hatched when the record gives its years as "about", as two dates, as ranges, as
  points or as open-ended: Herod, Philip, Agrippa II, Pilate, the birth, the cross, Paul and Masada. The page says so
  under the drawing.
- **The anchors are the page's,** chosen from the design note's list (Luke 3:1, the forty-six years, Gallio, Festus)
  plus the birth, the cross, Agrippa's death and the fall. Their years are checked against the record's words.
- **No pictures.** All four pictures in the record (Pilate stone, Arch of Titus, JUDAEA CAPTA coin, Masada) have an
  empty licence, so none is shown; the page says so.
- **No source links.** The record's sources have no web addresses; they are listed with what was read of each.
- **Masada:** the design note asks for one plain sentence with no detail; the record's own Masada record says the
  defenders "chose death rather than capture" and that seven survived. That text is shown as written, only inside its
  record sheet. If the safe-messaging rule wants less, the record needs editing, not the page.
- **The heading's lead** ("Every ruler the New Testament names is a thread out of the Bible. Follow it, and ask what
  it fixes.") is the page's own line, drawn from the design note; the record's premise follows it in full.

## Which page this could be confused with, and why it is not

- **Israel among the empires** has its own empire timeline: its lanes are empires and its unit is the reign. Here the
  lanes are offices, and the unit is the crossing line ("who fixes this year?").
- **Manuscripts** draws a dot field on one line of time ("what survives"). Here nothing is a dot field.
- **Between the Testaments** (not yet a page) is told as steps; on this page steps appear only as record lists and
  inside the sheet.
